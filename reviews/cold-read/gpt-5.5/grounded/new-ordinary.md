## Reader reaction

I felt this chapter in my chest more than anywhere else. The sex is hot, yes, especially because it starts with that almost comic, breathless undressing through the house, but what landed hardest was the way this has become domestic. Not dull domestic. Claimed domestic. Her drawer, her bag, his bathroom, the shirt that has to be “filled” again. The title feels exact: this is the new ordinary, and the ordinary is wildly intimate.

Vee feels so happy here that it almost hurts to read. She is trying to be reasonable, trying to keep school and sleep and selfhood intact, and then she lasts five nights before she’s back across the dark grass. I loved the honesty of that contradiction: she needed the time away, and she also wanted him to miss her enough to resist it. “The plain grace of that had cost her more than a fight would have” felt painfully true. She is not being coerced there; he gives her room. But some part of her wants his want spoken with more force, because he so often answers through action instead of words.

Pace remains intensely attractive to me in the same specific way he has been: not because he says the perfect thing, but because he makes room, receives, notices, and acts. The door open, the slow kiss, the hands on her face. Then later, when she hands him the flannel and he thinks, for one second, that she is giving it back, that little hurt made him feel more vulnerable than usual. And then he hears what she is really asking and just puts it on. No teasing her, no making her explain the need twice. That was the swoon of the chapter for me. “He stood there now wearing two shirts in his own warm kitchen because she’d told him to” is absurd and tender and devastating.

The erotic charge worked because it felt like hunger after restraint. Five nights matters. Her body “published the number” before she could, and that embarrassment is intimate rather than humiliating because he does not punish her for it. The peak for me sexually was not only the first rush, though that was strong; it was the way the hurry gives over into his slowness, “the long work of him,” and then into food. The chapter makes sex, appetite, and being housed by someone feel like one continuous sensation.

The pizza scene should not be erotic, exactly, but it is charged because it is Pace’s attention made visible. I loved that she realizes he is not performing the cooking for her: “He’d have made the pizza exactly like this for an empty house.” That makes his care feel less theatrical and more rooted. And the tasting continues the book’s whole waking-up-of-the-mouth motif: pizza stops being “flat orange sameness” and becomes separate, countable pleasures. She is learning discernment through him, not just indulgence.

I don’t feel new suspicion in this chapter from the scene itself. The Randi/Pace secret still sits under everything I know, and that makes the tenderness dangerous in the larger shape of the book, but on the page here Pace’s behavior does not feel off to me. If anything, the chapter makes the secret hurt more because this domestic trust is so real. Vee is building a life-rhythm around him without knowing the whole room she is standing in.

The chapter title, “The New Ordinary,” first sounded gentle, maybe even transitional, but after reading it it has teeth in a quiet way. This is what normal now means: sex after five nights away, a drawer in his bathroom, dinner after, the shirt going back and forth between them like a private custody arrangement of scent and need. *A Polite Invitation* keeps feeling less like a single invitation and more like a whole life being made invitational one small permission at a time. *With a Long Spoon* still gives me that sense of intimate danger: sweetness, feeding, distance, caution. There was no front-cover tagline; the blurb’s closing beat promises “every open door is a temptation and every step is hers,” and this chapter feels exactly there. She walks through the door herself. That is the heat, and also the problem.

What I want next is for this ordinary to keep deepening, because I am completely pulled into it. What I dread is that the more real and tender it becomes, the worse the concealed Randi/Pace truth will feel when it breaks. My guess from here is that the shirt, the drawer, and the “school nights” rhythm are going to make Vee feel more like she belongs at Pace’s house than anywhere else, which makes any later rupture much harder.
