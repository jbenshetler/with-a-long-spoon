## Reader reaction

This chapter felt like a held breath released underwater. After the sweetness and danger of Pace’s attention in the last chapter, I expected maybe more date momentum, but instead the book drops me into Vee with Cassie, and it makes Vee’s body feel real in a different register: not erotic first, but exposed, remembered, watched, then suddenly private again. I felt protective of her almost immediately. The suit fitting “and giving her nowhere to hide” hurt, because I’m carrying all of Vee’s mother-shame and appetite-shame and body-awareness with me, and here the page gives her a public female body before it gives her comfort.

Vee moved me a lot here. The chapter sharpens how practiced she is at surviving being looked at. Her “face” on the pool deck is not exactly confidence and not exactly falsehood; it is a survival costume, “the walk of a girl who had *meant* to arrive.” I admired her for it and hated that she needs it. The boys in the bleachers are gross in a very ordinary way — not grand villains, just the background weather of having a body other people feel entitled to comment on. “You won’t need floaties with *those!*” is exactly ugly because it is stupid and casual and socially rewarded by the laugh afterward.

Cassie rises enormously for me here. I already trusted her, but this chapter makes that trust bodily. She does not rescue Vee by making a scene; she rescues her by giving her exactly the small private pressure-valve she needs. “Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman.” is funny, but the tenderness is in the fact that she keeps it low, just for Vee. Later, when Vee comes up changed, Cassie again does not ask, does not force confession, does not make Vee’s sadness perform. She just starts a water fight. That is one of the most loving things anyone has done in the book so far, precisely because it is not elegant.

Pace is absent, and that absence matters. After the last chapter, where his attention felt almost medicinal to Vee, this chapter reminds me Vee has sources of relief and selfhood that do not come from being chosen by Pace or Randi. The water belongs to her before them. Cassie knows some version of her before them. That makes me feel a little less helpless for Vee, but it also makes me dread more sharply the possibility that Pace and Randi’s beautiful attention will get into a place that was once hers.

Randi is absent too, but I felt her shadow only indirectly: the contrast between Randi’s polished, socially managed femininity and Vee’s “nowhere to hide” abundance is strong. Vee’s body is not styled into power the way Randi’s often is. Vee has to carry it through spaces where people comment. The chapter does not make Randi suspicious here, because she is not on the page, but it reminds me that the kind of being-looked-at Vee is used to may make Pace’s and Randi’s more careful looking feel overwhelmingly different.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no new suspicion generated by this chapter’s action. Cassie feels more trustworthy than ever. The “off” feeling remains around the larger triangle because I know from the jacket and prior chapters that Vee is being drawn into something arranged without her knowledge, but this chapter itself is clean of manipulation. Its ache comes from ordinary public exposure and old private loss.

Erotic charge is low but not absent, because the chapter is about bodies, swimsuits, being watched, and Vee’s fullness in a suit from when there was “less of her to fill it.” But it is not trying to arouse me so much as make me feel the cost of being visible. The strongest sensual writing is underwater: “the swallowing green,” “the cold coming up from below,” “the light coming down through the surface in slow sheets.” That is bodily and intimate, but not sexualized. The peak is Vee underwater, weightless and unseen. The boys’ comments are anti-erotic; they make looking feel cheap.

Friction: I did not have much. The line “Nobody was going to look at Cassie in it. Cassie knew that, and it didn’t bother her one bit” is blunt in a way that briefly made me check the narrator’s cruelty, but it settles because Cassie’s own ease is the point: she is not diminished by not being looked at, and the chapter proves her power is elsewhere. I bought it by the end.

The chapter title, “Water Wings,” is lovely after reading. On the surface it points to floaties, especially because the boys make the gross joke about Vee not needing them. But by the end it feels more like what helps Vee stay afloat: the old swimming body, the underwater privacy, Cassie’s friendship, the childishness of splashing, maybe the parts of herself from before “having a body” became trouble. Water wings are childish aids, but here the water itself supports her. The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, keeps feeling like a warning about appetite, distance, and caution around sweetness. This chapter adds another kind of distance: underwater distance from the gaze. The tagline still promises that Vee’s choices will be real and happy and hers, but this chapter makes me remember how much a “yes” can come from hunger for gentleness after years of being watched wrong.

What I want next: I want to know whether this pool scene sits before or after more time with Pace — it feels like Vee recovering into herself after the date, or maybe just continuing ordinary life. I want Cassie to stay close. I dread Vee comparing the safe anonymity underwater with the intensity of Pace’s attention and choosing the attention because it feels like being seen without being mocked. Guess from here: Pace’s careful looking will land even harder because the chapter has shown what ordinary careless looking does to her.
