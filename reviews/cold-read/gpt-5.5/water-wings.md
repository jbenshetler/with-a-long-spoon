### Chapter record

The chapter is titled “Water Wings.”

Vee appears at the rec pool with Cassie. She wears a green-and-white one-piece suit from when she was sixteen and did synchronized swimming; it still fits, but there is more of her now, and it gives her nowhere to hide. She rolls her towel tightly from one end with practiced hands and carries it at her side rather than covering herself. She puts on a public face: chin level, shoulders open, walking like she meant to arrive.

Cassie appears with Vee at the rec pool. She fights her way into an old plain navy suit “like it had insulted her family.” She has a boxy frame and would rather wear a t-shirt and cut-offs. She wears the suit because it has a job to do, not because she likes it, and she is not bothered that it does not flatter her. She bangs her locker shut, loops a stretchy wristband with the locker key over her wrist, calls the men in the bleachers “Tossers,” and privately jokes to Vee, “They will never know the love of a woman.” In the pool she wades only to the middle of her ribs, plants herself, spreads her arms along the surface, and says she will be right there “Standing. Like a person.” Later, seeing Vee come up changed, she does not ask what is wrong; she sends a sheet of water toward Vee, starting a splash fight.

The rec pool requires a real swimsuit: no gym shorts, no sports bra. A lifeguard at the desk checks. The space smells of chlorine and floor cleaner. The route to the water runs along the bleachers. The pool has wet tile, shallow water sloping toward a colder smoothness, a dark stripe painted across the bottom where the shallow end gives out, green underwater light, surface light moving in sheets, and a large noisy room above with a lifeguard’s radio and flat booming sound.

Unnamed guys in the bleachers call out at Vee. One says, “You won’t need floaties with those!” and the bleachers laugh. Another says, “Legs for days.” Vee answers the first with a long blink and a half-pursed mouth, and the second with an eyeroll aimed at the ceiling.

Vee sets the rolled towel on the bench before entering the deep water. Underwater, the room’s noises vanish. She feels cold below, light above, and her own heartbeat close and steady. She hangs weightless with nothing holding her and nothing to hold. Her hands remember an old synchronized-swimming scull, palms feathering in figure-eights to keep her down and turned. She looks up at the surface from below. She thinks nobody up there can see her and nobody down here wants anything. She remembers loving this before she had a body to be a problem, feels that the water has never been taken, stays until her lungs ask and a beat past that, and grieves a little.

Vee surfaces slowly, blinking the light, noise, chlorine, and body-publicness back into focus. Cassie notices without pressing. Cassie’s first splash barely reaches her, which makes the outrage funnier. Vee swims ten feet back toward Cassie, reaches the shallow where she can stand, and splashes her back. Cassie takes the wave full in the chest without changing expression. Vee makes an “ugly” shriek-laugh she has not made in a while and splashes Cassie again. The splashing wins over grief. The chapter ends with the body that was a problem seeming to stay up on deck with the rolled towel, waiting.
