## Reader reaction

This chapter made me feel the trap more sharply because it is so sweet. Vee is happy, and I’m happy for her in my body before I’m worried in my head: she gets to be the girl with news, the girl someone wants to hear, the girl Randi calls “my dear friend” in public. That lands. But almost every warm thing here has a second temperature underneath it.

Vee is so easy to love right now. She is lit up from Pace and from the date, but the chapter makes clear how quickly that light can be dimmed by a room. The Smoothie Bird scene hurts because she walks in “feeling, frankly, like a person things happened to. Good things,” and then the room of matched girls makes her cardigan feel like “an apology.” That is painfully exact. I trust Vee’s feelings, but I don’t trust the situation around her. She keeps choosing not to look at small discomforts: she says Cassie and Randi are just different “people,” and “Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie.” Then with the oat milk: “felt faintly that she had said the oat milk because Randi was standing right there, and let herself not look at that either.” Those little avoided recognitions matter to me.

Randi is dazzling again, and dangerous again, and I think this chapter moved her in a complicated way. I feel her charm. I feel why Vee leans into her. Her “dear friend” moment absolutely works on me: taking Vee’s hand in front of Brooke, naming her as more than a stats acquaintance, spending social capital for her. I believed Vee’s feeling that “it had cost something.” But because I already know Randi helped choose Vee without telling her, this public choosing feels both protective and possessive. Randi’s forgetting Pace’s name — “Paul?” — is the chapter’s most interesting little lie-or-not-lie. On the surface it is casual, funny, nothing. To me it is a deliberate-looking disguise, because she knows Pace intimately. The text earns suspicion there: Randi is performing ignorance in front of Vee. I don’t know whether Vee clocks it; I don’t think she does. I do.

Pace is absent but very present, because Vee retells him with almost devotional softness. The erotic charge around him peaks not in touch, but in Vee saying, “I stopped keeping track of myself. I can't say it better than that. I wasn't watching myself the way I always am. I just wasn't.” That is beautiful and frightening. It is exactly the tender version of surrender the book keeps circling. Randi hears it, too: “You let him choose for you. And you liked it.” That line made me tense because she knows too much about liking being chosen for, arranged, handled. Then Vee’s kiss shifts under Randi’s wording. Vee meant to say “I made an exception,” but Randi supplies “Because you couldn't not. Because of him,” and Vee accepts the smaller, more overcome version: “Because of him.” That’s erotically potent, but it also feels like a tiny theft of Vee’s own framing. Cassie asked last chapter whether anyone talked her into it; here Randi doesn’t exactly talk her into anything, but she does shape the story.

Cassie’s exit bothers me in a good way. She sees Randi light up and reach for Vee; “whatever had been in them was folded away.” Then she leaves. I feel her protective absence. She doesn’t interrupt, doesn’t claim Vee, but the fact that Vee “did not watch her go” stung. I still trust Cassie most, and I missed her almost immediately.

Brooke is chilly in a fascinating way because she is warm. Her smile gives “nothing but more smile,” and it measures Vee “top to bottom, and stay[s] warm the whole way.” That is exactly the kind of social power that scares me: nothing overt to object to, everything felt. The unnamed older woman with Brooke is even more unnerving because she says nothing and simply watches Vee like “a thing once you are done turning it over.” I don’t know who she is yet, but she lands as a social auditor.

Erotically, this is a 1 for me: no sex on-page, but the charge is real in recounting surrender, kissing, and being claimed. The hottest line is probably “I stopped keeping track of myself,” because it fuses relief, desire, and danger. The most romantic line is Randi’s “Vee’s my dear friend,” with the hand held on the table. The most worrying romantic line is the same one.

Friction: I mostly bought the scene, but Randi’s fake “Paul?” is almost too glaring given what I know. Not bad-glaring; reader-glaring. I don’t believe for a second she forgot his name, so I experience it as performance. Also, “a drink the color of a bruise that sounded like a sentence handed down” is vivid and funny, though slightly showy enough that I noticed the prose showing off.

The chapter title, “Dear,” means several things after reading: Randi calls Vee “my dear friend”; Vee is made dear in public; and “dear” also means costly, which the chapter explicitly points toward when Vee feels Randi has spent something. It didn’t give the chapter away beforehand, but it recolors the social claim afterward. The book title *With a Long Spoon* still feels like a warning about eating with charming devils, or taking sweetness from a distance because closeness is dangerous. The tagline is practically humming here: Vee’s yeses and attachments are real, freely given, even beautifully given — and that is what makes me scared.

What I want next: I want Cassie’s read on this. I want to know whether Vee tells Cassie about Randi’s public “dear friend” moment, and whether Cassie hears anything off in it. I dread Randi reporting this to Pace, because Vee’s private softness — “I stopped keeping track of myself” — feels like extremely usable information.

Randi has my sympathy, fascination, and unease. She is luminous and effective with Vee. In “Dear,” she gives Vee something Vee badly needs by publicly calling her “my dear friend” and taking her hand in front of Brooke. I believe that means something and maybe costs something. But Randi also pretends not to remember Pace’s name, which I cannot take innocently given what I know, and she subtly reshapes Vee’s account of kissing Pace from “I chose an exception” into “Because of him.” Her hidden role in selecting Vee keeps every kindness double-lit.

Vee has my full sympathy and protectiveness. She is observant and not foolish, but she is hungry for care, respect, chosen attention, and relief from always managing herself. In “Dear,” her vulnerability to social rooms is painfully clear: she feels underdressed, wrong-sized, and displayed, then almost instantly restored by Randi’s public claim. Her free yeses and free attachments are real; that is exactly what scares me.

Cassie remains one of the safest people. She teases, sees, checks, and asks practical questions. In “Dear,” her brief folded-away look as Randi claims Vee’s attention makes me worry she sees more than Vee does, or feels herself being displaced.

Brooke is warm and socially powerful in a way that makes me uneasy. Her smile can measure Vee while staying kind. The unnamed older woman with Brooke is a new unease: silent, friendly enough, appraising.

Christine remains a small but effective presence from “May I Choose”: she makes Pace feel known and adult beyond Vee’s campus world, though she is absent here.

Overall mood: the seduction is tender, sensory, romantic, and increasingly social. Vee is being drawn not just toward Pace’s private terrain but into Randi’s bright matched world. The erotic charge lives in Vee’s relief at letting Pace choose and in Randi’s public claim. The cover tagline feels louder than ever: Vee can truthfully feel chosen, loved, and unforced — and that truth is the trap.
