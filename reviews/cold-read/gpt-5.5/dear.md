This chapter made me feel the pull of Randi very hard, and also made me feel how dangerous that pull is without needing anything overtly sinister to happen. Vee comes in glowing from Pace, and then Randi catches that glow and makes herself the person Vee wants to tell. Vee’s explanation that Cassie has her own people sounds reasonable, but the text gives me “Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie,” and that made the rationalization feel like something Vee does not want to inspect.

Randi is intoxicating in this chapter. I believe the warmth lands for Vee because it landed for me: the wrist-grab, the demand for the report, the delighted “You little minx,” and then especially “Vee’s my dear friend.” That line has force. It does feel like Randi spends something socially by saying it, and I understand why Vee feels picked. But I also do not fully trust it, because Randi’s warmth keeps arriving exactly where Vee is most hungry. I don’t think the page asks me to call it fake. It asks me to sit in the unresolved mix: genuine tenderness, social power, maybe strategy, all in the same hand.

Brooke feels warm but assessing; the unnamed taller woman feels cooler, more measuring. Brooke’s “We should all get dinner sometime” is perfectly polite and perfectly empty. The chapter is so good at making politeness feel like a velvet rope.

Erotically, there is no sex, but there is charge. It peaks for me in Vee telling Randi about letting Pace choose, then Randi reflecting it back: “You let him choose for you. And you liked it.” That is intimate and exposing, almost more charged because they are clothed in a smoothie shop. The hand on the table in front of Brooke is also charged: public, possessive, validating, and a little heavy.

The friction I felt was not boredom; it was social discomfort, and I think the page earned it. The line that caught me was: “in a room of bare arms and cropped quarter-zips it covered her like an apology.” That is painfully exact. The title “Dear” lands on Randi’s “dear friend,” and maybe also on the way Vee becomes dear by being named so publicly. What I want next is Cassie back in the room with Vee, because Cassie saw something and Vee chose not to watch her go. My guess from here is that Vee will carry “picked” as proof, while Cassie may carry the same moment as warning.

Vee thinks it is fine to have separate people, since Cassie has the team and lab and other things Vee is not part of, but something small turns over underneath and she lets it lie.

Randi is in class beside Vee, turned in her seat and lit up, reaching for Vee’s wrist. She has a good bag and sunglasses slid down out of her hair. She says she has forty minutes and refuses to be responsible with them. She initially calls Pace “Paul,” then repeats “Pace” when corrected. At the Smoothie Bird she orders beet, wheatgrass, egg white, and oat milk blended with ice; the drink is bruise-colored. She sits easily on the high chair. She tells Vee a story about a Friday date with a tall, dreamy, wealthy-looking man who drove them half an hour to a candlelit French place and then talked all night about himself, Switzerland, and lacrosse; Randi told him she had an early morning. She says a man who cannot listen across a dinner table will not take care of you anywhere else. She listens to Vee’s account of Pace, reflects that Vee let him choose and liked it, calls Vee “You little minx,” swats her arm, introduces Vee to Brooke as “my dear friend,” takes Vee’s hand on the white table in front of Brooke and the taller woman, knocks knees with Vee under the table, hugs Vee outside, says “Love you, girl,” and leaves radiant back into the matched crowd after her phone lights up and she says she has to “go be a person.”

Pace is named and discussed but does not appear. Vee tells Randi he took her to a tiny, unfancy Middle Eastern restaurant she did not know existed; she could not read the menu and told him so; he looked at it and said, “May I choose?” Vee let him order a long line of little things, saying the names low. Vee says the whole night went soft and she stopped keeping track of herself. She says he gets a six-year-old excitement in his voice when talking about some bridge, who built it, and why it still stands. Vee says she kissed him after knowing him two days, something she does not do and has never done. Randi frames it as because Vee could not not, “Because of him,” and Vee agrees.

The weather in Blacksburg is kind. Light comes long and clean off brick; the mountains are hard high blue. Vee feels like a person good things happen to: a man looking at her as if she were an answer, and a girl choosing to spend forty minutes with her.

The Smoothie Bird is on a corner with windows full of orange. Inside: cold air conditioning, loud blenders and voices, white walls with a hot citrus band near the ceiling, smell of fruit, hairspray, and perfume. The room is full of girls in leggings, hair up, lightly and exactly done faces, bare arms, and cropped quarter-zips. Vee feels underdressed at the doorway. Vee orders strawberry banana with oat milk, faintly aware she says oat milk because Randi is there. The counter boy asks Randi to repeat her order. Only seats are a high white table in the window with two tall chairs that leave feet hanging. Vee feels set up on a shelf with the window at her back, the bright room in front, and her sweating pink drink in hand.

Brooke appears at the Smoothie Bird, calling “Randi!” from across the room. She is beautiful like the whole room but more finished, with her smile arriving ahead of her. She says they missed Randi Thursday. She warmly asks who Randi’s new friend is. When Randi says “This is Vee,” Vee says they have stats together. After Randi says “Vee’s my dear friend” and takes Vee’s hand, Brooke’s smile takes it in and does not change. Brooke says, “We should all get dinner sometime,” which is lovely, means nothing, and ends the exchange. Brooke leaves with the taller woman toward the door, with a wave and a “love you” tossed back across a shoulder.

The unnamed taller woman with Brooke appears at the Smoothie Bird. She is taller, carries a coffee in one hand, and her eyes make a slow, easy circuit of the table and Vee. She says nothing. Her gaze gives Vee nothing but more smile. She later gives Vee one last friendly-enough look, “the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over,” then leaves with Brooke.
