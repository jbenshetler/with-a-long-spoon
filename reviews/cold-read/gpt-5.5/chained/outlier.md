### Chapter record

The chapter is titled “The Outlier.”

The scene opens in a cold lecture hall with tiered rows. Vee, Randi, and Cassie sit two-thirds up where they always sit: Vee in the middle, Randi on her left with a latte she is not drinking, Cassie on her right with nothing in her hands.

The professor explains the semester project. It is worth twenty-five percent. Students must take modeling from invented textbook numbers and apply it to real messy data. They must pick a subject that means something to them, use data rather than analogies, choose a few samples to motivate the model, look at the whole, and examine outliers. He names the work exploratory data analysis. He clicks slides backward and forward.

Vee writes “25%” and boxes it, then writes “something that means something to you.” She misses some lecture because Randi tips her head against Vee’s shoulder and murmurs something unrepeatable about a boy two rows down.

The group forms naturally: Vee, Randi, and Cassie have been a three since September, with Vee’s middle seat assumed and texts assuming the other two.

The project subject is discussed while walking out of the building, waiting at a crosswalk, and during coffee afterward.

Cassie wants to study PPP loans. She believes pandemic loans meant to keep people on payroll were not used for workers. Her father was laid off in 2020.

Randi thinks Cassie is wrong, saying the story is more complicated than one number. Randi says her father took the loans for several businesses and kept people on with them. She says, “We’ll see.”

Vee sits between Cassie and Randi and thinks she loves them both: Cassie with the laid-off father on her right, Randi with the loans on her left. She feels the room step somewhere she cannot follow and looks at her coffee.

They divide work. Cassie will pull federal and state data. Vee will build the model because the math is hers and she is an economics major. Cassie is pre-nursing and does not build models. Randi, marketing, will make the findings visually legible. Cassie tells Vee, “You do the smart part.”

Later, the library gives them a glass-walled room on the fourth floor with a latched door. It is a gray afternoon the week before break. Their laptops are open across the table.

For the first hour, the work goes well. The model holds. Randi’s graphs are clean, fast, and readable at a glance. The aggregate supports Cassie’s claim: loans went up while payrolls fell, county after county. Cassie sits back satisfied but does not gloat.

Randi says, “The country isn’t my county.” They plot the counties they are from. Cassie’s county sits on the line. Vee’s county sits on the line. Randi pulls up companies in her own county, expecting a counterexample.

Randi’s county has a large point well off by itself, up and to the right. The graph drops tags on points when hovered over. The tag on this point reads “MIRH.”

Randi goes still. Her hand rests on the trackpad. Vee notices a shift in the air and watches Randi rather than the screen.

Randi clicks the point. It opens into a record, then a name: Miranda Holdings, LLC.

Vee knows Miranda is Randi’s formal name, the long version used on chapter paperwork. The government page shows Miranda Holdings over the worst point in the county, with several loans, one company under another, and payroll not preserved.

Vee understands there can be no other Miranda. She looks from the screen to Randi before choosing to.

Randi’s face loses color. Her jaw sets. She takes a breath that is too long and level. She says, low to the screen, “Cheating bastards.” The words sound ragged and newly said, aimed too near to be for strangers.

Cassie cannot see the record because she is on the far side of Vee and the screen is small and angled toward Randi. Cassie hears only Randi’s words and takes them as agreement. Cassie says, “That’s the whole thing, right there,” and says one company that size cutting workers anyway is not the exception, only the one easy to see.

Randi nods at Cassie and closes the record with one keystroke. Randi does not look at Vee and does not know Vee has looked at her.

Vee slides the laptop toward herself and starts managing the write-up: who will take which section, how to frame the aggregate, what the professor will want up front. She asks Randi answerable things and keeps Randi included. She does not look back at the point, does not say the name, and does not tell Randi then or afterward what she saw.

They work another hour. Randi gradually returns: color, jokes, pride in the image on the screen. By the time they pack up, she is brighter than she has been all afternoon.

Vee walks out into the gray between Randi and Cassie. She thinks Randi has been dealt a rotten thing by a man she had the luck to be the daughter of, with no hand in it and none of it earned. Vee thinks the kindest thing a friend can do is not make Randi carry it in front of anyone.

Behind them, on the closed laptop, the point becomes one among a hundred thousand again: the biggest one, off on its own in the white, easy to see if you know to look, invisible if you do not.
