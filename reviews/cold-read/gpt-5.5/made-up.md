## Reader reaction

This chapter feels like a held breath, and the heat of it is almost entirely in restraint. No sex happens, no touching even, and somehow Pace staying in the recliner because Vee put him there is as charged as anything. I felt the erotic peak at: “He hadn’t left the chair. She’d put him there, and he’d stayed put.” That landed hard because it reverses the usual shape between them without making him smaller. She commands the scene, and he obeys beautifully.

Vee feels more powerful here than she has before. Not invulnerable — she’s still blushing “down and down,” still aware of the ridiculous boots, the cold, the small mirror cutting her off — but she is arranging desire now. She brings the dress back to its maker because “the finished thing had crossed a stage for everyone but its maker,” and that feels tender and just. Then she brings him the second “costume,” which is not a costume at all: the black heels and nothing else, the truth under the public silk. I love that she names it playfully — “Tonight they’re costumes” — because Halloween gives her a way to show him two selves without having to over-explain either one.

Pace is extremely attractive to me here, maybe especially because he does less. He washes dishes, waits, turns up the lamp by one click, says “Take your time,” and then does not intrude. That “one click brighter” is such a Pace gesture: he prepares the viewing, but he doesn’t seize it. When she appears in the dress, his body wants to rise, but she puts out her hand and he sits back down. “She watched him make himself do it.” That is hot because he is visibly affected and visibly choosing discipline. My trust in him, in this chapter specifically, goes up: he lets Vee stage herself and lets looking be enough.

Randi is not present, but she is threaded through the shoes: “the dear black straps Randi had knelt to buckle in the boutique.” That makes the naked second costume feel not only for Pace, even though he says, “This is just for me.” The shoes carry Randi’s touch into the room. I don’t think Vee is consciously thinking that as danger here; she is thinking of the heels as finishing the dress and the line. But as a reader, with the cover always murmuring in the background, Randi’s absence does not make her gone.

Trust versus suspicion: on the page, nothing in this scene feels “off” between Vee and Pace. If anything, the scene earns trust: he respects her instructions, lets her take time, and does not rush to touch. The larger suspicion is still not from this room but from the book’s framing and everything I know: Vee still does not know Randi and Pace started by choosing her. So the tenderness is real, but it is happening inside a trap she cannot see.

The erotic charge works strongly for me because it is visual and delayed. The first walk in the dress is gorgeous: “the slit falling open off her bare leg to the hip with every step, given and taken back and given.” Then the second walk strips away the public costume and leaves only the private fact. It never goes clinical. It stays embodied: cold, boots, zipper, buckles, lamp, music, breath.

The title, “Made-Up,” is lovely and a little sly. It points to Halloween costumes, to pretending, to being “made up” as in dressed and presented, to the made dress, and maybe to invented selves. But the chapter also turns the phrase inside out: the second costume is not made-up at all. It is the truth under the made thing. It didn’t give anything away before I read; afterward it recolors the whole scene as a question of what is costume and what is revelation.

The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels very alive here. Vee invites Pace to look, but politely, formally almost: sit there, wait, choose. He accepts politely. The series title *With a Long Spoon* still feels like hunger with caution — all this feeding, cooking, wine, curry, dishes, and now a kind of visual feast served at a distance. The tagline, “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap,” keeps shadowing the fact that Vee’s yes here is so free, so beautifully hers, and still not fully informed.

What I want next: I want Pace’s answer to become touch, eventually, but I also want this looking to be allowed to matter first. I dread Vee telling Randi, because Randi will understand the shoes, the dress, the nakedness, and the “just for me” in a way Vee maybe does not. My guess from here: this chapter is going to make Randi’s claim, or envy, or desire sharpen, because Vee has just given Pace a private unveiling built partly out of Randi’s chosen heels.
