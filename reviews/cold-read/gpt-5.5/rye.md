## Reader reaction

This chapter lands softly for me, almost like an exhale after the truck scene. I feel the soreness in Vee before anything else: “lowered herself into her desk chair like a much older woman,” “my legs are going to file a complaint.” It’s funny and bodily and sweet, and it lets the previous chapter become something she can tell someone, not just something burning privately in her.

Cassie remains the safest person in the book to me. Her first look goes “straight to the flannel,” and she understands immediately that it is not Vee’s. I love that she lets herself joke first — “No windowless van,” “I had your blood type” — but she is not only joking. The humor is how she checks the facts without humiliating Vee or puncturing her joy. She had “a whole witness statement ready,” which is funny because it’s true: Cassie has been carrying the danger-file for Vee while Vee is carrying the glow.

Vee is so dear to me here. She is still erotically lit up, but the dominant feeling is pride: “Me. I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain.” That moved me. The title “Rye” pulls the erotic charge toward food and effort rather than direct sex: the rye sandwich becomes part of the seduction because she had to climb to taste it that way. “And then we ate,” said “the way other girls said *and then we kissed*,” is maybe the center of the chapter for me. It is funny, intimate, and very Vee: appetite as revelation.

Pace is absent in person, but he becomes even more attractive in the retelling. Vee’s report preserves the things that worked on me last chapter: the kit, the moleskin, the privacy, the not touching. “He looked at me like I’d handed him something. Like a gift. And he didn’t touch me.” That still works. It really works. It also worries me because the jacket is never far away in my head: every yes was freely given. Vee’s yes here feels absolutely free; she says “On purpose. Me.” That is exactly what makes it feel dangerous.

Cassie’s softness at the end gets to me: “Vee,” she says, “like setting something down where it wouldn’t break.” That line made me trust her even more. She is not scolding, not competing, not romanticizing. She sees that something important has happened and handles it carefully. Then she brings it back down to earth: “Borrow real boots next time.” That is such a Cassie form of love.

Suspicion-wise: nothing in this chapter makes Cassie feel off. If anything, she steadies the room. Pace is not on page, so my unease with him is carried over rather than newly earned here. Vee’s retelling is so trusting that I feel protective, not skeptical of her. The “I kept the shirt. I didn’t ask. He didn’t ask for it back” feels like a small claim, almost a token from a lover, and I’m both warmed and nervous because the shirt is now an object between them that will need to be returned or explained.

Erotically, the peak is still the truck story in miniature: “I knocked on the glass and I called him back in. And I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose.” It’s less hot than the scene itself because it’s mediated through roommate retelling, but it has a different kind of heat: Vee owning the act aloud. The chapter is more afterglow than ignition. It never goes clinical for me; it stays embodied and conversational.

The only tiny friction is that the chapter mostly recaps what I already saw in “Leave No Trace,” but because Cassie’s reception matters, it doesn’t feel wasted. The new thing is not plot information; it’s Vee choosing what to tell Cassie, and Cassie’s face changing around it. I did notice that Vee edits the ending a little: “he put the heat on and drove me home” leaves out the campus cop, the interrupted “Pace —,” and the fire-lane trouble. That omission feels important because I know it happened, and Cassie doesn’t get it here.

The chapter title, “Rye,” seemed almost comically modest before reading, and afterward I like it. It points to the sandwich, but really to Vee’s appetite and pride: the hike becomes edible, memorable, something she can tell Cassie as if food were sex and achievement and care all together. The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like a warning about intimacy at a distance, about tasting from something dangerous or dining with someone you shouldn’t sit too close to. The tagline is loud here: Vee says the important words herself — “On purpose. Me.” Nobody has to fake her yes. That is the trap.

What I want next: I want to know whether Cassie notices what Vee does not say — the cop, the interrupted sentence, the degree of erotic charge. I want to know whether Vee sleeps in the flannel, returns it, hides it, or lets Randi see it. And I dread Randi finding out secondhand or too late that Pace and Vee now have this private shirt-object, this private mountain, this private looking.
