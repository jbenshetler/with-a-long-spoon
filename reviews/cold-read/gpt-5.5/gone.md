I believe Randi’s fondness. I also do not trust the situation she is in, or the way she and Pace can lie together and discuss Vee’s readiness as if Vee is a beloved project.

Pace is attractive here in the familiar way: patient, perceptive, controlled. “I want her to get there on her own” is exactly the sort of sentence that makes him feel careful and decent inside the moment. But because I know from the jacket and earlier chapters that he and Randi selected Vee without telling her, that sentence lands with a crack in it. “On her own” is not fully true if the road has been laid out by two people in bed together. Still, the chapter does not make him cartoonish. Vee, absent, becomes almost painfully vivid. Randi’s account of the shoe-store silliness makes me love her more: the big ugly shoe, the stomping, the growl, the sudden mortification when she sees the older couple. That is such a live, funny, human version of her. And Pace’s description — “She comes in and the whole thing goes brighter. Like somebody found the lamp” — is beautiful. It feels true to how Vee has been reading on the page lately: embarrassed, hungry, increasingly alive. But it also hurts, because she is being cherished in a room she does not know exists, by two people whose connection to each other she still does not understand.

Trust versus suspicion: the text earns suspicion here very directly. Randi says, “She's not ready, though. She'll get there. Pace’s “I like taking my time with her” is sexy, but it is also part of a hidden conversation about Vee’s future consent before Vee knows the terms. The title, “Gone,” sharpens that for me. Randi says Vee has “got it bad,” and Pace says Vee is ashamed but alive; Vee is gone over Pace, Randi is maybe gone over Vee, and the original neat game may be gone too. The line that gives me friction — in the good, uneasy sense — is “She'll get there.” I don’t think it is badly written; I think it is supposed to make me feel the hidden arrangement. But as a reader who loves Vee, I recoil a little. “Get there” where? To what? With what knowledge? And why are two lovers deciding the pace of her arrival?

The chapter title “Gone” feels clear but layered. Vee is gone over Pace. Randi is gone on Vee more than she meant to be. Maybe Pace is gone too, in his quiet way, describing Vee lighting a room. And maybe the old version of the “game” is gone, because real feeling has entered. The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like a warning about eating with/devouring/desiring something dangerous from a distance — and the tagline keeps getting louder. Every yes here may be real. Randi’s desire is real. Pace’s patience is real. Vee’s joy is real. That is exactly why the trap feels worse.

What I want next: I want Vee to discover something, or at least for the hidden Pace/Randi room to get closer to her. I dread the induction now, because the dress and shoes are not just Vee’s triumph anymore; they are also something Pace and Randi are discussing, desiring, and aiming toward together. My guess from here: the “third” is approaching not through a crude proposition, but through Randi’s friendship and Pace’s patience converging until Vee thinks she arrived there by herself.
