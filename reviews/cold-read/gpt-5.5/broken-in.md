This chapter gave me a bodily exhale before it gave me any certainty. Vee wakes in shame, but not collapsed: cold water, bare legs in October, apple, stride. I felt her coming back into herself, and then I felt the dangerous pleasure of Randi taking over. Randi is wonderful here in the most troubling way: awake ten minutes, bare-faced, affectionate, funny, precise, able to name Vee’s jealousy without condemning her. I wanted to trust her because Vee needs exactly that kind of friend. But the moment she says “Not today” and begins staging the apology — the day, the dress, the shoes, the scarf, the entrance — I felt the old jacket-copy unease flare. Vee came to fix what she did to Pace, and Randi quietly turns repair into presentation.

Vee’s remorse feels real. She understands she hurt Pace by shaming his care. Her account of the dance keeps the erotic charge alive: his hand on her back, the lead arriving before thought, the body knowing. The hottest part here, though, is not explicit sex but dressing and tending: Randi’s hands under Vee’s skirt, the hidden ankle straps, the hand high on her thigh, the thumb behind her knee, the scarf being taught as tension. It works because Vee can’t see what Randi is doing; she can only feel being arranged and steadied. The chapter peaks at “somebody kneeling to *tend* to her” and again when Vee sees herself in the mirror as “nobody’s clown.”

My suspicion of Randi is page-earned, not just jacket-earned. She comforts Vee beautifully, but she also redirects the problem away from apology as speech and toward apology as a performed entrance: “you don’t go in there sorry… You go in there *sure*.” That may be wise. It may also be control. She also says Pace “leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing,” which is shrewd about him, but maybe too shrewd for someone pretending not to know him deeply. Vee, of course, does not notice that.

Friction: Brooke calling Vee “the stats friend” is deliciously chilling — “the house had a folder on her anyway” — and I bought Vee’s class/social unease there completely. The only slight snag is that Randi’s transformation of Vee’s shame is almost too effective, too fast: “the thing Vee had been holding clenched… let go… because Randi had said so.” But that is also the point of Randi’s power over her right now, so it reads as seductive more than false.

The title “Broken In” lands on several things at once: Vee after the rupture with Pace; the borrowed shoes being already broken in by Randi; Vee being trained into a new way of entering rooms; maybe even being broken into the sorority house and Randi’s private room. It also has an erotic edge — not broken *down*, but broken *in*, made wearable, made ready, softened for use. The book title and tagline darken this: every yes here is Vee’s, and yet Randi is shaping the conditions around what Vee will want to say yes to.

What I want next: I want the apology with Pace badly, and I dread it because Vee is not going simply as herself; she is going in Randi’s shoes, Randi’s scarf, Randi’s timing, Randi’s theory of what Pace will read. Guess from here: Pace will receive the apology, but the hidden triangle will be more present than Vee knows, literally on her body.

- **How I feel**
  - Pace/Peter remains intensely attractive: careful, prepared, practical, restrained until invited, adult, disciplined, strong, brilliant, skilled with math, wood, sewing, cooking, dancing, and bodily attention. “Lesson” increased my trust in his public safety through the bartender’s testimony; “Broken In” makes me ache for him because Vee’s remorse clarifies how unfairly she hurt him. Suspicion remains because he and Randi selected Vee without her knowledge, arranges conditions beautifully, and his communication with Randi remains hidden. His absence/silence now feels like dignity and room, but Randi’s confidence about that silence is unsettling.
  - Randi is seductive, warm, funny, perceptive, and concerning. I believe her care for Vee in “Broken In”: she listens, names jealousy without shame, steadies Vee, makes her laugh, and gives her a way to walk back toward Pace. I also distrust how completely she directs the repair — timing, tone, clothing, shoes, scarf, even emotional posture. She gives Vee language and beauty, but also turns Vee into “her own good work.” I trust her emotional intelligence more than her honesty or restraint.
  - Vee feels newly alive but very influenceable. Her remorse is real, and her ability to admit she bit the hook and hurt Pace makes me respect her. She is funny and vivid in shame: sad clown, Edna Mode, evil stepsisters. But she lets Randi’s word release her clenched feeling and accepts Randi’s marching orders almost totally. I sympathize with her and worry about her at the same time.
  - Cassie feels deeply trustworthy and perceptive, though pushed aside. Her low “Hrm” under Vee’s bunk says she notices enough not to need words. The fact that Vee hides from her and goes to Randi makes me more worried about isolation.
  - Brooke feels warm, polished, and socially measuring. Her recognition of Vee as “the stats friend” makes the sorority house feel organized around knowledge and hierarchy.
  - Meg remains warm, dry, calm, and likable in memory.
  - Kayla remains delightful, comic, and somewhat recklessly enthusiastic.
  - Theo remains genuinely nice and safely ordinary.
  - Christine remains warm in memory.
  - The blonde country-pretty woman remains sharp/territorial/possibly jealous; her hook did real damage.
  - The older bartender remains trustworthy and practical; her account steadies Pace’s moral standing.
  - Vee’s mother’s voice remains cruel/proper/shaming, but this chapter shows Vee can answer shame with cold water, makeup, apple, walking, laughter, and borrowed beauty.
  - Overall mood carried forward: anticipatory, dressed, dangerous. The immediate goal is repair with Pace tomorrow night, but the repair has Randi’s hands all over it — literally in the shoes and scarf, emotionally in the timing and script. Vee is walking toward Pace in someone else’s beautiful broken-in shoes, freely saying yes to the help, and that is exactly why the tagline keeps humming.
