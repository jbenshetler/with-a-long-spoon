### Chapter record

Vivienne Thorne / Vee appears on Pace’s porch before dawn and later in the dorm laundry room. She sits on the porch couch with Pace under his quilt, her legs across his lap. She has been awake for an hour. She drinks from the second cup of coffee Pace has set on the low table for her. She has been thinking for weeks about a sentence meant to carry “I love you” without saying it. She believes Pace must say “I love you” first and cannot say it ahead of him. She tells him, “I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal. I didn’t know what a meal was until you fed me one. And now I know.” She looks at the moon rather than at him. After he answers, she feels a small “wait” behind her breastbone but lets it sink back under the warmth of being held. Two days later she is in the dorm laundry room with her clothes in the dryer, sitting on the folding table, and realizes her sentence and his were not the same shape. She decides she will not bring it up and folds her laundry when the dryer stops.

Pace / Peter appears on his porch with Vee. He has kept the porch heater turned down overnight. He has been awake longer than Vee and has placed a second cup of coffee on the low table for her. He sits with Vee on the couch under his own quilt, which lives on the back of the couch and had been on his bed before he put it on the porch for cooler months. His hand rests on Vee’s shin under the quilt. When Vee says she wants to tell him something, he says, “Okay.” After her cracker-and-meal sentence, he goes still for a beat, his hand stops, presses once, stops again, and he takes two breaths. He makes a small “mm” sound. He says, “I love how good we are together,” then says, “I never had this either. What you do — what your body does — I’ve never. I’m glad you feel it too.” He pulls her closer, adjusts his arm around her shoulder, kisses the top of her head for longer than usual, rests his cheek against her crown, and holds her. Later he moves them inside and refreshes the coffee.
