## Reader reaction

I came into this expecting Vee because the jacket put her in my hands first, and the chapter very quickly corrected me: this is Randi, and the correction matters. She arrives with money in the leather bag, the sorority polish, the blue eyes she has apparently learned to live behind, and I felt both the pull of her glamour and the brittleness of it almost immediately. Pace, meanwhile, is intensely attractive here in that controlled, observant way: the apple sliced the way she will eat it, the chair turned because she wants him that way, the way he notices without making her explain herself. That attentiveness is hot. It is also powerful enough to frighten me by the end.

The kitchen scene absolutely works on me. The apple could have gone precious, but it lands as intimate: he has learned the exact conditions under which she can allow appetite, and she turns that permission into play. “Bite, kiss, bite” is simple and lush, and the lap-sitting has this lovely mutual current: she directs him, he likes being directed, then he lets the pressure build until she physically places his hands where she wants them. That is the first swoon for me, not because it is tender in a soft way, but because wanting is allowed to become visible before either of them names it.

The bench itself changes the air. I felt Randi’s excitement before I felt dread: “That’s not a table” / “Not only a table” is genuinely delicious, and her “Show me” feels awake and consenting. The undressing is gorgeous in its patience, especially him folding each piece of clothing and letting her stand in the lights. The erotic charge peaks for me less at the spanking itself than in the slow education around it: the safety word, the checking in, his coming around to her face to say “Hi,” the foot/heel removal, the refusal to hurry. The page makes the consent feel real moment to moment, not just declared once and then forgotten.

And yet the scene earns the unease. Not during the physical scene, exactly; there I trusted him almost completely. The unsettled part comes after, when Randi’s interior opens and I understand the wound he touched is deeper than even he knows. “There you are, Randi” is devastating: on his side it is tender recognition, but inside her it becomes exposure so total she has to retreat behind the face. The flinch at “You’re beautiful” hurt more than the spanking. It tells me that tenderness is the more dangerous instrument here.

Pace moves for me from almost impossibly trustworthy to tragically limited. He does so much right: he waits, asks, checks, cares for her, respects the locked bathroom door because of Daphne and the old lesson he learned. But the final gap between what he thinks happened and what Randi is actually deciding is enormous. He thinks this was “a remarkably good evening.” She is lying with her face arranged, deciding the real self he found is “not coming out again.” I do not think he is cruel. I do think his confidence in having found and answered her hunger may be one of the most dangerous things in the room.

The title, “The Bench,” starts literal and becomes almost ominous. It is furniture, device, altar, trapdoor, witness. By the end, when it will become a table again tomorrow, it feels like the chapter’s whole secret: ordinary surface over hidden purpose. The volume title, **A POLITE INVITATION**, now feels sharper: this chapter is all invitation, all manners, all consent and “please,” but the politeness is not the opposite of danger. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE** still feels like a warning about dining with something dangerous while wanting the meal anyway. There is no front tagline, but the blurb’s closing beat, “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem,” feels painfully exact now.

What I want next is not simple reassurance. I want to know whether Randi can keep the face in place, whether Pace will notice the lock turning inside her, and how this connects to Vee, who is only promised by the jacket but absent from the room. My guess from here is that Randi is not just a glamorous guide into danger; she is already inside her own version of it, and this chapter made me feel the cost before I even know the larger game.
