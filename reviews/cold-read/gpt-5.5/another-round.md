## Reader reaction

This chapter undid me in a quieter way than the roadhouse did. I came in carrying Vee’s dread of Sheri, and the chapter lets me feel how ridiculous and how real that dread is at once. The opening is almost painful: Pace says “Sheri,” and Vee’s whole invented blonde returns instantly — “the hair went gold. The leg went tan.” I felt the cold arrive with her. Even knowing, because Vee knows, that she “built her on the nights I felt stupid” doesn’t save her. That felt very true to jealousy: insight is just “a coat of paint.”

Pace is lovely here, but mostly by being seen through other people. He isn’t performing; he’s at the sink, then in the taproom, then quietly getting coats and driving Sheri home. The chapter keeps showing me that his care exists when Vee is not there to receive it. That matters. Sheri’s story — him driving her home without questions, showing up with coffee and donuts, fixing the lock and the faucet, inviting her cycling — made him feel more trustworthy to me, not because he says the right thing, but because he evidently has a whole pattern of showing up for damaged people without making a speech of it. And then the end, where he waits with the headlights on Sheri’s door until her porch light comes on, is almost devastating because it is so unannounced. Vee sees it: “the exact same easy unannounced way he apparently did everything, everywhere, for everyone he kept.” That line warmed me and hurt me a little. It also makes his silence around love feel even more pointed. He has the deeds. He has so many deeds. But the missing words still sit there.

Sheri instantly works for me. I was ready to dislike her because Vee was ready to, and then she turns out to be “five foot nothing, boots included,” loud, blunt, funny, queer, perceptive, and absolutely not the fantasy rival. Her first full look at Vee is a great erotic/social reversal: Vee braces for ranking, pricing, judgment, and instead realizes, at the shoes, that Sheri is “enjoying her.” That was the heat peak for me, honestly — not explicit sex, but the sudden release of rivalry into being appreciated by another woman. It’s charged, funny, exposing. Sheri’s “And you get to motorboat them things?” is vulgar, but because Pace blushes, it becomes bizarrely tender. Pace embarrassed is new, and I loved seeing Vee love seeing it.

Erotically, this is simmering rather than sexual. The keyhole shirt, the borrowed short skirt, Randi’s black induction heels in a January taproom — all of that is Vee armoring herself to compete, but it becomes something else once Sheri’s look lands as desire/appreciation rather than threat. The erotic charge peaks at Sheri’s slow inspection and Vee’s laugh of relief, then again with Pace blushing. After that the chapter becomes warmer, friendlier, more intimate than sexy.

Randi is absent in person but everywhere in trace: the heels especially. Vee arms herself with the shoes Randi bought her, “four inches of thin black strap,” and that means Randi is still helping shape how Vee enters rooms, even rooms Randi is not in. I don’t feel direct suspicion of Randi in this chapter because she’s not here acting, but the earlier unease remains alive through the shoes and through how much Vee’s emotional preparation still runs through what happened in Randi’s room.

The only friction I had is very small: Sheri is such a perfect pressure-release valve for Vee’s jealousy that for a second I wondered if the chapter was being almost too kind — the imagined rival not only isn’t a threat, she validates Vee immediately and tells her Pace is devoted. But Sheri’s roughness, volume, family wound, and little calibrations keep her from feeling like just a device. The line that saves her from flatness is: “the volume was real and the makeup was crooked, but there wasn't one uncalibrated thing about what Sheri chose to say and when.” That makes her feel canny, not merely comic.

The chapter title, “Another Round,” is plain on the surface — another round of drinks, another round at the bar, another social outing after the roadhouse. But it also means another round of testing Pace in public, another round with jealousy, another round of Vee being brought into one of Pace’s worlds. It didn’t give anything away; it sits neatly after the chapter as both bar-language and emotional repetition. The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, keeps gathering food, caution, feeding, distance, and danger. Here the spoon is almost dry: Pace hands Vee “she dances” “like a dried spoon,” a fact with no comfort on it. The tagline still hums under everything: every yes is freely given, and Vee says yes to meeting Sheri, yes to entering the taproom, yes to liking her — but all of this is still happening while the central Pace/Randi secret remains outside her knowledge.

What I want next: I want the dance hall. I want Vee at country-western with Sheri and Pace, because that is going to hit every old jealousy and body-confidence wire. I also now want Sheri to meet Randi, badly and fearfully. Sheri sees too much; if anyone outside the secret arrangement might smell something odd, it could be her. My guess from here: Sheri will like Vee and maybe protect her, but she may also become someone who can name what Vee cannot.
