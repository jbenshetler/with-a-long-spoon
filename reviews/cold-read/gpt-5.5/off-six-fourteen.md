## Reader reaction

I came into this chapter still held in the little suspended “will he kiss her?” moment, and the first bodily feeling here is warmth spilling over. Vee is incandescent. She is so full of the kiss and the date that the room is “too small to hold what she had to do with it,” and that landed for me as pure delight before I had time to be wary. Her little spin — “*Look at me*” — is one of the most openly happy things she’s done. I felt happy for her, almost despite myself.

The kiss itself being told after the fact works for me because the point isn’t the mechanics; it’s possession. “He was already walking away and I pulled him back and I did it. Me.” That “Me” is the peak of the chapter for me. It turns the whole prior boundary/exception into something Vee is claiming, not something Pace extracts. And Cassie asking, immediately and dryly, “How much did he give you to drink?” made me love Cassie more. It’s funny, but it’s also a real friend checking the floor under the romantic story. When Vee says, “Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose,” I believe that she believes it, and on the page, in this immediate scene, I mostly believe it too. But because I carry the cover and the secret selection, the sentence also rings the tagline like a bell: every yes was freely given. That was the trap.

My feeling about Pace doesn’t exactly move here because he isn’t present, but the report of him accepting her no and beginning to walk on makes him more attractive again. That is exactly the thing Vee needed to be true: he could have the charged moment, receive the boundary, and not punish her for it. But the hiking invitation shifts the unease back in. Not because hiking is sinister by itself, but because “off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel” suddenly reminds me of the jacket: the young mathematician who lives alone at the end of a long drive. The chapter title, **“Off Six-Fourteen,”** gave me that little cold line under the warmth. Before I knew what it meant, it sounded like a location; after reading, it points to Pace’s house-world, the long drive, the place out west where Vee has not been yet. It didn’t exactly give something away, but once Cassie repeats it — “That’s the whole address. *Off six-fourteen*” — it becomes both romantic and alarming: a place vague enough to be intimate, adult, hidden, and hard to explain to state troopers.

Cassie is wonderful here. She lets Vee have her happiness. She asks just enough practical questions to puncture the balloon without popping it: alcohol, name, location, shoes. She is not suspicious in a melodramatic way; she is sane. Her “Borrow good ones” is so plain, but it leaves me with a protective ache. She can’t stop Vee from going; she can only tell her to have good shoes.

Erotically, this is charged but not explicit. The charge peaks in the retold kiss and in Vee’s insistence on agency. It’s not arousing in a graphic way; it’s intimate, giddy, bodily. The warmth from him is still in her when she enters the room, and the cold air can’t touch it. The chapter’s sexiness is in afterglow before sex: the kiss, the claim, the “I said yes so fast,” the future trail.

The only friction I felt is that Vee’s certainty is so total that I want to reach through the page and slow her down. But that is character-friction, not disbelief. The line “See. I know where he lives and everything” is painfully funny because she actually doesn’t know much at all. She knows “off six-fourteen.” She has not been to the house. She does not know whether he is rich. She does not own shoes. The chapter knows this; Cassie knows this; Vee does not feel it as danger.

The book title, **With a Long Spoon**, still has not clicked into a literal meaning for me. It feels more and more like a warning about how to approach something tempting and dangerous: feed, taste, accept, but from a distance. The tagline is blazing now. Vee’s “Nobody did anything to me” and “I did it because I wanted to” are true inside the scene — and exactly what makes the larger setup frightening.

What I want next: I want the hike, because I want to see Pace’s off-campus world through Vee’s eyes. I dread it because that is presumably closer to his house, his privacy, his long drive, his built furniture, maybe eventually the bench-world. My guess from here: the hike will feel wholesome and beautifully attentive on the surface, and it will deepen Vee’s trust in him before she knows anything about Randi’s role.

**Running memory — how I feel:**  
Pace is intensely attractive and concretely alarming. His care is real on the page: he asks permission, gives space, listens, feeds, attends, and does not press when Vee states a boundary. In Vee’s report in “Off Six-Fourteen,” his restraint at the kissing boundary looks genuinely important: he lets the no stand and begins to walk on. But he also knowingly uses timing, framing, bodily evidence, and chosen surrender; I know he and Randi secretly selected Vee. The hiking invitation pulls Vee toward his private off-campus world, which makes him feel more seductive and more dangerous.

Randi has my sympathy, fascination, and unease. She is luminous and effective with Vee but absent again, which makes Vee’s joy feel clean to Vee and compromised to me because I know Randi and Pace chose her in secret. I still do not know how much Randi is coordinating or feeling now.

Vee has my full sympathy and protectiveness. She is observant and not foolish, but she is hungry for care, respect, chosen attention, and relief from always managing herself. In “Off Six-Fourteen,” her happiness is radiant and bodily; she claims the kiss as hers, sober and intentional. That makes me happy for her and more afraid, because her free yes is exactly what the book’s tagline warns me about.

Cassie remains one of the safest people. She teases, sees, checks for alcohol, asks practical questions, jokes about state troopers, and tells Vee to borrow good shoes. She can celebrate Vee without surrendering her common sense.

Christine is a small but effective presence from the previous chapter: she makes Pace feel known and adult beyond Vee’s campus world, though she is absent here.

Overall mood: the seduction is tender, sensory, romantic, and now moving outward toward Pace’s private terrain. The erotic charge is not graphic; it lives in Vee’s afterglow, her claimed kiss, the hiking yes, and the road “off six-fourteen.” The cover tagline feels louder than ever: Vee can say, truthfully, “Nobody did anything to me,” and that truth is exactly what scares me.
