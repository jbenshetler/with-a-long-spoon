## Reader reaction

This chapter feels like the afterglow chamber after the almost-kiss — or really after the chosen kiss, since we now get confirmation that Vee did close the distance. I felt warm reading it, almost helplessly. Vee comes in “still warm from the evening, from the walk, from him,” and that is exactly the state of the prose: spilled-over, breathless, a little giddy, unable to sit still. I like being with her in this joy. It’s hard not to.

Vee is radiant here. The most moving thing is not just that she kissed him, but that she needs Cassie to understand the agency of it: “Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.” That line lands hard because it is both true on the surface and terribly shadowed by what I know from earlier: no, he did not press in that moment; yes, she chose the kiss; and also the whole frame around her has been prepared without her knowledge. The book’s tagline is practically glowing under this scene. Every yes was freely given. That was the trap. This chapter gives me one of the cleanest, happiest yeses so far — the kiss, then hiking — and that makes me more uneasy, not less.

Cassie continues to be the grounding force I trust most. Her joy for Vee feels real: “Cassie's whole face was open with it, glad, all the way in.” But then she immediately asks, “How much did he give you to drink?” I loved her for that. It is funny, dry, and also protective in exactly the right way. She doesn’t puncture Vee’s happiness just to be cynical, but she checks the conditions. And when Vee says no, there was only tea, Cassie accepts it: “Okay.” I believe that Cassie believes what she can see. My worry is still that she cannot see enough.

Pace is mostly offstage here, and honestly that makes him more powerful. We only get him through Vee’s excited retelling: he did “nothing,” he let her rule stand, he asked her hiking, he lives “out west of town, off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel.” The offstage details make him feel alluringly adult and outdoorsy and separate from campus — but also, because of the jacket and what I already know, that road out to gravel immediately prickles. The jacket said he lives alone at the end of a long drive. Now the book gives me “off six-fourteen,” and I feel the story turning toward that house. I am excited and worried in equal measure.

Erotically, this chapter is not explicit, but it has a strong charge because it is all aftermath and anticipation. The peak for me is Vee whispering, “He was already walking away and I pulled him back and I did it. Me.” It’s erotic because the action has already happened and she is reliving her own wanting. The little spin in the room — “arms going up, hair going everywhere” — is not sexual exactly, but it is bodily freedom, and that has been one of Vee’s sexiest qualities all along: her joy comes through her whole body.

The chapter title, “Off Six-Fourteen,” is beautifully ominous to me. Before reading, it sounded like an address, and after reading it becomes the first real direction toward Pace’s private world. Vee says it like reassurance: “See. I know where he lives and everything.” Cassie repeats it and immediately exposes how thin it is: “That's the whole address. *Off six-fourteen*.” That repetition turns the title from romantic destination into warning label. It points out of campus, past pavement, toward gravel, toward his house, toward whatever happens when Vee is no longer in dorm rooms and coffee shops and public restaurants.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like appetite plus distance plus caution. We’ve had literal spoons at Chi Latte and froyo, then eating by hand at dinner, and now this road outward — another “long” approach. The cover tagline is no longer abstract at all. Vee insists on the freedom of her choice, and the page makes me believe her. That is what hurts.

I want to keep reading badly. My guess from here: the hiking invitation is the next step toward Pace’s house or at least his terrain, and “real shoes” is going to matter because Vee is about to be taken out of the soft social spaces where Cassie can easily reach her. I dread how happy she is going to be to go.
