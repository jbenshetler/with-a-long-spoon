## Reader reaction

This is a small chapter, but it feels buoyant — almost entirely afterglow. I feel Vee’s body still fizzing from the kiss before I think about what it means. The cracked window, the cold line off the sill, and Vee not minding anything because she is “still warm from the evening, from the walk, from him” puts me right inside that post-date giddiness where everything in the room is suddenly part of the story.

Vee is adorable here, and I mean that in the best, most tender way. She is overflowing. She can’t keep the dinner inside herself; she has to narrate the tea, the tree, the kiss, the hiking invitation, the road, the shoes. The strongest thing for me is how fiercely she wants Cassie to understand that the kiss was hers: “Nobody did anything to me. I wasn’t talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.” That line lands with real happiness and real danger at once. On the page, she is right: Pace did not pressure her in that moment. He gave her room, and she crossed the room herself. But because I remember the jacket and everything before this, the freedom of that yes is exactly what makes my stomach tighten.

Cassie is wonderful here. She is glad for Vee — “Cassie’s whole face was open with it, glad, all the way in” — but the gladness has that “flat dry floor” under it, and that floor matters. She asks the protective question immediately: “How much did he give you to drink?” It doesn’t feel like shaming; it feels like checking whether Vee’s agency is intact. When Vee says nothing, tea, no wine, Cassie accepts it. That makes me trust Cassie more. She is not trying to ruin the happiness. She is testing the seams around it.

Pace is absent except through Vee’s report, and from inside her report he remains almost painfully attractive. He let the kiss go. He did not punish her boundary, did not wheedle, did not hover at the edge of it. Then he invited her hiking afterward, which feels like a natural next intimacy: daytime, outside, something he likes, a trail he knows. At the same time, “off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel” jolts the book’s jacket back into my hands: the young mathematician alone at the end of a long drive. Vee hears it as something romantic and discoverable; I hear it as the private world where Randi has already been, where the bench is, where the game began. That doesn’t make the hiking invitation sinister on its face, but it makes it loaded.

Trust versus suspicion: I have no new evidence that Pace did anything wrong during the kiss or dinner. In fact, Vee’s account confirms the opposite at the immediate level. But the chapter sharpens my suspicion because it moves Vee closer to Pace’s actual house/world while she still does not know about Randi and the selection. Cassie’s jokes do some useful reader-work without feeling artificial: “what to tell the state troopers when you go missing on a mountain” is funny, but it names the basic fact that Vee is going somewhere remote with a man she barely knows. The title makes that remoteness feel important.

Erotic charge is mostly afterglow rather than heat. The kiss itself is not shown, but Vee’s retelling is intimate because she is so alive with having chosen it. The peak is “I pulled him back and I did it. Me.” That is more erotic to me than a detailed kiss would have been here, because the charge is in agency, pride, and the body needing to spin around the room afterward. The chapter does not go slack exactly, but it stays intentionally light — a sparkle after the last chapter’s warmer sensual fullness.

Friction: very little. If anything, I felt a tiny ache of wanting the kiss itself, since the previous chapter stopped just before it and this one gives it in summary. But I also buy the choice: the important part is not what his mouth felt like, but that Vee gets to tell Cassie, “Me.” The only line that catches with ominous irony is Vee’s “See. I know where he lives and everything.” As a reader, I know she does not know nearly enough, and the confidence hurts.

The chapter title, “Off Six-Fourteen,” seems to point directly at Pace’s place: not a full address, not a domestic known quantity, but a road, a direction, a movement out west until things go to gravel. It feels like a threshold title. It also gives me the sensation of leaving campus, coffee shops, dorm rooms, and restaurants for the more private territory promised by the jacket. The title does not give away an event, but it tells me what kind of danger or intimacy is coming: remoteness, access, his ground.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, keeps feeling like appetite plus caution. Vee is still eating from Pace’s world — tea, restaurant food, now maybe trail and house — and she is not holding him at a distance. The cover tagline, “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap,” feels almost written under this chapter’s central declaration. Vee’s “I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose” is beautiful. It is also terrifying in context.

What I want next: I want the hike. I want to see Pace’s house through Vee’s eyes, because I know it will not be neutral for me. I dread her discovering the bench or Randi’s connection too late. I also want Cassie to stay near the edge of this story, because she is the only one asking practical questions while still loving Vee’s happiness. Guess from here: the hike may be the bridge from public romance into Pace’s private, prepared world.
