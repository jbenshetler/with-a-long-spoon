## Reader reaction

This chapter made me melt more than it made me tense, which is almost the problem. It’s so small — a text, frozen yogurt, a walk — and it works on me because Pace gives Vee exactly the kind of attention she is starving for without making a spectacle of it. The line that got me most was: “She wasn't used to being the thing a person waited on.” That feels like the whole seduction from Vee’s side right now: not dazzlement only, not sex yet, but being paused for, listened through, allowed to take up time.

Vee is painfully dear to me here. Her first instinct when the phone lights up is bodily — “the drop of a stair missed in the dark” — and then she tries to protect herself by making a joke about desperation: “It was easier to go looking for the flaw than to sit in the wanting.” That is so recognizably human. She wants the message badly, and she knows she wants it, and she is embarrassed by how quickly she has become reachable. Her mother’s voice failing to appear over the too-full frozen yogurt cup also felt like a real little loosening: “Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, *that's plenty, that's enough now.* Today there was nothing.” I felt happy for her there before I remembered the broader trap.

Pace is almost unbearably attractive in this chapter. That is my honest read. He texts quickly; he chooses something casual and sweet; he studies frozen yogurt “with the same attention he'd given her calculus”; he has a system; he pays smoothly; he holds the door with his back because both their hands are full; he listens without rushing. I understand completely why Vee feels cared for. And yet because I carry the earlier chapters, especially the hidden selection and the fact that she still does not know who he is to Randi, the tenderness has a shadow. Nothing he does on the visible date is wrong in isolation. That’s what’s unnerving. The page earns suspicion not through him being creepy here, but through the gap between what Vee thinks is happening and what I know has already happened.

Randi continues to feel warm and dangerous. Her “You should go” is friendly, excited, almost big-sisterly, and also a push. She takes Vee’s phone out of her hand to read the message, which is intimate and maybe presumptuous, but socially plausible for the closeness she’s created. Her line — “He looked like a man who wants you” — has a charged double edge because she knows much more than Vee does. Then: “He wants to see you naked.” That is overtly teasing, but I can’t unhear the jacket copy and the prior game inside it. I don’t think Vee hears it as anything but Randi being bold and funny. I hear it as a little too close to the hidden plan.

Cassie remains my anchor. “As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again” is funny, dry, normalizing, and it gives Vee a life outside this seduction — video games, one semester of staying up too late, keeping “a business major alive.” Cassie does not directly warn Vee here, but she is still positioned as someone who watches and punctures the mood without breaking it.

The erotic charge is mostly anticipatory and emotional rather than bodily explicit. It peaks, weirdly, at “He looked like a man who wants you” / “He wants to see you naked,” because Randi says aloud what the story has been moving toward. But the deeper sensual charge is the frozen yogurt itself: tart, chocolate, sugar, dripping, too much in the cup, no mother-voice stopping her. Vee’s appetite becomes visible and unpoliced for a minute. That is erotic in the book’s language even though nobody touches.

I didn’t feel bored. The scene is tiny, but it earns its length because the book has made small yeses matter. If I had a moment of friction, it might be Pace’s “I've thought about most things.” It’s attractive, but it also comes close to being too perfectly Pace — almost a thesis statement from him. Still, Vee laughs, and he doesn’t overplay it, so I bought it.

The chapter title, “The Long Way,” lands very clearly after the final page. Vee and Pace “walked the long way, not toward anything,” but emotionally it also means the long way into her: not straight to sex, not even straight to romance, but through listening, food, patience, and small ordinary kindness. It also echoes Vee walking home “the long way” after Chi Latte, warm from Randi’s attention. That makes this chapter feel like the Pace version of that earlier Randi initiation. The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like warning-distance to me — handle dangerous sweetness carefully, maybe. The tagline is very alive here: Vee’s yes to answering, yes to frozen yogurt, yes to walking with him, yes to being honest — all free, all real, and therefore increasingly frightening.

What I want next: I want Vee to have this kindness and not be hurt by it. What I expect, from here, is that Pace will keep escalating slowly, carefully, plausibly. I dread Randi and Pace discussing Vee afterward in a way that confirms how planned this was. I also want Cassie to stay close, because Vee needs someone in the room who is not intoxicated by either of them.
