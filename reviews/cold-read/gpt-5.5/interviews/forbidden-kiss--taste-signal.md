## Answers

1. After she recoils from the first kiss, Pace goes still inside her and starts to withdraw, carefully. That mattered: he takes her “Ugh — gross” and “Wrong” seriously as a stop. But Vee’s body refuses the larger stop before she can articulate it: she locks her legs around him and says, “No — not that… Don’t stop.” He reads the distinction, wipes his mouth once, and continues without making her explain or punishing her for the reaction.

Then he avoids her mouth entirely. He kisses her throat, jaw, temple, places that are safe and already welcome. That made the space around her mouth feel newly charged because he leaves it to her. After a while, she pulls him down and kisses him herself. That is the second kiss: not taken from her, but reclaimed by her.

At first it is normal, “as he always kissed her,” and then her tongue starts searching. She maybe finds “a faint edge of sharp and salt,” or maybe nothing certain. Her body answers before thought can catch up, clenching hard, and she orgasms suddenly into his mouth. He follows her, comes, gathers her in, and they end held together. Afterward he sleeps, and she lies under the cashmere, warm and spent, replaying what happened.

The aftermath is all self-explanation and almost-recognition. She realizes he liked tasting her, really liked it. She also recognizes that he did not bring his mouth back until she chose it. Then she settles on the tidy explanation that he “must have forgotten to wipe,” but her tongue betrays her by searching her own lip again. The question underneath does not vanish: “how many times, and how carefully.”

2. I think the second climax comes because several charged things converge at once: she takes back the kiss herself, he has respected her no, she tastes the trace she had just called wrong, and her body recognizes desire before her mind can censor it.

The exact trigger is the combination of agency and taboo. The first kiss shocks her because it arrives before she has consented to what it means. The second kiss is hers. She chooses it, and then some part of her goes looking for the forbidden thing. The line “her tongue went hunting — she didn’t send it; it went” is the key for me. Her desire moves ahead of her permission again, but this time she is inside the choice rather than startled by it.

So what sends her over is not simply kissing Pace. It is kissing Pace after he has been on her, wanting the trace of herself there, and discovering that the shame and the wanting are fused.

3. In the moment, I read the “sharp and salt” as possibly the taste of herself still faintly on him. The chapter keeps it uncertain — “found — maybe” and “Or found nothing” — so I do not want to overstate it. But emotionally, yes, that is what it felt like she might be tasting.

Other explanations occurred too: sweat, sex, salt from skin, the ordinary taste of him after exertion. The text gives her a way out, and she takes it later: maybe he forgot to wipe this once. But I did not fully believe that as the whole answer, because the chapter immediately asks “how many times, and how carefully,” which opens the possibility that he has usually wiped before kissing her, meaning this boundary has been managed around her without her noticing.

It connects to the whole chapter’s food language. He opens the orange, wakes the fruit with salt, eats her with “open relish,” and then the forbidden taste returns as sharp and salt in his mouth. It also connects to Vee’s recurring shame around her body’s abundance: there is “so much of her,” and he likes it. That is the thing she can barely hold.

4. I half-believe her, but only half. I believe that missing his kiss is genuinely part of it. The chapter earns that: when he withholds her mouth respectfully, it makes her want to reclaim it. Her body going over when she takes it back does make emotional sense.

But I do not believe that is all. Her explanation feels deliberately “tidy and kind,” and the prose tells me she takes it “grateful,” which makes it sound like a comfort she chooses because the other possibility is too destabilizing. The fact that her tongue then goes over her own lip “looking for something” gives her away. She may have missed kissing him, but she also wanted the thing she had forbidden.

So I believe her feeling, not her full interpretation.
