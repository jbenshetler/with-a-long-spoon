**Running memory — how I feel:**  
Pace attracts me strongly: his patience, competence, care, restraint, and erotic confidence are persuasive. I also distrust his certainty. He is not reading the same ending Randi is living, and that gap feels dangerous. After “Standards,” the old line “He’s hard to say no to” made me more uneasy around him, even though the page has shown him asking, waiting, and checking consent. After “The Pointing Game,” my unease is sharper: he knowingly asks an unfair question during sex, then builds a whole daylight structure around the answer her body gave. He is tender and frightening because he believes this is a gift.  
Randi has my sympathy and fascination. She is beautiful and controlled, but the book keeps revealing the cost of that control. Her bathroom decision after the bench hurt; her numb morning after in the sorority house hurt in a different way. In “The Pointing Game,” her desire for women, and specifically for Vee, feels real and exposed, but she immediately needs to make it survivable by handing it to Pace as a threesome “with you.” I understand why she hides, and I dread what hiding will do.  
Vee/Vivienne draws me immediately. Her entrance feels like sunlight and danger: lush, red-haired, laughing, bodily free, not organized by Randi’s standards. I want her protected because she does not know she has been chosen in a game.  
Brooke now feels personally formidable: not just imagined social pressure but a warm, seated authority who can interrogate without seeming to. I distrust her.  
Sloane and Kinsey feel like the common room’s social weather: not individually threatening yet, but part of the watching system that makes Randi perform.  
Randi’s mother feels like an old source of deprivation/shame, especially around wanting, food, and the childhood voice Randi no longer uses.  
Overall mood: aroused by Pace’s control and by Randi’s desire becoming visible, but increasingly anxious. The book has moved into the jacket’s promised trap: yeses are being carefully obtained, and that care itself is what makes me nervous.
