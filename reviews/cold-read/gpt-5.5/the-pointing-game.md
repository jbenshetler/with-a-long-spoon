This chapter made my stomach drop in a very different way from “The Bench.” It’s erotically hotter on the page than “Standards,” obviously, but the heat is braided so tightly with Pace’s watching and steering that I spent a lot of it aroused and alarmed at once. The title, “The Pointing Game,” sounds almost childish before reading; afterward it feels chillingly simple. He points. She picks. Except of course he has chosen the rules, the timing, the route, the frame, the first question in bed, the move from private fantasy into daylight, and finally the command: “Go meet her.”

Pace is the character I feel most conflicted about, and this chapter pushes me further toward distrust while also making him very compelling. He is observant to an almost supernatural degree, and the prose stays mostly inside his satisfaction: “He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it.” That’s sexy as competence, but also immediately controlling as certainty. He keeps telling himself the ethical version of what he’s doing — “He wanted her gathered back into herself when he asked, free to weigh it and choose; that mattered to him more than the answer did” — and I half believe that he believes it. He asked it anyway.” That line is the chapter’s hinge for me. The page earns suspicion there. It is not me importing distrust from the jacket; the text says he knows the timing is unfair and does it anyway.

Randi breaks my heart a little here, because she is so alive in this chapter. She is not just damaged or acted on; she is witty, socially brilliant, erotically awake, hungry, funny. I love the moment where she starts actually playing: “She was shopping.” That made me laugh and wince at the same time, because yes, she’s participating, and yes, the participation is real. Her “No. Too expected,” and “She’d be a project,” and “You’d get both or neither” are sharp and wonderfully characterful. I can feel her taste emerging, not just Pace’s hypothesis about her taste. But the chapter also keeps showing that she needs disguises to survive her own wanting. The big one is devastating: “not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy.” That feels true to Randi as I know her so far — her social self instantly finding a livable costume — and it makes her later bare “Yes” land hard.

Her warmth and openness are attractive, but also exactly what makes her vulnerable in this setup.

Not a deal-breaker; just a place where the chapter’s intelligence is very visible.
