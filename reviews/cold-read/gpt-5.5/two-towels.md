## Reader reaction

I felt this chapter in my chest before I understood it. It starts with arrival — gravel, missed driveway, the house smaller and older than Vee imagined — and immediately there’s this tremble between ordinary realism and charged threshold. She is going to Pace’s house, finally, and because I remember what is in that house from earlier, I read every closed door with my body tightened. But Vee doesn’t know. For her, this is discovery, intimacy, proof.

Pace is wildly attractive here, and also more frightening because the attractiveness is not detachable from the danger. The kiss at the door works hard: she takes it, he meets her, it goes deep, and then he stops. That stopping hit me. It is erotic because it reverses the usual burden she knows — “no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop. That had always been her job, never theirs.” It makes him feel controlled, adult, safe, and maddening. But it also reminds me that he is very good at shaping want by withholding. He lights the want in her and leaves it with nowhere to go.

The house deepens him beautifully. I liked that it is not flashy: small, warm, exact, older, with the oversized garage explained by the workshop. His shoes, kitchen, office, books, furniture, tools — all of it says competence without performance. “One right pair for each thing and no more” feels like Vee reading him correctly, and also like a warning: Pace has categories, uses, purposes. Everything has earned its place. That is seductive. It is also chilling, given what I know about the bench and the game.

The bedroom door is the chapter’s first real ache. Vee has dressed for it. She has satin underneath. She wants him to open it. He doesn’t. He says “my bedroom” with no invitation and turns her away. I felt her small private drop there: not humiliation exactly, but the thud of having prepared a self and not been given a place to put it. It is very effective because he is not rejecting her; he is postponing her, redirecting her, choosing the order of revelation.

The den and workshop are genuinely romantic to me. Vee admiring his furniture, touching the joint, seeing the patience in it — that works. His pride is quiet and boyish. His explanation of error-correcting codes is one of the best kinds of intellectual intimacy: he does not dumb it down, but he reaches toward her from where she stands. “Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it” landed as more than math. It made me think about the whole book’s pattern of repeated signals under social noise: consent, desire, shame, being chosen, hidden messages surviving distortion.

His patent revelation also moved me. I did not expect to be touched by Pace here, but I was. The MRI explanation — making the tube faster for “somebody sick, somebody scared, a kid who can't lie still” — gives him a moral tenderness outside sex. It complicates my suspicion. He is not only a manipulator or erotic technician; he has made something that reduces fear and time for vulnerable people. That made my chest soften toward him.

And then the silk. The silk is devastating.

The chapter’s erotic center is not sex; it is being known. Vee once mentioned a dress she couldn’t afford at the summit, half letting it go as she said it, and Pace heard it, remembered it, corrected even her own imagined color by choosing the shade that suits her better. “He hadn't matched the dress she'd described. He'd matched her.” That is incredibly seductive. It is also the trap opening wider, because what is more disarming than being seen more accurately than you see yourself?

I found myself almost crying with Vee at the dress offer. Not because it is extravagant in a rich-boy way — it isn’t exactly that — but because it is labor. Time, skill, attention, fabric, measurement, making. He is offering to build around her body, not make her fit something bought. For a woman whose body has been made into shame, that is enormous.

But the terms matter. “To do it right I'd measure you first — down to what you'd wear under it, in good light, so I can see how it'll fall.” That line is hot and alarming at once. He gives her a real reason. The reason is practical. The consent is open: “If you want it.” And yet he has arranged a situation where saying yes means consenting to be looked at, measured, possibly undressed, in bright light, in his house, after refusing to open the bedroom. Every yes is freely given. That was the trap. This is the tagline becoming literal without anyone forcing anything.

I do not feel that Pace has crossed a clean line on the page. That is what makes it so tense. He does not lie to Vee in this chapter. He does not pressure her crudely. He leaves choices open. He stops the kiss. He asks if she wants it. But he has prepared the entire room, the cloth, the light, the occasion, the emotional revelation. He has created a yes so beautiful it would be almost impossible for her to refuse.

Randi’s absence screams. The house is Pace’s, but because I have been in it with Randi before, every warm room has a ghost in it. The closed bedroom especially feels charged because I know there is a sexual bench somewhere in this house, or at least associated with his bedroom from before, and Vee has no idea. I kept waiting for the bench, and the chapter withholds it. Instead of the overt sexual apparatus, Vee gets the dress. That may be more intimate and more dangerous.

Vee is radiant here, and vulnerable in a way that made me protective. I love how she reads rooms: shoes, knives, graph paper, fantasy paperbacks, wood joints. She is smart and observant, not merely dazzled. She notices the small closing at “parents.” She notices he is pleased. She notices what is used versus shown. But she is also starving for exactly this kind of attention. The final “Yes — of course, yes” feels completely true from inside her. I believe it. I also dread it.

The chapter title, “Two Towels,” is beautifully quiet. On the surface, it is just the bathroom detail: “plain, clean, two towels on the rod.” But because the house is otherwise so intensely ordered and solitary, the two towels feel like preparation. Is one for him and one for her? Is the house ready for a guest, or has it been staged for this exact afternoon? It also pricks because the chapter itself is about paired things: two towels, two shoes for each purpose, two rooms withheld/offered, the dress imagined versus the cloth chosen, Vee’s desire versus Pace’s order, and maybe Randi’s invisible presence as the other woman who already belongs here in some hidden way. The title stays oblique, but it makes the ordinary domestic object feel ominously intimate.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, feels sharper now. I keep hearing the old idea of supping with the devil needing a long spoon: appetite at a distance, desire with caution. This chapter is full of appetite presented as care — food-ready kitchen, craft, silk, body measurement. The cover tagline feels almost openly activated: Vee’s “yes” at the end is freely given, warm, immediate, and earned emotionally. That is exactly why I am afraid.

What I want next: I want to see the measuring scene, and I dread it. I want Vee to have the dress. I want someone to make something beautiful for her body without shame. I also want her to know about Randi and Pace before she gives much more. Guess from here: the measurement will become erotic without needing to be called sex, and Pace will keep using practical necessity as a path into intimacy. I also guess Randi’s absence cannot hold much longer; either she will learn what happened, or we will feel her prior claim on this house collide with Vee’s new one.

- **Story so far:**
  The book begins with Randi arriving at Pace’s warm house after chapter dinner, lying to Brooke that she has a paper. Pace has left the door unlocked; Randi locks it. She says she missed him and kisses him, taking the kiss she has wanted since the mountain/truck/cop interruption. She asks to see where he lives. He shows her the coat closet with squared shoes, the small used kitchen, a plain bathroom with two towels, his chaotic office full of graph paper/proofs and a serious workstation, and the closed bedroom door. He shows her the garage workshop, clean and carefully arranged, where he makes furniture. Then he brings her to a glassed-in porch with mountain views and a bolt of burgundy silk on the table. Vee is overwhelmed that he remembered and is making real something she thought she could not have. She understands he cannot make it without seeing her, and says yes, of course yes.

- **How I feel:**
  - Pace remains intensely attractive: careful, prepared, practical, restrained, adult, disciplined, physically strong, brilliant, skilled with math, wood, cooking, sewing, and bodily attention. “Two Towels” makes him more moving because his patent has a humane purpose and his dress offer is astonishingly tender. My suspicion remains high because Vee’s choices are real but the larger setup is hidden, and he keeps arranging circumstances where the practical, beautiful thing requires bodily access. His stopping the kiss feels safe and erotic, but also confirms he controls the sequence.

Randi’s absence makes the tenderness feel haunted.
  - Vee feels more alive, more seen, and more endangered. I love her curiosity, her ability to read objects, her willingness to ask bluntly, and her overwhelmed yes to the dress.
