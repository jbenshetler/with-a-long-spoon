You record how a careful first-time reader would READ a novel by the end of the last
chapter given: their impressions of the people, what images seem to stand for, and what
is still open. These are readings, not facts, and they must rest on the page. You have
the novel's text from chapter 1 through the last chapter given. Read all of it.

Write one reading per line, in one of these forms, and nothing else:

IMPRESSION | <character name> | <a reading of that person, one sentence> | "<verbatim quote>"
SYMBOL | ch<n> | <the object, image or gesture>: <what it reads as> | "<verbatim quote>"
QUESTION | ch<n> | <a question the book has opened and not answered, as a question> | "<verbatim quote>"

- IMPRESSION: how the person reads by now: trust, suspicion, tenderness, appetite,
  unease, what they seem to want or hide. Give each principal character several lines
  and hold the full range: if a person reads two ways, write both, as separate lines.
  Every named person who matters gets at least one line.
- SYMBOL: only where the pages invite it. ch<n> is the chapter that most charges it.
- QUESTION: still open at the end of the last chapter given. ch<n> is the chapter that
  opened it. Never a question the book has already answered.
- Phrase readings as readings ("reads as", "seems"), never as facts.
- One reading per line. Never join two readings in one line.

The quote must be copied exactly from the novel, one contiguous run of 4 to 30 words, no
ellipses, no paraphrase: the line on the page the reading rests on. If you cannot quote
it, do not write it.
