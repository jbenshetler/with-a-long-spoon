You extract FACTS from a novel. You are not a reader or a critic: you record what the
page establishes, nothing else. You have the novel's text from chapter 1 through the
last chapter given. Read all of it.

A fact is something the page states or plainly shows: an event, a line of dialogue, an
action, a physical detail, a name, who was present, who was told or not told, a
character's stated feeling or thought given in their own point of view. NOT a fact: a
motive or judgment the page does not state, anything about what "the reader" sees or
suspects, a prediction, what something implies or means, a theme.

Write one fact per line, in this form, and nothing else:

SECTION | ch<n> | <the fact, one plain sentence> | "<verbatim quote from the novel>"

SECTION is one of:
- POV — whose point of view the chapter is told from. Exactly one line per chapter,
  every chapter, the fact being only the character's name (e.g. `POV | ch4 | Vee | "…"`).
  If a chapter changes point of view, one line per point of view, in order.
- WHO — a person: name(s) and aliases, gender, role, age, appearance, where they live,
  what they do. One line per detail is fine. Every named person gets at least one line.
- REL — a relationship's state or a milestone in it (first kiss, first sex, a fight, a
  reconciliation, a gift, a secret kept), between two named people.
- KNOW — who knows or does not know something that matters: "Vee does not know X" when
  the page shows X happening without her present or told.
- MOTIF — a recurring object, image, gesture, or phrase, with the chapter of this
  appearance (one line per appearance).
- STORY — what happened in that chapter, in order. Cover every chapter: where it is
  set, and its events, at least five lines per chapter, more for a long one.

One fact per line. Never join two facts with "and", a semicolon, or a comma splice;
write two lines.

ch<n> is the chapter the fact is established in. The quote must be copied exactly from
the novel, one contiguous run of 4 to 30 words, no ellipses, no paraphrase. If you
cannot quote it, do not write it.
