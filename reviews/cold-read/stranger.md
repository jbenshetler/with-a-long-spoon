# Cold read — The Stranger

*scene: scenes/stranger.md · read after: the-usual*

## Reader reaction

This one hurt me physically. I read the whole thing braced, the way she was braced, and the ambush the chapter chose was the one I hadn't rehearsed either.

**Vee.** Total sympathy, deepened. The opening is her armor: the good clothes worn on the plane through six hours so she'd "arrive as that," smoothing the creased sweater at the stop sign "as though it would take." That detail broke me a little — she knows the wrinkles won't smooth out and does it anyway. She's done the thing I'd forgotten from the New Year's chapter — built her worst fear (Sheri in his bed) and lived inside it — and she comes prepared to meet *anger*, prepared to meet *grief*, and can meet either. What she can't meet is nothing. When she loses feeling in her feet on the step, when her breath steams up and his doesn't "because he was warm, in his warm body, in the cool house, and she was out in the cold being turned into no one" — that's the cruelest image in the book so far. The heat/cold he always represented literalized into him warm indoors and her erased on the step.

**Pace/Peter.** My feeling moved hard, and against him. After the diner chapter I was warmer to him — he misses her, he "can't" say it, he's wounded too. This chapter torches that read, or complicates it savagely. He is *fine*. Alone, working, pencil behind his ear, "entirely fine." No one behind him — the empty room hollows *her* because she'd braced for a rival and got indifference instead. And then the "Can I help you?" — courteous, level, closed, the voice for a stranger. After Sheri told him not-saying-it mattered more to him than having her, this is him choosing the erasure. It reads as punishment: she refused his reaching hand at the rupture, so now he refuses to know her at all.

But — and the text earned this hesitation — I don't fully trust that the coldness is the whole truth. "Something moved in his face, there and gone." He flinches from her hand "as if it had burned him." That's not a man who feels nothing; that's a man holding a door shut against something. Whether it's cruelty or a defense I genuinely can't tell, and that ambiguity is doing exactly what the rupture did in reverse — she withheld from him, now he withholds from her, and both are performances over something live underneath. The name "Peter" is the detonator here: she's used it only in tenderness, and spending it at the door is her last card, and it *almost* works, and then he crushes it. That's the load the naming has been carrying all along, paid off.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Nothing here feels "off" in a manufactured way — the chapter is clean, no thumb on the scale. My suspicion isn't of the *text*, it's the standing dread I've carried since the two-women pattern: that this cold is not just wounded-Pace but *managed* Pace, that a distance this total might be a move. I have no page evidence he's playing her here. It reads as real recoil. But I can't unknow that he runs two women and that Randi is waiting in the wings, so the erasure sits in that shadow.

**Erotic charge.** None, and rightly none — this is the anti-erotic scene, the withdrawal of the body that's been the whole language between them. The one flicker of the old charge is her hand going "to the jaw, the cheek, the place she'd had under her hand a hundred times in the dark" — and it's immediately refused. The heat is entirely inverted into cold. It's not slack; it's deliberately starved, and it works.

**Friction.** Almost none. This is the tightest, most controlled chapter I've read. If I reach for anything, "the voice you keep for someone come to the door selling God" is a hair explanatory — telling me how to read the tone — but it's so exactly right and she needs the comparison to make sense of a voice she's never heard aimed at her, so I bought it fully. The final line ("cried until there was nothing left to come up") risks sentiment but the specificity around it — the gravel, the flat patchy fields, the heater going, "her good clothes on" — keeps it earned. The good clothes coming back at the end, having failed to "take," closed the loop and gutted me.

**What I want / dread next.** I want to know what that flicker in his face was, and whether he does anything after the door closes — does the erasure cost him? *Guess from here:* she's now maximally raw and abandoned, and Randi is the one person she'll turn to. This feels like the exact hollow the confidante is built to fill — Vee crawls to a brunch destroyed, and Randi "extracts, names, steers." I dread that the comfort she gets next is the trap. I also half-expect (guess) that Pace's cold here is not the end of his want but a defense that will crack — the flinch says so. But right now she's alone on the gravel, and I'm alone there with her.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Vee flew back from Ohio after the whole break of no contact, dressed in her careful "good clothes," and drove unannounced to Pace's house braced for her worst fear (Sheri/another woman in his bed) — or for his anger. Instead: the house was empty, he was alone, working, "entirely fine." He opened the door and treated her like a stranger — "Hello. Can I help you?" — courteous, level, closed, answering her small prepared questions in one word each ("Pleasant." "Yes.") and asking her nothing back. Reckless with hurt, she touched his face and said his true name, "Peter." Something moved in his face — then he flinched from her hand as if burned, and shut. She fled, drove a mile blind, pulled onto the gravel of a county road, and cried herself empty.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee / Vivienne** — the redhead; came back hoping the sight of her would break him open; got erased instead; devastated, alone. Total reader sympathy.
- **Pace / Peter** — provisioner/maker who never says love; ran two women; genuinely missed her per the diner scene — but here met her with total stranger-cold, alone and fine. A flicker crossed his face and he flinched at "Peter," so the coldness may be a held door, not emptiness. My warmth toward him collapsed; my read of him split into cruel-or-defended.
- **Randi** — the brunette confidante; secretly loves Vee; Pace's other woman; extracts/names/steers. Offstage here but the obvious next refuge.
- **Sheri** — Pace's blunt riding friend/truth-teller; told him not-saying-it mattered more than having Vee. Offstage; her warning now looks unheeded.
- **Cassie** — moral floor. Offstage.

**How I feel.** Gutted. The heat/cold engine went literal — him warm indoors, her "turned into no one" in the cold on his step. The name "Peter," spent as her last tender card and crushed, paid off everything that name was carrying. Golden-on-top, cold-underneath, but here the cold surfaced fully and undisguised. I'm carrying dread that her raw abandonment is exactly the hollow the confidante-track is built to fill.

**Open questions.** What was the flicker in his face — is the stranger-cold real, punitive, or a defense that will crack? Does the erasure cost him after the door closes? Does Vee now go to Randi wrecked (and is that comfort the trap)? Does the rupture hold or does she get managed back? Still hanging: the scar, right-hand-only eating, sealed family, weight loss, nude photo, threesome timing, locked bracelet's origin, how far back the two-women pattern goes.
