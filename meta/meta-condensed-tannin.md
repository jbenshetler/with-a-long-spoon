The deadbolt goes over before he is off the couch — she always locks it behind her, first

One glass of red stands on the low table, poured an hour earlier and left to open; he had
known she would come since the second block of the walk home. He has poured none for
himself. She straddles him on the den couch, upright, facing him, reaches past his

He gives her almost nothing at first — *"We got frozen yogurt." "We walked."* — so she
takes his hands off her hips, draws them up under the wire, closes his fingers over her
breasts, lets him watch her take the pleasure of it, and puts one word hot against his

his palms and her thighs go tight against his and stay — the readiness he knows from the
floor and his own bed — and he reads it correctly as the want from the lawn, grown larger
with no help from him. Then, on his own gladness (*"She's easy to listen to"*), she goes

at the corner of her mouth arrives a fraction late. He reads that as the same want, held
harder. What he misses is the jealousy.

He does not hand over what Vee said on the walk — the true thing she asked him to pretend
she hadn't. *"It was hers."*

He draws her down against him: *"She's fun. You'll like her."* / *"I already do,"* — easy
and bright. She finishes the glass in one long swallow, takes his face in both hands,
kisses him with the last of the wine, gets up off him and holds out her hand.

**Close:** he had not had a drop of it. He could taste nothing else.
