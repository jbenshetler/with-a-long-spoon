# Distribution & positioning — *With a Long Spoon* (series-wide; Vol 1 leads)

*Decisions recorded 2026-08-14 from an extended distribution/positioning workshop.
Supersedes the earlier open-fork draft. **Distribution/marketing domain only** — it
defers every canon/thesis/character claim to `meta-thesis.md`, `meta-arch-bible.md`,
and the `meta-craft-*`/`meta-arch-*` docs, which own them; reconcile any framing
below against those. Companions: `meta-plan-free-sample.md` (discovery funnel),
`meta-blurb.md` (comps + genre positioning), `meta-plan-pen-name.md` (the firewall),
`meta-plan-lawyer.md` (ALLi platform consult), `meta-cover.md`.*

## 1. The lane — self-publish (decided)

- **The novel is self-published; a trad-novel deal is declined on creative-control
  grounds.** The book's length is the *mechanism*, not padding: the explicit detail
  is characterization (her interiority carries it inside the sex; Pace's is
  recapitulated in the following cooking scene because he is opaque during the sex),
  so both the sex and the paired cooking scenes are load-bearing structure, not heat
  (canon: bible/thesis/`meta-craft-vivienne.md`). Trad acquires and sells by
  category, so it would (a) force a no-comp book into an established channel and
  (b) cut length — and this book's structure is *quiet*, so a market-driven editor
  reads the cooking scenes and the shame-as-characterization sex as digression to
  trim. As a debut with no comp and no leverage, the set of editors who would both
  *acquire* it and *protect* the architecture is ~empty. Self-pub keeps full control.
- **Prestige is not the target, and finishing the trilogy does not court it.** A
  ~1300-page explicit *trilogy* is anti-prestige grammar (prestige = the singular
  novel; "trilogy" = genre/commercial; explicit erotic heat ≠ prestige's cold
  register). The completed trilogy (Book 2 early 2027, Book 3 fall 2027) is a
  *self-pub* asset — rapid-release, read-through — not a prestige one.
- **Trad/prestige is reordered to inbound-after-success, not acquisition-first.** If
  the self-pub series breaks out, print/audio/foreign/film interest arrives on the
  author's terms, structure intact, leverage maximal (the *Fifty Shades* sequence
  minus the fanfic). Hold one narrow door only for a specific editor who
  demonstrably gets it; do not reorganize the work around it.
- **Prestige short-form stays available** as credential/discovery — the adapted
  `{{Two Towels}}` + `{{A Round}}` short to *Erotic Review* / *Best Women's Erotica*
  (`meta-plan-free-sample.md`): author-controlled excerpts, no novel-editing risk.

## 2. Store strategy — wide, not KU/Select (decided)

- **Sell wide:** Amazon (KDP) + Kobo + Apple, the non-Amazon stores via
  Draft2Digital. **Not** enrolled in KDP Select / Kindle Unlimited.
- **Why not KU**, despite the length-monetization draw (KENP page-reads on a long
  bingeable series): Select's 90-day exclusivity covers *any portion* of the enrolled
  work, which forecloses the serialization/free-sample discovery funnel; and the
  flip-to-Select-later door does not reliably reopen — scraped/mirrored copies persist
  beyond your control and Amazon's automated "free elsewhere" crawler flags copies you
  don't own, so serialization is effectively a durable commitment to wide. Since
  discovery for a no-comp book must run off-Amazon anyway (§4), wide is the coherent
  choice.
- **No direct sales** (own-site storefront). Route the payment-processor relationship
  through the big platforms so you are not the one deplatformed (§3).

## 3. Platform & payment-processor risk (decided posture)

- **A big platform's weight protects the *platform*, not you.** July 2025: under
  anti-porn-lobby pressure on Visa/Mastercard, Steam (132M users) and itch.io purged
  tens of thousands of adult titles with no notice, to "protect core payment
  infrastructure." Size makes you a cheap sacrifice, not a shield.
- **Therefore wide = business-continuity insurance.** No single platform's purge or
  one processor's pressure can zero the catalog if you're on several stores. This is
  the primary risk argument for wide, independent of reach.
- **The content is on the safe side of the processor line** — a real asset. The
  prohibited themes (D2D's Erotica Certification System / card-network rules) are
  underage, incest, bestiality, and rape or "dubious consent framed for titillation";
  the book is consent-forward. **But enforcement is dumb and keyword-based** (2025
  case: "forced feminization" auto-flagged "non-consensual," appeal lost, the author's
  *entire catalog* taken down). So **police the metadata, not just the prose** —
  blurb, keywords, and categories must read consent-forward and must not pattern-match
  to manipulation/dubcon (see §4 packaging note). Certify accurately via D2D's ECS;
  conservative covers + PG-13 descriptions everywhere, regardless of interior heat.
- **Retailers:** Kobo friendliest/clearest; Apple viable but vaguer; B&N effectively
  closed to erotica (2017 purge); library channels exclude erotica. AI-cover
  disclosure is now moot (cover is human-made — `meta-cover.md`).

## 4. Audience & positioning

- **"No comp" is a precise empty coordinate, not a vague absence.** Three axes:
  **explicitness** (more than literary fiction gives), **interiority / emotional
  maturity** (more than erotic romance gives), **warmth** (not prestige's
  cold/cruel/detached register). Every adjacent lane maxes two and drops the third —
  erotic romance (explicit + warm, shallow); prestige literary (explicit + interior,
  cold); upmarket/Rooney/*Acts of Service* (interior + warm-ish, under-explicit: *cold
  + smart − explicit*). The book is the sole occupant of the corner high on all three.
  Comp shelf owned by `meta-blurb.md`.
- **Primary reader vector: up from erotic/dark romance, not down from literary.**
  Primarily women who've read widely in spicy romance and want depth and emotional
  maturity without losing the heat, and who don't want prestige's coldness. She lives
  in the largest, most reachable reader ecosystem (romance BookTok/Bookstagram,
  KU-romance readers, romance cross-promo), not the tiny literary-erotica niche.
- **Register — the non-negotiable correction (thesis-owned; the marketing must carry
  it faithfully):** **no one in the book is a villain or a predator — if anyone can be
  classified as a villain, the book has failed.** Pace and Randi are loving, generous,
  complex people; the originating wrong is *a game and a single lie* / a bad choice
  Pace can't back out of, not a scheme. Everyone is devastated, not only Vee. **Vee
  ends hurt but ascendant** — the loss is her coming-into-power, not her ruin. Thesis
  touchstone: the Cassie beat — told the whole truth, she still says *"the best lover
  of your life, the best friend of your life, all for a full year — where do I sign
  up?"* The reader is meant to end agreeing with Cassie. (Canon owned by
  `meta-thesis.md` / `meta-arch-bible.md`; reconcile wording there.)
- **Consequences for positioning:**
  - **Further from dark romance than from anything** — dark romance is built on the
    dangerous/morally-gray man; no-villain is its structural opposite. Do **not** court
    the "wreck me with a bad man" reader — wrong reader.
  - **Register is literary bittersweet** — tragic complicity among good people,
    ascendant through loss. Ending-valence reference (tone, not content): *Call Me By
    Your Name* (warm, interior, no villain, a love ending in loss that *enlarges* the
    protagonist).
  - **Sell emotional *maturity*** (grown-up, earned, psychologically true feeling) —
    the sharp differentiator from dark romance's emotional *intensity* (lurid). The
    target review is the maturity version of "this wrecked me": *"it cost her
    everything and she'd do it again."*
  - **Positioning language generalizes `meta-blurb.md`'s anti-*Fifty Shades* move:**
    speak to romance's own dissatisfaction — "outgrown the tropes but won't give up
    the heat."
- **Packaging must inoculate against the con-misreading.** The premise's *shape* (a
  game, a lie, dramatic irony she's inside of) pattern-matches to "con/predator" for a
  skimming browser or a hostile reviewer — a jump even a fully-briefed reader can make.
  Copy and the opening surface must pre-frame *complicated love, not a con*. (The
  Cassie beat and the ascendant ending are the on-page mechanisms; the copy must carry
  the same load for people who haven't read yet.)
- **CW-as-marketing:** `meta-blurb.md` ruled no content-warning list for the test round
  (it telegraphs). Revisit for wide — calibrated to the consent-forward, no-villain
  content, as an *invitation* to the reader, not *Haunting Adeline*–style thrill-bait
  (H. D. Carlton's — the dark-romance negative comp from `meta-blurb.md`, not a
  chapter of ours).

## 5. Marketing under the firewall

- **The pen name (Helen Rivers) is load-bearing marketing infrastructure, not just
  privacy.** An older cis male author is a real liability in the female-authored,
  female-read romance/erotica space and an ownvoices-scrutiny surface for
  sapphic-adjacent explicit content. (Neutral-to-fine in literary fiction generally —
  but this book is marketed into the female-reader lane.) Firewall rationale owned by
  `meta-plan-pen-name.md`.
- **Closed by the firewall:** in-person events, book fairs, signings, face-video, and
  voice (a male voice under a woman's name is an outing vector). So the dominant
  indie-romance engine — author-persona / parasocial marketing — is unavailable; the
  book fights one-handed.
- **Open, and to be used:**
  - **The email list** — the one uncensorable, owned channel; everything feeds it. The
    CTA/mailing-list page is required (also an editorial-checklist front/back-matter
    item).
  - **Faceless social** — faceless BookTok/Bookstagram is normal (books, hands,
    aesthetic b-roll, text overlay, trending audio).
  - **Text-first presence** — newsletter, written interviews, guest posts, text AMAs.
  - **Author cross-promo** — BookFunnel/StoryOrigin (platforms allow adult; build an
    adult-friendly, *sensibility-matched* partner network; newsletter swaps). The
    concrete substitute for the ad channels that are closed.
  - **Suppression-routing** genre promo newsletters + review networks that screen for
    dungeoned titles.
  - **The book's own word-of-mouth** — a starved taste-audience evangelizes hard — and
    **series read-through** (Book 2/3 as the engine).
- **Persona:** thin, consistent, work-forward, minimal falsifiable biography — which
  suits both the taste-audience (cares about the book, not the author) and the firewall
  (nothing to be caught out on).
- **Discovery is slow-and-compounding, not a launch spike** — ads are largely closed
  (Meta/Amazon/Google and most promo newsletters exclude explicit). This suits a long
  series: each volume grows the list that sells the next.
