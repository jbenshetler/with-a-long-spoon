# Note — The Usual

Companion note for `scenes/the-usual.md`. Placement: ~late December, Christmas break, after {{Believe Me}}. **Pace close third.** Third panel of the break triptych ({{Fairytale}} | {{Old Acquaintances}} | **The Usual**); the one bond on the page that isn't lonely.

## Format — face-to-face, NOT side-by-side

The scene is a diner **booth, across the table**, eyes engaged. The overshare loosens through easy company and food — and the load-bearing Sheri-decency is that he *looked at her* through her troubles. (Format rationale and the retirement of the old reading: `meta-arch-sheri.md`, render disciplines.)

## The engine

Pace answers Vee's demand for a **declaration** with a **ledger** — an itemized case for his devotion. The *form* is the tell: an emotional question ("do you love me / what are we") met with an accounting. Love-as-currency, his mother's wound running in him. Rendered inside his POV as a conscious failure he cannot yet overcome, never as narrator diagnosis; **the vow against declaration is never named in this scene.**

- **"She wants a declaration… I said it, out loud, in words."** He knows what she wants and what it weighs: he heard the cracker-and-meal figure as her displaced declaration, wanted to answer it, and deliberately routed his answer into the safer truth *"I love how good we are together."* He knows *"you're the most important thing in my life"* is another insufficient surrounding truth.
- **person → thing.** Intentional amplification of the fight canon (`believe-me.md`: "most important **person**"). "Thing" is the objectifying tell — the ledger-mind rating its top asset — invisible on first read, lethal on the pattern.
- **"Is that so hard?" is answered only by "I can't."** Asked, dodged into the ledger ("the one he had answers to"), then answered naked once Sheri strips the dodge away. *"I'm not them"* cannot make honesty his excuse: Pace knows the declaration would be true. The remaining *I can't* carries the guilt and weakness of failing to say it; protecting himself still wins.
- **"Her name is Vee."** He insists on her name to Sheri while withholding the one naming Vee actually wants. Uncommented.

## Pace's chivalric register = restraint, not cleverness

Where his good register lands is Sheri's loss (Paige, the family estrangement): nothing clever he can lay over it. Plainness chosen by a sophisticated man — *"People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice."* Not folksy (that's Sheri); precise.

## Sheri

- **Lechery / making-up-for-lost-time** (see `meta-arch-sheri.md`): frank, warm, coarse ("the one with the big tits," "pleased with herself for saying it"), her delight in her own boldness the safeguard against crudeness.
- **Her own wound, glanced not told.** Paige left because Sheri isn't in contact with her family; *"ironic, isn't it"* plants the estrangement as an unexplained weight. This is the **plant**; the disowning *story* stays reserved for {{Another Round}}. "From this seat, that feels pretty shitty" carries a second floor on reread (the seat of the one not chosen).
- Warmth **is** the fierceness: she hands him the hard truth and steals his fries in the same beat.

## Randi — one glancing ping (guard held)

"Bet she's a lot" is the canon **one-encounter-one-verdict** (`meta-arch-sheri.md`). She reads Randi's surface (vanity, "never did learn my name"), never the configuration — protect the Cassandra asymmetry.

**NEW CANON established here:** Pace brought Randi to the **CW dance hall once, ~November** (after the late-Oct CW Dance). This does not breach the "rarely dates *from* it" / "doesn't bring dates" canon (he didn't pick her up there; she's a pre-existing entanglement) — but note the **deliberate irony**: at the late-Oct CW Dance the bartender comforts Vee with *"Pace don't bring dates — tonight he brought one [you]… wasn't him they were jealous of, honey,"* framing Vee as the singular exception. Pace having brought Randi too quietly **hollows that reassurance** — Vee's manufactured specialness, on-thesis. The November timing keeps the bartender's line true when spoken.

## Tone / craft

- **Earn the dark by being light.** Surface is warm and funny (the busboy arithmetic, the lechery, the "philosophy of your food" tease that punctures his intellectualism); the hard center sits inside a genuinely happy scene; it ends **bright** — Sheri lifts him rather than leaving him gutted.
- **The two-with-no-family-table parallel is wordless** — the *Leave It to Beaver* family under the tabletop glass, the jukebox breaching the one refuge from the Christmas loop ("Somebody always plays it"), a Christmas neither of them was driving home for. Pace's POV holds the parallel; neither says it.
- **Food-as-care motif** surfaces via "the philosophy of your food" — Vee *receives his deed-grammar*, which is the exact tragedy (she reads the deeds and still needs the words). No running threads seeded (not a sex scene).

## The close — home, the drink, the phone (author 2026-10-07)

Added so the night is rendered rather than reported: panel two had the
whiskey only as consequence (*"the whiskey had taken his balance"*). This also
makes it the hinge of the planned diptych (see below), so the drinking stops
being a gap between chapters.

**The drink and the music are the one place he breaks the usual.** The chapter's
usuals are the diner order and the freezer rows; the whiskey is the single
exception in it, and the morning closes on *"He ate anyway"* — the apparatus
coming back. **Usual → break → usual**, and the break is the measure. He **does
not drink alone** — *"There had never been an occasion for it and he had never
gone hunting one"* — which is also why the night wrecks him as thoroughly as the
bleach and the mop in the hall say it did.

**The music obeys `meta-note-music-thread.md`, and an earlier draft did not.** He
is alone, so the house sounds like **Pace** — jazz, his own lane — and the
departure is *within* it (the late things, a horn with the shine off). A beat
where he scrolled **past Vee's playlist** was drafted and **cut**: it breaks the
thread's central rule (*"Pace is not curating… never caused by, and never
tracking, whom Pace expects"*) and would make him aware of a pattern that exists
only for the reader. **Do not reinstate it.** No artist is named, matching the
unnamed rendering in the morning panel — and the album runs on unheard into the
dark, so the music is still going when he wakes. **The playlist is the seam; the
diptych needs no bridge.**

**The photographs are all consented, and that is the point.** *"He had only ever
taken the ones she was in on purpose."* The two of them in the leaves, the
blurred kiss, Vee in on a Friday, Vee on a Sunday with the ruined coffee. **No
picture of her asleep** — she could not say yes, so it does not exist; the
absence is never remarked. The Friday photo rests on `meta-arch-bible.md:115`
(*"the Friday reunion is a date night she dresses for"*): they do not go out, she
dresses anyway, *"for a house with one person in it,"* and he is the entire
audience — which is what makes *on purpose* pay twice. *Looking straight into
the phone instead of dropping her eyes* ties to {{On Her Floor}}.

**The spiral is Vee → Daphne → Randi → Vee**, with drinks and album changes
punctuating.

- **Daphne gets an image and nothing else** — brown hair, glasses, the laugh
  behind her hand. No facts, no declaration, **no name**: `meta-note-first-love.md`
  reserves the break for {{Excavations}} (*"if it becomes the break, it steals
  {{Excavations}}"*), and `meta-arch-bible.md:195` reserves *Peter* for Vee. The
  division that results: **night is the ache, morning is the lesson** — the sober
  panel does the analysis (*"he had taught himself that the words were the
  difference"*). The laugh behind her hand is a silent contrast with Vee, who
  *"smiles readily and with her whole face"* (`meta-arch-vivienne.md:49`); never
  point at it.
- **Randi is fond, specific and real — never a stopgap and never dismissed**
  (author 2026-10-07). He thinks of her **with pleasure**: her weight back against
  his chest, the blanket she wants a minute after every time, *"Say something nice
  to me,"* and he means the smile. She is on another continent, and *"it would not
  have mattered if she had been in the next room"* — carried by **"Glad was not
  what was wrong with him,"** never by a simile about bandages, which would be an
  analysis he would not make. The blanket brackets the panels: he keeps it on the
  chair for her here, and folds it off the bed in the morning.
- Content note: he has done it *right* with Randi — never said it, nothing at
  risk, genuine warmth — and it is no protection at all tonight. The vow worked
  and did not save him. **Resonance only; he never draws the conclusion.**

## Panel two — folded in from `meta-note-not-enough.md` (merged 2026-10-07)

*The chapter formerly titled* Not Enough *is now panel two of this one. Its note is
absorbed below verbatim, headings demoted; `{{The Usual}}` references inside it are
references to this chapter's panel one. Panel two's own later rulings — the freezer
rows, the engine Pace knows is wrong — are recorded in the sections above.*

*Companion to the planned scene. Morning after {{The Usual}}, before {{My Friend Randi}}. Pace close third.*

### What this scene is

Pace alone after {{The Usual}}: the bodily cost of trying to drink Vee away, the domestic memories that prove he knew exactly what he withheld, and the Daphne-conditioned fear beneath that choice. His silence did not spare him profound grief; his grown life lets him carry it without being consumed as he was when Daphne left.

### Structure (sketch)

*Pace's house · morning after {{The Usual}} · Pace close third.*

1. **Body and routine.** Pace wakes through his dry, aching body and the smell of bleach. He steps around the mop and bucket left from cleaning his vomit, takes another hot shower, rinses his mouth in the stream, brushes his teeth, and dresses in sweats. He puts on water and the coffee maker; from the ordered freezer rows he takes vacuum-bagged chicken and broccoli and boils them. He sits with water, black coffee, and food. He is not hungry, but his shaky body needs food.

2. **“Prison food.”** The chicken and broccoli recalls an unnamed prior girlfriend lightly calling it *prison food*. She is not Daphne and carries no emotional charge or backstory: an ordinary adult relationship and ordinary departure. Pace takes the first bite.

3. **The arriving smile.** He remembers waiting on the covered stoop when Vee arrived: her attention finding the house, then him, her face lighting into **the smile she gives only to him**. Warm and wistful; he misses seeing her approach and knowing that, in that moment, her happiness was happiness at finding him there.

4. **The proof and the failure.** The arrival opens into the ordinary cycle of having Vee in his house: her walking toward the bathroom at night, the unperformed view of her backside; her asleep when he rises first; her face at the first bite of something he made. Arrival → night → morning → meal. The first-bite image returns him to {{Seconds}} and her crackers-and-meal declaration. He understood it then, wanted to answer, felt his hand stop on her leg and took one breath, then another. He chose the safer, smaller truth: *I love how good we are together.* He knew it was insufficient. He feels guilty and weak because the true answer was available to him and true. He had wanted to say it; he had known what it would mean to her; he had not been strong enough. **But he had said it once before.**

5. **Daphne: his whole world.** Do not state their ages. Pace remembers being young and new to the university; Daphne was older and knew how things worked. She was his tutor, although academics were the only place he did not need her help. She taught him the mechanics: making the most of his scholarship meal plan, registering for classes, knowing where to go and what everyone else seemed already to know. She was his tutor, best friend, first girlfriend, first lover, first love. He told her he loved her. She said it to him. He meant it. Then she left. He had no friend to tell because Daphne had been the friend: *There had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told.*

6. **The protective rule.** *Daphne was the only woman he had told he loved. She was the only one he ever expected to. Her departure consumed his life.* Pace believed saying it was what let the loss consume him—not what made Daphne leave, but what made losing her hurt that badly. He decided never to expose himself that completely again. Not at first, but eventually there had been other women. A few. He had needed time to learn how to be with someone without making her his whole world. He had grown. The relationships mattered while they lasted; when they ended, he went on. He never told any of them he loved them. He believed he had learned how to keep loss survivable. Then Vee came. He knew he loved her, knew what she offered, wanted to answer, and was afraid. He believed keeping it unspoken would limit the hurt. He feels guilty because Vee paid for what Daphne's departure taught him to fear, and weak because he could not say what he knew was true. *He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him. He had believed that not saying it would make this hurt less.*

7. **The failed remedy.** *Whiskey River* was a liar.

   > The whiskey had taken his balance. His dinner. Several hours he could not use. It had left every memory of Vee intact.

   The song need not be playing. The bleach, bucket, dehydration, shower, and forced meal are the proof. He expected at least an interruption; the whiskey did not help.

8. **The truck does not turn in.** A truck approaches. Pace's head lifts before he decides to move; his body anticipates Vee before conscious knowledge corrects it. The truck does not slow. He sees Vee finding him on the covered stoop, her face becoming the smile she gives only him. It passes. His hand shakes; he puts down his fork, his breath breaks, and he sobs until he can no longer hear the truck, then for a few breaths after. Brief, involuntary, non-cathartic.

9. **He continues.** The sob passes without resolving anything. The truck is gone. His coffee has cooled; so have the chicken and broccoli. Pace picks up his fork. He is not hungry. **He eats anyway.**

### Guards

- **Pace's interior.** He is capable of deep analysis and knows his own love, Vee's offer, his failure, and its immediate cost. His error is the Daphne-conditioned premise that an unspoken love will hurt less if lost. Never render him as confused or unable to introspect.
- **The vow's motive.** Pace is simply afraid that naming love will make a later loss hurt as badly as Daphne's did. The motive stays fear. **The room may hold what the reader knows and he does not** (author ruling 2026-09-28, revised from "add no qualifications or conditions", written when this was a single volume): Randi's cashmere blanket, named, seen and set down in the waking paragraph — a deliberate object of hers in a Vee-grief chapter; a continuity pass must not flag it. He draws no line from it; the reader does.
- **The other end of the arrival.** The stoop memory carries both directions: her arriving smile, and the last time — her taillights going down through the cut and him in the open door, not moving. The cost is his own act, in image, not a motive (added 2026-09-28).
- **Degree, not immunity.** Pace never believed silence would prevent pain; he believed it would keep pain from consuming his life. The present grief is profound and hollowing, but he remains able to clean, cook, eat, and continue.
- **Daphne.** Preserve the concrete lived asymmetry and Pace's experience of her competence, kindness, and indispensability. Do not state ages, mandate the reader's verdict, recap the sex, assign Daphne a motive, or imply suicide.
- **Other women.** A few serious relationships after Pace had time to recover and grow; neither a conquest list nor a claim that they meant nothing. The *prison food* girlfriend remains unnamed and is not Daphne.
- **Vee's remembered body.** The bathroom walk is affectionate and hungry, not anatomical. Sleeping is tenderness, not surveillance. The four images form one shared-life cycle, not a sentimental montage.
- **The sob.** Use *sobbed* directly. No masculine shame, forced mastery, catharsis, or prolonged collapse.
- **No phone.** Pace misses seeing Vee, speaking with her, and hearing her. He does not call, decide to call, or make another contact gesture.
- **No title drop.** Do not put *not enough* into the prose. The title gathers the safer declaration, protective silence, failed whiskey, and sustaining routines without announcing itself.

### Continuity

- Morning after {{The Usual}}, during the silence that follows {{Believe Me}}; before {{My Friend Randi}} and {{Nothing Underneath}}. Pace may fear Vee is gone, but the narration cannot declare a definitive breakup he does not know has occurred.
- The crackers-and-meal declaration is in {{Seconds}}. Pace's first-bite memory echoes his admission in {{In His Hands}} that he thinks about Vee's face when she tastes something he made and that remembering her is pale beside her presence.
- Daphne predates Pace's house and his ability to cook his own food. The *prison food* memory therefore belongs to a later girlfriend.
- Hold the truck until after the *Whiskey River* beat.
