## 1. The reader, made findable

We've defined her by taste (`meta-plan-distribution.md` §4): **explicit + emotional
maturity + warmth, no villain.** To *find* her on the platform, translate that into
observable behavior:

- **A spicy-romance reader who's read past the treadmill.** Late-20s to 40s, hundreds
  of romances deep, and *vocal about dissatisfaction* — the person in the comments
  saying "I need something with actual depth," "why is every book the same five
  tropes," "rec me something that will *ruin* me but is actually well *written*." That
  frustration is her signature; it's how you spot her.
- **A dual-shelf reader.** She follows "spicy" tags *and* literary ones — the overlap
  of the dark-romance audience and the Sally-Rooney / "sad-girl lit" crowd. She rates
  spice levels but her praise is about the *prose* ("the writing, though").
- **Off the clone-wave, not on it.** She is not looking for the tenth *Fourth Wing* or
  *Icebreaker* clone; she's tired of them. That fatigue *is* her.

She is not defined by a trope. She's defined by wanting depth-plus-heat-plus-warmth
and being underserved on all three at once. That makes her real but bannerless — the
core acquisition problem this doc exists to solve.

## 2. The platform fights this book (know the grain)

BookTok discovery runs on **tropes and "if you liked X, read these ten" clone-waves**
— enemies-to-lovers, grumpy-sunshine, why-choose, dark-mafia, romantasy. Tropes are
the shared language; a book that isn't a clone of a live wave has **no wave to ride.**
This is the discovery liability *and* the differentiation — the same coin as
everywhere else in the plan.

Consequence: the play is **not** "ride a wave." It's (a) claim the tropes you honestly
have, and (b) speak to the readers who are *off* the treadmill and frustrated. Both in
§5.

## 3. Who performs — NOT the author (the firewall resolution)

**The load-bearing fact: BookTok books break because *readers and creators* post
them, not because the *author* does.** The engine is the **ARC-to-creator model** —
advance copies go to spicy-romance BookTokers, and *they* make the videos, with
*their own* faces, hands, and voices, to *their own* audiences. The author is the
**supplier, not the performer.**

This dissolves the firewall problem completely. Helen Rivers is a woman's pen name
over a middle-aged man (`meta-plan-pen-name.md`); a male face, **male hands**, or a
male voice on camera would out her. Under the creator-seeding model none of that ever
happens — the bodies on camera belong to real readers. Two layers, and neither puts
any part of the author on screen:

- **Primary — creator-seeding.** You find the creators, send ARCs, give them the
  framing, let them post. This is where reach comes from. No body of yours anywhere.
- **Secondary — a thin own-account (optional, low-effort).** If "Helen Rivers" has a
  presence at all, it is **body-free**: aesthetic b-roll (candles, rain, a book propped
  in lamplight), trending audio, text overlay with a quote or hook — **no hands, no
  face, no voice.** A normal, common BookTok format. It's a credibility hub and a place
  to repost creators, *not* the engine. Male hands never enter the frame because no
  hands do.

**Do not** build the strategy around the author making hand/face/voice content. It
isn't necessary, and it breaks the firewall.

## 4. The two moves, in detail

### Creator-seeding (primary)
- **Target:** spicy/dark-*literary*-romance BookTokers whose comment sections are full
  of the §1 reader (the depth-hungry, writing-first crowd) — **not** the shock-value
  dark-romance creators (wrong audience, and they'll mis-frame it; see §7).
- **Mechanism:** ARC outreach. This is the adult-friendly cousin of the
  BookFunnel/StoryOrigin cross-promo in `meta-plan-distribution.md` §5 — same
  principle (borrow an existing audience), native to the platform.
- **Give creators the framing** so they tag it right (§5 hook, §7 caution). A creator
  who mis-slots it as dark romance draws the wrong reader and primes the
  predator-misread the whole positioning guards against.

### Thin own-account (secondary, optional)
- Body-free b-roll + trending audio + text overlay. Quotes and hooks (§6 clips).
- Purpose: exist, look real (thin work-forward persona — `meta-plan-pen-name.md`),
  repost creators, engage in text. Low effort; not the reach engine.

## 5. Tropes to claim, and the hook

**Tropes this book honestly has** (real entry-hashtags that connect to readers
searching them): **slow burn** (top-tier, perennial — and yours is *real*),
**worship / "he's obsessed with her"** (Pace's attention), **power dynamics / D-s**,
**"the third" / why-choose** (Volume Three payoff — hold for later), and — carefully —
**morally grey** (with the twist below).

**The hook — weaponize the anti-dark-romance angle.** Dark romance owns spicy BookTok
(*Haunting Adeline*, Rina Kent), but it's the book's **negative comp** (`meta-blurb.md`)
— built on the dangerous man. The differentiator borrows dark romance's *attention*
while distinguishing hard:

> **"Everyone said dark romance would wreck me. This one wrecked me — and there's no
> villain in it."**

True, legible in the platform's grammar, and it catches exactly the graduating reader
tired of the bad-boy formula. Pair with the "emotional maturity, not lurid intensity"
line from `meta-plan-distribution.md` §4.

## 6. Finding her — the search kit

TikTok's search bar takes both **hashtags** (`#slowburn`) and **natural-language
phrases** ("spicy books with good writing"); watching results to completion is what
trains the feed. These are *seed* terms to start reconnaissance — the platform teaches
you its current vocabulary as you lurk, and tags drift, so refine from what you see.
Grouped by job:

- **A · Tropes this book honestly has** (surface near readers searching them):
  `#slowburn` · "slow burn romance" · `#powerdynamics` · "power dynamics romance" ·
  "worshipped by him" / "he's obsessed with her" (soft-obsession, *not* possessive) ·
  `#morallygrey` (with the §7 caution) · `#whychoose` / `#menage` (Volume Three — recon
  only for now).
- **B · Frustration-plus-quality** (the dissatisfied, writing-first reader — your
  sharpest signal): "spice with substance" · "spicy books with good writing" ·
  "beautifully written spicy" · "books that wrecked me" / "books that made me sob" ·
  "romance for people who don't read romance" · "literary romance" · "morally grey done
  right" · "underrated spicy books" · "tired of the same tropes."
- **C · Comp-by-sensibility** (readers of the right neighbors; `meta-blurb.md` owns the
  comp shelf): "Acts of Service book" · "literary fiction with spice" · "sad girl
  books" / Sally Rooney readers · Anne Rice *Beauty* / Roquelaure readers · "Call Me By
  Your Name" (the ending-valence tonal comp).
- **D · Anti-dark-romance** (ride the attention, distinguish — §5 hook): "dark romance
  but no villain" · `#darkromance` / "dark romance recommendations" (to *locate* the
  audience, then distinguish) · *Haunting Adeline* readers (adjacent audience; the
  negative comp you position *against*).
- **E · Creator-finding** (build the ARC list — §4): `#booktok` · `#romancebooktok` ·
  `#spicybooktok` · `#darkromancebooktok` — then filter to creators whose taste is
  *writing-first* and whose comment sections are full of the §1 reader.

**Then follow the accounts that rec the *higher* end**, and read their comment
sections: those accounts *are* your creator target list (§4); those comments *are* your
reader map.

## 7. Con-misread caution (mis-slot risk)

BookTok's trope shorthand will try to file this as **dark romance** (a *Haunting
Adeline* neighbor). That is the wrong reader *and* it primes the predator-misread the
positioning exists to prevent (see `meta-plan-distribution.md` §4, con-misread bullet).
So: vet creators toward the writing-first end, hand them the §5 framing, and keep any
content-note calibrated to the **consent-forward, no-villain** truth — an invitation to
the right reader, never *Haunting Adeline*-style thrill-bait.

## 8. Excerpts for clips

`{{A Round}}` is the **flagship** — the "this is what the book feels like" piece:
warm, self-contained (a fitting), Vee-POV, the thesis in miniature (dread of exposure
met with delight → *"had not, once, been found wanting"*), and **con-misread-safe** (no
hunt on the page).

**Platform constraint:** explicit content is throttled, so clip the **emotional /
coded** beats, never the graphic ones — "keep still," "he never looked down," "not
found wanting." The graphic scenes stay **behind the click** (the sample / Look Inside),
where they convert a reader already hooked.

Menu by job (flagship first):

| excerpt | what it sells | on-page heat |
|---|---|---|
| `{{A Round}}` | worship + shame-into-worth; the whole book in one scene | high, restraint-coded (clippable) |
| `{{The New Ordinary}}` (the washed shirt) | devastating tenderness; "smelling like you" | low — pure emotional clip |
| `{{Rock}}` (math is beautiful) | "he's deep, not just hot" — the interiority sell | none — character bait |
| `{{All the Time}}` (the brunch) | "finally hungry out loud" — friendship/glamour warmth | low — the Randi axis |

Note the division of labor: `{{A Round}}` is *also* the prestige short (with
`{{Two Towels}}`, per `meta-plan-free-sample.md`); the retail free *sample* is still
`{{The Bench}}`→`{{The Pointing Game}}`. Social bait, prestige short, and retail sample
are three different jobs — one chapter can serve more than one.

## 9. Method (learning it as a newcomer)

Standard newcomer path: **lurk for weeks before posting.** TikTok's engine is trained
by *searching* — search the §6 niche terms, watch to completion, follow, comment; the
feed reorganizes around you fast. Absorb the coded language (TBR, ARC, DNF, spice
levels 1–5, trope shorthand). The #1 author mistake is treating it as a sales channel —
it reads as inauthentic and repels.

But note the reframe: because **creators do the performing**, your own learning is
mostly to (a) *identify and vet* the right creators and (b) *speak the language* in
outreach — not to become a creator yourself. The lurking is reconnaissance, not
rehearsal.
