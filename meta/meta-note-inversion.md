## Reality baseline — internal member discipline

The plausible model is a **chapter's own Standards Board hearing for an individual member**, governed by chapter and national bylaws. Do **not** model it on an NPC College Panhellenic, university, or campus Greek-board case against a chapter. Those systems generally adjudicate organizational conduct; NPC's current process expressly accepts reports against chapters, not individuals.

A sorority-specific chapter constitution provides a useful procedural skeleton:

- A vice president or chair of Standards presides neutrally with four additional members, preferably spread across class years; alternates cover conflicts.
- Any chapter member may submit a timely written complaint. The write-up identifies the incident and cites the major or minor infraction.
- The respondent receives prompt notice; the hearing follows within roughly a week.
- Proceedings and member information are confidential.
- A board member who reports the matter recuses from that hearing. A Standards member may herself be brought before Standards and cannot adjudicate her own case.
- At minimum, the member receives notice of the charges and an opportunity to answer, present a defense, and be heard before the board decides.
- The board investigates, deliberates without the respondent, votes, and gives a written outcome. Serious sanctions carry an appeal path through chapter or national authority.

The atmosphere should remain **peer governance rather than a criminal trial**. Formality can live in small things — a written allegation, minutes, the cited rule, a familiar order of questions, a closed door — without lawyers, courtroom rhetoric, or a campus official taking over an ordinary attendance/conduct case.

## Reality baseline — violations and proportionate outcomes

The chapter can plausibly translate the house's hostility into neutral membership language:

- repeated unexcused absences from mandatory events;
- failure to fulfill membership or officer obligations;
- failure to cooperate in the chapter's best interest;
- public conduct alleged to be detrimental to the chapter or its reputation.

Attendance and social-probation rules vary by organization, but real chapter bylaws support sanctions such as:

- required attendance at chapter meetings or mandatory functions;
- make-up attendance, service, philanthropy, or educational work;
- advance documentation for future excuses;
- temporary social probation or loss of specified social privileges;
- sober-sister or risk-management duties;
- removal from office for continuing nonperformance;
- escalating probation or disaffiliation only for repeated or serious noncompliance.

For the visible conduct currently planned, **attendance probation is the proportional baseline**. Expulsion-level stakes would overstate a missed-events ledger and a socially embarrassing mixer incident unless the chapter has accumulated materially more.
