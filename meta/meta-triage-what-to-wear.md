## Left standing — do not re-litigate

- **Same-speaker tag after a paragraph break** (line-edit item 1) — author
  ruling, and a **book-wide convention**, not a call on this line: when a
  paragraph break falls inside dialogue, tag the speaker in the new
  paragraph, *especially* when it is the same speaker. `"So what's Pace do,
  then?" Kayla said, Theo already forgotten.` stands. The tag is what
  disambiguates a break in the alternation, not a redundancy. Do not flag
  this pattern anywhere in the volume.
- **"I'm soaked" / "soaking to the skin"** (line-edit item 4) — author ruling:
  the echo is the transfer. She was soaked; then he is; the shirt moved
  between them. That is the story Vee is telling, and the repetition carries
  it. Do not vary either rendering.
- **"and not one word about what it was for"** (line-edit item 6) — flagged
  as restating the dialogue two lines above ("It doesn't say for what. It
  doesn't say anything"). Author ruling: keep both. The dialogue version is
  Vee performing bafflement for the table; the interiority version is the
  same fact landing on her privately with no audience — a different beat, and
  the paragraph's turn. **Reader-praised:** claude-fable-5 quoted the clause
  directly — "'not one word about what it was for' is the whole jacket
  tagline in miniature: an open door, no information, every step hers."
- **"Then I backed him into a tree and we made out" vs. {{May I Choose}}
  canon** (audit item 3) — author ruling: designed retelling drift, the same
  pattern triaged in {{Off Six-Fourteen}} (Vee's retellings maximize her
  agency; here escalated for the girls'-night audience). The scene licenses
  it on the page: "She could hear the story getting better in the telling
  and did not care" governs the whole session. Do not align the retelling
  with canon.
