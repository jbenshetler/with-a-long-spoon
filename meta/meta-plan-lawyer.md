# Finding and briefing the lawyer — legal/content read

Companion to `meta-plan-legal-read.md` (the findings pre-sheet). This doc: how to
find the right counsel, what to ask them, and the legal-landscape notes from the
2026-08-01 scan session that shape the consult. Assistant-researched background,
not legal advice — the consult exists to confirm or correct all of it.

## The specialty to search for

**"Pre-publication review"** (also "manuscript vetting," "publication clearance")
— a recognized service line among media/publishing attorneys covering defamation,
real-person/real-place identification, trademark, and lyric permissions. That
term, not generic "IP attorney," finds the right people. Geography barely
matters; the work is federal/NY-flavored and remote.

Sources, in order of efficiency:

1. **The Authors Guild** (authorsguild.org) — **NOT available pre-launch for
   legal (corrected 2026-08-30, verified on the live site).** Legal services
   (contract review, copyright advice, dispute intervention) exist only in the
   **Regular** and **Associate** tiers ($149/yr each). A self-published debut with
   no prior book income qualifies only for **Emerging Writer ($100/yr)**, which the
   site states explicitly "does not include legal help at this time." The
   legal-bearing tiers are income-gated: **Regular** needs $5,000+ self-pub income
   in the past 18 mo; **Associate** needs $500+ self-pub/freelance income (past 18
   mo) *or* a trad-publisher contract offer *or* an agent representation offer — none
   met before this book publishes. Two further caveats: (a) even at Regular/Associate
   the legal-services page advertises *contract review / copyright / disputes*, **not
   pre-publication manuscript review** for defamation/right-of-publicity/trademark —
   the earlier "staff attorneys do pre-pub review daily" claim is **unconfirmed**;
   confirm by email before relying on it; (b) the fee is **$149**, not ~$135.
   **Verdict:** the Guild is a good *post-launch* membership — upgrade to **Associate
   the moment cumulative sales clear $500** — but it does **not** solve the
   *pre-publication* legal read. Use a paid pre-pub media attorney (source 3/4 below)
   for that.
2. **Alliance of Independent Authors (ALLi)** (allianceindependentauthors.org) —
   the self-publishing/KDP angle: vetted partner directory + advice center;
   their people know Amazon's erotica-category moderation in practice.
3. **Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts** — state clinics (Virginia has one)
   referring media attorneys at reduced rates.
4. **Direct search** — "pre-publication review attorney fiction" → boutique
   media firms doing flat-fee manuscript reads.

## Legal-landscape notes shaping the consult (session 2026-08-01)

For the lawyer to confirm/correct; rationale behind the pre-sheet's risk grades.

- **Trademark mentions in fiction** — a passing brand mention, even entangled
  with sexual content, has essentially never been successfully actionable absent
  source/sponsorship confusion. Expressive works get *Rogers v. Grimaldi*
  protection (cf. Louis Vuitton losing against *The Hangover Part II*); the
  federal dilution statute excludes noncommercial, non-source use, so
  tarnishment (e.g. *Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders v. Pussycat Cinema*) requires
  use *as a mark*, not in-prose mention. Basis of the Hermès downgrade to 1.
  The Chili's items stay at 2 only for the factual-sounding kitchen claim
  ("cheese out of a bag / heat out of a packet") — opinion about taste is
  protected; factual-register assertions are the wrinkle.
- **The Rock backstory (risk 3)** — legally protected: CSAM statutes cover
  visual depictions only; text reaches liability only via *Miller* obscenity,
  and an elided, harm-framed backstory fails both prongs. The real risks are
  (a) **platform**: KDP erotica moderation is keyword-driven and literal — the
  bare numeral pair "nineteen and… fifteen" is the machine-quotable exposure;
  (b) **representation precision**: the accurate disclosure is "no sexual
  activity involving anyone under 21 is *depicted*; one non-depicted statutory
  relationship is referenced and framed as abuse." The origin itself is not on
  the table (author, 2026-08-01). A class-year rephrasing ("she was a
  sophomore") is workshopped, uncommitted.
- **"Of and concerning" (Dr. Marsh)** — name-sharing is never the test (every
  plausible surname has real bearers); identification requires the fictional
  *slot* to be occupied: a real Marsh at VT in the relevant department, within
  the recognition window. Common surnames weaken any individual claim but raise
  slot-occupancy odds. Temporal scope: judged **at publication** — check
  current faculty plus ~5 years of living memory (Wayback snapshots of
  department pages, archived catalogs, student-facing review sites); 20 years
  back is effectively out; the dead cannot be defamed; someone hired *after*
  publication has no claim (single-publication rule; a substantially new
  edition may reset the snapshot date — confirm with counsel). Ephemeral
  records cut in our favor: reasonableness is the diligence standard, and
  identification decays with the same records the search does. Cheap
  alternative if the slot is occupied or ambiguous: invented surname (one-word
  cost). Record any check **with the date it was run** in
  `meta-plan-legal-read.md`.
- **Claim window** — defamation limitations are 1–2 years from publication in
  most states, single-publication rule.

  comply with (published ToS are silent or imagery-only), and the *private*

- **~~Contract/termination advice~~** — author ruling: platform ToS are adhesion

  Nothing for counsel to add.
- **Dr. Marsh — reclassified, not closed.** The *defamation* rationale is dead:
  the portrayal is now praise (*"even her eight a.m. fills up"*), and praise is
  not actionable even if false. What the directory check actually tests is
  **identification**, and that matters more in Volume 2, not less: Marsh is a
  recurring character there with a specific physical portrait
  (`covering.md:15` — tall, spare, short silver hair with a black streak, fleece
  vest, boots), attached to a real name, a real university, and a real

  real person who objects to the association — not a tort. **Demoted to author

## Platform disclosure — the decision not to disclose (author ruling, 2026-09-13)

**Ruling: do not send a pre-upload disclosure. Hold the letter below as a
response kit instead.** Send it within the hour if anyone ever asks — which is
when it has maximum force and zero cost.

Reasoning, in order of weight:

1. **You cannot un-ask.** A support agent handed an email containing "fifteen"
   and a question about the under-18 clause has no upside in saying yes and
   every incentive to say "we'd rather not." A written refusal is strictly worse
   than silence: it cannot be retracted, and it converts any later upload from an
   unremarkable act into a documented knowing one.
2. **It seeks permission for something not prohibited.** The clause bars
   depicting a person under 18 in a sexual or suggestive context. The book does
   not do that (`meta-plan-legal-read.md`, full Volume 1 sweep). Asking a
   gatekeeper to bless lawful content invites a standard stricter than its own
   written one, and binds you to the answer.
3. **The discovery surface is near zero.** Private delivery to 8–15 invited,
   screened readers. No public listing, no browse, no search, no storefront.
   Enforcement at these platforms is complaint-driven, and there is no complaint
   vector when the only readers are ones you invited.

**Standing limit:** not volunteering is not misrepresenting. If a platform ever
asks directly, answer straight and send the kit. The distinction between "did
not raise it" and "misrepresented it" is the whole case if this is ever
adjudicated.

**Does not carry to publication.** At retail the audience is public, complaints
are the enforcement vector, and the stakes are the business rather than one beta
round. Revisit at the publication-stage consult.

### The safe contact, if any is wanted

There is no "mature flag" to set — the term was an assistant placeholder and
**no such mechanism is documented on any of the four platforms** (see the ToS
scan in `meta-plan-test-readers.md`). Ask instead for *more* restriction, never
for permission:

> I'm delivering fully explicit adult fiction to a private review team. How do I
> make sure only adults can access it — what access controls and reader-side
> filters do you recommend?

No platform penalises that question. It is operational, it yields a usable
answer, it asks nothing about the passage and invites no adjudication, and it
quietly creates a record of diligence — worth more than a permission slip if
anything ever arises. It also matches the author's actual goal: an adults-only
cohort, not YA reach.
