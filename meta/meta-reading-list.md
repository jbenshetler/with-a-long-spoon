# Reading list — author TBR (comp-adjacent)

*Curated 2026-08-16 to the author's stated taste. Doubles as comp-candidate
scouting: books near the novel's coordinate (`meta-blurb.md` owns the comp
shelf). Not canon — a personal reading list.*

**Taste screen (what these are picked for):** characters an adult wants to be
*inside*, interiority that **sharpens** rather than circles, heat that isn't
held at arm's length, feeling that's earned **without a villain**. Screened
*out*: cold/glass-slide remove (the *Acts of Service* failure) and
immature/grating narrators (the *Haunting Adeline* failure).

Heat flags: 🔥 explicit/high · 🌡 moderate/on-page · 🕯 sensual-not-explicit /
mild. Genre flags noted where it isn't genre romance (no guaranteed HEA).

## Closest to the coordinate — smart *and* hot (also comp candidates)

- [ ] **Tiffany Reisz — *The Siren* (The Original Sinners)** 🔥 — literary BDSM
  erotica that is *actually* morally serious; the series is canonically **bi +
  poly**, with F/F deepening as it goes. Nearest existing book to this novel's
  own corner. **Comp candidate.** *(Started; keep going — the bi/poly is further
  in.)*
- [ ] **Sierra Simone — *American Queen* (New Camelot trilogy)** 🔥 — explicit
  **poly why-choose** (a woman, two men, no one loses), with real moral/political
  weight. A working model of the Volume 3 structure. **Comp candidate.**
- [ ] **Sierra Simone — *Priest*** 🔥 — high heat welded to genuine conscience;
  transgression that *costs*. The moral weight is load-bearing, not decorative.
- [ ] **Alexis Hall — *For Real*** 🔥 — D/s romance (M/M, age-gap, younger man
  dominant) where the kink *is* characterization, not set dressing. The novel's
  own method — desire rendering character — executed cleanly.

## Literary F/F — top-tier characterization (the *Acts of Service* promise, delivered)

- [ ] **Sarah Waters — *Tipping the Velvet*** 🕯 — Victorian F/F, explicit-ish,
  picaresque; interiority that sharpens scene by scene.
- [ ] **Sarah Waters — *Fingersmith*** 🕯 — tighter, more devastating plot;
  same top-shelf characterization. (Pick this over *Tipping* if you want plot
  over sensuality.)
- [ ] **Carmen Maria Machado — *Her Body and Other Parties*** 🌡 — queer,
  literary, erotically charged short fiction with a cutting mind. *Stories, not
  romance — no HEA.*

## Genre F/F romance — smart, warm, on-page heat, HEA intact

- [ ] **Olivia Waite — *The Lady's Guide to Celestial Mechanics*** 🌡 — F/F
  historical; intellect/competence as the seduction. The anti-glass-slide, with
  a real HEA.
- [ ] **Casey McQuiston — *One Last Stop*** 🌡 — contemporary F/F; likable
  adults whose heads are a *good* place to be (the Adeline complaint, inverted).

## Smart & warm, characterization over spice (M/F)

- [ ] **Kennedy Ryan — *Before I Let Go*** 🌡 — literary contemporary; a
  divorced couple circling back, real adult stakes (grief, mental health), prose
  that earns its feeling.
- [ ] **Laura Kinsale — *Flowers from the Storm*** 🌡 — landmark "smart romance";
  a hero with aphasia, interiority nearly experimental. Proof the genre can do
  profound characterization.
- [ ] **Talia Hibbert — *Get a Life, Chloe Brown*** 🌡 — warm, funny, adult,
  well-drawn; chronic-illness protagonist.
- [ ] **Emily Henry — *Beach Read*** 🕯 — the voice and interiority you'd love,
  but heat is mild. *Only if you'll trade spice for wit.*
