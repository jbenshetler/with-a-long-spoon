# Notes — Not Enough

*Companion to the planned scene. Morning after {{The Usual}}, before {{My Friend Randi}}. Pace close third.*

## What this scene is

Pace alone after {{The Usual}}: the bodily cost of trying to drink Vee away, the domestic memories that prove he knew exactly what he withheld, and the Daphne-conditioned fear beneath that choice. His silence did not spare him profound grief; his grown life lets him carry it without being consumed as he was when Daphne left.

## Structure (sketch)

*Pace's house · morning after {{The Usual}} · Pace close third.*

1. **Body and routine.** Pace wakes through his dry, aching body and the smell of bleach. He steps around the mop and bucket left from cleaning his vomit, takes another hot shower, rinses his mouth in the stream, brushes his teeth, and dresses in sweats. He puts on water and the coffee maker; from the ordered freezer rows he takes vacuum-bagged chicken and broccoli and boils them. He sits with water, black coffee, and food. He is not hungry, but his shaky body needs food.

2. **“Prison food.”** The chicken and broccoli recalls an unnamed prior girlfriend lightly calling it *prison food*. She is not Daphne and carries no emotional charge or backstory: an ordinary adult relationship and ordinary departure. Pace takes the first bite.

3. **The arriving smile.** He remembers waiting on the covered stoop when Vee arrived: her attention finding the house, then him, her face lighting into **the smile she gives only to him**. Warm and wistful; he misses seeing her approach and knowing that, in that moment, her happiness was happiness at finding him there.

4. **The proof and the failure.** The arrival opens into the ordinary cycle of having Vee in his house: her walking toward the bathroom at night, the unperformed view of her backside; her asleep when he rises first; her face at the first bite of something he made. Arrival → night → morning → meal. The first-bite image returns him to {{Seconds}} and her crackers-and-meal declaration. He understood it then, wanted to answer, felt his hand stop on her leg and took one breath, then another. He chose the safer, smaller truth: *I love how good we are together.* He knew it was insufficient. He feels guilty and weak because the true answer was available to him and true. He had wanted to say it; he had known what it would mean to her; he had not been strong enough. **But he had said it once before.**

5. **Daphne: his whole world.** Do not state their ages. Pace remembers being young and new to the university; Daphne was older and knew how things worked. She was his tutor, although academics were the only place he did not need her help. She taught him the mechanics: making the most of his scholarship meal plan, registering for classes, knowing where to go and what everyone else seemed already to know. She was his tutor, best friend, first girlfriend, first lover, first love. He told her he loved her. She said it to him. He meant it. Then she left. He had no friend to tell because Daphne had been the friend: *There had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told.*

6. **The protective rule.** *Daphne was the only woman he had told he loved. She was the only one he ever expected to. Her departure consumed his life.* Pace believed saying it was what let the loss consume him—not what made Daphne leave, but what made losing her hurt that badly. He decided never to expose himself that completely again. Not at first, but eventually there had been other women. A few. He had needed time to learn how to be with someone without making her his whole world. He had grown. The relationships mattered while they lasted; when they ended, he went on. He never told any of them he loved them. He believed he had learned how to keep loss survivable. Then Vee came. He knew he loved her, knew what she offered, wanted to answer, and was afraid. He believed keeping it unspoken would limit the hurt. He feels guilty because Vee paid for what Daphne's departure taught him to fear, and weak because he could not say what he knew was true. *He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him. He had believed that not saying it would make this hurt less.*

7. **The failed remedy.** *Whiskey River* was a liar.

   > The whiskey had taken his balance. His dinner. Several hours he could not use. It had left every memory of Vee intact.

   The song need not be playing. The bleach, bucket, dehydration, shower, and forced meal are the proof. He expected at least an interruption; the whiskey did not help.

8. **The truck does not turn in.** A truck approaches. Pace's head lifts before he decides to move; his body anticipates Vee before conscious knowledge corrects it. The truck does not slow. He sees Vee finding him on the covered stoop, her face becoming the smile she gives only him. It passes. His hand shakes; he puts down his fork, his breath breaks, and he sobs until he can no longer hear the truck, then for a few breaths after. Brief, involuntary, non-cathartic.

9. **He continues.** The sob passes without resolving anything. The truck is gone. His coffee has cooled; so have the chicken and broccoli. Pace picks up his fork. He is not hungry. **He eats anyway.**

## Guards

- **Pace's interior.** He is capable of deep analysis and knows his own love, Vee's offer, his failure, and its immediate cost. His error is the Daphne-conditioned premise that an unspoken love will hurt less if lost. Never render him as confused or unable to introspect.
- **The vow's motive.** Pace is simply afraid that naming love will make a later loss hurt as badly as Daphne's did. Keep the motive entirely on that fear; add no qualifications or conditions.
- **Degree, not immunity.** Pace never believed silence would prevent pain; he believed it would keep pain from consuming his life. The present grief is profound and hollowing, but he remains able to clean, cook, eat, and continue.
- **Daphne.** Preserve the concrete lived asymmetry and Pace's experience of her competence, kindness, and indispensability. Do not state ages, mandate the reader's verdict, recap the sex, assign Daphne a motive, or imply suicide.
- **Other women.** A few serious relationships after Pace had time to recover and grow; neither a conquest list nor a claim that they meant nothing. The *prison food* girlfriend remains unnamed and is not Daphne.
- **Vee's remembered body.** The bathroom walk is affectionate and hungry, not anatomical. Sleeping is tenderness, not surveillance. The four images form one shared-life cycle, not a sentimental montage.
- **The sob.** Use *sobbed* directly. No masculine shame, forced mastery, catharsis, or prolonged collapse.
- **No phone.** Pace misses seeing Vee, speaking with her, and hearing her. He does not call, decide to call, or make another contact gesture.
- **No title drop.** Do not put *not enough* into the prose. The title gathers the safer declaration, protective silence, failed whiskey, and sustaining routines without announcing itself.

## Continuity

- Morning after {{The Usual}}, during the silence that follows {{Believe Me}}; before {{My Friend Randi}} and {{Nothing Underneath}}. Pace may fear Vee is gone, but the narration cannot declare a definitive breakup he does not know has occurred.
- The crackers-and-meal declaration is in {{Seconds}}. Pace's first-bite memory echoes his admission in {{In His Hands}} that he thinks about Vee's face when she tastes something he made and that remembering her is pale beside her presence.
- Daphne predates Pace's house and his ability to cook his own food. The *prison food* memory therefore belongs to a later girlfriend.
- Hold the truck until after the *Whiskey River* beat.
