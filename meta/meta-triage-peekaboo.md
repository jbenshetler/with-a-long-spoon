# Triage — Peekaboo (line-edit pass, 2026-08-07)

Source: `audits/line-edit/peekaboo.md` (10 findings) plus cold-read enrichment
(five readers) and the style linter, reviewed with the author item by item.
**Left-standing entries are authorial decisions — do not re-flag without new
evidence** (line-edit verdicts re-open on a text edit to the passage).

Context for later passes: another heavily reader-praised chapter, and unusually
frictionless — across five cold reads only **three** lines drew any criticism at
all, and two of those the editor missed. The mirror sequence, the underwear
removal, and the crying beat were praised by all five with zero reservations and
were not touched. Two passages are the most-quoted in the chapter and are now
protected: `:39` "somewhere behind her ribs the looking stopped being shame"
(fable-5 + terra, terra calling it the chapter's emotional peak) and `:49` "she
was the one who'd taken the edge away" (quoted verbatim by four of five).

## Fixed

- `:19` — (line edit, cost-vague, 2026-08-11) "the part that looked like **it
  had cost him nothing**" → "the part that looked like **no trouble at all**".
  Book-wide `cost-vague` back-sweep (vague "cost something/nothing/anything"; rule
  + author ruling in `style/style-rules.toml`). Initially flagged for *protect*
  as the constructed-illusion beat, but the author ruled it a counterexample:
  even framed as appearance ("looked like"), saying a high-effort thing cost
  nothing snags, and the paragraph already earns the effortlessness concretely
  ("no seam of effort showing on it anywhere," "like it had been bought that
  way"). The recast rides the sentence's own trouble/trouble/trouble figure and
  buttons it by inversion (took the trouble → hid the trouble → shows no trouble),
  keeping the concealed-effort meaning without the idiom. The rule's `why` now
  cites this as the standing counterexample; "looked like" does not license the
  vague construction.
- `:15` — cut "He was grinning at the dress now like he still couldn't quite
  believe it had come out right." Same gesture and same characterization as
  `:9`'s kid-with-a-surprise grin three paragraphs earlier; a rerun, not an
  escalation. **Reader evidence was lopsided and decided it:** `:9` is quoted
  approvingly by three of five readers (fable-5 "the most disarming he's ever
  been on the page"; opus-4-8 "the least calculating I've ever seen him"; 5.5
  "young and human and delighted"), and *no* reader quoted or defended `:15`.
- `:7` — "both of them already most of the way to somewhere" → "both of them
  already past stopping". Fixes the doubled measure-phrase with `:3` ("most of
  the way to a decision her body had already made"), which is load-bearing:
  `:3` states the body-decides-first engine the mirror sequence pays off. Also
  retires a `the-way` hit. Noted at the time and accepted: "past stopping"
  makes `:9`'s break a real reversal rather than a pause — read as evidence of
  how completely the dress outranks everything for him.
- `:19` — "It got in behind her ribs and opened the small place the tears came
  from" → "It got in and opened…". One of three ribs-as-seat-of-feeling
  locations in the chapter. **The editor recommended keeping `:19` and
  reworking the other two; the reader evidence inverted it** — `:39` is the
  single most-cited line in the chapter's five cold reads and became the
  keeper. "It got in" alone is blunter and better: got in *past* something,
  which is the paragraph's subject.
- `:61` — "a laugh living under her ribs" → "a laugh coming up in her every
  time". Third ribs location; the replacement keeps the recurrence ("kept
  coming back to" / "every time") and drops the anatomy. "Chest" was
  deliberately avoided — `in-her-chest` is a flagged 13-occurrence locus tic,
  cut from `famished:65` for the same reason.
- `:39` — "She was smiling before she'd decided to" → "She was smiling and
  hadn't decided to". **Ration fix under echo ruling #8**, not an improvement
  brief: the exact five-word string `before she'd decided to` is rationed, three
  of six Vol-1 slots are spent, and the remaining occurrences vary by default.
  The smallest move that clears the ledger was chosen deliberately. No reader
  quoted this line; a keeper slot was proposed against and not granted.
- `:45` — "her body answering the glass before her head could" → "…on its own".
  Part of the same body-before-mind cluster; the tight collision was with
  `:31`'s "her skin knew before her head did" — identical noun, fourteen lines
  apart. The construction itself is PROTECTED (#8); only the local pileup of
  four-in-five-pages was addressed, keeping `:31` (structural — introduces the
  fitted-to-her revelation) and `:49` (reader-praised by three).
- `:49` — cut "where the pale edge had cut it a minute ago" from the clean-line
  sentence. **The editor's recommendation was unusable as written** — it
  proposed cutting through to the end of the sentence, which would have removed
  "she was the one who'd taken the edge away," the most-quoted phrase in the
  chapter (fable-5, 5.5, sol verbatim; terra on the same choice). The real
  redundancy was the middle clause, which replays `:47`'s pale edge / cut /
  long line two sentences earlier.
- `:49` — "and there was nothing left in front of it to hide" → "and she let it
  come." Two consecutive section-closes ran the same "there was nothing left…"
  cadence; `:55` is the stronger sentence in the more important position (the
  scene-out into white space), so `:49` gave way. Also clears a
  `there-was-were` hit.
- `:53` — cut "He hadn't gone far — too pleased to, she'd known he wouldn't —".
  The not-far fact is established a page earlier at `:27` in stronger wording
  ("not far, never far"), which terra quotes approvingly; the restatement plus
  the she'd-known gloss put her mind on the page at the exact moment her body
  had just carried her into the room, delaying the payoff of the stride. Also
  retires a spaced em-dash pair. The one thing lost — her demonstrating she can
  now predict him — is made better and more quietly at `:63` ("the way she was
  learning he did").
- `:7` — "the kiss went deep and warm and certain" → "deep and certain"
  (linter sweep, group C(b)). Thins the opening `warm` cluster of three in five
  lines; "deep and certain" carries the kiss without it.
- `:21` — "the dumb happy kind she could never get out ahead of" → "that always
  beat her to it" (linter sweep, group E(b)). `out-ahead` is a genuine flagged
  tic, not a false positive, and the replacement chimes with "She hadn't decided
  to cry" without repeating it.

## Left standing — do not re-litigate

- **`:15` "He'd set the whole thing up for her."** Flagged as interiority
  labeling what the moved pedestal and bright screw heads just showed, and as
  working against the chapter's own hide-the-trouble argument. **Kept:**
  gpt-5.6-terra quoted it as the hinge of their reaction ("he has made a whole
  little stage on which she can see herself as beautiful… all of it got me in
  the same soft place it gets her"), and with the grin already cut from the same
  paragraph, removing this too would leave the reveal as pure inventory with
  Pace absent from it and the tableau closing on hardware.
- **The `the way` construction stays at five (`:9`, `:15`, `:31`, `:63`,
  `:67`) — finding declined.** `:9` is reader-praised by three; `:67` earns its
  slot as the third beat of a three-fragment sequence; `:63` is the quiet home
  for the beat cut from `:53`. The proposed fix at `:31` ("where water wouldn't
  have") was applied and then **reverted at the author's direction**: "good
  cloth does" grounds the sentence in the real object before invoking water as
  its foil, and removing it left a literal claim resting on a simile's vehicle.
  The chapter runs high on this tic partly because it is *about* a made object,
  and "the way X does" is how a person thinks about craftsmanship.
- **`:55` "And after that there was nothing left for either of them to
  decide."** gpt-5.6-sol filed the chapter's only substantive line-criticism:
  "in a book so attentive to choosing, it briefly blurs what specifically
  happens and what each chooses… the phrasing feels more fated than the
  surrounding scene's careful agency." Weighed and **kept**: claude-opus-4-8
  quoted the same line as the right elision ("the charge was all in the walk to
  him"), the sentence is true *of that instant*, and the chapter has already
  banked her choosing twice (the underwear, the stride). The echo was resolved
  by varying `:49` instead.
- **`:3` the opening recap** ("There'd been one night before this…" / "A man
  leaving the choosing to her like that — she was still getting used to it").
  claude-opus-4-8 flagged it as "a touch of recap I didn't strictly need this
  soon after Famished" and pardoned it in the same sentence ("but it's short
  and it re-grounds the POV cleanly") — the same self-resolving pattern that
  left `famished:11` standing. Kept whole: "she was still getting used to it" is
  the chapter's first statement of the being-given-the-choice theme that the
  underwear removal pays off.
- **`:69` "getting away with something" — finding *withdrawn*, not merely left
  standing.** claude-fable-5 flagged it as "doing so much work it nearly tips
  into telegraphy," then reversed in the next breath ("That's the sentence the
  book has been building toward, and it lands like a bruise"). The other four
  readers all report the *double* reading without naming the machinery —
  opus-4-8 "I felt the floor tilt"; sol "both delighted and ominous"; terra
  "scared on her behalf." Four independent readers describing the
  innocuous-surface / second-floor mechanism landing is the design's success
  condition. Flagged as a watch-item for the end-of-volume cold-read re-run,
  where telegraphy would show as accumulation rather than a single line.
