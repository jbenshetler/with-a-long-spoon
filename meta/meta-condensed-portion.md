## Summary

**The rupture.** The only fall scene in which Randi turns on Pace, and neither of them
ever names what happened.

He has had the pot on since three — chicken and potatoes in a white sauce, the peas and
the carrot in it, the wine in early and the cream at the end — a loaf out of the machine,
the salad washed, the dressing in a jar to shake at the last minute, and on the table the
bottle the pot drank from. **Two bowls set out: hers the small one, the one he slices her
apples into.** Henley and jeans; her music low in the front rooms. He pours her glass when
he hears the gravel.

Her bag goes on the chair by the door; he sees it and sets it aside. The kitchen reaches
her. *"You cooked."* A beat. *"You shouldn't have."* He pulls out her chair and does not
answer it — and it reads two ways.

She draws the spoon through the sauce along the top rather than down into the bowl, tastes
what it brings up, sets the spoon on the cloth, and says ***"It's wonderful"*** — the
bright coin she has never once spent on him in this kitchen, and the food has been better
than this. He tears the end off the loaf onto her plate. She does not touch it. She eats
the salad, leaf by leaf, and **the stew never moves.**

*"How was this morning?"* / *"Good."* — and the rim of the glass goes in under her
thumbnail and presses. *"She's happy. She told me about Friday."* It comes up in his face
before he can do anything about it and he does not try; he is glad all the way down.
**Randi watches him be glad.**

**The turn.** *"How long, Pace?" … "How long are you going to take?"* / *"As long as she
needs. She's not ready."* / *"She's not ready."* — flat, with nothing on it, a register he
has never heard from her — *"Or you're not."* / *"I want her to get there on her own"*
(the sentence {{Gone}} will hand back to him). *"On her own. With you." … "That's how it
works for you."* / ***"It's selfish, Pace."***

He feels it land — not where she aimed, but in the place that knelt an hour at a pedestal
not touching a girl who had been wet through her clothes for him, and finished the hem.
His one flash is exact rather than loud: *"How do I get her from where she is to us, on
your schedule?"* and then *"She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't
anything."* He hears how it came out and does not take it back.

**Her whole reply is the bottle.** She reaches across, takes it by the neck and pours her
own glass, a full one. He has poured every glass she has drunk in this house. He watches
her hand do it and it goes in somewhere and stays.

She finishes the salad with the second glass, and then the bedroom: she is out of the
skirt before he has said her name, up over him, and she does **not** do the inch and the
stop and the eyes — she puts him in in one motion and drives, eyes shut, nails in his
chest. **And she does not say *tell me*.** Every night in this house she has said it; he
waits under her and it does not come, and after a while he understands it is not going to,
and that whatever this is, it is not for him. He gives it to her anyway. She comes loud
and goes off him at once, rolls herself in the cashmere with her back to him, says nothing
and does not move.

He goes over all of it — the speed, the shut eyes, the missing *tell me*, the back — and
the pieces will not make anything he can name: *it was the want, with an edge on it
tonight, from somewhere.* He does not put his hand in her hair.

**Close:** when her breathing changes he gets up. The pot with most of it still in it. Her
bowl on the table, the sauce gone to a skin over the peas and the carrot, the one line
still drawn through it. He looks for what he did, back through the evening from the gravel
forward, and it is not there. He covers the bowl with a plate and puts it in the
refrigerator, **for her**, and turns the light off over the stove.

## What only this chapter establishes

- The small bowl as hers, the one he slices her apples into.
- *"I want her to get there on her own"* — the sentence Randi hands back to him at {{Gone}}.
- Her first and only accusation: *"It's selfish, Pace."*
- The glass-pouring reversal: she pours her own for the first time.
- The absence of *"Tell me"* — the only night in the house without it.
- That he ends an evening not knowing what he did, and still puts her dinner away for her.
