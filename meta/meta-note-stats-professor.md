# Note — The Stats Professor (the fall mirror-class prop)

*Device · Volume One, fall semester · Vee POV · companion to `meta-note-anthro.md` (the spring successor), `meta-note-outlier.md`, `meta-triage-outlier.md`. The abstracted rule lives in the Bible's Global Craft Rules, **The coursework mirror**.*

## What he is

**A prop, not a character** (author ruling, 2026-08-09). The unnamed statistics professor is the fall vehicle for the coursework mirror: a course whose subject silently diagrams the plot, transcribed by Vee and connected to nothing.

**The design goal, in the author's words:** *first-time readers skim past the boring stats professor saying the chapter thesis, trying to get to the hot stuff; second-pass readers get a chuckle.* This is the scene-title engine's mechanism — innocuous surface, reread charge — applied to a recurring prop. He is deliberately skimmable. Being skimmed is him working, not him failing.

## The construction rule (the mechanism — do not break)

**The gesture recurs; the simile rotates. Never reuse a vehicle.**

Two constant gestures — scrolling the slides forward and back before class, and the flat affectless delivery — each rendered through a *different* comparison every time: road / tire pressure; phone bill / cereal box / course catalog / weatherless.

This is what makes the goal achievable. A rotating family is **invisible on first pass and cumulative on reread** — the reader can only collect it deliberately. A vehicle repeated *verbatim* reads as a callback, which is the one thing a skimming reader does catch, and it stops the skim. (`practice-room:13` repeated "the back of a cereal box" from `all-told:35` and was varied to "a course catalog" on 2026-08-09 for exactly this reason.)

Corollary: incongruity is the engine. The vehicles are things nobody reads by choice and nobody reads aloud, and none of them is academic. **A syllabus or a course reading fails** — in-domain, so the simile collapses into a description of his actual job. **Avoid vehicles carrying their own charge** — *a fire-exit map* was rejected 2026-08-09 for smuggling in a sense of danger, which functions as a tell.

## The nudge ban

**The narration never remarks on Vee's failure to apply the lecture to herself.** The lecture lands, she writes it down, the scene moves on. She is allowed to be visibly not-thinking; the narrator is not allowed to point at it.

This is where every reader complaint about the device actually lives — not with the professor, who is skimmable, but with the elbow-nudge, which is not. A first-time reader can skim a boring lecture; they cannot skim a sentence telling them they should have noticed something. gpt-5.5: *"the author putting a highlighter in my hand."* gpt-5.6-sol on `practice-room:19`: *"similarly announces the irony I had already understood."*

- **Cut 2026-08-09:** `practice-room:19` "She did not think about herself at all." (Reader evidence was split — kimi-k3 took it as deadpan — but sol read it as announcement, and the paragraph ends stronger on "She gave the board another honest minute.")
- **Sanctioned exception:** `see-you-later:61` "the word sat there in her notebook, underlined once, going nowhere" — image work, not commentary. One instance of the pattern may say the quiet part; it is spent here.
- **Open:** `all-told:69` "underlined it, and gave it no further thought" — flagged, not yet ruled. Cutting re-opens a `reviewed` chapter.

## Carve-out against the walk-on principle

The {{All Told}} review established: *"a character's signature gesture spent on a walk-on is an argument for cutting it, not for protecting it"* (echo ledger, Pace's smile / *unhurried*).

**That principle does not apply to the stats professor's own appositions.** The `a man who…` frame and the rotating flat similes are *his* signature, not a register leak from Pace, and the reread design requires them to accumulate. Without this carve-out a later reviewer will strip precisely the material that makes the device work. The principle still governs any *Pace* vocabulary that drifts onto him.

## Evidence base

Four current cold reads (gpt-5.5, gpt-5.6-sol, gpt-5.6-terra, kimi-k3), traced across all five appearances, 2026-08-09:

- **The professor is released as a walk-on by every reader, every time** ("Walk-ons… released to the record" — kimi-k3). Only the *lectures* carry forward, tracked by all four as standing "what Vee doesn't know" entries. So the irony accrues; the prop does not. This is correct for a prop and is why the recurrence must live in tics rather than in the reader's memory of a person.
- **The reader who assembled a portrait is the only one who never felt the thumb.** kimi-k3 collected the road-checker, the cereal-box voice, and the trap smile across three chapters and complained of no authorial visibility at any of them; the other three saw furniture and all reported the thumb. **The comedy is the defense, not a risk.**
- **No reader names it as humor.** kimi-k3 comes closest — *"played so deadpan… I took it as a shiver, not a lecture"* — and converts it to dread. Expect the joke to under-report in cold reads: the output format has a `Symbolism:` heading and none for wit, so a laugh gets filed as a theme.
- **Discount the thumb complaints, with care.** Cold readers read one chapter at full attention and then write an analysis; humans skim toward the hot stuff. These reviewers see the machinery more clearly than any human will and the wit less clearly. Both biases inflate the thumb reading — but the one reader who saw the man is the one who felt no thumb, and that is the actionable half.
- **The joke is not gated on statistics.** He glosses himself in plain English every time (*"a thing can run one way across every short stretch you measure and the opposite way across the whole of it"*). A reader who has never heard of Simpson's paradox still gets it. Comprehension depends on attention, not numeracy.
