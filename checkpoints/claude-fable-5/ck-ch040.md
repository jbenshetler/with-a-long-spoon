### Impression
Vee I love the way the book wants me to — her hunger, her jokes, her courage in increments (the buttons in the truck, the long stride, the uncropped photo) keep being the realest thing on the page. I ache for her precisely because I hold the one fact she doesn't: nothing was taken from her, and everything was arranged.
Pace pulls me two ways at once and the pull is the book. Minute by minute his care is flawless — the moleskin, the heat turned up hours early, the drawer, the flannel — and I believe he cherishes her. But I watched him build a bench in secret, break a woman open on it and receive her begging with a connoisseur's satisfaction, and then point across a quad and set all this in motion. The tenderness and the engineering are the same hand. I trust his gentleness with her body completely and his purposes not at all — and Daphne, the scar, and "I don't go back" say the machinery has a wound under it I haven't been shown.
Randi frightens me and moves me in equal measure. The mask ("not ever") makes every warmth suspect, and her lunches are extraction sessions — yet the extraction keeps paying out real gifts, and the moments she loses her own footing (the stillness under Pace's praise, the roughness in "Just us," the glaze-kiss) read like a woman being caught by the very trap she's baiting. I think she loves Vee and cannot afford to; I don't know what she'll do about either.
Cassie I trust without reservation — the one person in the book with no second channel. Her flat watchfulness is the reader's proxy, and "Goodbye, Vee" still rings.
Where trust sits: I doubt no one's affection and everyone's disclosure. The dread is specific now — the sorority knows Randi's boyfriend's name, Vee saw MIRH, and the bench is standing in the bedroom with a thong on it. Every fuse is lit and the people holding the matches have started to mean it.
