### Story so far

Throughout, the professor's stats lessons (latent variables, Simpson's paradox, innovational outliers) shadow the story of a plan Vee cannot see.

### Impression

I'm charmed and uneasy in exactly the way the book wants. **Pace** reads, scene to scene, as the gentlest imaginable lover — the courtesy, the patience, the dress, the shirt, "always beautiful" — and I catch myself trusting him even knowing he staged the meeting and ran the pointing game; the Daphne history (19-and-15) and the thing he did to "a door" once are quiet warnings I can't fully weigh yet. **Randi** is where my wariness sits: dazzling, generous, addictive, and clearly working Vee with method — draining her secrets to feed back to Pace, tuning her arousal in a piano closet — while also, apparently, genuinely loving her; the ch1 bench and the bottle-cap under the thumbnail make me pity her more than fear her, which may be its own trap. **Vee** I ache for straightforwardly: alive, funny, starving to be seen, handing herself over gladly and freely to two people who chose her before she knew there was a choice. **Cassie** feels like the one uncompromised compass, and her flat "Goodbye, Vee" haunts me. No one has yet done Vee visible harm — every yes has been hers — and that's precisely what's frightening. I trust the tenderness is real and distrust what it's for.
