### What I know that they don't

- **Caring/complicating side:** Pace is deeply, patiently attentive to Vee — noticing her hunger (literal and emotional), pacing everything to her comfort, never pushing past a "no." His care is genuine even though its origin is a game.
- **Using/engineered side:** The reader knows (from ch3) that Pace and Randi devised a "pointing game" on campus in which Pace pointed out women and Randi chose — and that Randi's selection of Vee was a real, involuntary "yes," which the couple then turned into a plan: Randi staged a "collision" and friendship with Vee (ch3→ch4), and Pace's "chance" appearance and rescue at the Wilson Street coffee shop (ch5) was very likely coordinated (Randi was texting privately just before he arrived). Vee believes both meetings were pure chance and has no idea a "game between the two people now closest to her" (per jacket) put her in Pace's path.
- The reader knows Randi's composed, "dear friend" warmth toward Vee sits over a private vow (made after ch1's bench scene) never again to let herself be truly seen — even as her attraction to Vee appears to be growing on its own, separate from the plan.
- The reader has seen Randi's private breakdown (crying alone in Pace's bathroom, the door locked for the first time ever, the self-harming press of a bottle cap under her thumbnail) and her subsequent decision to hide it — none of which Pace, Vee, or the sorority sisters know about.

- Pace's past with Daphne (a boundary he once violated and now guards against repeating) is known to the reader but not shared on the page with Randi or Vee.

### Impression

**Pace** reads, on the surface, as extraordinarily attentive, patient, and tender — genuinely moving in his care for both Randi's and Vee's specific, private hungers. But the reader knows his courtship of Vee began as a game with his girlfriend, which recasts every "coincidence" as engineered, and colors his warmth with an uncomfortable undertone of calculation even where the feeling itself seems real. Trust in him is provisional — he seems to mean well by his own lights, but he is running an operation neither woman knows about.

**Randi** is the most complicated figure so far: dazzling, generous, and seemingly effortless in her warmth toward Vee, but the reader has watched her construct that warmth deliberately, has seen her most guarded, broken-open self in private, and knows she has vowed to hide it forever. There's real tenderness for her — her food anxiety, her mother's voice in her head, the self-harming bottle-cap gesture, the loneliness under the polish — alongside real unease about what she's doing to Vee, especially since her attraction to Vee reads as genuinely hers and not just theater for Pace.

**Vee** is easy to root for and ache for — believable, specific, starved for exactly the kind of attention she's now receiving, and utterly unsuspecting. The dramatic irony is heavy and growing: every warm thing that happens to her is shadowed by what the reader knows and she doesn't.

**Cassie** is the one steady, unimplicated point of trust in the book so far — dry, loyal, protective, and rightly wary of Randi without any ulterior motive visible. She reads as safe.

Overall: warm, sensuous, unsettling by design — the reader is meant to feel both drawn into and complicit in the seduction, per the jacket's framing, and that's exactly how it's landing.
