### Impression

Pace reads as genuinely tender, attentive, and skilled at making both Randi and Vee feel chosen and safe — and simultaneously, structurally, as someone running a long, calculated seduction of two women at once with their knowledge (Randi's) and without (Vee's). His care for Vee doesn't feel performed in the text, which makes the reader's foreknowledge of the game underneath it genuinely uneasy rather than simply cynical — he may love her and still be complicit in withholding the truth from her. Randi is the most complicated figure so far: capable of real warmth, real generosity, and — per ch. 1 and ch. 24 — real vulnerability and unplanned feeling, but also skilled at managing/extracting/shaping Vee's inner life for her own (and possibly Pace's) pleasure, with a practiced, protective mask that makes her hard to fully trust even when she seems most sincere. Vee is enormously sympathetic — a hungry, shame-carrying, generous young woman finally getting the attention and warmth she's starved for — and the dramatic irony is sharp and painful: everything that feels to her like being chosen is also, invisibly, a system she didn't choose into. Cassie is the one wholly trustworthy figure on the page, and her silent unease functions as the reader's own conscience. There's no reason yet to suspect anyone of outright cruelty — the tone is "psychologically merciless in its tenderness," per the jacket — but there is strong reason to worry for Vee, and to watch Randi's own unraveling as closely as Vee's awakening.
