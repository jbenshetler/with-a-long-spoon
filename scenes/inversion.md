# Inversion

*Diptych. Panel one: Vee POV, close third — Thursday, 8:20, Dr. Marsh's room letting out; Randi leaves for Standards. Panel two: Randi POV, close third — the sorority house, the Standards room, the hall; Vee is not present.*

---

Thursday Marsh had talked about kinship for an hour and twenty minutes, who a village counted as family and who it didn't and how much of the law was only the counting, and Vee had taken in about half of it, which was twice what she'd managed on Tuesday. Randi's knee had been against hers the whole hour. She had the Lifesavers out before Randi asked, this time, the soft roll waiting on the desk between them, and when Randi's hand turned over for one Vee put it in the palm and took her own hand back fast, and the wintergreen came off them both for a while, one lecture, one flavor, and she sat inside it with her leg held still and did not look over.

There was no dinner tonight. She had known that since Tuesday, and had spent two days being glad of it, and now, with the room standing up around her and Randi bending for her bag, she was not glad at all.

Out in the corridor it was floor wax and radiator heat and the doors at the end going open and shut on the cold. Randi stopped short of them and turned, and the hug came the way it always came, Vee pulled in against the small warm fit of her, the bitter orange, green at the edge, and Vee went into it and let it be good, and stood with her face in Randi's hair waiting for the rest of it. The rest of it didn't come. There was no table tonight, no bottle of red, no walk; there was nothing to say goodbye at the end of, so there was only the hug, and the hug ended the way hugs end, and Randi stepped back with both hands still on Vee's arms.

The relief went through her first. The other thing came right behind it, and she could not have said which of the two she'd have picked.

"I hate this," Randi said. "I'd so much rather be sitting across from you watching you clean your plate." Her mouth went rueful and her eyes didn't; her eyes had a gleam in them, bright and private, the look of a woman on her way to something she was going to enjoy. "But duty calls." She squeezed Vee's arms once. "A girl needs a spanking!"

Vee laughed. It came out a half-beat late and a note too high, and under the laugh her body had already heard the word and gone where the word went: a kitchen, a wet hand, the small sound of it and the sting that wasn't small; a bare bottom and a bet she'd lost; Randi across a table with her head tipped asking *did you deserve it*, and herself saying *probably*, and Randi's smile going down through her like a swallow of something warm. All of that in the time it took to laugh. Heat, low and sudden, and the shame right there on top of it, and the shame did nothing to put the heat out.

"Go," she said. "Be terrifying."

"Oh, I will." Randi's hand came up and put a piece of Vee's hair back behind her ear, and Vee felt it all the way down. "Saturday, gorgeous."

Then she was off down the corridor toward the cold, quick heels on the waxed floor, her coat swinging, and Vee stood where she'd been left with her own coat open and the roll of Lifesavers still in her fist and watched her go, and could not have said who the gleam had been for.

She put the Lifesavers back in her pocket and went out the other way.
