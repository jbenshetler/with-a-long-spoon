*Vee POV — the dorm room, the same night, straight on from the goodnight with Randi. Present: Vee, Cassie.*

# Some of Mine

She hadn't moved out of the doorway.

Her coat was still on. Cassie's was too — she'd not been in long enough to take it off — and between them the question sat in the warm room like a third person nobody had offered a chair.

She didn't know what Cassie had seen. That was the whole trouble of it. The cold was still coming off her in the warm room — she'd been outside, coming in, while down on the walk Randi's heels went off into the dark; and how close had she passed while the two of them stood in the light of the entrance saying the long goodnight? How long had the hug looked from a few feet off, how deep — and had she seen the rest, the mouth at her neck, the sound Vee hadn't caught in time to stop. Her neck was still live where it had been. She stayed aware of it the way you stay aware of a burn, and it seemed to her, absurdly, that it must show — that Cassie could read the whole night off her skin like a postmark.

"It's —" She reached for the easy thing, the sorority thing, the version that fit the room she and Cassie kept between them. "You know how Randi is. It's a whole —" There were too many words in her mouth and not one of them the size of an answer, and she heard herself setting them down anyway, one over another, the way you stack things over a stain. "It's a sorority thing. She's like that with everyone, it doesn't —"

She ran out. She never ran out.

Cassie let it sit — she had a way of leaving a silence open between you to see what you'd put in it — and Vee put nothing in it, because she had nothing.

"Is this what you want," Cassie said.

Not what happened. Not what is she to you. The flat, plain way, the same way she'd once asked do you love him — except the worry was still under it, the part that wasn't flat.

And Vee went looking for the answer. That was the terrible part, the part she wouldn't have believed of herself an hour before: she actually went and looked, because Cassie had asked it so simply it seemed like a thing a person would just know about herself. She reached for it. There was a shape there, close and large, and she came up against the edge of it and stepped back off before she could make out its size. Her neck said yes. Her body had been saying yes all night, on the walk, on the stairs — but that was the body, that was nothing, a thing skin did on its own. The question itself had no floor under it she was willing to stand on.

So she reached for the nearest thing that would take her weight, and it was the old one, the one they'd agreed a long time ago not to run again.

"You don't like her," she said. "That's what this is. You've never liked her, you've never liked any of it — and you're allowed, but you don't get to stand there and look at me like that."

Cassie didn't take it. The argument was years old, and Vee had laid it out on the floor like a plank to walk out on, and Cassie stepped over it without once looking down. She only watched Vee a moment longer, and whatever was in her face she kept — the way she'd kept it the night of the skirt, the eyebrow that went up and stayed and said nothing at all.

"You didn't have dinner," she said.

It wasn't a question, and it wasn't quite a change of subject. It was just true, the way the things Cassie said were true. She reached down beside the bunk and came up with the box and held it out.

"Do you want some of my Cocoa Pebbles?"

"Yeah." It came fast and whole, the first whole thing she'd said since she walked in, because here was a want she could find — a want the size of a bowl. "Yeah. Thanks."

She crossed the room and took the box, and that was the size of what she could answer tonight, standing there in her coat: not the other question, the one still open on the desk with no floor under it — only this one, small enough to hold in two hands.
