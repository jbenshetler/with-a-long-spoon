1. **:11 — the slide-clicking, three times.** "clicking the slide forward and then back to the one before it and then forward again" / "and clicked to the next slide" / ":13 He clicked back a slide. Then forward."
   *Class: repetition.* The tic is fully established by the first rendering; the third — two fragments doing the man's whole nervous system — is the keeper and has to fight the middle one for its effect.
   **Recommendation:** cut the middle instance ("and clicked to the next slide").

2. **:11 — "checking the deck like a man checking the pressure in a tire."**
   *Class: repetition.* "Checking" lands twice inside one clause, dulling the simile at the exact moment it should sharpen.
   **Recommendation:** recast one of the two "checking"s.

3. **:11 — "It was messy and complex and, he said, often surprising."**
   *Class: precision.* "Complex" is the abstract passenger between two concrete words, and the only one the chapter never cashes — "messy" and "surprising" both get paid off (the outlier, the name).
   **Recommendation:** drop "and complex."

4. **:17 / :19 — "the coffees after" / "half the coffee after."**
   *Class: repetition.* Two adjacent short paragraphs both close on "the coffee(s) after"; the second ("half the coffee after") is the sharper line and shouldn't have to share the phrase.
   **Recommendation:** vary or cut the earlier "the coffees after" at :17.

5. **:11 / :21 / :23 / :79 — "flat," the chapter's default adjective.** "flat, weatherless" (professor); "came out whole and flat" (Cassie); "Flat." (Cassie); "the flat certainty" (Cassie).
   *Class: repetition.* Cassie's three are a designed voice-print and the one-word "Flat." at :23 is the best of them; spending the word first on the professor pre-taxes Cassie's tell — the walk-on principle behind #26.
   **Recommendation:** vary the professor's adjective at :11 (keep "weatherless"); the author may also weigh whether "the flat certainty" at :79 needs "flat" when "Certain —" already opens it.

6. **:25 — "Randi thought Cassie was wrong. Not rudely — Randi thought the story was more complicated than any one number."**
   *Class: rhythm / repetition.* "Randi thought" opens two consecutive sentences with no anaphoric design; the doubled stem reads as a stall in a paragraph that should turn.
   **Recommendation:** recast the second opener off "Randi thought."

7. **:29 — "had a thin, quick sense that the room had stepped somewhere she couldn't follow."**
   *Class: precision.* Doubled modifier ("thin, quick") on an already-hedged noun ("sense"), and "the room had stepped" asks a room to do what only the two of them did — the reach for abstraction sits one line before a perfectly concrete close ("She looked at her coffee").
   **Recommendation:** keep one modifier and give the stepping to the conversation, not the room.

8. **:31 — "because the math was hers — economics major, the department's award just this fall, the one at the table numbers came easy to."**
   *Class: overwork.* Three appositives for a fact the clause just stated; the CV also reads as the narration vouching for Vee rather than the chapter showing her.
   **Recommendation:** keep one appositive (the award is least redundant); cut the other two.

9. **:33 — "and drank nothing, and looked satisfied with the arrangement, and that was that."**
   *Class: rhythm / overwork.* The paragraph's true last beat is "and drank nothing" (the :7 callback). "Looked satisfied" names a mood the line already carries — and pre-spends the "still, satisfied face" at :41 — while "and that was that" is a narrator closing a section that closes itself.
   **Recommendation:** end the section on "and drank nothing."

10. **:37 / :39 / :75 — the glass room, three times.** "a glass-walled room" / "the soundless glass room" / "set loose in the glass room."
    *Class: repetition.* The set is established once; "the soundless glass room" is the decorative use, and the :75 instance (the two words having nowhere to go) is load-bearing.
    **Recommendation:** cut "glass" from :39.

11. **:41 — "she didn't gloat, there was no gloat in her."**
    *Class: overwork.* The same denial twice inside one dash-pair, delaying the genuinely fine close it precedes ("watching the world admit something she'd told it about itself").
    **Recommendation:** keep one half of the pair.

12. **:45 — "because this was the one she'd been aiming the whole picture at — home, the proof, the bright counterexample that was going to complicate Cassie's clean sad story with something real."**
    *Class: overwork.* "leaned in, driving now" has already shown the aim; the three appositives then explain it, and "clean sad story" doubles a modifier where "clean" alone is the chapter's own established word (":11 was not clean").
    **Recommendation:** cut to one appositive and let "driving now" carry the rest.

13. **:49 — "the kind of point the professor had named at the front of the cold hall."**
    *Class: overwork.* The clause reaches back and explains the diptych plant the structure was built to make silently — and it lands *before* the reveal (MIRH, the name), softening it.
    **Recommendation:** cut the clause; let "alone in the white. A big one." stand.

14. **:53–:55 — the stillness rendered four ways.** "Randi went still." / "Not a motion — the withdrawal of motion, the sudden absence of all the small unconscious movements a person makes just by sitting in a chair." / "Randi's hand rested on the trackpad and did not move" / "the sense you get when someone beside you stops breathing."
    *Class: overwork.* "The withdrawal of motion" is the definitive rendering and is unimprovable; the "absence of all the small unconscious movements" restates it, "did not move" restates it again, and "stops breathing" is a second simile for the same thing. Four for one at the scene's hinge.
    **Recommendation:** thin to the strongest, keeping "the withdrawal of motion" and, if the felt/saw distinction is wanted, "Vee felt it more than saw it"; drop the "stops breathing" clause and/or "did not move."

15. **:63 — "Vee read it before she knew she was reading it. She knew the name Miranda — everyone who knew Randi knew it…"**
    *Class: repetition / precision.* Four forms of *know* in two sentences, three in eleven words, with no rhetorical design under the pile. (Note also the stranded "of the name" eight words from its head later in the same sentence — the reader has to re-parse mid-flight in the paragraph that most needs to move.)
    **Recommendation:** recast the second sentence off *know*, and lift the "chapter paperwork" detail out of the modifier chain so the sentence runs.

16. **:63 / :75 / :89 — "something a person could ___," three times.** "something a person could stand to answer to" / "something a person could hold" / "the small stack of decisions a person could pick up and be grateful to be holding."
    *Class: repetition.* The same generalizing closer on three consecutive key sentences, twice with the *hold* verb — a habit of phrasing rather than a figure.
    **Recommendation:** keep the :75 instance (where the abstraction is the point) and recast the other two.

17. **:61 / :83 — "Randi read it. Vee watched her do it —" / "Randi took it. Vee watched her take it —".**
    *Class: repetition.* Two paragraphs built on the identical engine twenty lines apart, the second running three "watched" clauses off the same stem. At :61 the watching *is* the drama; at :83 it merely stages three actions, so the weaker instance recycles the stronger.
    **Recommendation:** rebuild :83 off the actions themselves and keep the :61 pattern unique.

18. **:83 / :95 — the closing image spent twelve lines early.** ":83 put the point back among the hundred thousand others where it was only the biggest one and not a name" / ":95 the point went back to being one among a hundred thousand — the biggest one, off on its own in the white."
    *Class: repetition / rhythm.* **The chapter's most serious finding.** The final paragraph is a near-verbatim reprise (back among a hundred thousand, the biggest one) of a sentence the reader met a page earlier, so the closing turn lands as recycling. The genuinely new material in :95 is only "easy to see if you knew to look for it, invisible if you didn't" — and "easy to see" is itself Cassie's phrase from :79.
    **Recommendation:** strip the reprise from :83 (Randi closing the record is enough) so :95 is the only place the hundred thousand is said; weigh whether the close still wants "easy to see" given :79.

19. **:93 — "that Randi had been handed a rotten thing by a man she'd had the luck to be the daughter of — no hand in it, none of it earned — and that the kindest thing a friend could do was not make her carry it in front of anyone."**
    *Class: overwork.* Two faults in one sentence. "Handed" and "no hand in it" collide within twelve words, and "none of it earned" restates "no hand in it." More consequentially, the closing clause states as a *principle* the ethic the scene has already dramatized (the busywork at :89, the withheld name) — interiority narrating the theme the action showed. The hedge "not that she let herself think about it much" is the one part earning its place (it characterizes her turning away from the thing), which is an argument for keeping the hedge and thinning the moral gloss around it.
    **Recommendation:** cut one of the two "hand" restatements, and consider trimming the "kindest thing" clause so the scene isn't told what it just showed.
