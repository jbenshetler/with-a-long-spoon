# Line edit — outlier (2026-08-07)

**18 findings** — 4 rhythm, 6 within-chapter repetition, 5 overwork, 3 word precision. No dialogue-shape findings. No pre-ruled FIX-AT items in `echo-rulings.md` name this chapter; see the note under Echo candidates for the two rulings that brush it.

The chapter is at its best when it trusts a flat declarative to carry a large thing. "Cassie didn't carry things into rooms she meant to walk out of" (:7) and "The subject took the walk out of the building, the wait at the crosswalk, and half the coffee after" (:19) are both exact and unhurried by the sentence's own shape rather than by adjective. The reading beat at :61 — the column read once fast, then again slower, "as if the second pass might turn up the line that took it back. It didn't." — is the chapter's finest piece of writing and the model everything else should be measured against. "Randi's face was doing very little, and the very little was the whole of it" (:71), the ruling on the two words at :75 ("aimed too near to be meant for strangers"), and the two-sentence pivot at :85 ("Randi didn't look at her. Randi had no idea she'd been looked at.") are the other peaks. The findings below are mostly places where the chapter says a good thing and then says it again.

---

1. **:11 / :13 — the slide-clicking, three times.** ":11 clicking the slide forward and then back to the one before it and then forward again"; ":11 and clicked to the next slide"; ":13 He clicked back a slide. Then forward."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The tic is established fully by the first rendering; the second is filler, and the third — which is the strongest, two fragments doing the man's whole nervous system — has to fight the second for its effect.
   **Recommendation:** cut the middle instance ("and clicked to the next slide") so the opening description and the closing fragments frame the paragraph.

2. **:11 — "It was messy and complex and, he said, often surprising."**
   *Class: word precision.* "Complex" is the abstract passenger between two concrete words and is the only one the sentence doesn't later cash; "messy" and "surprising" both get paid off (the outlier, the name).
   **Recommendation:** drop "and complex."

3. **:11 / :21 / :23 / :79 — "flat" as the chapter's default adjective.** "flat, weatherless" (professor), "came out whole and flat" (Cassie), "Flat." as a one-word paragraph (Cassie), "the flat certainty" (Cassie).
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Cassie's three are a designed voice-print and the isolated "Flat." at :23 is the best of them; the professor's use spends the word on a walk-on before Cassie can own it — the same argument ruling #26 made about a signature spent on a walk-on.
   **Recommendation:** vary the professor's adjective at :11, keeping "weatherless," and consider whether "the flat certainty" at :79 needs "flat" when "Certain —" already opens it.

4. **:21 / :27 / :79 / :83 — certain / certainty, four times.** "the small certainty coming off her"; "and let it go, certain"; "Certain — the flat certainty of someone…"; "give Cassie's certainty a nod."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The word is doing real work at :79 and :83 (Cassie's certainty is what Randi has to swallow), which is exactly why the two earlier uses shouldn't have pre-spent it.
   **Recommendation:** recast the abstract noun at :21 into what Vee actually feels on her right side, and cut "certain" from :27, where "let it go" already carries it.

5. **:29 — "had a thin, quick sense that the room had stepped somewhere she couldn't follow."**
   *Class: word precision.* Doubled modifier ("thin, quick") on an already-hedged noun ("sense"), and "the room had stepped" asks a room to do something a room can't — the sentence reaches for abstraction in a paragraph whose last line ("She looked at her coffee") is perfectly concrete.
   **Recommendation:** pick one modifier and give the stepping to the conversation or to the two of them, not the room.

6. **:31 — "because the math was hers — economics major, the department's award just this fall, the one at the table numbers came easy to."**
   *Class: overwork.* Three appositives for one fact already stated in the clause before them; the CV items also read as the narration vouching for Vee rather than the chapter showing her.
   **Recommendation:** keep one appositive (the award is the least redundant) and cut the other two.

7. **:33 — "and drank nothing, and looked satisfied with the arrangement, and that was that."**
   *Class: rhythm / overwork.* The paragraph's true last beat is "and drank nothing" — the callback to :7. "Looked satisfied" tells the reader the mood the line already has, and "and that was that" is a narrator closing a section that closes itself; it also pre-spends the satisfied face at :41.
   **Recommendation:** end the section on "and drank nothing."

8. **:37 / :39 / :75 — the glass room, three times.** "a glass-walled room on the fourth floor"; "the soundless glass room"; "set loose in the glass room."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The room is established once; "the soundless glass room" is the decorative one, and the third is the load-bearing one (the two words having nowhere to go).
   **Recommendation:** cut "glass" from :39.

9. **:41 — "she didn't gloat, there was no gloat in her."**
   *Class: overwork.* The same denial twice inside one dash-pair, immediately before the sentence's genuinely fine close ("watching the world admit something she'd told it about itself"), which it delays.
   **Recommendation:** keep one half of the pair.

10. **:45 — "because this was the one she'd been aiming the whole picture at — home, the proof, the bright counterexample that was going to complicate Cassie's clean sad story with something real."**
    *Class: overwork.* "Leaned in, driving now" has already shown the aim; the three appositives then explain it, and "clean sad story" is a doubled modifier where "clean" alone is the chapter's own established word (":11 was not clean").
    **Recommendation:** cut to one appositive and let "driving now" do the rest.

11. **:49 — "the kind of point the professor had named at the front of the cold hall."**
    *Class: overwork.* The reader has just watched the diptych's first panel plant this exact thing; the clause reaches back and explains a connection the structure was built to make silently, and it arrives before the reveal, softening it.
    **Recommendation:** cut the clause and let "alone in the white. A big one." stand.

12. **:53–:55 — the stillness rendered three ways.** "Randi went still." / "Not a motion — the withdrawal of motion, the sudden absence of all the small unconscious movements a person makes just by sitting in a chair." / "Vee felt it more than saw it, the sense you get when someone beside you stops breathing."
    *Class: overwork.* Three consecutive statements of one perception; the middle one is the definitive rendering, and the third ("stops breathing") is a second simile for what "the withdrawal of motion" has just made unimprovable.
    **Recommendation:** cut the "stops breathing" clause, keeping "Vee felt it more than saw it" if the felt/saw distinction is wanted.

13. **:63 — "Vee read it before she knew she was reading it. She knew the name Miranda — everyone who knew Randi knew it…"**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition / word precision.* Four forms of *know* in two sentences, three of them in eleven words, with no rhetorical design behind the pile.
    **Recommendation:** recast the second sentence off the verb *know*.

14. **:63 — "the long formal version on the chapter paperwork of the name her parents had given her and then, with a sense of humor, shortened to something a person could stand to answer to."**
    *Class: rhythm.* "Of the name" is stranded eight words from its head, so the sentence has to be re-parsed mid-flight — in the one paragraph that most needs to move at reading speed.
    **Recommendation:** move the paperwork detail out of the modifier chain, or split the sentence at "her parents had given her."

15. **:63 / :75 / :89 — "something a person could ___," three times.** "something a person could stand to answer to"; "something a person could hold"; "the small stack of decisions a person could pick up and be grateful to be holding."
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The same generalizing construction used as the closer of three consecutive key sentences, twice with the *hold* verb. It reads as a habit of phrasing rather than a figure.
    **Recommendation:** keep the :75 instance (it is the one where the abstraction is the point) and recast the other two.

16. **:61 / :83 — "Vee watched her do it — watched her… watched her…"**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The chapter builds two separate paragraphs on the identical engine, the second running three "watched" clauses off the same stem twenty lines after the first ran two. The second is also the weaker: at :61 the watching is the drama; at :83 it merely stages three actions.
    **Recommendation:** rebuild :83 off the actions themselves, keeping the :61 pattern unique.

17. **:93 — "Randi had been handed a rotten thing by a man she'd had the luck to be the daughter of — no hand in it, none of it earned."**
    *Class: overwork / word precision.* "Handed" and "no hand in it" collide within twelve words; "none of it earned" restates "no hand in it"; and the interposed hedge "not that she let herself think about it much" breaks the sentence's spine before it has one.
    **Recommendation:** cut one of the two restatements and move or drop the hedge so the sentence runs.

18. **:83 / :95 — the closing image is spent twelve lines early.** ":83 put the point back among the hundred thousand others where it was only the biggest one and not a name"; ":95 the point went back to being one among a hundred thousand — the biggest one, off on its own in the white."
    *Class: within-chapter repetition / rhythm.* This is the chapter's most serious finding: the final paragraph is a near-verbatim reprise (back among a hundred thousand, the biggest one) of a sentence the reader met a page ago, so the last image lands as recycling rather than as the closing turn. The genuinely new material in :95 is only "easy to see if you knew to look for it, invisible if you didn't" — and note that "easy to see" is itself Cassie's phrase from :79.
    **Recommendation:** strip the reprise from :83 (Randi closing the record is enough) so the final paragraph is the only place the hundred thousand is said, and check whether the close still wants "easy to see" given :79.
