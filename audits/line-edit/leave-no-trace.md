**Verdict: 18 findings — 8 overwork, 7 repetition, 2 rhythm, 1 precision, 1 dialogue. No pre-ruled FIX-AT phrases occur in this chapter; protected constructions ("it came out," the body-before-mind shape at line 11, Pace's negative-space register) were checked and left alone.**

The chapter's own high-water marks: the summit paragraph ("the world fell open in front of her… and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part of all of it") and its coda ("The world swallowed his blue whole"); the cherry-pit lesson ("She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her"); the unbuttoning sequence with its epizeuxis ("She held them there") and "He'd been a long way from the question of whether he was cold." These passages trust the action and the image and stop on the beat. Everything below is measured against them.

## Findings

1. **"It came out the way you reach for a thing before you've decided you want it."** (¶3, after "the yes out before she'd weighed it") — *overwork*. The sentence restates what "the yes out before she'd weighed it" already did; the paragraph's real move is the turn to "There had been no wall of trees in the yes. There had been him." Recommend cutting the restating sentence and letting the yes go straight to the trees.

2. **"The shirt gave her the rest." / "gave her the rest of the looking alone."** (blue-shirt ¶ and summit ¶) — *repetition*. The distinctive "gave her the rest" construction lands twice, and the summit use is the one doing thematic work. Recommend varying the blue-shirt instance.

3. **"The grade steepened." / "The grade would ease." / "The grade pitched up another notch."** (three consecutive paragraphs mid-climb) — *rhythm*. Three same-shaped "The grade…" openers in a page; the ratchet may be intentional, but the middle one is her hope, not the trail, and could carry a different subject to break the run. Recommend reshaping one of the three openers.

4. **"A noise came out of her, half a laugh." / "pulled a gasp out of her, half a laugh."** (boot scene; horseradish bite) — *repetition*. The exact tag "half a laugh," both times appended to an involuntary sound pulled "out of her," renders the same beat twice. Recommend varying the second occurrence.

5. **"her body came back to her by pieces… The body came back after the seeing, slow, the way a sound comes back after it's been too loud to hear."** (summit ¶) — *overwork*. The second sentence re-renders the first and then explains it with a simile after the pieces (tired, hungry, ice) have already shown it. Recommend cutting or drastically compressing the second sentence.

6. **The cherry paragraph ("the sweet came first… and then the acid… and then the small bitter")** — *rhythm*. It reprises the sandwich-bite paragraph's "and then… and then… and then" tasting-catalog shape only three paragraphs later; the sandwich version is the stronger of the two. Recommend giving the cherry its flavor in a differently built sentence.

7. **"That was the trick of it. That was what it was for."** (end of the eating ¶) — *overwork*. Two same-shaped closers where one belongs; the paragraph's true last sentence is one of these, not both. Recommend cutting one.

8. **"on the rock between them"** (cherries set down; wax paper smoothed open — two uses five lines apart, plus the earned callback with the dress) — *repetition*. The third use ("the dress stayed where she'd set it down on the rock between them") is the designed payoff; the first two spend it before it pays. Recommend varying one of the first two.

9. **"it found her hair, always her hair, and lifted it and laid it across her face" / "The wind laid her hair across her face."** (summit; after the dress exchange) — *repetition*. The same gesture rendered twice with the same verb ("laid… across her face"); the second time she answers it ("pushed it back… and let it go"), which is the new information. Recommend varying the wind's action in the second instance so only her response repeats.

10. **"didn't make anything of it" / "without making a thing of it"** (his reading of her face at the trailhead; the shared water bottle) — *repetition*. The same Pace-move in near-identical wording; both moments deserve it, but not in the same words. Recommend rewording one.

11. **"His only dry shirt… and he'd put it in her hands instead." then "For a moment it made no sense to her… His shirt, and then the truck. Both."** (truck section) — *overwork/rhythm*. The earlier paragraph fully spells out the shirt sacrifice, so the two-beat realization ("Then she looked down at the flannel in her lap. / His shirt, and then the truck. Both.") arrives half-pre-empted; "Both." would land harder if the first paragraph withheld the full accounting. Recommend trimming the earlier paragraph's explanatory second half so the realization happens once, where it's staged.

12. **"The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice, the two run together inside her now"** (unbuttoning) — *overwork*. The chiasmus is complete at "the heat was the voice"; "the two run together inside her now" explains it after it lands. Recommend cutting the trailing clause.

13. **"all of his attention on her and nothing asked for it" / "His face went to the full attention"** (dress exchange; truck) — *repetition*. Twice-named "attention" as Pace's signature state within one chapter; the truck instance is the load-bearing one. Recommend rendering the earlier moment through behavior rather than the named quality.

14. **"He was shivering. He'd been shivering. He was in a soaked shirt and soaked pants and he'd been out in the rain twice over, on the trail and standing at the truck, and he was cold enough that his hand trembled in hers."** — *overwork*. "He was shivering. He'd been shivering." is the whole discovery; the causal inventory that follows restates what the reader has watched for two pages and re-lands the paragraph's opening (shaking → shivering → trembled). Recommend cutting or halving the third sentence.

15. **"He'd parked illegal so she wouldn't have to cross the rain."** (dorm arrival) — *overwork*. The fire lane, the red-painted curb, and the overhang "where the rain wasn't" have already said this; it is the chapter's third tell-after-show (with findings 11 and 14), and the pattern dulls each instance. Recommend cutting the sentence.

16. **"and she was going to put a small thing where the big thing went, because the small thing was the one that fit"** (her aborted goodbye) — *overwork*. The small-thing/big-thing figure is done at "where the big thing went"; the "because" clause explains it. Recommend cutting the final clause.

17. **"the air out of the vents was cold. 'It'll only blow cold till the truck warms up,' he said."** (climbing into the cab) — *dialogue*. Narration and speech state the same fact back to back; his line carries it better (and adds "Give it a minute"). Recommend cutting "and the air out of the vents was cold" and letting the dialogue do it.

18. **"the meat under it, red and full and substantial"** (sandwich bite) — *precision*. "Substantial" is the one abstract latinate word in a tasting sequence built of loud, cold, sharp, sour — the almost-right word in a passage that elsewhere has the exactly right ones. Recommend replacing it with a concrete quality of the meat.

Minor, noted without a numbered ruling: "in her lap" recurs ~6 times through the truck section (the flannel, the shirt, the balled shirt); most uses are literal furniture, but the density is worth one thinning pass.
