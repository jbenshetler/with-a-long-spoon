# Line edit — sorority (2026-08-07)

**Verdict:** 14 findings — 3 rhythm, 4 within-chapter repetition, 4 overwork, 2 word precision, 1 dialogue shape (plus 1 pre-ruled FIX-AT to apply and 8 echo candidates).

The chapter is strongest where it trusts a single flat sentence after a long one. The boutique fitting is the best sustained run in the file: Randi going down on one knee "in her own beautiful clothes," the held foot, the frosted plum polish, and then the whole beat resolved on one syllable — `"Mm," Randi said.` / `That was all.` The shoe paragraph that follows (`:97`, "less like wearing a shoe than being stood up by one") is the chapter's cleanest metaphor — earned by physical fact, landed and dropped. The credit-card paragraph (`:105`) is its best piece of interiority: the debit between her fingers, the pause, the refusal to let it reach her face. The dorm comedy (`:41`–`:49`) has real timing — the canoe-sized shoe set down "without a word," the sandal line — and `:49`'s "the real laugh, the one that costs her something to give" is a keeper. Everything below is measured against those.

## Findings

1. **`:5`, the opening sentence** — "The knock came earlier than Vee was ready for… all of it suddenly visible again, smaller, lit by what she'd brought in with her."
   *Class: rhythm.* The sentence's spine breaks: the subject of "all of it suddenly visible again" is stranded ninety words back behind the bunked beds, the milk crates, the poster, the album title, and a relative clause about freshman year. The chapter elsewhere runs long sentences that keep their grammar underfoot (`:105`, `:109`); this one asks the reader to hold too much before the verb arrives.
   *Recommendation:* Split after the room-reorganizing clause so the inventory of objects and their re-seeing land as their own sentence.

2. **`:5`–`:15`, "the way" ×5** — "the way rooms did" / "the way Cassie did everything" / "the way she'd been seen in his mirror" / "the way you take in a thing" / "the way she'd turned at the mirror."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Five instances of one filtered construction in eleven lines, all in the chapter's opening movement. Two of them are load-bearing (the mirror callbacks); the other three are reflex. The rest of the chapter shows the direct image instead.
   *Recommendation:* Keep the two mirror callbacks and recast the other three into direct images.

3. **`:17`–`:19`, "cool" ×3** — "the silk sliding cool over her knuckle" / "the silk cool there and Vee warm under it" / "The cool press of the silk where Randi settled it."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The same tactile adjective on the same fabric three times inside three paragraphs, the third re-reporting the second from Vee's side rather than adding a sensation.
   *Recommendation:* Cut the third; the temperature has already been established from both sides at `:17`.

4. **`:19`** — "The cool press of the silk… the small stillness, the pleasure of being looked at — and one faint thing under it she didn't stop to name."
   *Class: overwork.* A three-item list of abstractions ("the small stillness," "the pleasure of being looked at") restating what `:13`–`:17` already staged in action, and "she didn't stop to name" announces the withheld thing rather than withholding it — a telegraph the chapter otherwise avoids.
   *Recommendation:* Cut the abstract list and let the unnamed thing sit without the label announcing itself.

5. **`:21` and `:101`, "the whole of it" ×2** — "stepped back to take in the whole of it again" / "A small betrayal of the whole of it."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The same totality construction bookending the chapter without design linking them; the second sits inside the chapter's most important interior turn and shouldn't be reaching for a phrase already spent on a dress.
   *Recommendation:* Recast the `:101` instance to something concrete.

6. **`:39`** — "and the hunt tipped over, as hunts do when they stop paying out, into the other thing."
   *Class: overwork.* The interposed gloss explains the mechanism of a turn the next four paragraphs are about to dramatize brilliantly. The chapter's comedy works because it isn't introduced.
   *Recommendation:* Cut the "as hunts do…" clause.

7. **`:43`** — "Making herself enormous, apparently, was the answer to being called big-footed."
   *Class: overwork.* Interiority restating the joke the image just made — six-inch bedazzled platforms after a big-feet insult needs no reading. The neighboring gags (`:41`, `:47`) land without any narrator standing next to them.
   *Recommendation:* Cut the sentence and go straight from the approach to the grave turn of the foot.

8. **`:51`** — "a little flush, there and gone, just two girls being silly over shoes — and she let it go before it could be anything."
   *Class: overwork.* Three consecutive softenings of one small moment; "just two girls being silly over shoes" supplies the couple's reading and Vee's dismissal both, and then "before it could be anything" says the dismissal a second time.
   *Recommendation:* Keep the flush and one of the two dismissals, not both.

9. **`:61`, `:65`, "delight" ×3** — "Something delighted broke over Randi's whole face" / "delighted with the pair of them" / "the delight had folded into something warmer."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Three in five lines, plus "delighted" already at `:7`. The first is vivid; the second is a flat restatement of it; the third needs the noun to do the folding, so it should be the one protected.
   *Recommendation:* Cut "delighted with the pair of them" at `:65`.

10. **`:83`** — "The word dropped in easy and glamorous, *lover*, more than Vee had ever let herself call him, and exactly right the instant Randi said it, so that Pace rearranged in her own chest into something adult and lit."
    *Class: dialogue shape.* Randi's line is a two-beat jab; the narration then stands between it and her next line for forty words explaining what the word did, including a second gloss ("exactly right the instant Randi said it") of the first. Randi is mid-motion here and the paragraph rides a rhythm the analysis interrupts.
    *Recommendation:* Keep the "rearranged in her chest" clause and cut the intermediate glosses so Randi's speech and her movement stay continuous.

11. **`:85`, pre-ruled** — "a heel like a held breath."
    *Class: cross-chapter echo, FIX-AT (echo-rulings #6).* "A held breath" is a spent metaphor book-wide; the Vol-1 keeper is `standards.md:141`, and `sorority` is named among the occurrences that vary at this review.
    *Recommendation:* Vary the figure here (replacement wording to be drafted for the author at this review).

12. **`:79` and `:93`, the floor twice** — "the floor did a quiet thing under her" / "felt the floor of her tip, a small swing of the room."
    *Class: within-chapter repetition (candidate design).* The same destabilization figure — the floor moving — used for two different shocks (the price, Randi's thumb) fourteen lines apart. If it's designed as a rhyme it's under-marked; as written it reads as one image reached for twice.
    *Recommendation:* Vary the `:93` instance to a bodily register distinct from the money shock, or mark the rhyme deliberately.

13. **`:105` and `:109`, the "nothing under it" figure and the price restated** — "There was nothing under it" then "nothing behind it but next month"; and at `:109` "a multiple of Vee's, several times it, the price of the shoes Vee was white-knuckling and then several times that again."
    *Class: overwork / repetition.* `:105`'s two "nothing under/behind it" figures are the same image four lines apart. `:109` states the multiple three times in one clause chain; the fact is already devastating on the first statement, and the triple-count is the narrator insisting.
    *Recommendation:* Keep one of the "nothing" figures at `:105`, and cut two of the three price restatements at `:109`.

14. **`:113` and `:117`** — "her mouth went dry and fond" / "the laugh came up out of her real and whole and unrehearsed, the kind that bends you at the middle, the kind you cannot perform."
    *Class: word precision / rhythm.* "Dry" is the almost-right word — the sense wanted is wry or amused, and "dry" reads as thirst against a mouth. At `:117` three stacked modifiers are followed by two "the kind" appositives, and "unrehearsed" and "you cannot perform" are the same claim twice; the closing paragraph is the chapter's emotional landing and is carrying more freight than any earlier one.
    *Recommendation:* Replace "dry" with the wry sense, and cut either the modifier triple or one "the kind" clause at `:117`.
