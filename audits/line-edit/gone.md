## Findings

1. **The same gesture rendered four times.** :19 "Randi pushed her face into his chest"; :21 "He felt her smiling against his chest"; :39 "set her cheek back on his chest"; :45 "settled back onto his chest." Class: **within-chapter repetition**. Her face returning to his chest is the chapter's resting position and it should be established once and then assumed; rendered four times in twenty-five lines it stops reading as intimacy and starts reading as blocking. Recommendation: keep :19 (where the hiding is doing work) and one late return, and let the others be implied by the fact that she never got up.

2. **"chest" as the anchor noun, eight times.** :7, :11, :19, :21, :39, :45, :47, :69. Class: **within-chapter repetition**. Related to #1 but separable — even after the gesture is thinned, the noun carries every contact in the scene and goes invisible from overuse, which costs :47 ("her breasts dragging across his chest") the impact it should have as the one time the chest is *touched* rather than rested on. Recommendation: vary two or three of the remaining instances to other parts of him, or cut the prepositional phrase where the position is already known.

3. **"Something" as the subject of Randi's concealment, four times.** :39 "Something crossed her face and was gone"; :45 "Something in Randi went quiet at that"; :59 "Then something gathered under it"; plus :31's "something more careful." Class: **within-chapter repetition**. The vague-subject construction is the right instrument for a woman being rendered from outside, but at four uses it becomes the chapter's default rather than its signal, and :59 — the one the cold reread turns on — arrives sounding like the third instance of a habit. Recommendation: keep :39 and :59 and find another grammar for :45.

4. **The stillness paragraph continues past its true last sentence.** :59 "Randi went still on him. Not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that costs something to hold. Then something gathered under it — a readiness, in the faintest tightening of her against him." Class: **overwork**. "the kind that costs something to hold" has already told us everything; "a readiness" then names the thing the tightening was about to show, which is a metaphor explained after it lands. Recommendation: end the paragraph on the physical detail and drop the abstraction that labels it.

5. **:61 doubles the beat :59 just made.** "she said, when she'd been quiet a beat too long, lighter than the stillness." Class: **overwork**. "a beat too long" already tells us the lightness is applied; "lighter than the stillness" measures it a second time against the noun we just spent a paragraph on. Recommendation: cut one of the two qualifiers.

6. **The interiority at :49 restates itself.** "…wanting something out loud in his bed and not able to stop it. Her, stepping over her own line, of her own accord." Class: **overwork**. The second sentence is the first sentence paraphrased into a summary, and "of her own accord" re-argues "not able to stop it" in the opposite direction. Recommendation: keep one of the two sentences.

7. **:47's stage direction explains the move it just made.** "…one way and then the other, showing him with her own body the thing she was describing." Class: **overwork**. The action is unmistakable and the tag turns the chapter's best physical beat into a caption. Recommendation: cut "showing him with her own body the thing she was describing."

8. **:51 announces its own hinge.** "He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold. What came up in him was the other thing." Class: **rhythm**. The first sentence is precise and complete; the second is a pointer to the dialogue that immediately follows and delays it by one beat. Recommendation: cut the second sentence and let "You know what she does to a room" arrive off the silence.

9. **Three "A beat" dialogue tags in the same shape.** :19 "A beat, fond."; :27 "A beat."; :43 "A beat." Class: **within-chapter repetition**. Bare "a beat" is settled as ordinary idiom (rulings #20 scope note), but three identical one-word sentence-fragment tags inside a short chapter is a local pileup, and the two unmodified ones do less than the pause they mark. Recommendation: vary or cut the two bare instances, keeping "A beat, fond."

10. **Two "the way" constructions inside the first sentence.** :7 "warm the way he kept it when she was there… loose in the way she only went afterward." Class: **rhythm / within-chapter repetition**. The opening sentence is the chapter's establishing breath and it leans on the same subordinating move twice before the reader is oriented, which flattens what should be two distinct observations. Recommendation: recast one of the two.

11. **"fold" twice in the opening paragraph.** :7 "folded down onto his chest" and "that cool voice with the warmth folded under it." Class: **within-chapter repetition**. The fold figure is protected as design elsewhere, but here the two uses are unrelated — one literal posture, one musical metaphor — and their proximity makes the second read as an accident rather than a rhyme. Recommendation: change the Sade description.

12. **"let her set the pace of it" collides with his name.** :69. Class: **word precision**. The idiom is invisible until the reader registers that the man being ridden is called Pace, at which point it reads as an unintended pun in the chapter's last sentence — the one place nothing should snag. Recommendation: replace the idiom with a plainer statement of who is governing the tempo.

13. **"It was so cute" is the weakest beat in the chapter's best speech.** :19, between "wanting the floor to take her" and "She was *so* embarrassed." Class: **dialogue shape**. Randi compresses when she's enjoying herself, and here the speech runs three flat assessments in a row where the anecdote has already delivered all three; "so cute" in particular is the generic word standing where a Randi-specific one belongs. Recommendation: cut it and let the descriptive beat run straight into "That was kind of hot."
