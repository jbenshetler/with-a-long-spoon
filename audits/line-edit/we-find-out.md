# Line edit — we-find-out (2026-08-07)

**19 findings** (1 pre-ruled FIX-AT · 6 within-chapter repetition · 6 overwork · 3 word precision · 2 rhythm · 1 dialogue shape) — a strong chapter whose weakest habit is adding one interpretive sentence after a beat has already landed.

**Calibrated against the chapter's own best.** The bet sequence is the standard: `:45` "Either you admit you lost, or we find out." and `:57` "Then admit it, or we find out." — the title earned twice, compressed both times. `:61`'s "Too easy. The ease went through her worse than any sting had. Her body had decided without her." is the chapter's cleanest thinking: three short sentences, each one adding, none restating. `:65`'s "Like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good." and `:87`–`:89` (the quiet hand, "a tap, a question," "The sound of it small in the quiet room") are the register at full strength — plain diction, no explanation, the reader trusted. `:93`'s "she could not have said which one of them she was lying to" and `:99`'s "Shame wants a witness" are the interiority working at the level the rest should meet. Everything below is measured against those.

1. **`:95`** — "her palm passed over the old scar **high on the inside of his thigh**, smooth and raised in its clean surgeon's line"
   *Class: pre-ruled FIX-AT (echo-rulings #4).* One of the two remaining Vol-1 occurrences of the framing phrase; `famished:77` is the ruled keeper (the *found* beat) and `rock:247` was varied 2026-08-07. This occurrence is retrospective — the reader already holds the fact — so varied wording costs nothing.
   **Recommendation:** vary the framing phrase only; the anatomical location is canon and must not blur.

2. **`:9`** — "a shake in one hand, **the salt of the gym still on him**" … five sentences later "This close he smelled of himself, warm and dark and a little animal **under the gym**"
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The gym-smell is rendered twice in one paragraph, and the second rendering is far the better one — it discovers something (the animal under the clean she knows). The first pre-spends it.
   **Recommendation:** cut "the salt of the gym still on him" so the close-up smell arrives new.

3. **`:9`** — "He opened the door still **in his gym shorts, no shoes**" … "and against **his bare feet and gym shorts** it felt like she'd tried too hard"
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The same two-item inventory of his undress, verbatim in substance, twelve lines apart in one paragraph; the second is doing contrast work that a shorter pointer would do better.
   **Recommendation:** compress the second reference to one item rather than re-listing both.

4. **`:7`** — "She texted him after stats, **which she had never done, had never once been the one to reach first.**"
   *Class: overwork.* Two clauses for one fact, and the second is the informative one. The chapter's own standard (`:61`) is short sentences that each add.
   **Recommendation:** cut "which she had never done," and let the second clause carry it.

5. **`:11`** — "and hit him in the thigh with the side of her fist, **the way you hit a door that sticks. It was like hitting a door,** except the muscle gave a little"
   *Class: overwork.* The simile lands, then is immediately restated to be turned. The turn is worth keeping; the restatement is the seam showing.
   **Recommendation:** cut "It was like hitting a door," and let "except the muscle gave" attach to the simile directly.

6. **`:21`** — "and rubbed his thighs **as if to make it better, contrite, all apology**"
   *Class: overwork / doubled modifiers.* Three renderings of one attitude, and the paragraph then gives it a fourth in dialogue ("I shouldn't have poked you") and a fifth in "She kept it soft and sorry."
   **Recommendation:** cut "contrite, all apology."

7. **`:23`** — "**With hands faster than she believed,** he grabbed her by the waist"
   *Class: word precision.* The idiom wants a conditional ("than she'd have believed"); as written it reads as a slip, and the abstract "hands faster than X" is weaker than the action it introduces, which is already fast.
   **Recommendation:** fix or cut the clause — the yank does the speed without being announced.

8. **`:25`** — "and found out something she hadn't known, **which was that** she was faster than he was"
   *Class: word precision / rhythm.* A nominal hinge in a sentence about quickness; the chapter's diction elsewhere is Anglo-Saxon and direct.
   **Recommendation:** break the relative clause so the discovery lands without the connective.

9. **`:25`** — "**She had never in her life done such a thing.** The house made her brave, or the game did, or the week of wanting did, and **some small astonished part of her stood off and watched her do it and could not believe it was her.**"
   *Class: overwork.* Three consecutive sentences saying she astonished herself. The triple "or the … did" is the good one — it thinks. The flanking two both mean "she couldn't believe she'd done it."
   **Recommendation:** cut the final "and could not believe it was her" clause, or cut the flat first sentence — not both, but one must go.

10. **`:25`** — the whole paragraph (chase, flash, flight into the bedroom)
    *Class: rhythm.* Twenty-plus lines of accelerating action in one block, and it contains the chapter's only comic set piece plus its turn. The paragraph is running fast in shape but reading slow on the page.
    **Recommendation:** break it before "It worked. Something changed in his face." so the turn gets white space and the run at the hall reads as a separate impulse.

11. **`:11` / `:67`** — "The smallest thing, a tightening at the corner of his eyes, **there and gone**" / "a small moist motion she felt more than made, **there and gone**."
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* A distinctive three-word closer used twice, both times as the last beat of a micro-observation. `:11`'s is the load-bearing one (the flinch is the chapter's premise).
    **Recommendation:** vary or cut the `:67` occurrence.

12. **`:67`** — "Her tongue touched her lip before she knew it meant to, **a small moist motion** she felt more than made"
    *Class: word precision.* "Moist" is the almost-right word — it is the chapter's own subject-word for arousal three paragraphs earlier (the finger, the damp), and spending it on a tongue-flick borrows a charge that belongs elsewhere; "small … motion" is also two abstractions where the sentence has already given the concrete.
    **Recommendation:** cut the appositive entirely and let "before she knew it meant to" close the sentence.

13. **`:61` / `:97`** — "her body took him **without a question**, … **as though it had been waiting** all afternoon to be asked" / "her body took him the way it had taken the one finger, **without a word of argument**, **as though it had settled the matter hours ago and was only waiting** for her to stop asking"
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The `:97` sentence is deliberately a callback and should be — but it repeats the *construction* almost exactly (took him + negation-of-resistance + "as though it had … waiting"), which reads as recycling rather than return. `:97`'s content ("settled the matter hours ago") is the stronger idea.
    **Recommendation:** keep both beats; recast one so the return is recognized by its sense, not by its grammar.

14. **`:63`** — "and that was the old shame doubled, **the wet and then the shameful plenty of the wet**, the thing about her body she had spent her whole life trying to keep secret even from herself, held up now in the soft light where **they could both see exactly how much of it there was.**"
    *Class: overwork / rhythm.* The paragraph's true last beat is "the shameful plenty of the wet"; the closing clause restates the plenty in flat terms and the middle clause explains the shame the whole chapter has been dramatizing.
    **Recommendation:** end the sentence earlier — the trailing "how much of it there was" is the sentence explaining its own image.

15. **`:71`** — "and did not move the finger from where it was between them, **and that was the whole of his answer.**"
    *Class: overwork.* The sentence has just shown a man answering by not answering; the final clause tells us so.
    **Recommendation:** cut "and that was the whole of his answer."

16. **`:81` / `:83`** — "She was being offered up to her own wanting, **and both of them knew it**, and she stood still and let the place be made." / "**She had given herself up to him by degrees** because he'd said to, and there was a kind of terrible ceremony in it"
    *Class: within-chapter repetition (same beat twice) + overwork.* Two consecutive paragraphs land the same insight — the surrender is a ceremony she is consenting to be the offering in. `:81`'s "the place where the debt would be paid, and she the payment" is the sharper rendering; `:83`'s "the keeping of them made the rest of her nakedness louder" is the sharper *sentence*. The connective interiority in each is what doubles.
    **Recommendation:** cut "and both of them knew it" and let `:83` carry the ceremony idea alone, or fold the two paragraphs' best sentences into one.

17. **`:79` / `:85`** — "and **the worst of it was that** they were not answering the spanks he'd already given" / "She couldn't see him. **That was the worst of it**"
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The same superlative frame six lines apart, ranking two different things as worst, which cancels both. `:85`'s is the structurally necessary one (it sets up the not-seeing that the whole spanking sequence turns on).
    **Recommendation:** vary the `:79` frame.

18. **`:101`** — "**He hadn't planned any of what came next, she could tell**, and that was the thing that undid her. **No routine to it, nothing practiced.**"
    *Class: overwork.* The fragment restates the sentence before it, and the juice that follows demonstrates both without help.
    **Recommendation:** cut "No routine to it, nothing practiced."

19. **`:105`** — "she found the scrunchies, **a whole form of them**, deep green with small white dots"
    *Class: word precision.* "Form" reads as an error where a mass noun is wanted; the paragraph's whole payload is quantity, so the one word carrying it has to be exact. (Note also that the paragraph then says the quantity twice more — "more of them than a person could go through quickly, far more" and "the plenty of them" — so the noun could simply go.)
    **Recommendation:** replace or cut the appositive; the "far more" sentence already delivers the count.

20. **`:107`** — "**"Why do I keep pretending we're not going to sleep together,"** she said to the glass. **"Every time I've been here since that first night, we have."**"
    *Class: dialogue shape.* The first line is exactly right — a woman talking to a mirror, compressed, rhetorical. The second runs grammatical and evidentiary, as if she needed to convince someone; she is alone, and she already knows.
    **Recommendation:** compress or cut the second sentence — the "Victorian LARPing" line two beats later is the same joke landed better.

21. **`:107`** — "flinched at the **sticky** animal evidence of herself" … ""does me no good at all when I'm sweaty and **sticky**.""
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Four sentences apart; the dialogue occurrence is the one with the joke in it.
    **Recommendation:** vary the narration's adjective.

22. **`:111`** — "When he set the bowl in front of her **it looked like a dozen gravy-covered dishes from her childhood. It tasted like none of them.** The spice was pungent and the heat came up late and caught her by surprise, and she was glad of the beer."
    *Class: rhythm (section ends one beat late).* "It tasted like none of them" is the chapter's button — the whole afternoon in four words. The sentence after it is competent description that lowers the volume on the last line of the chapter.
    **Recommendation:** consider ending the chapter on "It tasted like none of them," or moving the heat-and-beer detail ahead of it.
