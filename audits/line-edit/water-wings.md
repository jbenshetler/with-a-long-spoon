# Line edit — water-wings (2026-08-04)

**Verdict:** 7 findings — 2 overwork, 2 precision, 1 repetition, 2 pre-ruled FIX-AT (echo ledger). No rhythm or dialogue findings.

The chapter's own high bar: the underwater paragraphs ("The room switched off… Something in her chest that had stood at attention all afternoon quietly sat down"; "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken"), Cassie's held-down kindness ("She'd kept it for Vee"), and the closing sentence ("The body that was such a problem stayed up on the deck with the rolled towel, where it could wait"). The paired "She put her face on" / "come back with her face on wrong" is a designed bookend and is not flagged. Everything below is measured against those passages.

## Findings

1. **"Cassie did not go to a pool to be admired; she'd sooner put her boxy frame in a t-shirt and cut-offs. Nobody was going to look at Cassie in it. Cassie knew that, and it didn't bother her one bit."** (¶1) — *overwork*. Three sentences make the same point (Cassie is unlooked-at and unbothered) that the fought-into navy suit already made behaviorally; the last sentence in particular restates what "she wore it because it had a job to do" showed. Cut or fold one of the last two sentences so the paragraph ends one beat earlier.

2. **"the old, nostalgic smell of chlorine and floor cleaner"** (¶2, Vee on the wet tile) — *precision*. "Nostalgic" is an evaluative label doing work the scene does concretely two lines later (the suit worn at sixteen, synchronized swimming); "old" alone carries it. Cut "nostalgic".

3. **"Then, further along, lazy as a summer breeze: 'Legs for *days*.'"** (bleacher walk) — *precision*. "Lazy as a summer breeze" is stock simile in a chapter whose other similes are exact and earned ("like a woman leaning on a fence"); the register deserves better than a postcard phrase at the second catcall. Replace or drop the simile, keeping "lazy" if wanted.

4. **"fifty yards of held breath going out at once"** (Cassie's "Tossers" beat) — *pre-ruled, FIX-AT (ledger item 6, "a held breath")*. This is an occurrence of the spent held-breath metaphor; note it is a strong, transformed instance ("fifty yards of") and a plausible keeper — flag to the reviewer for the keeper/vary decision rather than automatic varying.

5. **"She stayed until her lungs asked, and a beat past that"** (end of the underwater section) — *pre-ruled, FIX-AT (ledger item 2, "a beat past", keep 2–3 strongest)*. This occurrence sits at the chapter's emotional center and reads as a candidate keeper; reviewer to rule against the other seven occurrences.

6. **"gone in the swallowing green" / "in the green and the quiet and the old uncomplicated belonging" / "still half in the green"** (three occurrences across the dive, the stay, and the surfacing) — *repetition*. Green-as-the-underwater-state is clearly a designed motif and the first and last occurrences frame it well, but the middle one, inside an already list-heavy sentence, spends the word without adding a state change. Cut "in the green and" from the middle occurrence (or otherwise thin that list) so the motif lands twice, at entry and exit.

7. **"Not hard, not asking; just seeing… She didn't ask. Cassie never asked — asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger."** (Cassie watching Vee surface) — *overwork*. "Ask" appears four times in three sentences; "She didn't ask" restates both the "not asking" before it and the "never asked" after it, blunting the aphorism that is the paragraph's real payload. Cut "She didn't ask."

Also reviewed and deliberately not flagged: "That was the genius of it — outrage out of all proportion to the actual amount of water, which was the point" — "which was the point" flirts with explaining a landed joke, but "the genius" and "the point" name different things (the design vs. the disproportion) and the doubled insistence suits the splash-fight register; left standing.
