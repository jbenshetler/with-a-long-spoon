## Findings

### 1. `:47` / `:51` — the scheduling asymmetry is delivered twice — **Rhythm (structural)**

`:47` ends: "The year would come in with Randi already penciled into it — school back, the brunches back, texts and phone calls — and Pace a blank, an empty week wherever she tried to set him, nothing scheduled, nothing promised, no place she could be sure of him." `:51` opens: "She'd have Randi back the day the term started… And Pace came with none of that: no hour she could name, no room he'd surely be in. A day she could circle for the one and nothing at all for the other." Same argument, same Randi-then-Pace order, same closing run of negations, four lines apart with only `:49` between. `:51` is the better version (it has the circled day, and it is where the chapter wants to end), so `:47` spends the payoff early and makes the last paragraph read as recap. `:47`'s true last sentence is "and did not believe it now any more than she had the last time she'd made herself say it," a clean scene-out into `:49`'s poppers.

**Recommendation:** cut `:47`'s closing clause from "The year would come in…" to the paragraph's end, letting `:51` own the asymmetry unopposed.

### 2. `:49` — "She had no one to turn to." — **Overwork**

Sits between "and then the year was over" and the whole dramatized reach for his arms. Six flat words stating the thing the next sentence enacts — the only abstract summary in a paragraph otherwise built entirely of body and image.

**Recommendation:** cut; the poppers should hand straight to "She lay still with her feet at the footboard."

### 3. `:49` — "the strong **warm weight** of them" — **Pre-ruled echo (FIX-AT #5)**

Ruling #5 protects the sensory move and rations the exact three-word string; the Vol-1 keeper is `the-bench`. The ledger (line 116) names `old-acquaintances` as ruled "on its own merits at its review" — so this is presented, not forced. The doubled modifier "strong warm" is soft on its own, and "his arms… drawing her back into him" already carries the weight without naming it.

**Recommendation:** vary or cut the three-word string; cutting is available and improves the line.

### 4. `:7` — "Vee's bedroom chilled at night because her parents were careful with money and at night you burrowed under the blankets." — **Word precision / rhythm**

"At night" twice in nineteen words, and the second clause is not a consequence of the first the way the "because" chain promises — the burrowing is separate childhood knowledge shackled to a thermostat.

**Recommendation:** drop the second "at night" and let the burrowing stand as its own beat.

### 5. `:9` — "…and the year had forty minutes left with each one creeping by her." — **Rhythm**

A sentence that has already landed twice (the dark hall, the furnace in savings mode) picks up a fifth clause whose "each one" reaches back past two nouns for its antecedent. The clock is the paragraph's real point but arrives as an afterthought hung off a semicolon chain.

**Recommendation:** break the clock off as its own short sentence rather than trailing it on the furnace clause.

### 6. `:13` — "She'd been breathing bigger air for months, and come home to a place that no longer fit her." — **Overwork (low confidence)**

The paragraph's physical last image, "but now, on her back, the ceiling came down close over her," has already made the whole argument; the following sentence explains it and then restates it a second way ("no longer fit her"), which is also the chapter's closing figure at `:51` ("the bed her legs had outgrown years ago, small in it again"), spent early. The line is handsome and in-register, so this is for the author's eye, not urged.

**Recommendation:** consider ending the paragraph on "the ceiling came down close over her."

### 7. `:13` / `:15` — "fit" three times in two paragraphs — **Within-chapter repetition**

"a place that no longer fit her" (`:13`), then "no answer that would fit in this house. She was no longer sure she had one that fit anywhere" (`:15`). The `:15` pair is designed and is the good one; `:13`'s occurrence makes the reader hear the pair as a tic. Resolved if finding 6 is applied.

**Recommendation:** if `:13`'s last sentence stands, vary "fit" there; otherwise the cut in finding 6 clears it.

### 8. `:19` — "…out where a whole room could see, **which had never happened before**." — **Overwork**

"For once" (two words earlier) and "which had never happened before" carry the same information; the trailing clause glosses the phrase it follows.

**Recommendation:** cut the trailing clause.

### 9. `:19` / `:21` — totalizing phrases cluster (with a handed/handled collision) — **Within-chapter repetition**

Within two paragraphs: "All of her handed to the frame at once," "she had every part of it handled," "She'd sent that one to Pace whole, all of her," "She looked at all of it a while" (`:19`), then "wanted it, every part of it" (`:21`) — roughly five *all-of-it / every-part-of-it* totalizers, and "handed… handled" land adjacent as a near-homophone. "All of her handed to the frame at once" is the load-bearing one (the picture's meaning) and should stay; the surrounds dilute it.

**Recommendation:** keep "All of her handed to the frame at once," thin one of the two "every part of it," and break the handed/handled adjacency.

### 10. `:25` — "Nothing came back. The minutes went by and nothing came…" — **Within-chapter repetition**

Two sentences, the same verb phrase, no design in the doubling; the paragraph's real move is the set-down/pick-up/set-down and the empty field.

**Recommendation:** cut "Nothing came back." and open on the minutes, or vary the second occurrence.

### 11. `:27` — publicness stated three times before its designed pair — **Overwork**

"in front of everyone," then "where everyone could see," plus "no one to know" and the "behind his door" / "behind a shut door" pair four lines apart. The last two sentences ("Randi lived where everyone could see. Vee kept the best of hers behind a shut door.") are the designed close and are excellent; the earlier "in front of everyone" pre-empts them.

**Recommendation:** cut "in front of everyone" and vary one of the two "door" phrases; let the closing pair land clean. (Secondary: even the closing aphorism restates a public/private split the paragraph has already dramatized — surfaced for judgment, since the two crisp sentences may be the earned register.)

### 12. `:21` — "Randi's feed was another country and dressed like another world." — **Overwork / word precision**

Two figures for one idea in eleven words; "another country" is the sharp one and "dressed like another world" softens rather than extends it, fighting the paragraph's otherwise hard-edged inventory (goggles, chalet windows, bracelet, solitaire).

**Recommendation:** keep one of the two clauses.

### 13. `:33` — "Vee thought about how fun her brunches with Randi were." — **Word precision / rhythm**

The one sentence written below its own register: a filter verb ("Vee thought about") the chapter uses nowhere else, an abstract intensifier ("how fun"), no image, and a name collision with the prior sentence ("write back to Vee. Vee thought"). The surrounding gush ("wonderful," "amazing," "devastatingly handsome billionaire") is Vee's borrowed vocabulary and earns its flatness; this one doesn't, because it reports her rather than voicing her.

**Recommendation:** render the brunch thought as a concrete detail in her voice, or cut, letting "Randi, the only girl she could talk to about real things" land directly.

### 14. `:45` — "The floor was gone. There was nothing under the thought at all." — **Overwork**

The second sentence explains the first, and the first already restates "they went straight through her hands" one clause earlier — three renderings of unsupportedness in one breath, where the chapter's best paragraphs use one.

**Recommendation:** cut one of the two closing sentences (bare "The floor was gone." is the stronger scene-out).
