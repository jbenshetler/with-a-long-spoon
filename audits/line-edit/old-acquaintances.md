**Verdict: 14 findings** — 1 pre-ruled (FIX-AT #5), 3 rhythm, 4 within-chapter repetition, 4 overwork, 2 word precision; 0 dialogue-shape. A strong chapter; nearly all findings are subtraction, and one is structural (the closing asymmetry is delivered twice).

## Findings

### 1. `:47` / `:51` — the scheduling asymmetry is delivered twice — **Rhythm (structural)**

`:47` ends: "The year would come in with Randi already penciled into it — school back, the brunches back, texts and phone calls — and Pace a blank, an empty week wherever she tried to set him, nothing scheduled, nothing promised, no place she could be sure of him." `:51` opens: "She'd have Randi back the day the term started — the one sure thing the new year held… And Pace came with none of that: no hour she could name, no room he'd surely be in. A day she could circle for the one and nothing at all for the other."

Same argument, same Randi-then-Pace order, same closing run of negations, four lines apart with only `:49` between. The second is the better version (it has the circled day, and it is where the chapter wants to end), so the first spends the payoff early and makes the last paragraph read as recap. `:47`'s true last sentence is "and did not believe it now any more than she had the last time she'd made herself say it" — a clean scene-out into `:49`'s poppers.

**Recommendation:** cut the closing clause of `:47` from "The year would come in…" to the end of the paragraph, letting `:51` own the asymmetry unopposed.

### 2. `:49` — "She had no one to turn to." — **Overwork**

Sits between "and then the year was over" and the whole dramatized reach for his arms. It states in six flat words the thing the next sentence enacts, and it is the only abstract summary in a paragraph otherwise built entirely of body and image.

**Recommendation:** cut the sentence; the poppers should hand straight to "She lay still with her feet at the footboard."

### 3. `:49` — "the strong **warm weight** of them" — **Pre-ruled: echo-rulings FIX-AT #5**

Ruling #5 protects the sensory move and rations the exact three-word rendering; the Vol-1 keeper is `the-bench`, and `old-acquaintances` is named explicitly as an occurrence that "varies or cuts at [its] chapter's review."

**Recommendation:** vary or cut the three-word string here — cutting is available, since "his arms… drawing her back into him" already carries the weight without naming it (and the doubled modifier "strong warm" is itself soft).

### 4. `:13` — "She'd been breathing bigger air for months, and come home to a place that no longer fit her." — **Overwork**

The paragraph's true last sentence is "but now, on her back, the ceiling came down close over her" — a physical fact that has already made the whole argument. The following sentence explains that image and then restates it a second way ("no longer fit her"), which is also the chapter's closing figure at `:51` ("the bed her legs had outgrown years ago, small in it again"), spent early.

**Recommendation:** end the paragraph on "the ceiling came down close over her."

### 5. `:13` / `:15` — "fit" three times in two paragraphs — **Within-chapter repetition**

"a place that no longer fit her" (`:13`), then "no answer that would fit in this house. She was no longer sure she had one that fit anywhere" (`:15`). The `:15` pair is designed and is the good one; `:13`'s is the accident that makes the reader hear the pair as a tic.

**Recommendation:** resolved by finding 4 — cutting `:13`'s last sentence leaves the `:15` pair clean.

### 6. `:7` — "Vee's bedroom chilled at night because her parents were careful with money and at night you burrowed under the blankets." — **Word precision / rhythm**

"At night" twice in nineteen words, and the second clause is not a consequence of the first the way the "because" chain promises — it is a separate, warmer piece of childhood knowledge grammatically shackled to a thermostat.

**Recommendation:** drop the second "at night" and let the burrowing stand as its own beat.

### 7. `:9` — "…and the year had forty minutes left with each one creeping by her." — **Rhythm**

A four-clause sentence that has already landed twice (the dark hall, the furnace in savings mode) picks up a fifth clause whose "each one" reaches back past two nouns for its antecedent. The clock is the paragraph's real point and arrives as an afterthought hung off a semicolon chain.

**Recommendation:** break the clock off as its own short sentence rather than trailing it on the furnace clause.

### 8. `:21` — "Randi's feed was another country and dressed like another world." — **Overwork / word precision**

Two figures for one idea in eleven words, the second weaker and near-synonymous with the first; "another country" is the sharp one and "dressed like another world" softens rather than extends it. (It also fights the paragraph's method, which is otherwise pure hard-edged inventory: goggles, chalet windows, bracelet, solitaire.)

**Recommendation:** keep one of the two clauses.

### 9. `:19` — "…and for once it was out where a whole room could see, **which had never happened before**." — **Overwork**

"For once" and "which had never happened before" are the same information; the trailing clause is a gloss on the phrase two words earlier.

**Recommendation:** cut the trailing clause.

### 10. `:19` — "All of her handed to the frame at once." / "She'd sent that one to Pace whole, all of her." — **Within-chapter repetition**

The same phrase two sentences apart, and the second is where it matters (it sets up the cropped version she sends her mother). The first occurrence dulls it.

**Recommendation:** vary or cut "all of her" in the earlier sentence, keeping the phrase for the sending.

### 11. `:25` — "Nothing came back. The minutes went by and nothing came…" — **Within-chapter repetition**

Two sentences, the same verb phrase, no design in the doubling; the paragraph's real move is the set-down/pick-up/set-down and the empty field.

**Recommendation:** cut "Nothing came back." and open on the minutes, or vary the second occurrence.

### 12. `:27` — "the whole lit spectacle of a life lived out loud, **in front of everyone**" … "Randi lived where everyone could see." — **Overwork**

Publicness is stated three times in the paragraph's last four lines ("in front of everyone," "where everyone could see," and by contrast "no one to know"), plus "behind his door" and "behind a shut door" four lines apart. The last two sentences are the designed pair and are excellent; the earlier statements pre-empt them.

**Recommendation:** cut "in front of everyone" and vary one of the two doors.

### 13. `:33` — "Vee thought about how fun her brunches with Randi were." — **Word precision / rhythm**

The one sentence in the chapter written below its own register: a filter verb ("Vee thought about") the chapter uses nowhere else, an abstract intensifier ("how fun"), and no image. The surrounding gush ("wonderful," "amazing," "devastatingly handsome billionaire") reads as Vee's borrowed vocabulary and earns its flatness; this sentence doesn't, because it isn't in her mouth — it's a summary of her.

**Recommendation:** recast as a concrete brunch detail or cut, letting "Randi, the only girl she could talk to about real things" land directly.

### 14. `:45` — "The floor was gone. There was nothing under the thought at all." — **Overwork**

The second sentence explains the first — and the first is already the second time the paragraph has said it ("they went straight through her hands"). Three renderings of unsupportedness in one breath, where the chapter's best paragraphs use one.

**Recommendation:** cut one of the two closing sentences (the bare "The floor was gone." is the stronger scene-out).
