# Line edit — all-the-time (2026-08-07)

**Verdict: 25 findings** — 9 within-chapter repetition, 7 overwork, 3 cross-chapter echo, 3 word precision, 2 rhythm, 1 dialogue shape (3 of these are pre-ruled ledger items: #8b at `:55`, ruling #30 on *delighted*, ruling #21 on `a beat past`).

The chapter is at its best when Vee talks. The aria at `:37`–`:41` ("He takes *forever*… I don't know how one person is both") and its scared coda at `:45` ("I've got sex brain. I used to be a person with thoughts") are the most alive prose in the chapter — speech that compresses, self-corrects, and outruns her. Randi's absolution at `:51` earns its solemnity and then breaks it correctly ("…Does he have a brother?"). The yolk paragraph at `:55` is the chapter's one great piece of narration. `:57`'s "Like I'd been eating with the sound off" and `:71`'s "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached" are the two lines the chapter is built to deliver, and both land. `:89` ("And to.") is the best beat of dialogue shape in the book's SATC register — Randi finishing nothing and getting everything. The close at `:141` is right, and right-length. Nearly every finding below is the narration crowding one of these, not the dialogue.

1. **`:7`** — "with the glare still swimming in her eyes and could make out almost nothing, the whole room gone to dark after all that white sky." **Overwork.** Three renderings of one blindness inside a single sentence; the third ("all that white sky") is the only one that supplies new information. *Recommendation: cut one of the first two clauses.*

2. **`:15`** — "The smell **reached** her before the booth did: … a smell that **reached** the stomach before the eyes." **Within-chapter repetition.** Same verb twice in one sentence, and "smell" twice; the second construction is the good one, so the first is what gives. Compounded by `:79`'s "of course it had reached her." *Recommendation: change the verb or cut the first clause and let the list arrive bare.*

3. **`:17` / `:21`** — "because they toasted before they drank" … then "they always touched the rims first, some small rite neither of them had ever proposed out loud, no words to it." **Overwork.** The ritual is explained twice, four lines apart, and the second explanation doubles itself: "never proposed out loud" and "no words to it" are the same claim. *Recommendation: cut `:17`'s because-clause and the trailing "no words to it."*

4. **`:19`** — the paragraph continues past its true last sentence: "That was the luck of it. She still couldn't get over being the one Randi picked." **Rhythm.** Two consecutive closers for one thought; "That was the luck of it" is the harder, quieter one. Within the same paragraph, "Vee pulled in two and glad of it, wanted this hard at both ends at once" states the tug-of-war a third time after "the happy tug-of-war" and "the man wanting all of her and the friend wanting her piece." *Recommendation: end the paragraph one sentence earlier and drop the third statement of the tug-of-war.*

5. **`:23`** — "felt the old caution give, and ordered the eggs Benedict … and felt the small illicit lift of it — the daring a little easier each time, she was finding." **Overwork.** "Felt" twice in one sentence, and the trailing clause is the narrator explaining an escalation the sentence's own order has just enacted; "she was finding" is an editorial tag. *Recommendation: cut from the dash.*

6. **`:13`** — "warmed, a little, that Randi had even thought to make joining her easy like this." **Word precision.** "Make joining her easy" is a nominalized knot in a sentence otherwise moving; the courtesy is already fully shown by the hostess knowing the name. *Recommendation: compress to the warmth and cut the gloss on why.*

7. **`:37` / `:45`** — "It's the opposite of that." / "It's the opposite." **Dialogue shape / repetition.** The identical reversal-formula, eight lines apart, introducing the same rhetorical move (expectation, then flip). It reads as reflex rather than voiceprint because the surrounding speech is so various. *Recommendation: vary the second.*

8. **`:55` — PRE-RULED (ledger #8b).** "her hand had gone back for more before she'd decided anything." Logged as an unruled occurrence of the `before s/he'd decided` family. Raised here for the author's ruling. The line is strong and the beat is the chapter's best narration, so it has a real claim on a keeper slot — but see #9: it is one of five in this chapter.

9. **Body-before-mind local density — `:55`, `:91`, `:95`, `:103`, `:135`.** **Within-chapter repetition** (ruling #24: the construction is protected, local density is the issue). Five instances: "before she'd decided anything" (`:55`), "that she caught only after it had happened" (`:91`), "before she'd quite decided to" (`:95`), "before the rest of her had voted" (`:103`), "She did it before she'd thought" (`:135`). `:95` and `:103` are eight lines apart and both about the same object — the phone — which is the collision that matters. `:103`'s "voted" is the freshest figure in the set; `:135`'s "before she'd thought" is the flattest. *Recommendation: keep `:103` and `:55`, and cut or recast `:95` and `:135`.*

10. **`:95` / `:101` / `:103`** — "turned the screen **across** the table" … "slid the phone **back across**" … "turned the phone **back across**." **Within-chapter repetition.** Three *across* in nine lines and "back across" in consecutive paragraphs; the phone's traverse is the scene's escalation ladder, so the identical rendering flattens what should climb. *Recommendation: vary the middle instance so the three crossings differ.*

11. **`:63` / `:93`** — "Randi turned her glass a slow quarter-turn and back and said nothing for a second, letting Vee have it." / "Randi turned her glass once and let the word be answered." **Within-chapter repetition — the SAME gesture rendered twice**, thirty lines apart, doing the identical work (a hand-motion holding a silence open). Per the method in ruling #38, this is a free beat, not a structural one. *Recommendation: cut or replace the second; `:93`'s silence can be carried by the bare repeated word.*

12. **`:81`** — "the **beam** came up full again, and she was herself, all of her, the grin arriving **like a room light**." **Cross-chapter echo + within-sentence doubling.** Two lamp figures in one sentence; the beam is the protected attention-as-lamp (#18) and should stand alone, while "like a room light" is near-verbatim `how-its-done:45` ("turned the full warm beam of her attention on, all at once, like a room light coming on") — the same simile on the same character. *Recommendation: keep the beam, drop the room-light simile.*

13. **"delighted" ×4 — `:25`, `:85`, `:115`, `:117`. PRE-RULED (ledger #30).** Four uses on Randi in one chapter, and **not one has delight as the grammatical subject doing something** — all are adverbial labels pinned to speech or a movement Randi's own dialogue already performs ("Tell me why, so I can grab some"; "Of course he does"; "Showing him your toes, eh?"). The ruling's test cuts all four. *Recommendation: cut `:85` and `:115` outright, keep at most one of `:25`/`:117`.*

14. **The grin as beat-marker — `:51`, `:81`, `:107` (twice), `:115`.** **Within-chapter repetition.** "the grin came back" / "the grin arriving" / "The grin came." / "Randi grinned and let her" / "the grin came slow." `:107` carries two seven words apart. `:69`'s "no grin, no line to cap it" is load-bearing (it defines the register by its absence) and should be protected; the free instances around it are what dull it. *Recommendation: cut one of `:107`'s two.*

15. **`:105` vs `:135`** — "past a glance, **past what a verdict needed**" / "close, steady, a beat **past what a goodbye needed**." **Within-chapter repetition / echo.** The same frame twice, both marking Randi holding longer than the social form allows — the chapter's central tell, spent twice thirty lines apart. `:135` is ledger #21's **presumptive keeper** for the book-wide "a beat past" slate. *Recommendation: recast `:105` so `:135` arrives unspent.*

16. **`:135` vs `one-bite:155`** — "the whole warm length of her, that bright green scent Vee had **stopped trying to name**" / one-bite's "the whole warm length of her and that bright green scent Vee had **never been able to** name." **Cross-chapter echo — the headline finding.** Near-verbatim, same beat (Randi gathering her in at the goodbye), and *both* then run "a beat past what a ___ needed/would" — the two occurrences ledger #21 nominates as its two presumptive keepers are the same sentence written twice. One Bite comes first in chronology. *Recommendation: raise to the author as a paired ruling — one of the two goodbyes must be rendered in different language, and only one can hold the "a beat past" slot.*

17. **`:75` / `:79`** — "It stopped Vee — she had never watched a story land on Randi like this, never seen it reach that far in." then "of course it had reached her." **Overwork.** Vee's misread is stated three times: the visible catch in `:75`, the doubled interior clause, then the whole of `:79` explaining it again. The metaphor is explained after it lands. *Recommendation: cut "never seen it reach that far in."*

18. **`:43`** — "…and Vee felt herself open under it like she always did, **the best friend she'd ever had**." **Overwork.** A narrator label appended to a paragraph that has just demonstrated the thing; and `:117` makes the same claim at length seventy lines later. *Recommendation: cut the tag.*

19. **`:117`** — "She'd never had this before: a friend she could hand all of it to… **The one friend she trusted with all of it. There was no one else, anywhere, she could have shown.**" **Rhythm / overwork.** The paragraph lands on "leave the good parts out" and then says the same thing twice more; "all of it" appears three times here and again at `:113`. *Recommendation: end the paragraph at "leave the good parts out."*

20. **`:135`** — "She did it before she'd thought. She noticed it, and decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." **Overwork.** Interiority restating the action the preceding clause just showed, and it sits *between* two physical beats (the held kiss, Randi's drawing back) whose rhythm it interrupts. See also #9. *Recommendation: cut both sentences and let the kiss run into Randi's drawing back.*

21. **`:139`** — "Stats was blue academic lighting and **public and full of other people**." **Word precision.** "Public" and "full of other people" are the same term twice, and "academic lighting" is an abstraction where the concrete (the fluorescents) is available. *Recommendation: cut one of the doubled terms.*

22. **"small" ×9 — `:19`, `:21`, `:23`, `:41`, `:75`, `:91`, `:109`, `:131`, and the diminutive at `:135`.** **Within-chapter repetition.** The book's default intensifier at nine uses in a short chapter, and it intersects the unruled "the small ___" family (#3). Several are doing no work: "the small news of Vee's life," "some small rite," "the small illicit lift," "the small pride of it." *Recommendation: thin the three weakest.*

23. **"warm" ×6 — `:13`, `:15`, `:47`, `:79`, `:85`, `:135`.** **Within-chapter repetition.** "warm bread," "warm as a bath," "went warm," "said, warm," "the whole warm length of her." `:47` is the best of them and `:85` the emptiest (an adverbial label, same problem as #13). *Recommendation: cut `:85`'s and one of the narration's.*

24. **`:57`** — "all his attention down on the one thing" … "all of that focus is pointed at making me something good" … "I've never had anyone's whole attention like that." **Overwork.** Three statements of attention inside one speech, and *attention* is then the announced subject of `:71` ("And the *attention*"), where it should arrive as new. This is Vee circling in speech, which is register — but the third sentence is the one that pre-spends `:71`. *Recommendation: cut the middle statement.*

25. **`:91`** — "her body gave one small rock in the seat, **forward and back**, that she caught only after it had happened." **Word precision.** "Forward and back" over-specifies a rock — the verb already contains the motion — and the doubled modifier slows a beat whose whole point is that it was faster than her. *Recommendation: cut "forward and back."*
