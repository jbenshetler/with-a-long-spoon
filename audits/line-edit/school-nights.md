# Line edit — school-nights (2026-08-11)

**Verdict: 11 findings** — 5 within-chapter repetition, 3 rhythm, 2 overwork, 2 precision, 1 echo candidate (the "held breath" pre-ruling is now **void** — see #1). No dialogue-shape findings; the speech is the chapter's best-tuned element.

*(Supersedes the 2026-08-07 pass. The one substantive change: finding #1 — echo-ledger ruling #6 was re-ruled 2026-08-08, voiding the "a held breath" FIX-AT pre-ruling; it is presented here neutrally, not as an item to apply.)*

The chapter is short, warm, and mostly does what it means to. Its strongest passages are the Cassie dialogue throughout — "You're a public utility," the "Mm," said Cassie, which was Cassie for whatever you say gloss, and the close ("She didn't say a thing against it, and she didn't say I know, only went back to her book. 'Go to sleep, streetlight.'"), which lands the whole scene's argument without touching it. The three-beat *How he cooked / How he watched / How he noticed* anaphora (`:13`) is the best rhythmic move in the piece, and the "fine" triple in the confession paragraph (`:25`) — "the small word, fine … had been fine … Pace wasn't fine" — is the chapter's cleanest hammer. The "With not enough on" / "she never once thought about how much she had on" callback across `:15`–`:17`, the cold/warmth opposition, and the book-turned-facedown (`:11`) / reopened (`:31`) frame are all designed and protected. The findings below are almost all local friction in the narration between those good things.

## Findings

1. **`:17` — "the house stayed warm as a held breath now"** — *Echo candidate / per-instance quality; NOT pre-ruled.* Echo-ledger ruling #6 (re-ruled 2026-08-08) **voids** the former FIX-AT pre-ruling on this line: there is no density case (one per ~23,000 words across Vol. 1) and no quota. The only live question is staleness — "a held breath" is a worn figure — which is a per-instance quality call, not a repetition fix. **Recommendation: author's discretion on whether the simile earns its place in this aside; no action required on echo grounds. If varied, keep the warmth-as-permission sense that sets up "never once thought about how much she had on."**

2. **`:11` — "Cassie turned the book facedown and looked, the dry held a beat and then given over, glad."** — *Precision / rhythm.* "the dry" as a bare substantive doesn't resolve, and the clause switches from active ("held") to bare participle ("given over") mid-breath, so the sentence stalls exactly where it should turn. **Recommendation: name what is held and keep one grammatical spine across the two halves.**

3. **`:13` — "…the shape her weeks had taken, which was the truer thing now, the nights all running into each other."** — *Overwork.* The sentence already delivers the substitution of weeks for one night; "which was the truer thing now" is the narration grading its own move, and the third clause then re-explains it. **Recommendation: cut one of the two trailing clauses — "the nights all running into each other" is the one that earns its place.**

4. **`:13` — "She told her about the soup…"** — *Rhythm.* The paragraph's *How he… How he… How he…* run reaches a natural crest and then continues into a fully narrated anecdote of comparable length, burying the anaphora's peak mid-block. **Recommendation: break the soup story into its own paragraph so the triad closes on its third beat.**

5. **`:13` / `:17` — "never once out of a box" / "she never once thought about how much she had on"** — *Repetition.* "Never once" twice in four lines, both in narration, no design; the second is load-bearing (it pays off Cassie's dry `:15`). **Recommendation: vary the first.**

6. **`:19` / `:27` — "let Vee run herself out" / "Cassie watched her run clean out of words"** — *Repetition.* The same figure (Vee running out of talk) rendered twice eight lines apart, with the second doing the real work — it earns "which Vee never did." **Recommendation: vary or cut the first.**

7. **`:25` — "…thinking that was the whole of what there was to have. … at the whole of himself turned on her…"** — *Repetition (also echo candidate).* Two "the whole of" constructions in one paragraph, close and both weight-bearing; "the whole of himself" is the keeper. **Recommendation: vary the first ("the whole of what there was to have").** See Echo candidates for the book-wide "whole of it" cadence.

8. **`:25` / `:29` — "at the size of it" / "because that still wasn't the size of it"** — *Repetition.* Likely intended as a callback, but the intervening distance is only four lines and the second does new work (the inadequacy of "maybe"), so the repeat reads as reach rather than return. **Recommendation: keep `:29` and find another measure-figure at `:25`, or accept it explicitly as designed.**

9. **`:25` — "She reached for the word after it and didn't have one, and laughed at herself reaching, and put both hands up at the size of it instead…"** — *Rhythm / repetition.* Three coordinated clauses on one "and" chain, with reached/reaching inside eleven words; the sentence then runs on into a four-item list and a closing image, so the best beat ("Pace wasn't fine.") is immediately followed by the chapter's longest sentence. **Recommendation: break the chain — the laugh at herself is a sentence, not a third clause.**

10. **`:25` — "until the rest of the room went quiet and dark around her"** — *Precision.* Doubled modifiers where one would cut harder, and "around her" is the second positional tag in the same sentence (after "turned on her"). **Recommendation: drop one adjective.**

11. **`:19` / `:23` / `:27` — "mild" / "Kindly, though." / "the same flat way"** — *Overwork.* Cassie's delivery is labelled three times in nine lines, and the dialogue already performs each register; "the same flat way she'd said the rest of it" additionally asks the reader to recall a flatness the earlier tags called *mild* and *kindly*. **Recommendation: cut one tag, and reconcile "flat" with what the earlier tags established.**

*Also noted, rhythm:* `:13` / `:25` / `:29` open with paragraph-initial **"And Vee"** three times in a short chapter (ledger #14, local-pileup case); the `:29` instance is strongest (its relative clause turns it). Consider recasting one of the first two. And Cassie's **"let"** constructions cluster three times — "let Vee run herself out" (`:19`), "let it sit" (`:27`), "let the maybe stand" (`:31`) — but all render one characterizing restraint and read as a deliberate mirror of Pace's PROTECTED "he let her" register; logged, not recommended for change. "cracked its two inches" (`:5`) is PROTECTED and untouched.
