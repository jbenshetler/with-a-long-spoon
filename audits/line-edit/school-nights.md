# Line edit — school-nights (2026-08-07)

**Verdict: 11 findings** — 1 pre-ruled (echo ledger), 4 within-chapter repetition, 2 rhythm, 2 overwork, 2 word precision. No dialogue-shape findings; the speech is the chapter's best-tuned element.

The chapter is short, warm, and mostly does what it means to. Its strongest passages are the Cassie dialogue throughout — "You're a public utility," the "Mm," said Cassie, which was Cassie for whatever you say gloss, and the close ("She didn't say a thing against it, and she didn't say I know, only went back to her book. 'Go to sleep, streetlight.'"), which lands the whole scene's argument without touching it. The three-beat *How he cooked / How he watched / How he noticed* anaphora is the best rhythmic move in the piece, and the "With not enough on" / "she never once thought about how much she had on" pairing across :15–:17 is a designed callback that should be protected. The findings below are almost all local friction in the narration between those good things.

## Findings

1. **:17 — "the house stayed warm as a held breath now"** — *Pre-ruled (echo ledger #6 / #29).* This is the named remaining Vol-1 "a held breath" occurrence; keeper is `standards.md:141`. Per ruling #29, prefer a replacement that earns something later in the chapter (the warmth/clothing thread at :17 is the obvious hook) over a like-for-like swap. **Recommendation: vary the figure, keeping the warmth-as-permission sense that sets up "never once thought about how much she had on."**

2. **:11 — "Cassie turned the book facedown and looked, the dry held a beat and then given over, glad."** — *Word precision / rhythm.* "the dry" as a bare substantive doesn't resolve, and the clause switches from active ("held") to bare participle ("given over") mid-breath, so the sentence stalls exactly where it should turn. **Recommendation: name what is held and keep one grammatical spine across the two halves.**

3. **:13 — "…the shape her weeks had taken, which was the truer thing now, the nights all running into each other."** — *Overwork.* The sentence already delivers the substitution of weeks for one night; "which was the truer thing now" is the narration grading its own move, and the third clause then re-explains it a second time. **Recommendation: cut one of the two trailing clauses — the concrete "the nights all running into each other" is the one that earns its place.**

4. **:13 — "She told her about the soup…"** — *Rhythm.* The paragraph's *How he… How he… How he…* run reaches a natural end and then the paragraph continues into a fully narrated anecdote of comparable length, so the anaphora's crest is buried mid-block. **Recommendation: break the soup story into its own paragraph so the triad closes on its third beat.**

5. **:13 — "never once out of a box" / :17 — "she never once thought about how much she had on"** — *Within-chapter repetition.* "Never once" twice in four lines, both in narration, no design; the second is the load-bearing one (it pays off Cassie's dry ":15"). **Recommendation: vary the first.**

6. **:19 — "let Vee run herself out" / :27 — "Cassie watched her run clean out of words"** — *Within-chapter repetition.* The same figure (Vee running out of talk) rendered twice eight lines apart, with the second doing the real work — it earns "which Vee never did." **Recommendation: vary or cut the first.**

7. **:25 — "at the size of it" / :29 — "because that still wasn't the size of it"** — *Within-chapter repetition.* Likely intended as a callback, but the intervening distance is only four lines and the second is doing new work (the inadequacy of "maybe"), so the repeat reads as reach rather than return. **Recommendation: keep :29 and find another measure-figure at :25, or accept it explicitly as designed.**

8. **:25 — "She reached for the word after it and didn't have one, and laughed at herself reaching, and put both hands up at the size of it instead…"** — *Rhythm / repetition.* Three coordinated clauses on one "and" chain, with reached/reaching inside eleven words; the sentence then runs on into a four-item list and a closing image, so the best beat ("Pace wasn't fine.") is followed by the chapter's longest sentence. **Recommendation: break the chain — the laugh at herself is a sentence, not a third clause.**

9. **:25 — "until the rest of the room went quiet and dark around her"** — *Word precision.* Doubled modifiers where one would cut harder, and "around her" is the second "…on her / around her" positional tag in the same sentence (after "turned on her"). **Recommendation: drop one adjective.**

10. **:19 / :23 / :27 — "mild" / "Kindly, though." / "the same flat way"** — *Overwork.* Cassie's delivery is labelled three times in nine lines, and the dialogue already performs each register; "the same flat way she'd said the rest of it" additionally asks the reader to recall a flatness the earlier tags called *mild* and *kindly*. **Recommendation: cut one tag, and reconcile "flat" with what the earlier tags established.**

11. **:13 / :25 / :29 — "And Vee went on…" / "And Vee tried to say it…" / "And Vee, who hadn't stopped talking…"** — *Rhythm (opener pileup).* Three paragraph-initial "And Vee" in a sixteen-line chapter; per ledger #14 this is exactly the local-pileup case. The :29 instance is the strongest (the relative clause turns it). **Recommendation: recast one of the first two.**
