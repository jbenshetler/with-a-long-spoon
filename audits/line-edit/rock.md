8. **`:51`** — "It moved through her more tenderly than she expected. She had not been trying to make him laugh. That was the trouble with him. She could be trying only to tell him a thing, and he would receive it so exactly that the telling itself became a pleasure."
   **Class:** overwork.
   The last sentence is the paragraph's real thought and it is preceded by two sentences that pre-explain it; "It moved through her more tenderly than she expected" is the vaguest line in the chapter's first half, and "That was the trouble with him" telegraphs the reveal it introduces.
   **Recommendation:** cut the tenderness sentence and let the paragraph run from "She had not been trying to make him laugh" to its true last sentence.

9. **`:77`** — "It was only the size of it, flat and factual, next to the warm sprawl of hers, and the difference was there, and it told her something."
   **Class:** overwork / word precision.
   "the warm sprawl of hers" is exact; the two clauses after it retreat into abstraction and tell the reader that a difference the sentence has just *shown* exists and signifies — the weakest ending in the chapter, immediately before one of the strongest lines (`:81`).
   **Recommendation:** end the sentence at "the warm sprawl of hers."

10. **`:151`** — "Carefully; the carefulness seemed to matter, though she could not have said why."
    **Class:** overwork / word precision (nominalization).
    "Carefully." as a one-word sentence is the move; the clause after it explains the gesture the moment it lands, and *carefulness* buries the adverb in an abstract noun. The chapter elsewhere trusts the reader completely with exactly this kind of beat (`:145`, `:85`).
    **Recommendation:** cut everything after "Carefully."

11. **`:173`** — "It was not arrogance in his voice. It was almost grief, and then not grief, because he went on before it could settle into anything so heavy."
    **Class:** overwork.
    Three hedges in two sentences — not arrogance, almost grief, then not grief — and a *because* clause that narrates the paragraph break that follows it. The chapter's better mode is to name one thing and stop.
    **Recommendation:** keep one of the two negations and drop the causal tail; the next paragraph already shows him going on.

12. **`:175`** — "and something in him leaned forward, quiet but lit, and she recognized it as the thing that came into him when he was about to give her something he cared about."
    **Class:** rhythm / overwork.
    "quiet but lit" is the sentence; the trailing clause announces the speech before the speech, so the horn paragraph — the chapter's peak — arrives pre-labeled as important.
    **Recommendation:** end the paragraph at "quiet but lit."

13. **`:245`** — "and stayed, not going anywhere, only being there, present as she had just named it, and the accuracy of it moved through her."
    **Class:** overwork.
    Four consecutive restatements of one fact (his hand stays), then the prose names its own accuracy — after Vee has *just* named it aloud at `:243` ("You're right here. It's the lift"). The interiority is repeating the dialogue. "moved through her" also duplicates `:51`.
    **Recommendation:** cut to the hand settling and staying; drop "present as she had just named it" and the accuracy clause.

14. **`:213`** — "held too level, carried the careful way a person carries a full glass so it won't go over the rim."
    **Class:** word precision.
    The full-glass simile is excellent and the *carried/carries* repetition inside eleven words is the only thing dulling it; "held too level" also anticipates the simile's content, so the line pays twice.
    **Recommendation:** break the verb repetition (one of the two carries) and consider dropping "held too level."

15. **`:195`** — "He said it without self-pity, and that made it worse and better at once."
    **Class:** word precision.
    "worse and better at once" is a placeholder for a feeling the chapter can specify — everywhere else it does (`:69`'s "only the ordinary amount of sad that everything was", `:221`'s "named it luck"). The second half of the paragraph, the picture of him at fifteen, is exact, which makes the abstraction in front of it more visible.
    **Recommendation:** replace the abstraction with the specific reaction, or cut the clause and let the picture do it.

16. **`:55`** — "My mother still calls me Vivienne."
    **Class:** dialogue shape.
    Unattributed, and it follows Pace's `:53`-adjacent turn in a passage where the speakers have been alternating; on a first read it can register as Pace's line for a beat before `:57`'s "Not Vee?" corrects it — a stumble the chapter can't afford at the start of the names exchange, which is where the "name that means love in one mouth" payoff comes from.
    **Recommendation:** anchor the speaker at `:55` (a beat or a tag), without lengthening the exchange.

Also noted, not flagged: `:91`'s double *because* clause ("unmeasured by the usual campus categories … born-where and brother and mother") runs abstract for one clause longer than the chapter's norm, but the closing catalogue earns it; and `:159`/`:217` render the withheld *sorry* twice — that reads as designed escalation (the second withholding is larger and costs more) and should stay unless the author wants the first thinned.
