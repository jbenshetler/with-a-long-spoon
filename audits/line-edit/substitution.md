# Line edit — substitution (2026-08-04)

**Verdict:** 15 findings — 1 pre-ruled (FIX-AT), 1 rhythm, 8 repetition, 3 overwork, 2 precision, 0 dialogue.

**Strongest passages (the calibration bar):** The Euler's exchange (ll. 121–133) — "She hadn't known she was allowed" and "a plain answer with no circle left in it" close the circling motif planted at l. 31 with total economy. The "may I" paragraph (ll. 211–213) and the closing beat (l. 243, the facedown phone she does not turn over, "She let herself want it to light up") end exactly on time. Randi's one-word introduction ("'Randi,' said Randi. One word.") and her coffee-cup pantomime (l. 175 — the cup coming up twice with no sip) show the chapter at its most disciplined: the reader given the tell, nothing underlined. The l. 137 anaphora rush is the right design even where one clause inside it sags (see finding 6).

## Findings

1. **Pre-ruled (FIX-AT, echo ruling #3 — "the small private").** l. 35: "thumbs quick, eyes down, the small private focus of a girl writing to someone who had her attention." One of 7 occurrences book-wide; keep only the strongest across chapters, vary the rest. Apply the ruling here at review — vary the phrasing unless this is chosen as a keeper.

2. **Repetition.** l. 15: "who had her own laptop open … Cassie sat with her own work spread out in her own quiet manner" — "her own" three times in one paragraph without design. Recommend cutting one or two of the possessives; the parallelism isn't earning anything.

3. **Repetition / overwork.** l. 61 "she had been being looked at for what felt like, but could not have been, a long time" and l. 71 "Vee had been being looked at for the four steps it took him to cross the floor" — the same distinctive passive construction, ten lines apart, each time freshly explained. Keep one instance (l. 71 is the stronger, since it sets up "she did not want to do anything that would make the looking stop") and let the other carry the beat without the construction.

4. **Overwork.** l. 63: "He stopped at the corner. He was looking at her." — the second sentence restates l. 61 ("his eyes on her face the whole way … on her") and the corner restates "the corner of their table" two lines up; the paragraph adds no new information. Recommend cutting "He was looking at her" and letting the stop stand alone.

5. **Overwork.** l. 65: "She felt singled out. She felt, absurdly, chosen." — the smile image just did this work ("seemed to have picked her out of the whole day and decided she was the reason"); two interiority sentences restate it, and only the second ("absurdly, chosen") advances anything. Recommend cutting "She felt singled out."

6. **Rhythm.** l. 137: "The path he picked was — she could see this with her econ training, the muscle she had been developing for two years now, the eye for *how a smart person handles a problem they could handle in any of several ways* — was the path of a person who wasn't performing…" — a long credential-establishing aside parked at the paragraph's point of impact, forcing the doubled "was" and stalling the rush the anaphora is building. Recommend shortening the parenthetical to a single clause so the sentence lands on one "was."

7. **Overwork.** l. 149: "Randi's question had gotten the answer it had asked for — a single clean sentence — and the conversation had moved on." — narration restating the exchange the reader just watched, immediately before the italics ("*he answered her well … and he answered me longer*") render the same observation better. Recommend cutting the narrated sentence and going straight to "Vee filed it without filing it."

8. **Repetition.** l. 65 "She only felt the room settle a half-degree around where he stood" vs. l. 87 "the room tilting half a degree toward her" — the same distinctive room-instrument image twice in twenty lines; it may be a designed mirror (his gravity, then her borrowed poise borrowing it), but if so it wants the exact-wording match sharpened or the second one varied — as it stands it reads as reuse, not rhyme. Flag for the author's call.

9. **Repetition.** l. 81 "the courtesy of a stranger acknowledging that there was another person at the table — and at Cassie at the next table over, the same courtesy. Both women returned the brief acknowledgment." — courtesy/acknowledging/acknowledgment three times in one sentence-pair. Recommend cutting "Both women returned the brief acknowledgment" or replacing the abstract noun with the concrete gesture the women actually make.

10. **Repetition.** "in her experience" at l. 161 ("which was the question, in her experience, that nice people asked at parties") and l. 213 ("a word a man her age was not, in her experience, in possession of") — a distinctive hedging aside used twice for the same to-think-about-later register. Keep the l. 213 instance (it belongs to the *may I* beat) and cut the earlier one.

11. **Repetition.** l. 161 "because no one had ever asked her that question" and l. 171 "which she had not yet articulated to anyone in this much detail, because no one had asked" — the same observation, same causal shape, ten lines apart; the second is the payoff and the first pre-spends it. Recommend cutting "because no one had ever asked her that question" from l. 161 (the invented-answer turn carries the point without it).

12. **Repetition.** "small" appears ~20 times across the chapter ("small frustrated curl," "small social registers," "small private focus," "small *huh*," "small *got it* nod," "small wave," "small and tidy," "small unfinished way," "small mental note," "small lift," "small busy-ness," "small sip," "small and stupid," "the small *oh*," "small and formal," "small crossed mark"…) — the uses attached to Pace's economy are doing design work; the rest are default modifier. Recommend thinning the incidental uses so the Pace-adjacent ones keep their charge.

13. **Repetition / precision.** "warm" three times in close succession — l. 87 "Pace's mouth did something warm at the corner," l. 171 "his laugh was warm and short," l. 175 "The smile was warm and unbothered" — the same evaluative adjective for three different characters' three different expressions flattens all three. Recommend varying or cutting one (l. 175's reads weakest, since Randi's smile is already fully specified by the italics before it).

14. **Repetition.** Three chest-events on near-identical chassis: l. 61 "Something in her chest went quiet," l. 203 "The thing that happened in her chest at the glance was small and stupid," l. 223 "Something in her chest was doing what her chest sometimes did when she had taken stairs too fast" — the third (the stairs) is the keeper; recommend varying the seat of one of the first two so the closer doesn't arrive pre-worn. Related: "she felt before she could have named it" (l. 81) and "she could not have named it" (l. 203) — the could-not-name move twice; keep one.

15. **Precision.** l. 213: "She had a fraction of a second to receive it and to respond to it and her body had already responded." — respond/responded doubled inside the sentence; the body-before-mind *shape* is protected (echo ruling #8), but the wording muddies it here. Recommend removing one of the respond forms (e.g., cut "and to respond to it") so the body's pre-emption lands clean.
