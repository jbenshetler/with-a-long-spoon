**Calibration — the chapter's strongest passages.** The measuring sequence (ll. 50–96) is the standard: the closeness/two-touches/withdrawal ritual, with "wrote it down" recurring until the standalone "Wrote it down." (l. 94) pays it off — designed repetition earning its keep. The grin-reversal ("He was grinning at her. … No, not grinning. ## Findings

1. **Pre-ruled (echo ledger #8, FIX-AT):** "before she'd decided to" appears twice — "She was learning the rhythm of it before she'd decided to" (l. 58) and "she was grinning before she'd decided to" (l. 82). The body-before-mind *shape* is protected; the identical five-word wording is what varies (Vol-1 keeper is the-bench:303). **Recommendation:** vary the wording of both occurrences here, preserving action-precedes-decision in each.

2. **Repetition — the eyes-closed-against gesture, identical wording.** "She closed her eyes for a beat against the word" (l. 26) and "She closed her eyes for a beat against it" (l. 92) — same gesture, same "for a beat against" frame, both triggered by the mother's voice. The recurrence of the gesture may be motif; the verbatim frame is not. Vary the rendering of the second so the motif reads as return, not copy.

3. **Repetition — "the fullest part of her" three times in five paragraphs** (l. 70 twice, l. 74). The second use in l. 70 ("here, across the fullest part of her") reads as deliberate echo-within-sentence and can stand; the third (l. 74) dulls all three. Vary or cut the l. 74 instance.

4. **Repetition — "dying over / dying" three times at the climax** ("the place she'd been dying over" l. 162, "the thing she'd been dying over" l. 182, "The whole time she'd been dying" l. 184). Two prepositional-phrase uses with identical shape plus a third within 22 lines; the reveal section is the chapter's best writing and doesn't need the crutch. Keep one "dying over," vary the other two.

5. **Repetition — the "X, of course X" doubling construction used three times** ("of course there was a riser… of course he'd built it" l. 48; "he'd had the water hot, of course he'd had the water hot" l. 102; "Of course he hadn't seen it" l. 116 after "He hadn't seen it"). The construction is a true Vee-voice move but the third use in 70 lines turns it into a visible trick. Keep two (48 and 102 are the strongest); recast the l. 116 doubling.

6. **Repetition — the wanting-with-nowhere-to-put-it image spent three times** ("the wanting it had left stacked up in her with nowhere to go" l. 96; "only now there was nowhere to put it" l. 142; "more wanting worked loose with nowhere on earth to put it" l. 160). L. 96 is the section-closer and earns it; the later two re-spend it with escalating italic pressure ("on earth") that reads as compensation. Vary one of ll. 142/160 into a different rendering of the same pressure.

7. **Repetition — "particular" clustered at l. 134** ("the picture had a particular her in it… The dress had a particular woman in it and the particular woman was her"), on top of "a particular kind… a particular quality" (l. 36 area, l. 26) and "the particular slide" (l. 106). The l. 134 triple may be intentional recursion, but with the earlier uses the word is spent six times in one chapter. Thin the earlier incidental uses (l. 26/106) so the l. 134 recursion keeps its charge.

8. **Overwork — l. 36:** "The main thing was that she was beautiful to him and he was glad. He saw her, fully, and he was glad." The second sentence restates the first; the doubled "and he was glad" is incantation without new information. Cut or compress one of the two sentences.

9. **Precision — l. 42:** the trailing one-word "Wryly." labels the tone the joke ("Except he'd thoughtfully turned up the heat") has already delivered — a metaphor explained after it lands. Cut "Wryly."

10. **Precision — l. 70:** "Being read back by hands that carried that much registered as a small disorienting thing" — after the superbly concrete callus catalogue, the paragraph closes on a nominalized abstraction ("registered as a … thing"). Recast so the close stays in the body, not in commentary; also note "depersonalize" (l. 128) sits outside the chapter's plain-diction register for a moment of this intimacy — replace with the concrete move the next sentence already performs.

11. **Overwork — l. 112:** "It did not stop. It never did. That was the whole problem. That had always been the whole problem." Four beats where two land harder; the doubled "whole problem" close copies the doubling move already used elsewhere (finding 5's construction family). Cut one of the last two sentences.

12. **Overwork — ll. 226–228:** the paragraph after "Keep still" itemizes everything the line meant, and then l. 228 re-itemizes it ("He wasn't telling her to stop… He was holding a line, and letting her hear that it was a line…"). Two paragraphs of interiority glossing a two-word line the reader already felt; compress the two paragraphs into one, keeping "and that the choosing cost him" or "holding it was the hardest thing he'd done since she walked into the too-warm house" but not both explanatory passes.

13. **Overwork / rhythm — l. 250:** "She felt, distantly, that this was going to matter to her later, in ways she couldn't yet see" — the preceding paragraph (l. 248) already withheld the naming ("would not… find words for any time soon"); this sentence tells the reader the future the withholding was protecting, and the chapter's true last beat is l. 252 ("She stood very still and let him work."). Cut the l. 250 sentence so the ending arrives one beat sooner.

14. **Repetition — five stomach beats as the register of every turn** ("Her stomach dropped a little" l. 22; "her stomach did something small" l. 74; "Her stomach went cold" l. 108; "It landed low in her stomach and stayed" l. 158; "her stomach contracted" l. 238). Each is individually fine; as the chapter's only somatic address for shock it becomes a tell. Vary the site or the rendering for one or two of the middle instances (74 and 238 are the most routine).
