## Findings

1. **"most of the way to a decision her body had already made" (¶1) / "both of them already most of the way to somewhere" (¶4).** Class: repetition. The same distinctive measure-phrase twice inside the opening page, undesigned — the second occurrence dilutes the first's arrival-on-the-step charge. Recommendation: vary or cut the phrase in one of the two, keeping ¶1's (it carries the body-decision beat).

2. **"*grinning*, helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth" (¶5) then "He was grinning at the dress now like he still couldn't quite believe it had come out right" (¶8).** Class: repetition. The same gesture and the same characterization (boyish disbelief at his own surprise) rendered twice three paragraphs apart; the second is a rerun, not an escalation. Recommendation: keep the kid-with-a-surprise version and let ¶8 give him a different physical beat, or cut the ¶8 clause.

3. **"He'd set the whole thing up for her." (¶8, after the pedestal/mirror description).** Class: overwork. Interiority restating what the moved pedestal and bright screw heads just showed — the chapter's own rule (hide the trouble) argues for cutting the label. Recommendation: cut the sentence.

4. **"behind her ribs" ×2 ("It got in behind her ribs…" ¶9; "somewhere behind her ribs the looking stopped being shame" ¶18) plus "a laugh living under her ribs" (coda).** Class: repetition. Three ribs-locations for feeling in one chapter, two in the identical wording; the anatomy stops registering. Recommendation: keep the ¶9 occurrence (it opens the tears) and relocate or reword the other two.

5. **Body-before-head construction pileup: "her skin knew before her head did" (¶15), "smiling before she'd decided to" (¶18), "her body answering the glass before her head could" (¶21), "almost before it was shame at all" (¶24).** Class: repetition. The construction itself is PROTECTED (echo ruling 8/12 — body-before-mind is the craft rule), but four renderings in five pages is a local pileup, which the rulings explicitly allow raising. Recommendation: keep the two strongest (skin/head and the shame-crossing) and let the other two beats show the body-first fact without the explicit before-clause.

6. **"The long line ran clean the whole length of her, where the pale edge had cut it a minute ago, and she was the one who'd taken the edge away." (¶24).** Class: overwork. The bare-leg step just showed all of this; the sentence replays the previous paragraph and then explains the meaning of her own act, a metaphor glossed after it lands. Recommendation: cut from "where the pale edge" to the end, letting the clean line stand.

7. **"there was nothing left in front of it to hide" (¶24) then "there was nothing left for either of them to decide" (¶27).** Class: repetition. Two consecutive sections close on the same "there was nothing left…" cadence; the second (a strong scene-out) is undercut by the first. Recommendation: reword or trim the ¶24 clause and reserve the shape for the section close.

8. **"He hadn't gone far — too pleased to, she'd known he wouldn't" (¶26) vs. "he stepped out into the den, not far, never far" (¶13).** Class: repetition. The not-far fact is established, in stronger wording, a page earlier; the restatement plus the she'd-known gloss slows her stride's arrival. Recommendation: cut "He hadn't gone far — too pleased to, she'd known he wouldn't —" and open the paragraph on her coming through in it.

9. **"the way light only comes through silk" (¶8), "the way good cloth does" (¶15), "the way she was learning he did" (coda), "The way the room would turn" (coda).** Class: repetition. Four "the way" constructions — the book's own flagged tic (`style-rules.toml`) — with two clustered in the dress passages; the daydream's "The way the room would turn" earns its slot, the others are habit. Recommendation: vary one of the two dress-passage occurrences.

10. **"She was smiling before she'd decided to" (¶18).** Class: echo — **pre-ruled**. Echo ruling 8: the construction is protected but the exact five-word wording "before she'd decided to" is FIX-AT, with the Vol-1 keeper already assigned to `the-bench.md:303`; this occurrence (not on the ruling's list — likely post-harvest) should vary its wording at this review.
