**15 findings** — 5 within-chapter repetition, 5 overwork, 3 word precision, 1 rhythm, 1 dialogue shape — plus 2 pre-ruled items from `echo-rulings.md` to apply. No structural problems; this is a strong, warm chapter whose faults are all local softening.

The chapter's own standard is set by four passages: the opening dorm-room inventory ("teeth and a clean shirt and the book bag off the floor… a tattletale in the cold morning light for anyone who cared to read it"), the two-sentence characterization of why each friend is early ("Randi was always early, a sorority habit, four years of never being the one they waited on"), the body-keeping-accounts paragraph at :53 ("Vee's body had been keeping the night's account since eight, a good soreness no gym had ever given her"), and the closing walk-out at :71, which earns its length. The Simpson's-paradox lecture threaded as unnoticed thesis — Vee underlining the line that indicts her and giving it "no further thought" — is the chapter's best structural joke and lands without a nudge. Everything below is measured against those.

## Findings

**1. ":3 / :7 — quaint-idiom pair in the first five lines."**
> "it didn't bother her a whit" … "The day went topsy-turvy after that."

*Class: word precision.* Two archaic/nursery idioms inside four paragraphs, in a chapter whose diction is otherwise plain and current ("underslept and overfull," "fit to burst"). "A whit" and "topsy-turvy" both reach for period charm the chapter doesn't otherwise use, and "topsy-turvy" in particular is doing vague work where the two following clauses give the precise version (late to her nine, barely made her ten). **Recommendation:** cut or replace one of the two — "topsy-turvy" is the weaker, since its own sentence's successors already say it concretely.

**2. ":31 — "started to tell them" twice in one paragraph."**
> "So Vee started to tell them." … "She started to tell them it was finished…"

*Class: within-chapter repetition.* The paragraph opens and re-opens with the same verb phrase four lines apart, and the repetition reads as accident rather than as the deliberate anaphora the chapter runs elsewhere (":43, "Cassie did not care about the dress. Cassie did not care about any dress"). **Recommendation:** vary or cut the second instance so the paragraph's forward motion isn't reset mid-stride.

**3. ":31 — the unfound word said twice."**
> "that she still hadn't found the word for what she'd seen in the glass and had been failing to find it all day"

*Class: overwork.* "Hadn't found the word" and "had been failing to find it all day" are the same fact in two grammars; the second clause adds only duration, at the cost of ending the paragraph on a slack participial. **Recommendation:** collapse to one clause and let the duration ride on "all day."

**4. ":39 — the paradox pre-explained before the professor delivers it."**
> "the man ahead in each season coming out behind across the two of them, the numbers refusing to add the way numbers were supposed to"

*Class: overwork.* This narrates the exact reversal the professor states at :67 ("Each year on its own, the first man is the better hitter. Put the two years together and the second man is"), so the lecture's payoff arrives already spent — and the chapter's design depends on that payoff landing hard enough for Vee to write it down and dismiss it. **Recommendation:** thin :39 to Vee's *attention* on the columns without stating the result, and let :67 own the reversal.

**5. ":39 / :41 — the same up-out-of construction twice, two lines apart."**
> "the good student coming up for air out of her own life" … "It was when she came back up out of the numbers"

*Class: within-chapter repetition.* The first is one of the chapter's best figures; the second reuses its shape as plain transition, which retroactively flattens it. **Recommendation:** re-cast :41's surfacing so the "up out of" move belongs to :39 alone.

**6. ":43 — Cassie's not-watching stated twice."**
> "her attention flat and steady on Vee and nowhere near the front of the room. She had not looked at the professor once."

*Class: overwork.* "Nowhere near the front of the room" and "had not looked at the professor once" are one observation in two sentences; the second is the sharper (it has a person in it, and a count). **Recommendation:** cut the abstract half of the first sentence and keep the professor sentence.

**7. ":43 — the paragraph continues one sentence past its landing."**
> "…because the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see."

*Class: rhythm.* The preceding clause — "Cassie had spent watching what the dress had done to her friend" — *is* the realization, dramatized; the trailing "because" clause converts it into a thesis statement about Cassie and explains a figure that had already landed. The chapter elsewhere trusts its own images (":69 gives Vee's underlining no gloss whatever). **Recommendation:** end the paragraph on "what the dress had done to her friend."

**8. ":5 / :43 / :53 — the bed-as-legible-text figure three times."**
> "a tattletale in the cold morning light for anyone who cared to read it" … "plain as the made bed" … "could read it off her like Cassie had read the bed"

*Class: within-chapter repetition.* The first two are earned — the second retroactively names what the first showed, which is good. The third re-explains the mechanism a third time, in a paragraph (the soreness inventory) that is otherwise the chapter's most purely physical and least in need of the analogy. **Recommendation:** cut the "like Cassie had read the bed" comparison and let the room-reading-her fear stand alone.

**9. ":29 / :51 / :57 — "delighted" three times for Randi."**
> "Randi told Cassie, delighted" … "low and delighted from the aisle" … "Randi had her by the wrist, delighted"

*Class: within-chapter repetition.* Randi's pleasure is the point, but the same abstract adjective three times reads as the narrator running out of ways to see her — and the chapter proves it has better ("loving every second of it," "already turning the dress over in her hands without a hand on it"). **Recommendation:** keep one, preferably the wrist-grab at :57, and render the other two through action.

**10. ":25 / :57 — "loving every second of it" / "loving every inch of it.""**
> "scandalized, loving every second of it" … "brooking nothing, loving every inch of it"

*Class: within-chapter repetition.* The same construction with one noun swapped, thirty lines apart, both times as the tail modifier of a Randi beat; the near-variation is more conspicuous than a straight repeat would be. **Recommendation:** cut one outright — the beats survive without either.

**11. ":55 — three figures for one snag, plus its mechanism explained."**
> "And there was the one snag in the gold of the day, surfacing because Randi had reached in and found it."

*Class: overwork.* A snag (fabric) that surfaces (water) because someone reached in (excavation) crosses three metaphors in fourteen words, and the "because" clause explains a causation the dialogue has just performed. **Recommendation:** keep the snag-in-the-gold image and drop the explanatory clause.

**12. ":57 — a three-modifier stack ahead of the line that does the work."**
> "Mock-stern then, brooking nothing, loving every inch of it. "Don't you dare tell me no.""

*Class: overwork.* "Brooking nothing" and "Don't you dare tell me no" are the same beat, one abstract and one in Randi's mouth; the abstraction pre-empts the line. **Recommendation:** cut "brooking nothing" and let the dialogue carry the mock-sternness.

**13. ":49 / :59 — "Something [verb]ed in [X]'s [body part]" twice, ten lines apart."**
> "Something eased in Cassie's face" … "Something turned over in Vee's chest"

*Class: within-chapter repetition.* Both are good sentences; used this close together the identical frame makes them read as one authorial reflex rather than two distinct interior events, and it's the second — the more important of the two, since it's Vee's — that pays. **Recommendation:** re-shape :59 so Vee's response doesn't arrive in the same grammar as Cassie's.

**14. ":59 — "grateful and warmed.""**
*Class: word precision.* A doubled abstract modifier where the chapter otherwise names feeling by its physical event ("a good soreness no gym had ever given her"); "warmed" is also a past participle standing in for a state, which softens it further. **Recommendation:** cut one of the pair, or replace both with what the chest actually does.

**15. ":71 — "the place that was hers.""**
> "Randi on the other already deep into Saturday, the place that was hers, a heel height Vee would need and hadn't known to need"

*Class: word precision.* "Place" is doing double duty here — the sentence is in a corridor, and the intended sense (a shop Randi knows) collides with the physical one; "hers" then floats between ownership and territory. Set beside the precision of "a heel height Vee would need and hadn't known to need," the vagueness shows. **Recommendation:** make the noun concrete (a store, a counter) so the possessive lands as expertise rather than real estate.

**16. ":25 — "Tell me everything. Leave nothing out.""**
*Class: dialogue shape.* Two sentences delivering one demand, and the second is the flattest thing Randi says in the chapter — she compresses everywhere else ("It is a Friday," "That's not a problem, that's a *date*"). **Recommendation:** cut the second sentence.
