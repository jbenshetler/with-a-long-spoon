# Line edit — the-induction (2026-08-07)

**13 findings** — 3 rhythm, 4 within-chapter repetition, 4 overwork, 2 word precision. (0 dialogue-shape; dialogue is clean.) Plus one pre-ruled item from `echo-rulings.md` and 11 echo candidates.

## What the chapter is calibrated against

Three passages set the bar. The turn itself — "She took a breath, and made the second step long." — is a one-sentence paragraph that does what the whole chapter is about, with no adjective spent. The stage sentence at `:25` ("She kept her face exactly where it was, took the woman's hand and held it the beat someone's camera wanted, took the scroll they set in her other hand — a rolled paper tied with a ribbon, absurdly light for the weight of the walk — and turned back with her name still hanging over the chairs, and the room knew, and not a soul in it could have said so out loud.") rides a long accumulating line to a two-clause close that never names the feeling. And Cassie throughout: "Go make your mother cry again," "You look—" left unfinished, "I'll buy you a terrible sandwich and you can be famous at me." The dialogue is compressed exactly where it should be. Most findings below are places where the narration does *not* trust that same restraint — where a beat already landed in action gets a second, explanatory rendering.

## Findings

1. **`:9`** — "and now a hand kept low at her hip while she sat, holding the seam closed over her thigh, **the small choreography of a woman with a secret keeping it**." — *Overwork.* The hand at the hip holding the seam *is* the choreography of a woman keeping a secret; the appositive names what the gesture just performed, and "a secret keeping it" is the chapter labelling its own subject three paragraphs before the walk pays it off. Cut the appositive and let the hand stand.

2. **`:11`** — "and Vee had stood there **resettled** forward onto the balls of her feet, because the dress wanted the height." — *Word precision.* "Stood there resettled" is a past participle doing adjective duty on a stative verb, so the one physical event in the sentence (weight shifting forward) arrives as a condition rather than a motion, and the chapter elsewhere never buries a verb this way. Recast so the shift of weight is the verb.

3. **`:11`** — "Her toenails sat bare in the straps, and her fingernails where they lay in her lap: a frosted plum that woke and threw the light when it moved, **the same highlight the silk gave up when it shimmered**." — *Overwork.* "Woke and threw the light" already makes the rhyme with the silk available to the reader; the trailing clause explains the metaphor after it lands, and "the same highlight" is the sentence grading its own image. Cut from "the same highlight" to the period.

4. **`:13`** — "He'd been **sorry** about it in the quiet way he was **sorry** about things, **no performance in it**, the night the dates came clear" — *Overwork + repetition.* "The quiet way he was sorry about things" already excludes performance; the third clause restates it, and "sorry" twice in eleven words (with a third at `:37`) makes a plain word conspicuous. Cut "no performance in it".

5. **`:13`** — "She hadn't told him it was only a dress. […] She'd tried telling him it was **only a ceremony**, and it had come out thin" — *Within-chapter repetition.* The same rhetorical move ("it was only a ___") is run twice in four sentences, once as a thing unsaid and once as a thing said, and the first firing spends the surprise of the second, which is the one that pays (it "came out thin"). Cut or recast the first.

6. **`:15`** — "She'd built a **picture** of this — up the mountain the day she named the dress, and again in his bed with it going dark on the door — walking in late, on purpose, the room turning to look." — *Rhythm + repetition.* Nineteen words of parenthetical citation sit between "a picture of this" and the picture's actual content, so the sentence's payload arrives after the reader has lost the thread; separately this is the chapter's third "picture/pictured" (`:7` twice, here). Move or trim the dash-clause so the imagined entrance follows its own setup, and vary one of the earlier "pictured"s.

7. **`:23`** — "**nothing** under the silk and **nothing** to guess at, and she let it travel down the rows, the small still beat where a warm dull room worked out that there was **nothing** between it and her but **the silk**." — *Overwork.* Three "nothing"s and two "silk"s inside one sentence say a single fact three times, and the sentence's real content (the room working it out) is the weakest of the three statements. Cut the middle restatement and let the first and last carry it.

8. **`:23`** — "the small still beat where a warm dull room **worked out** that there was nothing between it and her but the silk" … "**and a murmur went through the crowd** as she came, the sound a dull room makes when it wakes up." — *Within-chapter repetition (same beat twice).* The room's dawning comprehension is rendered at the head of the sentence-run and again at its end, forty words apart, and the second is unarguably the better version — it is dramatized, it is sound, and it lands the "dull room" setup from `:15`. Cut the first rendering.

9. **`:23`** — "the **give and take** of her in the good light" — *Word precision.* "Give and take" is the chapter's one genuinely vague figure — it names an exchange where the sentence means the alternating conceal/reveal of the slit — in a paragraph otherwise built from exact body coordinates ("calf to hip"). Replace with something that names what actually alternates.

10. **`:25`** — "The eyes found the leg **because she'd handed it to them**." — *Overwork / repetition.* "She gave the room her leg on purpose" is eight lines above; the causal clause re-explains a motive the reader has already been told in the same terms ("gave"/"handed"). Cut the because-clause.

11. **`:35`** — "Vee took the phone and knew to the heartbeat what second it was — **the one where she'd decided, and her body had come up to meet the decision**." — *Within-chapter repetition.* This is the third rendering of the decision/body beat: acted at `:21`–`:23`, glossed at `:25` ("her body agreeing… catching up to a thing she'd decided a half-second before her head was sure of it"), and now restated here — and "come up to meet the decision" is a weaker version of `:25`'s. Compress to the bare deixis and let "None of that was in the picture" do the rest.

12. **`:37`** — "and then, **because he'd been sorry to miss it and this was the nearest thing to handing it to him**, she sent it to Pace — **the whole frame, uncropped, the leg and the shoulder and the woman who had it handled**." — *Overwork.* The paragraph justifies the send before the send occurs, then re-inventories a photograph the reader saw item by item four lines earlier; the two strongest sentences in the paragraph are the last two, and they arrive after the reader has been over-prepared. Cut the motive clause (the next two sentences supply it) and trim the frame inventory.

13. **`:41`** — "**The danger of it was behind her now** — she could walk any way she pleased out to the car, so she let the slit swing on her bare leg with every step and didn't make herself small **about it**, and couldn't have told you…" — *Rhythm + word precision.* The abstract opening clause ("the danger of it") announces the release the swinging slit is about to enact, and the paragraph then carries four unstressed "it"s into a closing sentence whose final movement ("whether that was pride or only the same heat still running, or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing") is the best last line in the chapter and wants a clean run-up. Cut the danger clause and the trailing "about it".
