# Line edit — one-bite (2026-08-07)

**20 findings** — 8 within-chapter repetition, 5 overwork, 6 word precision, 1 dialogue shape — plus 2 pre-ruled items from `echo-rulings.md`.

The chapter is strong and mostly earns its own register. Its best passages are the confession-under-daylight beats, where the interiority is doing work the dialogue can't: `:49` ("she felt the print of his hand arrive again where the seat met her, low and bright and not remotely finished"), `:61` ("only the wet plain fact of herself was near"), `:73` ("the certainty of being looked at crawling warm and total up the backs of her bare thighs"), `:89` ("the porch was abruptly far too bright and far too full of people to be a body in"), and `:121`'s flat declarative stack in the stall. The close (`:159`, the glaze of a pastry she never touched) is the best sentence in the chapter and needs nothing. Randi's Radford monologue (`:95`–`:103`) is the most alive dialogue — "the engine running and nobody left to drive it" and "Is it warm in here. Was he interested." are exactly her compression. Findings below are almost all local: a handful of frames the chapter reaches for two or three times, and four places where a paragraph says its thing and then says it again.

## Findings

1. **`:9`, `:33`, `:101`, `:105` — the `Something [low] in her` frame, four times.** "something small in Vee sat up and warmed itself at it" / "something low turned over once, lazily, and settled lower" / "Something low in Vee answered the phrase straight off" / "It came from somewhere low in her." *Class: within-chapter repetition.* This is the frame that took its first FIX-AT in the Gone review (#32), and the chapter's own best arousal beats (`:49`, `:61`, `:73`) don't use it — they name the sensation and its location instead, which is the standard the chapter sets. Recommend keeping `:33` (the strongest, and the only one with a verb doing real work) and recasting `:101` and `:105` to the visible act.

2. **`:27` and `:103` — "a hand flat to her chest" / "laid a hand flat to her chest".** *Class: within-chapter repetition (same gesture rendered twice).* Both are Randi performing delight/mock-decorum, seventy lines apart, in near-identical words; the second ("in mock salute") is the one that needs the gesture, since the first already has the shriek doing the work. Recommend cutting or varying the gesture at `:27`.

3. **`:7` and `:151` — "her whole face came on, warm and sure" / "Randi's whole face opened, warm all the way through".** *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Same face, same adjective, same construction, at the chapter's two hinges — and `:151` is the more important one (it's the beat that disarms Vee), so the opening use dulls the payoff. Recommend varying `:7`.

4. **`:123` and `:155` — "looking at her the way she always did" / "Soft, the way she always was".** *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Two "the way she always ___" constructions thirty lines apart, both asserting Randi's constancy; the second sits inside the protected keeper sentence, so it should stand. Recommend recasting `:123`.

5. **`:119` and `:127` — "the whole bright porch" / "the whole bright wanting".** *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The `the whole bright ___` frame twice in nine lines, and "bright" is already the chapter's most-worked adjective (`:3` twice, `:5`, `:49`, `:89`, `:113`, `:119`, `:127`). Recommend varying `:127`, whose "wanting" is doing better work without the frame.

6. **`:49`, `:113`, `:123`, `:139` — "arrive"/"arrived" four times.** "she felt the print of his hand arrive again" / "The picture arrived in Vee entire" / "Randi's face arrived behind her eyes" / "the little plate that had arrived while she was gone." *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Three of the four are the same move — an image arriving unbidden in her — which is a designed rhythm, but the identical verb makes it read as one device rather than three escalations. Recommend varying `:113` (the weakest of the three, since "entire and asked nothing of her" already carries the involuntariness).

7. **`:33` and `:145` — "spun me clean out of my wrap skirt" / "took her breath clean out".** *Class: within-chapter repetition.* A distinctive collocation spent twice; `:145` is the load-bearing one (the cold restoring the world to size), and `:33` is inside dialogue where the spin already lands. Recommend varying `:33`.

8. **`:95` — "Randi sat back, turned her glass, and let her own night take the floor."** *Class: within-chapter repetition (business as beat-marker).* Glass-turning is used four times (`:15`, `:45`, `:95`, `:117`), and `:15`/`:45` are a designed mirror (Vee copying Randi to have somewhere to put her hands); `:95` is the free instance and spends the gesture between them and the ending. Recommend cutting "turned her glass" here.

9. **`:41` — "the light would have it whole, every degree of it, the way it had Randi's bare honest skin."** *Class: overwork / word precision.* `:11` has already made this observation better and wittier (the makeup "so finely done it passed for skin, nothing on her the morning hadn't put there"), so `:41` restates it and, worse, believes it — "bare honest skin" is a doubled modifier that flattens the joke the chapter already made. Recommend cutting from "the way it had" to the end of the sentence.

10. **`:127` — "She stopped. She held there... curdled into something low and ashamed and obedient. Frustrated and cowed in the same breath. For a moment she did not move at all."** *Class: overwork / rhythm.* The paragraph reaches its true last sentence at "obedient" and then says it twice more: the fragment glosses the triad, and "For a moment she did not move at all" restates "She stopped." Recommend cutting the final sentence and considering the fragment as well.

11. **`:141` — "And Vee could not tell. That was the whole of it, standing there with her pulse in her ears and her face on fire: she could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one."** *Class: overwork / rhythm.* The opening sentence announces the sentence that follows, and "could not tell" lands twice in twelve words. Recommend cutting "And Vee could not tell."

12. **`:125` — "She could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was, and the two would not hold in one body."** *Class: overwork.* The first clause has already stated the incompatibility in concrete physical terms; the second restates it as abstraction, and the paragraph's real force is in the short "But it was not the thinking, it was the looking." Recommend cutting "and the two would not hold in one body".

13. **`:77` — "the shame of it and the heat of it were not two things, they were one thing, and the one thing was sitting in her now with nowhere to go."** *Class: overwork.* Three statements of a single perception, with "thing" carrying all three; the chapter elsewhere fuses shame and heat by rendering them, not by asserting the fusion. Recommend collapsing to the negation plus the landing image, cutting the middle clause.

14. **`:123` — "wanted more than a fingertip, wanted variety, wanted filling."** *Class: word precision.* Two of the three terms are bodily and specific; "variety" is a shopping word and breaks the triad's register at its middle beat. Recommend replacing it with a concrete term or cutting it so the triad becomes a pair.

15. **`:153` — "The cold morning went briefly luminous."** *Class: word precision.* "Luminous" is the one Latinate abstraction in a paragraph of plain diction ("picked her, out of everyone, for the closest place there was"), and "briefly" hedges a moment the sentence should just have. Recommend a concrete light word and cutting the adverb.

16. **`:11` — "how much trouble Randi had gone to in order to look as if she had gone to none."** *Class: word precision.* "In order to" is flab inside the chapter's best joke, and the joke's timing depends on the two "gone to"s landing close. Recommend cutting "in order".

17. **`:5` — "She followed the girl down between the tables a half-step behind the morning."** *Class: word precision.* The figure is pretty but doesn't resolve — "behind the morning" names nothing the reader can see, in a paragraph otherwise built of hard specifics (menus, brunch-shift brightness, the back porch). Recommend making the half-step concrete or cutting the phrase.

18. **`:93` — "her face did something she couldn't call back and Randi watched it do the thing with frank, open pleasure."** *Class: word precision.* "Did something" then "do the thing" in one sentence leans on the chapter's most overused noun ("thing" appears at `:61`, `:77` three times, `:93`, `:119`), and "frank, open" is a doubled modifier. Recommend recasting the second clause so it doesn't repeat the placeholder, and dropping one of the two adjectives.

19. **`:105` — "'Ugh.' It came from somewhere low in her. 'So frustrating.'"** *Class: dialogue shape.* "So frustrating" states what "Ugh" already performed, and Vee under this much pressure would not supply the gloss; it also pre-empts Randi's punchline, which turns on frustration. Recommend cutting the second line of dialogue.

20. **`:33` — "The saying of it put his hands back on her."** *Class: word precision (verb buried in a nominalization).* The chapter's better version of this same move is `:73`'s active "She heard her own voice lay each piece of it on the table between them" — measured against that, "the saying of it" is the abstract form of a sentence that wants a verb. Recommend recasting so the telling acts.
