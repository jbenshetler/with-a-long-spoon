# Line edit — lesson (2026-08-07)

**17 findings** — 5 overwork, 4 within-chapter repetition, 4 rhythm, 3 word precision, 1 dialogue shape (plus 1 pre-ruled ledger item, #0).

The chapter's own high-water marks are the compressions: "She was dressed by five-thirty. She changed the shirt and changed it back." (:31) — a whole evening of nerves in two sentences; "By the time they had a table she had a count going she hadn't meant to start." (:131); "She said nothing. She was busy." (:119); "She'd let herself feel like an exception. She was looking at the rule." (:165); "She'd thought she was special. She was just next." (:197); and the bartender's whole run, which lands "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." (:221) without a syllable of setup. The dance instruction itself is the chapter's technical triumph — the frame taught as physics and cashed as thesis at :143. Everything below is measured against those; the failures are almost all the chapter saying a thing well and then saying it again.

1. **`:243` — "He made them feel safe, and like real people … He kept them safe." (final interior paragraph)** — *Within-chapter repetition.* The same assertion twice in consecutive sentences, inside a run of four consecutive `He made/He kept` sentences that is already at its limit. **Cut "He kept them safe."**

2. **`:243` — "She'd punished him for being the man she wanted him to be. The man she knew him to be. A good man. She'd done it to a good man."** — *Overwork.* The paragraph's true last sentence is "She'd punished him for being the man she wanted him to be."; the three that follow restate it in shrinking increments and spend "a good man" twice, which also blunts the bartender's "He's a good guy." **Cut the trailing stack back to one closing beat.**

3. **`:113` — "The pattern came through his body before it arrived in her feet. She felt the turn before it happened. Her feet landed where they were supposed to land."** — *Rhythm.* Three same-shaped before/after sentences in a row at the chapter's breakthrough, where the breakthrough should be sudden; the third also re-states the first. **Cut one of the last two.**

4. **`:75` — "going jelly the moment she tried to move, which meant he had nothing to push back against, which meant the lead went nowhere."** — *Overwork.* Two chained `which meant` clauses explain a mechanism his dialogue at :59 already taught the reader in his own words. **Cut from "which meant he had nothing" onward.**

5. **`:125` — "She stood in the entrance for a moment and felt the difference between the pad and this: the lesson had been private and deliberate, and this was alive, and she was going to be visible."** — *Overwork.* The colon glosses what the two preceding images ("the kind of warm that came from bodies," "The floor was already moving") already delivered; only "she was going to be visible" is new. **Cut the "the lesson had been private and deliberate, and this was alive" gloss.**

6. **`:179` — "She was already angry, and some part of her wanted to stay that way, and nothing he could put into that moment was going to make it all right."** — *Overwork.* The third clause repeats "it wouldn't have mattered what he said" from the sentence immediately before. **Cut the final clause.**

7. **`:253` — "had changed temperature overnight, was cool now instead of hot, and cool embarrassment was just information."** — *Overwork.* "changed temperature" lands; "cool now instead of hot" spells it out before the payoff clause reuses "cool" a third time. **Cut "was cool now instead of hot."**

8. **`:105` — "'There's no sorry' — slow, grave, a man delivering doctrine — 'unless you take off a foot.'"** — *Overwork.* Three modifiers where two carry it; "a man delivering doctrine" explains the joke the interruption is already making, and pre-empts the laugh. **Cut one of the three.**

9. **`:109` — "she was aware of how ridiculous this was and also aware that she did not want to stop."** — *Overwork / rhythm.* The doubled "aware … also aware" is the chapter's flattest sentence at a moment two lines from "And then something gave." **Compress to one clause.**

10. **`:85` and `:139` — "her feet scrambled to follow, a half-beat late, stepping on nothing, recovering wrong" / "stepping where the step had already gone, a half-beat late, recovering wrong."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* The recurrence is clearly designed (the pad failure returning on the floor), but the second instance repeats three of the first's tags verbatim in the same order, which reads as copy rather than callback. **Keep one tag as the recognition signal and cut the other two from `:139`.**

11. **`:85` "She said nothing. She adjusted." / `:119` "She said nothing. She was busy."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* The same two-beat construction thirty lines apart; :119 is the far better one and loses its snap to the earlier rehearsal. **Vary `:85`.**

12. **`:127` "the easy collision of people glad to see somebody" / `:145` "Once she wasn't braced for the collision"** — *Within-chapter repetition.* "Collision" is distinctive enough that the second use recalls the hugs rather than the dance floor, which is the wrong association at that moment. **Vary `:145`.**

13. **`:231` — "The driver found her in the mirror … he kept catching her eyes in the glass the whole way home while she kept hers on the window."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* The same gesture rendered twice in three sentences, the second at greater length; the section is a short cold coda that should not linger on the driver. **Cut the mirror to one occurrence.**

14. **`:97` — "He pressed play on the ancient boombox"** — *Word precision.* "Ancient" is a weaker restatement of :35's fully rendered machine ("battered, the handle wrapped in electrical tape … a decade she hadn't been alive for"), and it steals the beat from her granddad line in the next sentence. **Cut "ancient."**

15. **`:47` — "For the same beat, the three-step is easier, slower steps than the two-step."** — *Dialogue shape / word precision.* The appositive misfires grammatically ("is easier, slower steps") in the one register the chapter can't afford it — Pace teaching, where his authority rests on saying technical things cleanly; he would compress. **Recut the sentence to one clean comparison.**

16. **`:35` — "He was freshly shaved. He was carrying a boom box — battered…"** — *Rhythm.* The paragraph's power is its inventory of fragments (Recently polished boots. Jeans with a crease pressed into them.); the two full `He was ___` sentences at the end drop out of that grammar just as the most telling object arrives. **Bring the boom box into the fragment series.**

17. **`:161` — "makeup put on with intention and no apology for being makeup, the kind that worked in the dark and would be too much by daylight."** — *Overwork.* "No apology for being makeup" is the observation; the trailing clause explains it, and the sentence is already the third modifier in the same description. **Cut the "the kind that worked in the dark" clause or the "with intention" pair.**
