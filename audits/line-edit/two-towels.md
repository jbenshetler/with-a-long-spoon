# Line edit — two-towels (2026-08-06)

**Verdict:** 16 findings — 7 repetition, 6 overwork, 1 rhythm, 1 precision, 1 dialogue — plus 2 pre-ruled FIX-AT items from the echo ledger.

**Calibration.** The chapter's best passages set a high bar: the drive-in opening ("She was the hard part, and so was the gravel"); the stopped kiss and its cold-water turn ("no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop"); the closed bedroom door ("nothing withheld and nothing offered"); the error-correcting-codes exchange ("Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it"); and the shirt reveal ("the absurd answer was standing in front of her with his hands in his pockets"). These passages trust a single rendering of each beat. The findings below are almost all places where the chapter renders a beat twice — locally, or across its own length — against that standard.

## Pre-ruled (echo ledger)

- **FIX-AT #3 — "the small private."** "Something in her dropped, small and private." (the bedroom door, ¶ "It didn't."). One of the 7 ledger occurrences; vary the wording here unless this is chosen as the keeper.
- **FIX-AT #8 — "before she'd decided."** "before she'd decided whether to let him see her do it" (the lean at the bedroom door). The body-before-mind *shape* is protected; the near-identical wording is what varies. (Note "She crouched without deciding to" later in the den is the protected construction in varied wording — leave it.)
- Protected, do not touch: "It came out" (×2 — "I can't sew" and the final "Yes"); Pace rendered in negatives ("He didn't take her in," "He didn't preen").

## Findings

1. **"the stopping was his; he put the brakes on"** (the stopped kiss) — *overwork*. The second clause restates the first in a flatter idiom at the chapter's sharpest turn; cut "he put the brakes on."
2. **"That had always been her job, never theirs."** (same paragraph) — *overwork*. It re-explains "no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop," which already carried the whole insight; cut or fold.
3. **"pleased, and not troubling to hide it, a man glad to be asked… and he didn't keep the gladness out of the word"** (the "Show me" ask) — *overwork*. Glad is rendered three ways in two sentences; keep one of "glad to be asked" / "didn't keep the gladness out" and cut the other.
4. **"pleased" ×3** — "a man glad to be asked / a little pleased to have her looking / He was pleased — plainly, quietly proud" (door, closet, furniture) — *repetition*. Pace's recurring pleasure is right, but the same word carries it each time; vary one of the first two so the furniture moment ("plainly, quietly proud") keeps the strongest form.
5. **"the same logic as the shoes, one right thing for each thing he did"** (end of kitchen paragraph) — *overwork*. The shoe paragraph already closed on "One right pair for each thing and no more"; this end-of-paragraph clause explains the parallel after the reader has made it — cut from "— the same logic" on.
6. **"kind" pileup in the bookshelf sentence** — "the expensive kind… the kind you had to order" plus the earlier kitchen "the granite kind… the one-fork-and-a-jar-of-sauce kind" — *repetition*. The kitchen's paired "kind"s are designed; the bookshelf's doubled "kind" inside one sentence is not — vary one.
7. **"filed it under" ×2** — "filed it under the small mysteries of him" (the warm house) and "filed it under some category that made no sense" (the shirt) — *repetition*. A distinctive interiority move spent twice; the shirt occurrence is load-bearing (it pays off the tag), so vary the earlier one.
8. **"reflex" ×3** — "out of an old reflex" (kitchen), "the reflex of a woman who'd had the shade fixed" (the cloth), "a reflex, a small panic" ("I can't sew") — *repetition*. Three uses of a noticeable word; the "I can't sew" beat needs it most — vary one of the first two.
9. **The magazine-red recap** — "It was the silk she'd named at the summit… that brick-red she'd had stuck in her head for weeks" — *overwork*. The paragraph before already gave "For weeks she'd carried a single red in her head… the shade from the magazine, that flat brick-red"; the flashback re-states the shade and the weeks — trim the recap to what's new (the summit, the cherries, "that sounds like a beautiful dress").
10. **"her whole chest" / "fit in her chest"** — "didn't know what to do with her whole chest" (patent) and "tried to make the size of it fit in her chest, and it wouldn't go" (the offer) — *repetition*. Same somatic image at the chapter's two emotional peaks; the second is the bigger moment, so the first spends it early — vary the patent beat.
11. **"hands in his pockets" ×2** — "stopped beside it with his hands in his pockets" (the bolt of cloth) and "standing in front of her with his hands in his pockets" (the shirt reveal) — *repetition*. The same gesture twenty lines apart; the shirt-reveal instance lands harder ("the absurd answer was standing…") — cut or vary the first.
12. **"The afternoon poured through all that glass" / "the flat bright afternoon pouring through it"** (sunroom entry; his gesture at the glass) — *repetition*. The second also re-runs "flat and bright"; a nod toward the glass without re-rendering the light would do.
13. **"like her own coloring had been consulted"** (the mirror beat) — *overwork*. "mixed for exactly her" plus the next line's "He'd matched *her*" already carry it; the middle clause explains the metaphor between its landing and its payoff — cut it.
14. **"in a fraction of the passes… in a fraction of it"** (the patent speech) — *dialogue*. If the doubled "fraction" is his excited speech looping, it should loop harder or not at all; as written it reads like authorial repetition rather than character rhythm — vary one.
15. **Final sentence — "because everything in her was saying it at once"** — *rhythm*. The chapter's true last beat is the triple yes with "no hedge in it"; the explanatory because-clause continues one beat past it — consider ending at "no hedge in it."
16. **"waiting for night" / "waiting beside it"** — the sconce ("bulb dark under a cover, waiting for night") and the workshop rag/can ("waiting beside it") — *precision*. Minor: two personified "waiting" objects; the sconce's is the finer image — a plainer verb for the rag and can.
