# Line edit — fairytale (2026-08-07)

**Verdict: 22 findings** — 3 rhythm, 6 within-chapter repetition, 7 overwork (one of which is also a rhythm/paragraph-end fault), 6 word precision, of which 1 is pre-ruled by echo ruling #30 and should simply be applied.

The chapter is strong and mostly earns its length. Its best writing is the whole aftermath sequence from :115 to :125 — "waiting to see what the holding would develop into. She waited a while… Nothing developed," the sandwich, and the cold that "did not belong to the room. It was hers. She had brought it in with her and she would carry it back out," through the mirror and "Nothing had happened to her eyes." Also first-rate: the ski-run paragraph at :51 (the whole appraisal done in motion, no interiority needed), the elbow beat at :31 ("She did not need it. She took it anyway."), the stair descent at :83, and the closing text exchange at :145. Everything flagged below is measured against those.

## Findings

1. **:7, "In a hearty mood, he welcomed his adored daughter…" / "Her father ate heartily, as he did on vacation."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* The chapter's second and fourth sentences share a distinctive root twelve words apart, and neither is doing the other's work. Recommendation: vary or cut one of the two "hearty/heartily" uses.

2. **:7, "brilliantly lit, diffusely from the sky and spectacularly from the snow."** — *Word precision.* Three -ly adverbs in eleven words, and "diffusely"/"spectacularly" are the abstract report of a light the concrete nouns (sky, snow) already deliver. Recommendation: reduce to one adverb and let the light sources carry the contrast.

3. **:7, "Her father had the light at his back. Her mother sat to his left, and Randi sat across from her mother, on the right."** — *Rhythm.* Blocking laid out as furniture inventory in the middle of a paragraph otherwise built on appetite and performance; the flat coordinates stall a passage that has been moving fast. Recommendation: compress the seating to the one relationship that matters (light behind the father) and drop the left/right accounting.

4. **:7, "Finishing, Randi stood, exchanging affections with her parents before excusing herself to go skiing."** — *Word precision.* Two participles and the abstract nominalization "exchanging affections" where the paragraph elsewhere renders the same act concretely ("returned the smile warmly and hugged him, eating her revulsion"). Recommendation: replace "exchanging affections" with the physical act, in a finite verb.

5. **:9, "A presence loomed over her shoulder… looking first into the chest of a towering body."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* "Loomed" and "towering" state his size twice inside two sentences, and the second — her eyeline hitting his chest — already shows it. Recommendation: cut "loomed" or "towering," keeping the eyeline.

6. **:9, "feeling vaguely disconnected from the world by her helmet, goggles, and gloves."** — *Word precision.* "Vaguely disconnected from the world" is an abstraction sitting on top of a perfectly concrete list of muffling gear. Recommendation: name the specific sensory subtraction (sound, touch) rather than the vague state.

7. **:15, "Despite herself, Randi laughed delightedly."** — *Word precision — PRE-RULED, apply.* Echo ruling #30 ("delighted" as a narrator label for Randi — FIX-AT, applies book-wide without re-litigation): keep the occurrence where delight is the grammatical subject doing something, cut it where it is an adverbial label pinned to an action the line already performs. This is the pure adverbial case — "Despite herself" plus "laughed" has already told us. Recommendation: cut "delightedly."

8. **:51, "She had told him not to fall behind. There had been no danger of it. He could have left her behind whenever he wanted, and didn't. That was what she couldn't get past — that he had the run to spare and spent none of it proving it."** — *Overwork.* Four sentences on one perception; the third and fourth are the same observation stated twice, and the em-dash clause explains the metaphor the paragraph already landed with "made the same pitch look like less than it was." Recommendation: cut one of the two closing restatements and end on the sharper of them.

9. **:55, "His smile brightened into a chuckle."** — *Word precision.* "Brightened into" is the almost-right verb for a smile becoming audible, and it sits oddly against the plainness of the surrounding beats. Recommendation: choose a plainer verb for the smile-to-laugh move.

10. **:57, "she caught him looking at her more than once, and did not look away, and looked back until he caught her at it."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* Four inflections of *look* and two of *caught* in one clause chain, and :37 has already run the same mutual-assessment beat ("she… looked over anyway, and found him looking back"). Recommendation: cut one limb of the chain so the sentence turns once rather than three times.

11. **:71, "She wanted something she'd never worn, bought today, for tonight, for him. … She wanted to walk into that room in a dress she'd bought for him and have it be the one people remembered."** — *Overwork.* The paragraph states the want, gives its future-story image, then restates the want in near-identical terms. Recommendation: cut the second "a dress she'd bought for him" so the closing sentence carries only the new information (the room, the remembering).

12. **:77, "left her shoulders bare and gave the room her back. … woke them up and gave them to the room."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* The "gave X to the room" construction twice in three sentences, both as the paragraph's closing move. Recommendation: vary the second; it's the more load-bearing of the two and should not read as recycled.

13. **:85, "Coming down to him, she had the feeling again of a man who kept turning out to be more than she'd yet seen."** — *Overwork.* Abstract summary of exactly what the preceding sentence just showed concretely ("the bulk of the mountain gone off him and something long and easy left in its place"). Recommendation: cut the sentence and let the tailcoat do it.

14. **:93, "The band was live, a real orchestra"** — *Word precision.* "Live" and "a real orchestra" are the same assurance twice, and the doubling has a faint anxiety the rest of the ballroom description doesn't. Recommendation: keep one.

15. **:97, "His lead was firm and unmistakable; his hand told her body where to go, and there was never a half-beat where she had to guess, and she could have followed it in her sleep."** — *Overwork.* Four statements of one fact, and ":95" has already rendered it in action ("the turn of his hand sent her and she went"). Recommendation: cut to the one clause that isn't already on the page.

16. **:97, paragraph shape.** — *Rhythm.* The paragraph does the Pace comparison, the bracelet catch, the reel-in, the watching room, and "this is the one" in a single block; the bracelet — the chapter's quietest and most important object — is buried mid-sentence between two crescendos. Recommendation: break after "Easier was better," so the bracelet and the reel get their own run at "this is the one."

17. **:97 "She turned the thought over and found it true" / :137 "she took it out and turned it over."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* The same interior gesture rendered twice, and the second is the more distinctive. Recommendation: vary the first.

18. **:107 "and was taking her up" / :111 "She let John take her up."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* "Take her up" three times (counting "took her arm and started up the steps") in five lines; :111's is the button and needs the phrase clean. Recommendation: vary :107's.

19. **:121, "the print of her mouth on his chest, and lower, a ring of her lipstick, where she had taken him in her mouth and felt her own mouth do it from a long way off."** — *Within-chapter repetition.* Three *mouths* in one sentence; the third is the one the sentence is for. Recommendation: cut or vary the first "mouth."

20. **:121, "Her body had spent itself on him and she remained unmoved."** — *Rhythm / overwork.* The paragraph's true last line is the lipstick ring "from a long way off"; this sentence names the distance the image just delivered, and :117 has already delivered the verdict. Recommendation: cut, ending the paragraph on "from a long way off."

21. **:127, "This man had taken a beautiful woman to bed, brought her pleasure, but never found her underneath."** — *Overwork.* Restates :117 and :121 a third time, and "brought her pleasure" is the abstract euphemism in a chapter whose aftermath is otherwise exact. Recommendation: cut the first two clauses and keep "never found her underneath" attached to the safe/won-nothing turn that follows.

22. **:103, "She had wanted the night to be a story, and here he was writing another page of it."** — *Overwork.* The story motif is established at :71 and the sleigh has already performed it; the sentence glosses its own image. Recommendation: cut the second clause.
