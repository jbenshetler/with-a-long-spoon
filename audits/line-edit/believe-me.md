# Line edit — believe-me (2026-08-07)

**12 findings** — 3 rhythm, 3 within-chapter repetition, 4 overwork, 2 word precision. No dialogue-shape findings; no FIX-AT entries in `echo-rulings.md` name this chapter, so nothing pre-ruled is pending here.

The chapter is close to its own ceiling and the best of it is very good. The arrival sensorium (`:7`, `:13`) earns the whole confrontation by making the wanting physical before it is argued — "too warm for him and made that way for her" does in nine words what a paragraph of exposition would blunt. The bag left in the car is perfectly placed and perfectly unnarrated. The speech (`:35`, `:39`) holds its shape under pressure and lands its best line on the plainest diction in the chapter ("Like my body comes with me attached"). The exchange from `:51` to `:55` — "Words are cheap" turned back on him, then the arms folding — is the chapter's peak, and the close (`:61`) exits on exactly the right beat with exactly the right small word. The findings below are almost all about sentences that keep going after their work is done.

---

1. **`:13`** — "something slow that moved like water, the kind he played **on the nights she stayed**" … "level with the chair by the door where her bag went **on the nights she stayed**."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The identical five-word tag lands twice inside three sentences; the second use is the load-bearing one (it sets up the bag), so the first is spending the phrase before it pays. **Recommendation:** vary or cut the music line's tag.

2. **`:13`** — paragraph ends "The bag was in the car; she had carried nothing in. **She stayed where she was and went no further into the room.**"
   *Class: rhythm (paragraph past its true last sentence).* The final sentence restates the blocking already fixed by "She stopped on the mat" and dulls the bag, which is the strongest beat in the paragraph. **Recommendation:** end the paragraph on the bag.

3. **`:11`** — "There was a taste in her mouth like a coin held under the tongue, **thin and metal**."
   *Class: overwork.* The coin has already delivered thin and metallic; the appositive explains the figure after it lands. **Recommendation:** cut the trailing modifier pair.

4. **`:15`** — "His face opened when he saw her, **the way it opened every time**."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* This is the third *the way it [X] every time* frame in nine lines (`:7` "the way it always did," `:7` "every time," "every time since"), and unlike the acked lamplight instance it is doing no ritual work the verb hasn't already done. **Recommendation:** cut the trailing clause and let "opened" stand alone.

5. **`:15`** — "two glasses of wine stood **already poured**, one for her, **waiting**."
   *Class: overwork.* Three redundant signals of readiness stacked on one prop that the paragraph's whole logic already reads as prepared. **Recommendation:** drop one of the three.

6. **`:21`** — "**He offered it plainly, the whole good evening laid out for her to take.** 'That's what I thought we'd do.'"
   *Class: overwork (interiority restating the action).* His speech has just laid the evening out plainly; the narration between the two dialogue halves tells us the thing we watched. **Recommendation:** cut the narrative sentence and let the two speech beats abut.

7. **`:29`** — "'You're the most important person in my life.' He said it **simply, with certainty**."
   *Class: word precision / within-chapter repetition.* Doubled abstract modifiers where the line's own bareness already carries the simplicity, and "certainty" repeats the load-bearing "unhurried certainty" of `:15` fourteen lines earlier. **Recommendation:** keep one modifier at most, and not "certainty."

8. **`:35`** — "You touch me with **such care**, treat me like **this precious thing**."
   *Class: word precision.* The two softest, most abstract phrases in the speech sit adjacent, three clauses before the speech's most concrete and best line; "precious thing" is greeting-card diction the rest of the paragraph doesn't stoop to. **Recommendation:** replace one with something as physical as the food and the hands that surround it.

9. **`:39`** — "It **came out** ugly and she let it, because that was the whole of it, that with no name on it the best thing in her life **came out** of her mouth sounding like that."
   *Class: overwork + repetition.* The sentence explains a bitterness the dialogue just performed, and repeats "came out" inside twenty-five words. **Recommendation:** shorten to the observation and drop the explanatory clause.

10. **`:39`** — "Out there. **What is this relationship?** What am I to you out there?"
    *Class: rhythm.* Three closing questions where two land harder; the middle one is the abstract, flattest phrasing of a question the chapter asks better on either side of it. **Recommendation:** cut the middle question.

11. **`:47` / `:55`** — "Pace stood looking at her, **saying nothing**." … "and he **said nothing**. She waited for it. **Nothing** came."
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* His silence is named four ways in nine lines; the `:55` trio is the designed escalation, so `:47`'s is the one paying rent twice. **Recommendation:** render `:47` as a visible act rather than a second naming of the silence.

12. **`:59`** — "his hand lifting toward her, reaching to settle her with the hands that settled everything, **to make it right the only way he had**. **That was the one thing she could not take from him now.** Not the hand in place of the word."
    *Class: overwork (before a protected line).* Two consecutive restatements — the third infinitive phrase and then the flat summary sentence — stand between the gesture and the chapter's best short line, which needs to arrive off the image, not off a gloss. **Recommendation:** cut one of the two restatements so "Not the hand in place of the word" lands closer to the reaching hand.

Also noted, not counted as a finding: `:55`'s "He stepped back, and his arms came up and folded, and he held there, still, the still of a man…" runs a four-clause *and*-chain across a moment of shutting; the protected phrase is fine, but the approach to it is looser than the beat.
