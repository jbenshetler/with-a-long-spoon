# Line edit — my-friend-randi (2026-08-07)

**Verdict: 25 findings** — rhythm 4, within-chapter repetition 9, overwork 7, word precision 4, dialogue shape 1. No pre-ruled FIX-AT entries in `echo-rulings.md` name this chapter; two rulings touch it as **protection only** (see "Pre-ruled / protected" below).

## Strongest passages

The chapter's own standard is set by Randi's coaching dialogue, which is the best prose in the file: `:41` ("You backed him into a corner in his own house. Name it or lose me — the one thing he can't do"), `:45`'s close ("That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him"), `:57` ("fear needs a face to point at, so you gave it one"), and the ledge-walk at `:91`, which earns its length because every clause adds a fact rather than a synonym. `:99`'s raccoon line is the chapter's best joke and its best pivot in one move. On the narration side, `:7`'s opening drive is excellent — the long run-on *is* the exhaustion — and `:121` ("And Randi, who could narrate anything, who had told Vee across a dozen tables exactly what a man's mouth had done and where, stopped.") is the single finest sentence in the chapter: the whole withholding delivered by one verb held to the end. Most findings below are places where narration around this dialogue explains what the dialogue already did.

## Findings

1. **`:7` / `:11` — "somewhere in the long dark middle of it" / "lay there through the whole gray middle of the day"** (plus `:7`'s "the gray start of the light"). *Within-chapter repetition.* The same `the [adj] [adj] middle of ___` frame twice, and "gray" twice, four lines apart in the chapter's two most-worked paragraphs; the second lands as recycling of the first rather than as a return. Recommend varying the `:11` instance, which is the weaker of the two.

2. **`:7` / `:11` — "she'd held the picture of coming home" … "exactly as she'd pictured it" … "the picture she'd built on the worst nights"** *Within-chapter repetition.* Three picture-words in five lines; the third is load-bearing (it seeds Sheri and pays off at `:55`), so the first two are competing with it. Recommend cutting or recasting the `:7` pair.

3. **`:15` — "The warmth arrived ahead of her … and gave back nothing but more warmth."** *Within-chapter repetition.* "Warmth" twice inside one sentence, in a chapter that also runs it at `:57`, `:91` and `:101`; the doubling here is the pileup, not the motif. Recommend cutting the second occurrence's noun.

4. **`:15` — "It was the kindest anyone had spoken to her all day."** *Word precision.* "Kindest … spoken" is the almost-right construction (kindness is being predicated of a person via an adverbless superlative), and "all day" is a weak measure in a chapter that has already told us she has spoken to no one. Recommend recasting to put the kindness on the speech, not the speaker.

5. **`:17` — "coming down them fast the moment she saw the state of her, quick and light"** *Word precision.* Doubled modifiers: "fast" and "quick and light" are the same information twice, and the second undercuts the first's urgency. Recommend cutting one.

6. **`:19` — "It was a big hug and a long one … Randi was holding her tighter than she usually did. She held the hug past the end of it, past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that, and neither of them let go."** *Overwork + rhythm.* Five separate statements that the hug went long — "big and long," "didn't hurry her out of it," "tighter than she usually did," "past the end of it," "past where a hug stops," "the beat longer and the beat after that," "neither of them let go" — where the design needs exactly one unhurried hug and one held-too-long beat. The paragraph also continues past its true last sentence (the breath off her neck is where it should land, with the over-hold as its own short beat). Recommend keeping "past where a hug stops" and cutting the surrounding restatements.

7. **`:21` — "and it reached Vee even through everything that Randi hadn't apologized for the mess or seemed to see it, that the wrecked girl in her doorway had come first."** *Overwork.* The gesture (sweeping the pile off without looking) has already landed; the sentence then names the reading twice — the not-apologizing *and* the gloss "had come first" — inside a subordinated tangle at a beat of feeling. Recommend cutting the final clause and letting the unapologized mess carry it.

8. **`:23` — "He hadn't texted her once the whole break. Not one word, after she'd stood in his doorway and asked what she was to him and he wouldn't say it, after she'd walked out into the cold and he'd let her go."** *Within-chapter repetition.* Near-verbatim reprise of `:9` ("He hadn't texted. Not once, the whole break. She'd stood just inside his front door in December and asked him what she was to him and he wouldn't say, and she'd walked out into the cold and he'd let her go") fourteen lines later. The retelling-to-Randi is the right move, but at this fidelity the reader is reading the same sentences twice. Recommend compressing the reprise to the parts Randi's coaching later works on.

9. **`:45` / `:65` / `:91` — "That's not a man who's done with you, honey. That's a boy…" / "That is not a man with another woman, gorgeous. That's a man…" / "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing…"** *Within-chapter repetition.* Three uses of Randi's `That's not X. That's Y.` in fifty lines; the construction is book-wide idiom (fed:23, all-told:57, may-i-choose:27, what-to-wear:91), so the issue is local density, not the shape. Recommend recasting the middle one at `:65`, which is the least consequential of the three.

10. **`:27` / `:29` / `:73` / `:91` — the cooked-chicken / poured-wine / pre-heated-house recital.** *Within-chapter repetition.* Four full recitals of the same three-item inventory: Vee's telling, Randi setting it down, Randi's jealousy speech, and the ledge-walk. Two are structural (`:27` is the evidence; `:29` is the coaching move that reframes it); the later two are drumbeat. Recommend thinning `:73`'s list to one item, since `:91`'s "heats his whole house so you won't be cold in it" is the one the coat argument needs.

11. **`:71` — "I got a whole taste of my life without him over that break. More than anything. I want him back."** *Dialogue shape.* The repetition of "I want him back" is right; "More than anything" is the filler between them and runs grammatical where Vee is compressing. Recommend cutting **More than anything.**

12. **`:77` / `:83` — "A beat. She stopped there, and let the rest hang in the room, and waited for Vee to find it." … "And Vee found the rest of it, the end of the sentence Randi had left hanging"** *Overwork.* Four renderings of one pause (a beat / stopped there / let it hang / waited for her to find it), then the hanging sentence is named a second time when Vee completes it. The whole mechanism is what "You've got that good long coat." followed by white space performs by itself. Recommend cutting to a single beat at `:77` and dropping the appositive at `:83`.

13. **`:77` / `:99` — "like a thing being decided for her and by her at once" / "one piece of tonight already decided for her"** *Within-chapter repetition.* The same "decided for her" formulation twice; `:77` is the thematic one (the *and by her at once* is the chapter's argument), so `:99` is the one dulling it. Recommend recasting `:99`.

14. **`:85` / `:89` / `:93` — "the burn went straight up her neck into her face" / "it left her flushed to the ears" / "hot-faced, mortified … under the burn"** *Within-chapter repetition.* The blush is rendered three times in nine lines, with "burn" twice and "mortified"/"mortification" inside one sentence at `:93`. Recommend keeping `:85`'s burn and `:93`'s turn, and cutting the middle rendering.

15. **`:93` — "as it always did once the shame was lit, the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it"** *Overwork.* "As it always did" tells the reader this is a pattern rather than letting the pattern be felt, and the clause then explains the mechanism the next three sentences dramatize. Recommend cutting **as it always did once the shame was lit**.

16. **`:99` — "Vee took them — she was past looking at anything just now — grateful only that here was one piece of tonight already decided for her; Randi always knew the shoes."** *Rhythm.* The em-dash aside stalls a sentence whose last four words are the paragraph's out; the aside also adds nothing the crying has not established. Recommend cutting the aside so the paragraph ends on the shoes.

17. **`:99` (×2) / `:127` — "Make yourself into the thing." / "Just the thing for tonight." / "be the thing"** *Within-chapter repetition.* "The thing" three times, twice inside four sentences, and the last is the chapter's final cadence — the two earlier uses spend it. Recommend recasting "Just the thing for tonight," the weakest.

18. **`:101` — "wanting suddenly to give something back into the warmth"** *Overwork / word precision.* The action (wiping her face, asking about Randi's New Year) already performs the impulse, and "give something back into the warmth" is abstract where the scene has the concrete exchange available. Recommend cutting the clause.

19. **`:111` / `:115` / `:119` — three consecutive Vee-interjection paragraphs, each a short line of dialogue plus a *because*-gloss of what it cost her.** *Rhythm.* Three same-shaped units in a row turns the chapter's best structural idea (Randi's fairy tale read as Vee's deficit) into a rhythm the reader can predict by the second one. Recommend varying the middle one at `:115` — the gloss there is also the most explanatory.

20. **`:115` — "a story with a shape already, a man who was a story you'd tell before you'd known him a day"** *Word precision.* "Story" twice in eleven words, in two different senses. Recommend cutting the first appositive.

21. **`:113` — "She'd gone out that afternoon and bought a dress, brand new, a pale metallic blue"** *Word precision.* "Brand new" is carried by "bought … that afternoon," and the doubled modifier softens a sentence whose payload is the last clause. Recommend cutting **brand new**.

22. **`:121` — "And that was all she said about it. She looked down at her hands a moment."** *Overwork.* `:121`'s magnificent sentence has already delivered the stop; "And that was all she said about it" states the withholding a second time, and the looked-down hands then show it a third. Recommend cutting the middle sentence.

23. **`:127` — "Randi holding on like she'd missed her every bit as badly"** *Overwork.* The narration vouches for Randi's interior state at exactly the moment the chapter should make the reader supply it — the same move ruled against at `fed:23` (ruling #22). The reciprocity is better carried by the hug's length, which the clause has already given us. Recommend cutting the simile.

24. **`:127` — "into the cold" (with `:9`, `:23`, `:91`)** *Within-chapter repetition.* Four walks into the cold; `:91`'s "walking through the cold to hand him everything" is the load-bearing one and `:9`'s is the wound, so the two in between are competing with both. Recommend varying `:23` and `:127`.

25. **`:127` — the closing paragraph.** *Rhythm.* One block carries the second hug, the kiss, the front hall, the cold, the plan, the walk home, and then the two-sentence *Tonight* cadence — so the cadence has to fight out from under transit business. The paragraph's true last sentence before the turn is the goodbye kiss. Recommend a break before "Then Vee went down," so the *Tonight* pair lands clean.
