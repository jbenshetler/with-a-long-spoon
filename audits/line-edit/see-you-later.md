## Findings

1. **"better lit, better put together" (line 11) / "a brighter, better-lit life" (line 13)** — *repetition.* The same distinctive compound recurs in adjacent paragraphs of the flashback, dulling both; vary or cut one occurrence.

2. **"like it was a thing she'd been hoping for" (line 13) / "as though the thing she had been hoping for had in fact been this" (line 29)** — *repetition, possibly design.* If the recurrence is meant to expose Randi's rehearsed move, it works; if not, the second rendering should not reuse the first's exact frame — confirm intent with the author before touching.

3. **"Her face was done, lightly, with the kind of light hand" (line 33)** — *precision.* "Lightly" and "light hand" double the same modifier inside one clause; delete one.

4. **"Her face was done, lightly" (line 33, Randi) / "faces done lightly and exactly" (line 101, the Chi Latte girls) / "everything easy and exact" (line 33)** — *repetition.* The identical done-lightly/exact formula describes both Randi and the girls the chapter later insists she is nothing like ("Randi wasn't in it," line 113) — the shared phrasing quietly undercuts that claim; vary one side.

5. **"That was all. She didn't hand anything back. She didn't take the bit's open door — didn't do the *oh, mine's a whole thing too*…" (line 47)** — *overwork.* Three negations restate the flatness that "Cassie," Cassie said (line 45) already landed; one of the three clauses (suggest "She didn't hand anything back.") can go.

6. **"there was nothing in it, no sulk, no edge, just a person declining a thing that wasn't hers in a voice with nothing in it but the fact" (line 79)** — *precision/overwork.* "Nothing in it" twice inside one sentence; cut the first instance and let the second carry it.

7. **"her breath warm and citrus" and "smelling like cold citrus" (both line 57)** — *repetition.* The scent is planted twice in one paragraph (and twice more at 33 and 127); the paragraph needs it once — cut one of the two line-57 instances.

8. **"Vee had been past it forty times" (line 67) / "had read it forty times from the bus stop" (line 91)** — *repetition.* The same "forty times" beat is spent twice on the same building; keep the line-91 use (where it pays off into the sign) and vary line 67.

9. **"Vee had seen girls perched up there through the glass from the bus stop" (line 117)** — *repetition.* Third rendering of the outside-looking-in vantage (window at 67, bus stop at 91); the payoff sentence "*that's where you sit if you belong here*" is what the beat needs — the vantage set-dressing before it can be compressed.

10. **"suddenly up and lit in the window" (line 117) / "lit up at the window" (line 123)** — *repetition.* Same image in consecutive scene-beats; keep one (line 123's belongs to the strong transcript passage) and vary the other.

11. **"She had not been keeping the words. She had been keeping the feeling… and the transcript was blank." (line 123) → "It was the best hour she'd had in months. She could not have told you a thing that was said in it." (line 125)** — *overwork.* Line 125's second sentence restates what 123 just closed on; the words-are-gone beat is then rendered a third time at 135 ("Already the words were going… came up with none of them") — 135 is the earned payoff, so trim one of the two earlier statements (125's second sentence is the cleanest cut).

12. **"total, undimmed" (37), "generous, complete" (71), "warm and complete" (95), "quick, warm, complete" (127)** — *repetition.* The paired-absolute adjective formula for Randi's warmth runs four times; the construction is the character's signature but the identical shape at this density becomes a tell — vary the rendering of one or two.

13. **"flat helpless want" (line 113)** — *precision.* "Flat" is doing designed work in the Cassie/Randi rhyme (flat *Cassie* 47/51, "flat and finished" 83, "flat fact" 131); this fifth use in an unrelated register blurs the pattern, and "flat" is also the least precise of the three modifiers here — cut or replace it.

14. **"a half-step too bright" (31), "a half-second long" (49), "a half-second after the alphabet" (95)** — *precision, minor.* Three "half-" measurements of social timing; the construction is good once, twice at most — vary the weakest (line 49's).
