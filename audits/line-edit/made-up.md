1. **`:11`** — "talk she made to his back, because that was how the kitchen was built, him at the sink and her at the table, and neither of them had ever minded."
   *Class: overwork.* `:9` has already placed him at the sink and her at the little table, and given the reason ("the kitchen was too small for two"); this re-explains the same geometry with the same causal *because*.
   **Recommend:** cut the explanatory middle so the sentence goes from "talk she made to his back" straight to "and neither of them had ever minded."

2. **`:11`** — "It was a good watching." … "It was a good back."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Three sentences apart, the identical `It was a good ___` frame; the second is the better one (concrete noun) and the first is the throat-clear before it.
   **Recommend:** recast the opening sentence off the frame so "It was a good back" arrives unrehearsed.

3. **`:15`** — "The heels that **finished** it …" / "The **finished** thing had crossed a stage for everyone but its maker."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Two sentences apart; the closing line is the paragraph's payload and the earlier use spends its key word first.
   **Recommend:** vary "finished" in the heels sentence and leave the closing line verbatim.

4. **`:15`** — "the dear black straps Randi had knelt to buckle in the boutique, he had never seen **under it** at all."
   *Class: word precision.* The heels are not under the dress; "under it" is reaching for *beneath the hem / finishing the line* and lands on a preposition that fights the picture, in a sentence whose front-loaded appositive already makes the reader wait.
   **Recommend:** replace "under it" with a locating phrase that names what the heels do to the hem, or cut the trailing prepositional phrase entirely.

5. **`:25`** — "gave her his whole attention, which with him was a physical event, like a door coming open."
   *Class: overwork.* Two figures for one gesture: an abstraction ("a physical event") followed by the image that actually does the work; the image alone is stronger without the label warning the reader it is coming.
   **Recommend:** cut "which with him was a physical event," and let the door simile carry it.

6. **`:33`** — the paragraph from "She had to slide into his boots" through "one click brighter."
   *Class: rhythm.* Three distinct movements in one block — the trip to the car, the return and the den's inventory, the walk past him — so the lamp-click, which is the paragraph's true last beat and one of the chapter's best small gestures, arrives at the end of a long haul.
   **Recommend:** break the paragraph after "like something sleeping."

7. **`:33`** — "He didn't ask, and she loved that he didn't ask —"
   *Class: overwork.* Interiority restating the action in the same breath and the same words; the chapter's method everywhere else is to let Pace's not-doing stand.
   **Recommend:** cut the second clause.

8. **`:37`** — "She brought it **down** over her head and it came **down** her like it remembered her"
   *Class: word precision.* "Down" twice in eleven words, both as direction, inside a sentence already carrying four coordinated clauses — the repetition is audible and does nothing.
   **Recommend:** change the first verb phrase so only the second "down" survives.

9. **`:37`** — "stood up four inches taller into the long line **the two of them** made."
   *Class: word precision.* The intended antecedent is dress-and-heels, but Vee and Pace are the chapter's only established pair, and "the two of them" reads as them for a beat before self-correcting.
   **Recommend:** name the two objects rather than pronouning them.

10. **`:43`** — "The wine stayed where he'd set it. She watched him make himself do it."
    *Class: within-chapter repetition (same gesture rendered twice).* His self-restraint is shown concretely by the untouched wine, then narrated abstractly — and then rendered a third and best time eight lines later at `:51` ("the putting-away cost him something she could see").
    **Recommend:** cut "She watched him make himself do it." and let the wine and `:51` carry it.

11. **`:45` / `:69`** — "His eyes went down her and stopped at her feet" / "His eyes went down her slow and came back up slower."
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The two viewings are a designed parallel, but the identical four-word stem makes the return read as recycling rather than rhyme — the case ruling #37 settles ("a designed return wants a different rendering"). The adjacent "for a long moment" (`:45`) / "For a moment" (`:69`) doubles the collision.
    **Recommend:** keep `:69` intact (it is the better sentence) and re-render `:45`'s look off a different stem.

12. **`:49`** — "His face did what the room's murmur had done, except silently, and to her it was louder."
    *Class: overwork.* The comparison lands on "except silently"; "and to her it was louder" is the metaphor being explained after it works, and it also ends the paragraph on commentary rather than on him.
    **Recommend:** cut the final clause.

13. **`:57`** — "She was gone back down the hall before he could **raise an objection**."
    *Class: word precision.* Legalistic idiom in a paragraph otherwise built from plain Anglo-Saxon; it puts a formal register on a moment of physical speed.
    **Recommend:** replace with a plainer verb for what he might have done.

14. **`:63`** — "He hadn't left the chair. She'd put him there, and he'd stayed put."
    *Class: overwork.* One fact stated three times in fifteen words, with "put" doing double duty in the second sentence.
    **Recommend:** keep one of the two sentences — the second, which carries the authorship.

15. **`:63`** — "the house's warmth moving over every part of her at once so that there was no forgetting, anywhere, what she wasn't wearing."
    *Class: rhythm.* An existential *there was* plus two stacked negatives ("no forgetting" / "wasn't wearing") clot the clause at the exact point the sentence should be most physical; the linter flags the existential independently (`#aa97516b88fe`).
    **Recommend:** recast so the warmth is the subject of an active verb and only one negative survives.

16. **`:69`** — "and let the looking be the touch, a man being handed something and taking his time about the taking."
    *Class: overwork.* "Let the looking be the touch" is the finished idea; the appositive restates it in a second figure, and "taking his time about the taking" repeats its own noun to make the point a third time.
    **Recommend:** end the sentence at "the touch."

17. **`:69`, `:77`, `:79`** — "let the **looking** be the touch" / "He hadn't stopped **looking**." / "let him **look**".
    *Class: within-chapter repetition (local density).* Three uses of the chapter's key verb in three consecutive short paragraphs; `:79` is the closing sentence and should have the word to itself.
    **Recommend:** thin one of the two earlier instances — `:77`'s beat, which the surrounding dialogue already implies, is the least load-bearing.
