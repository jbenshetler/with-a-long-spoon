# Line edit — dear (2026-08-04)

**Verdict:** 12 findings — 7 repetition, 2 overwork, 1 rhythm, 1 precision, 1 pre-ruled (FIX-AT).

**Calibration.** The chapter's own high bar: the entrance scan of the Smoothie Bird ("it covered her like an apology"); the May-I exchange and Vee's "I stopped keeping track of myself. I can't say it better than that."; the hand on the white tabletop and the whole *dear friend* paragraph ("Randi had spent it on her without appearing to count it"); the deep-water simile near the close; and the last three sentences ("She felt picked. She carried it the whole way home."). Everything below is measured against those.

## Findings

1. **Pre-ruled (FIX-AT — echo-rulings §8).** "It came out flat and proud, before she'd decided to be either." (Vee's "Not six years old when I kissed him" beat.) The five-word phrasing "before she'd decided to" is FIX-AT with the Vol-1 keeper already ruled at `the-bench.md:303`; the body-before-mind *shape* is protected and stays. Recommendation: vary the exact wording here while preserving action-preceding-decision.

2. **Repetition.** "Leggings, the cropped quarter-zips, hair up" and, four sentences later in the same paragraph, "in a room of bare arms and cropped quarter-zips" (the entrance paragraph). The same distinctive garment named twice in one paragraph dulls the second, stronger use. Recommendation: vary or generalize the first mention and let the "bare arms" sentence own the phrase.

3. **Repetition.** "the bright matched crowd closed over them" (Brooke's exit) and "back into the bright matched crowd she had come out of" (Randi's exit) — the identical three-word coinage fourteen lines apart; the second may be a designed rhyme (Randi returning to what Brooke came from), but as verbatim repeat it reads as reuse rather than callback. Recommendation: if the rhyme is wanted, sharpen one occurrence so the repeat is audibly deliberate; otherwise vary the first.

4. **Overwork.** "She did not know what it had cost Randi to say it… that it had cost something, that Randi had spent it on her without appearing to count it" (the *dear friend* paragraph) and then "whatever that was worth, she had spent it without once looking at the price" (the post-Brooke settling paragraph). The second passage re-renders the first's exact insight (cost / spent / count / price) one page later; the first rendering is the stronger. Recommendation: cut or compress the restated cost-spending clause in the later paragraph and let "Randi had stood up into all of it" carry that beat.

5. **Repetition.** "handed back nothing but more smile" (the taller one's first look) and "gave back nothing but more of itself" (Brooke's smile taking in the *dear friend*). Same construction — "gave/handed back nothing but more [smile]" — used for two different women within a page, which blurs the pair the scene wants distinct. Recommendation: keep one and render the other woman's non-response in a different shape.

6. **Repetition.** "which Vee felt well before she could have named it" (entrance paragraph) and "that she could not have named and did not want to" (the *dear friend* paragraph). The felt-before-named move is the book's grammar, but the near-identical "could (not) have named" wording twice in one chapter flattens the second, more important use. Recommendation: vary the wording of the first occurrence.

7. **Repetition (possible design — author call).** "feeling, frankly, like a person things happened to" (walking out of the lecture) and "like her life had finally tipped over into the kind of life that happened to people" (after "You little minx"). The same idea in nearly the same words at both the setup and the peak; if the escalation-rhyme is intended it needs the second to visibly build on the first rather than restate it. Recommendation: decide whether this is a designed refrain; if not, vary the second occurrence.

8. **Repetition.** Three reader-address "the way you/[someone] X" similes: "the way you grab a jacket on the way out without looking at it," "the way a little boy tells you about dinosaurs," "the way you stay aware of deep water for a while after you are back in the boat." Each is strong alone; three of the same shape in one chapter make a visible mannerism, and the jacket and deep-water ones bracket the chapter in the identical second-person form. Recommendation: keep the deep-water simile (the best of the three) and recast one of the earlier two out of the "the way you" frame.

9. **Rhythm (sentence openers — pileup per echo-rulings §14).** Within one page of the Brooke encounter: "And Vee sat up on her high chair…", "And Vee, with the two finished women looking at her…", "And something went through Vee at that…" — three same-shaped "And [Vee]" launches in a page, plus the chapter twice runs the fuller identical move "And Vee, who had meant to [say X / apologize], [did the truer thing]" (after "Because of him" and after "Do what"). Recommendation: break one opener in the Brooke-page cluster, and decide whether the who-had-meant-to construction is a deliberate rhyme; if not, recast its second use.

10. **Precision.** "Vee felt whatever the look was warm and spread on her face" (after Randi's read-it-across-the-room line). The syntax garden-paths ("felt [it] warm and spread" reads first as two adjectives, then must be re-parsed as verb), and "whatever" doubles the hedge already used two paragraphs up ("whatever had been in them was folded away"). Recommendation: untangle the verb so "spread" lands on first read, and lose one of the paired "whatever" hedges.

11. **Overwork.** "a drink the color of a bruise that sounded like a sentence handed down" (the counter). Two metaphors stacked on one drink, and the second's referent slips (the *order* sounded like a sentence, not the drink); each figure is good, together they crowd. Recommendation: keep one of the two figures.

12. **Repetition.** "She let a beat pass, turning it over as she had the other thing" (Randi on the kiss) and "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over" (the taller one's exit look). The second is the striking, load-bearing use — the appraisal made cold — and the earlier casual "turning it over" spends it first. Recommendation: vary the earlier occurrence so the exit-look sentence gets the phrase fresh. (Note also "let a beat pass" sits adjacent to the FIX-AT "a beat past" family, echo-rulings §2 — worth the reviewer's eye against the inventory.)
