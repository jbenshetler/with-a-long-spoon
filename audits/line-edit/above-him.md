# Line edit — above-him (2026-08-07)

**17 findings** — 4 rhythm, 5 within-chapter repetition, 4 overwork, 2 word precision, 2 pre-ruled echo items (from `echo-rulings.md`). No dialogue-shape findings (the chapter has one spoken word and it earns its isolation).

The chapter is strongest wherever it trusts the object. **§i's** "They hadn't made the kitchen. They hadn't made the hall." and its close, "She hadn't known a man could do that. She hadn't known a man would want to." (:13); the list of unspoken sayings — "the door, the spin, the slowness, the salmon, the warm tomatoes, the coming back to her" (:21); "In the morning her hair had kept the whole night in it." (:31) and the surgeon's glance at the finished hair (:33); the whole discovery run at :55–:61, "He'd given her the top one. The easy one. The one your hand goes to first," through "There was no note. There was never a note," to the final "Hers." The findings below are almost entirely places where the prose says a thing the chapter has just shown — the same failure mode each time, and the fix is nearly always subtraction.

---

1. **:9 — "and let it come back to her in the order it had happened"** (opening sentence of §i). *Overwork.* The retrospective structure is self-evident once the recollection starts; the clause announces the chapter's method before it runs. Cut the clause and let the sentence end on the body.

2. **:11 — "A week since the last time, the longest they'd gone: he'd been away presenting at a conference, several days of it, and she'd had a brutal week on campus on top, so the midweek night they'd have stolen never happened and the whole seven days stood empty between them."** *Rhythm.* A logistics sentence with four coordinated clauses sits between "He had the door open before she reached it" and the lift off the porch, and it kills the momentum both surrounding sentences have; "A week since the last time" and "the whole seven days stood empty" also state the same fact at both ends. Compress to the shortest form that carries the week's length, or move it after the spin.

3. **:15 — "and the strange part was that her clothes had gone *looser* for it"** (the eating/weight paragraph). *Word precision.* "the strange part was that" is a narrator pointing at its own irony; the italicized *looser* already does the pointing. Recast so the fact lands without the frame.

4. **:13 "That was the thing she still couldn't get used to." / :17 "That was another thing she was new to, that there could be an *after*"**. *Within-chapter repetition.* The same interiority frame four lines apart, both introducing a Pace-is-unlike-other-men observation; the second flags itself as a repeat ("another thing"). Vary the second into the observation itself rather than an announcement of one.

5. **:19 — "the old scar high on the inside of his thigh"**. *Pre-ruled — echo ruling #4 (FIX-AT).* One of two remaining Vol-1 slots (with `we-find-out:95`); the keeper is `famished:77`, and this occurrence is retrospective, so varied wording costs the reader nothing. Vary the framing phrase only — the anatomical location is canon and must not blur.

6. **:21 — "A man didn't do all of that for a girl he was only passing a season with. He had to feel it. He felt it in everything he did and said it in nothing, and she found, tonight, that she could live clear inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the saying."** *Rhythm.* Three sentences on one thought: "He had to feel it" is swallowed whole by "He felt it in everything he did," and the paragraph's true last beat is "said it in nothing." The final clause is the chapter's thesis stated flat after the list at the start of the paragraph has already dramatized it. Cut "He had to feel it," and consider ending the paragraph at "said it in nothing."

7. **:27 — "and let the day finish letting go of her"**. *Within-chapter repetition (local).* "let…letting" inside five words, in a chapter already dense with *let* constructions (:19 "was let to be", :21 "let it be true", :37 "let her weight go", :43 "let her breath out… let the worry go"). Break the doubling in this clause; leave the others.

8. **:35 "held nothing but his razor squared to the edge" / :41 "his razor squared to its edge" / :49 "his razor at its edge"**. *Within-chapter repetition.* Three renderings of one fixture in fifteen lines; :35 establishes it and :49 pays it off, so the middle one is the free instance. Cut the razor from :41.

9. **:35 — "She'd started bringing the bag, finally, gotten over herself and brought it, and the bag had done this, had thrown her whole disordered self across…"**. *Within-chapter repetition.* "bringing the bag… brought it… the bag" in one sentence, with "gotten over herself and brought it" restating "She'd started bringing the bag." Collapse to a single naming of the bag.

10. **:35 "all of it flung across a stretch of counter" / :41 "here was all of her flung across his order"**. *Within-chapter repetition.* The same verb and the same "all of it/her" frame six lines apart; :41 is the one doing the emotional work. Vary or cut the verb at :35.

11. **:37 — "She felt the warmth of him before the door"**. *Word precision.* The compression asks the reader to supply "before she heard the door," and it stalls on a first read; "warmth of him" also sits ten lines from ":27 the warm of him." Name what preceded the warmth, or cut the comparison and start on his arms.

12. **:41 — "Only the surface worry of a guest who's left a ring on good wood, the wish not to be a trouble to him, this contained and careful man in his own house, his razor squared to its edge, and here was all of her flung across his order like she owned the place."** *Overwork.* Four appositives stacked on one feeling, and the guest-and-ring image is complete on its own before the three restatements arrive. Keep the ring image and one clause; drop the rest (see also findings 8 and 10, both inside this sentence).

13. **:43 — "he smiled, the small private one"**. *Pre-ruled — echo ruling #3, unruled sub-question.* One of the three surviving Pace "small private smile" occurrences (`new-ordinary`, `above-him`, `ignition-scalding`) awaiting the author's ruling on whether the recurrence is his tell (PROTECTED) or a FIX-AT. Raise it for the ruling; do not vary it unilaterally at this review.

14. **:43 — "She let her breath out and leaned into him and let the worry go, mostly, and carried only the smallest tail of it out of the room with her."** *Rhythm.* "let the worry go, mostly" and "carried only the smallest tail of it" are the same qualified statement twice, and the section then adds four more short beats (*Had he minded.* / She couldn't tell. / He hadn't said. / He never said.) after it. One of the two halves of that sentence should go so the four-beat close lands clean.

15. **:53 — "…and a clear run of liner with room to spare, room for more of her, laid out like something prepared and waiting."** *Overwork.* The inventory of her things in his drawer, ending on empty room, *is* preparation and waiting; the simile explains a figure that has already landed. Cut the final clause and end on "room for more of her."

16. **:55 — "pressed in and fitted too tight together, made to share the one drawer that had plainly been built to hold half of what was crammed in it now."** *Overwork / word precision.* "pressed in," "fitted too tight," and "crammed" state one condition three times, and "plainly" tells the reader how to read what the sentence has shown. Keep one rendering of the tightness.

17. **:59 — "She stood with a hand on each open drawer and understood that she'd asked him a question last weekend without meaning to… He hadn't told her it didn't matter. He'd gone and made her a place instead, squared his own life down small to clear it, and left it for her to find with nothing said over it at all."** *Rhythm.* The paragraph re-narrates :55 in the abstract and re-explains :57's "There was never a note" — the reader has just watched both. The posture (a hand on each open drawer) is the only new thing in it. Cut most of the paragraph, keeping the physical image and at most one clause of the understanding.
