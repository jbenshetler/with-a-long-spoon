## Findings

1. **Rhythm — the opening sentence.** ":3 — "The athletic cafeteria had the better food and, more to the point, somewhere to actually sit, so that was where they ended up, the two of them, neither an athlete, at a table near the windows."" Six clauses hung off one another; the good material ("the two of them, neither an athlete") arrives after the sentence has already spent its energy on a logistical *so that was where they ended up*. **Recommendation:** break it after the reason and let the pair land in their own sentence.

2. **Word precision — "syncopated."** ":3 — "A low murmur under the high ceiling, syncopated by the clink of steel on hard plastic."" Syncopation is displacement against an established beat; a low murmur has no beat to displace, so the word is doing sonic impressiveness rather than describing. **Recommendation:** replace with a verb that names what the clink actually does to the murmur (punctuates, breaks, pricks).

3. **Overwork — Cassie's high-school beat.** ":7 — "They still treat me like I'm in high school. Not overtly. But I can tell they don't quite see me as an adult yet."" The first sentence is complete and concrete; the second hedges it and the third restates it in the abstract ("as an adult") — three sentences doing one sentence's work, and the abstraction is weaker than the image it explains. **Recommendation:** cut "But I can tell they don't quite see me as an adult yet."

4. **Dialogue shape — "A little bit of a dork."** ":7." Cassie's speech elsewhere compresses ("I know how she does"; "Not to your dad. To you."); the four-word run-up "A little bit of a" is grammatical where she would clip. **Recommendation:** shorten the qualifier to one word.

5. **Overwork — the narrator scoring Cassie.** ":23 — "Cassie didn't laugh, which was the right call."" The behavior is the characterization, and the appended judgment vouches for it — the same move the 2026-07-29 triage cut in the final paragraph ("She had a gift for knowing when a door was closed"). Note that :35 then gives Cassie her laugh at the right moment, which proves the point without help. **Recommendation:** cut ", which was the right call."

6. **Repetition — the tray as beat marker, three times.** ":9 "She smiled down at her tray."; :27 "She picked at the edge of her tray."; :41 "Vee looked at her tray."" Same prop, same downward look, used as the interstitial beat at roughly even intervals. :41 is structural — the look down *is* the failure to answer Cassie — and :9 and :27 are free. (Compare ruling #38's inventory-and-cut-from-the-free-set procedure.) **Recommendation:** vary or cut one of :9 / :27 so the tray is not the third answer to the same rhythmic need before :41 has to carry weight.

7. **Repetition — five paragraphs opened with "And."** ":17, :21, :29 ("And then"), :33, :37." The anaphora is doing real work — it renders the telling as one long spill — but five consecutive paragraph-openers is past the point where a reader hears design and into where they hear a default. Ruling #14's local-pileup test (3+ same-shaped openers on a page) applies. **Recommendation:** unhook one of the middle two (:29 or :33) so the run reads as chosen.

8. **Word precision — "doing the math" beside "a math PhD."** ":29 — "He was doing the math on what it cost… To Dad, a math PhD is a man with grey coming in."" The dead idiom collides with the literal noun twice in the same paragraph (and with ":25 He's a math PhD candidate"), producing a pun the sentence does not want and dulling both. **Recommendation:** recast the idiom to something that names the actual act (pricing it).

9. **Word precision — "really" twice, both filler.** ":29 "someone older than Pace really is"; :31 "my father would really start looking for the catch."" Two intensifiers eleven words apart, neither adding force; "than Pace is" and "would start looking" are both stronger. **Recommendation:** cut both.

10. **Overwork — the cooks/owns list stated twice.** ":31 — "I didn't tell him Pace cooks for me. Or that he owns his house. A man who cooks for you and owns his house, my father would really start looking for the catch. Too good is its own kind of suspicious."" The third sentence repeats the first two nearly verbatim before adding its point, and the fourth then abstracts the point a second time — three statements of one idea. The aphorism is the keeper. **Recommendation:** cut the verbatim re-list from the third sentence so the catch arrives directly off the pair.

11. **Word precision — the hedge in front of the praised line.** ":37 — "In some ways weird is worse than wrong."" "weird is worse than wrong" is the reader-quoted formulation (four of four panelists); "In some ways" apologizes for it before it lands. Removing the hedge does not touch the ruled-standing sentences that follow. **Recommendation:** cut "In some ways."

12. **Overwork — telegraphing the turn.** ":37 — "And the strange thing is, I'm not ashamed of it."" The signpost tells the reader the next clause is strange, which is exactly the reaction the clause should produce unassisted; the bare admission is stronger, and "The opposite, if anything" already supplies the surprise. **Recommendation:** cut the signpost clause and start on "I'm not ashamed of it."

13. **Repetition — "fit" three times (no action recommended).** ":25 "the parts that fit"; :37 "doesn't fit anywhere in how he sees the world"; :41 "nothing that fit in Cassie's frame."" :25 and :41 are a designed bookend in the same sense (what can be made to fit a telling); :37 is a different sense and sits inside a passage the author ruled left-standing. Logged so a later pass does not raise it as new. **Recommendation:** leave as written; if :25/:41 are ever reworked, keep the pair and let :37 be the one that gives.
