# Line edit — broken-in (2026-08-07)

**Verdict: 21 findings** — 7 within-chapter repetition, 6 overwork, 4 word precision, 3 rhythm, 1 pre-ruled (echo ledger #30). No dialogue-shape findings; the speech in this chapter is the strongest thing in it.

The chapter is at its best when it stops narrating and lets a hard object or a flat sentence carry the beat. The morning-sound inventory (`:3`, closing on "Then the door, and the day starting without her in it") is faultless. "She had not, it turned out, washed her makeup off the night before so much as relocated it" (`:11`) and "So she fixed the clown" (`:15`) are the chapter's tonal thesis in miniature — comic, unsorry, unbothered by its own cleverness. The walk (`:21`–`:27`) earns its swing, and "She had her apple. She had a plan. She had somewhere to be" is the best tricolon in the file. Vee's confession (`:73`) and Randi's answer to it (`:85`) are both pitched exactly right, and the shoe fitting (`:169`, `:173`) is the chapter's real event — sensory, blind, and structurally load-bearing. Almost every finding below is the prose adding a clause after one of these has already done the work.

## Findings

1. **`:7`** — "She'd slept in his shirt with nothing under it — *the way* she slept in it now… folded it *the way* it deserved". **Word precision / repetition.** Two "the way" constructions in a five-sentence paragraph, in a chapter that elsewhere avoids the tic; the second is also the vaguer of the two, where the fold's care is the concrete thing. Recommend varying or cutting one.

2. **`:1`** — "steadied by having a plan; she'd had one sip of the warm cup of it before Cassie's alarm hauled her back." **Overwork.** The plan-as-warm-drink figure is introduced and cashed in the same clause, and the "one sip" gloss explains a deprivation the alarm already enacts. Recommend cutting the cup or cutting the sip, not keeping both.

3. **`:9`** — "she gave herself over to her ablutions, and they began with the water, which ran cold enough to bite." **Word precision.** "Ablutions" is the chapter's one arch abstraction where a concrete noun is right there, and the two relative clauses bury the only verb that matters (the water biting). Recommend collapsing to the water.

4. **`:19`** — "a woman with bare legs in October. The tan lace-up boots went on over the bare legs… which over bare legs in October was the whole point." **Repetition.** Three "bare legs" in one paragraph, two of them the identical four-word phrase "bare legs in October"; the framing repeat is designed, the middle one ("over the bare legs") is slack connective tissue. Recommend cutting the middle occurrence.

5. **`:19`** — "the bowl the dorm kept full for exactly the kind of morning she was having, the no-time kind". **Overwork.** "Exactly the kind of morning she was having" already lands; the appositive names what the reader has just watched happen. Recommend cutting the trailing gloss.

6. **`:25`** — "the same cold as the water, the same clean daylight verdict, the world telling her she was awake and in it." **Overwork.** Three appositives for one sensation, the second of which ("clean daylight verdict") stacks two abstract modifiers onto the callback and makes it explain itself, where the bare recurrence of "verdict" from `:9` would carry alone. Recommend keeping one appositive.

7. **`:29`/`:35`** — "the beautiful one whose *warmth* had somehow left her feeling small" … "gave back nothing but more *warmth*." **Repetition.** The distinctive word is Brooke's whole characterization and is spent twice in seven lines without escalation. Recommend varying the second.

8. **`:49`** — "She'd been asleep *ten minutes* ago and gotten up for this… where Vee had just spent *ten minutes* at the sink". **Repetition.** The parallel is almost certainly intended, but the identical unit reads as an accident rather than a rhyme because nothing else in the sentence pairs. Recommend changing one measure so the contrast is the freckles, not the clock.

9. **`:49`** — "bare to the morning and uncovered". **Word precision.** Doubled modifier: "uncovered" restates "bare" in the same breath, in a sentence whose point is Randi's indifference. Recommend cutting one.

10. **`:55`** — "and Randi said *of course it was, he can dance, the bastard,* warm, **delighted**, and Vee laughed". **Pre-ruled (echo ledger #30).** Ruling #30 (FIX-AT, applied twice) keeps "delighted" only where delight is the grammatical subject doing something and cuts it where it is an adverbial label pinned to a speech Randi's own dialogue already performs — which is exactly this instance. Recommend cutting the label per the standing ruling.

11. **`:55`** — "the way it does when someone's letting you tell it instead of waiting for their turn." **Word precision.** The observation is good but shifts into second person and generalized present, stepping outside Vee's morning to deliver a maxim the scene is about to demonstrate anyway. Recommend keeping the observation inside Vee's frame.

12. **`:73`** — "I took the best thing about him — *the way he cares about people* — and threw it back at him like it was something to be ashamed of." **Overwork.** The metaphor's target is named in the dash-gloss before the throw lands, and the surrounding speech (and `:85`) supplies the referent without help. Recommend cutting the appositive.

13. **`:73`** — "He stood there a while, then set the drinks down and left. Slow. And I stayed, and I called a cab." **Rhythm.** "Slow." is the true last word of the confession — the one-word sentence is the wound; the cab is administrative and drops the paragraph out of its own register. Recommend ending on "Slow."

14. **`:87`** — "And the thing Vee had been holding clenched since the dance floor let go of her, all at once — because Randi had said so." **Rhythm / word precision.** The sentence about release has no main verb and has to be reparsed mid-line, so the reader tightens exactly where Vee lets go; "the dance floor let go of her" also puts a second, competing letting-go inside the same clause. Recommend giving the release a verb and one owner.

15. **`:97`** — "Not a suggestion — an instruction, and Vee stopped." **Overwork.** "Not today." plus Vee stopping already establishes the register; the narration diagnoses a tone the two words performed. Recommend cutting the gloss and keeping the stop.

16. **`:109`** — "which was a surrender wearing the coat of a defense, and they both knew it." **Overwork.** The figure is exact and self-evident; the tag explains it, and "coat" also collides with the literal coat Vee has been buttoned into for four sections. Recommend cutting the tag and reconsidering the garment noun.

17. **`:123`, `:165`** (with `:29`) — "running her like a fit **model**" … "like a **model** to her mark" (and "a girl with the beauty of a shampoo **model**"). **Repetition.** The same profession supplies three similes in one chapter, and the last two are both Randi positioning Vee — the same figure for the same relation, forty lines apart. Recommend keeping one of the two Randi instances.

18. **`:133`, `:165`** — "Randi's eye went on down her, all the way to her **bare feet**." … "her eyes went down and rested on Vee's **bare feet** on the carpet, and something moved in her face". **Repetition — the same gesture rendered twice.** Randi's gaze traveling down to Vee's bare feet is the chapter's most charged small beat and it is staged twice with the same body part and the same direction of travel; the second is the one with consequence (it triggers the crouch and the shoe). Recommend cutting or radically changing `:133`.

19. **`:169`** — "She couldn't see any of it… all of it under the cloth where her eyes couldn't follow… and never watch it happen" (with `:173` "a catch she couldn't see"). **Overwork.** Not-seeing is the paragraph's engine and is asserted four times in eight sentences; asserted once and then simply obeyed, the blindness is felt instead of argued. Recommend keeping the strongest single statement.

20. **`:169`, `:173`** — "a strap drawn **snug** at the ankle" … "the straps drawn **snug**". **Repetition.** Identical three-word construction for the identical action in consecutive shoe fittings; the third use at `:189` ("Snug — there.") is Randi's word and should be the one the reader hears fresh. Recommend varying one of the first two.

21. **`:173`** — "There was a whole architecture built into the shoe she'd never once felt in a shoe" and "the strange thing, the thing she turned over standing there, was that the holding didn't bind. It steadied." **Word precision / overwork.** "Shoe… shoe" in one clause is a stumble in the chapter's best paragraph; and the double announcement ("the strange thing, the thing she turned over") pre-labels a realization whose payload — "Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less" — is strong enough to arrive unheralded. Recommend one noun in the first clause and no announcement before the last.

22. **`:209`** — "**a whole day** and a night in front of her… a beautiful pair of somebody else's shoes, and **a whole day** to get ready in." **Rhythm / repetition.** The closing paragraph reaches its true last beat at "at Pace, at his door," then restates the same inventory in a second tricolon that reuses "a whole day" verbatim, so the chapter ends one beat late and slightly slack. Recommend cutting one of the two inventories.
