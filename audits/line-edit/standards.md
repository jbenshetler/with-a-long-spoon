# Line edit — standards (2026-08-04)

**Verdict: 7 findings** — 1 pre-ruled echo (FIX-AT), 3 overwork, 2 repetition, 1 precision. No rhythm or dialogue findings; both are unusually strong here.

**Calibration.** The chapter's own standard is set by: "She had a voice the way other people had a perfume. It preceded her into rooms and stayed in them after she left" (the Brooke introduction); "Randi's laugh came at the same pitch as Kinsey's. The matching was very fast. She had not had time to choose the pitch"; "She had come to the closet to do something and could not, for a moment, locate what"; and the aborted duvet comparison ("The duvet was the duvet. It was light and synthetic and adequate. It was not — it was a duvet."), which trusts the reader with the unfinished thought. The interrogation's escalating "fraction-of-a-second" measure and the *did-not* anaphora of the bedroom section are design, not tics. The findings below are places where the prose explains what those passages prove it can leave shown.

## Findings

1. **Pre-ruled (FIX-AT, echo-rulings #6): "a held breath."** Final paragraph: "…the way a held breath stops feeling held. The breath was held. She did not feel herself holding it." The ledger rules "a held breath" (6×/6 ch) spent — keep only the strongest book-wide; this occurrence is a candidate to vary at review time unless the author elects it as the keeper. Apply per ruling; replacement wording to be drafted and author-approved at review.

2. **Overwork — same passage, metaphor restated after it lands.** "…the way a held breath stops feeling held. The breath was held. She did not feel herself holding it." The simile already does the work; the two flat sentences after it re-say it twice, and the chapter's own duvet passage shows it knows how to stop short. Recommendation: whichever way the FIX-AT ruling resolves, cut one of the two restating sentences so the figure lands once.

3. **Overwork — "The quiet was deliberate"** (¶2 opening), after ¶1 has already said "which was already wrong. Randi did not close doors quietly." The topic sentence restates the shown before the paragraph moves to the new information (the *why*). Recommendation: cut the opening sentence and let the paragraph begin at "She wanted the door shut…"

4. **Overwork — "The not-standing was the move;"** (the "Wait—" paragraph: "Friendly. The laptop still open. Brooke had not stood. The not-standing was the move; Brooke was the chapter president…"). The three fragments before it have already shown the move; naming it as "the move" is the one editorializing beat in an otherwise clean run, and the rest of the sentence (president, doesn't need to stand) carries the analysis. Recommendation: cut "The not-standing was the move;" and let the sentence begin at "Brooke was the chapter president…"

5. **Repetition — "small" cluster (7 uses).** "the small unhurried click" (¶1), "her small laugh" (Sloane), "The small bright laugh" (Kinsey), "the small relief" (Brooke), "the small ordinary noise… the small ordinary noise" (stairs paragraph — this doubling is designed), "the small laugh that meant *what could I do*" (duvet section). Individually each is in-voice; the density flattens the modifier so the designed instances (the click, the doubled "small ordinary noise") lose their charge. Recommendation: keep the click, the stairs doubling, and one laugh; drop "small" from two of the remaining laugh/relief instances.

6. **Repetition — the "laugh that meant X" construction twice.** "the laugh that meant nothing on its own and was therefore available for anything" (Sloane) and "the small laugh that meant *what could I do*" (Randi, duvet section). Both are excellent alone; the identical frame ~80 lines apart reads as formula rather than mirror, and if the mirror *is* designed it isn't visibly pointed. Recommendation: flag for the author — keep both only if the Sloane/Randi mirror is intended; otherwise vary the frame of the Sloane instance (the stronger of the two is Randi's).

7. **Precision — "warmly" (Brooke's third warmth in five exchanges).** "Brooke's voice was always warm" → "warm as ever" → "'Mm, that place is so good for that,' Brooke said, warmly." The first two establish the signature; the adverb on the third is the weakest form of the same note and the line's content already carries it. Recommendation: cut "warmly" from the attribution ("Brooke said.").
