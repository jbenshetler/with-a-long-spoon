# Line edit — recognized-method (2026-08-07)

**Verdict: 15 findings — 5 repetition, 5 overwork, 3 word precision, 1 rhythm, 1 dialogue shape.** No pre-ruled FIX-AT entries in `echo-rulings.md` name this chapter; nothing here touches a PROTECTED echo.

The chapter is strong and mostly lands where it means to. Its best passages set the standard the findings are measured against: the height reversal at :23 ("her eyes came level with his and kept going, came to rest a fraction above them… like a missed stair") and its social consequence at :27; the pie taken out of her hands at :31, with the scarf laid back "a half-second longer over the silk than the errand needed" and the vanity that lifts its head under the wreckage; Vee's confession at :55–:59, where the blonde's lilt comes out of her own mouth and she hates it, closing on "some part of me was just standing there waiting for one"; Pace's "Thank you for telling me what she said," which is the chapter's whole argument in seven words; "It landed. Once." at :101; the soaking pan left "exactly where it wanted to be" at :109; and the close at :121, "Not *you're beautiful*, which she'd have argued. *Always.*" These are the register at full power — clean, unexplained, trusting the reader.

## Findings

1. **:9 — "the way Randi kept her own" / "the way it had in Randi's room" / "the way a uniform helps"** (repetition). Three instances of the same filtered simile-connector inside a single opening paragraph, the reader's first contact with the voice; the construction is the book's, but the density here reads as reflex rather than choice. *Recommendation: keep one — "the way a uniform helps" is the load-bearing one — and recast the other two as direct images.*

2. **:9 — "There was a structure built into the shoe that took her foot and held it and steadied her, and steadying was most of what she needed. The leather was worth more than anything she owned. It helped to put them on, the way a uniform helps."** (overwork). Three sentences deliver one idea — the shoe as borrowed armor — and the third glosses what the first already showed; the chapter elsewhere trusts a single image (":101"). *Recommendation: cut one of the three, most likely the uniform gloss or the "steadying was most of what she needed" repeat-back.*

3. **:11 — "It had still seemed… like exactly the right amount of funny. It had seemed like a way to walk in… it seemed like what it was, which was a joke"** (repetition). Three "seemed" in four sentences; the paragraph's turn (then-funny / now-not) is doing good work but the verb machinery is visible. *Recommendation: vary the first two so only the closing "it seemed like what it was" lands on the word.*

4. **:23 / :27 — "took the bottom out of everything she'd rehearsed" and, four lines later, "the apology she'd built coming apart in her mouth into something that wasn't even sentences"** (repetition — same beat twice). The collapse of the rehearsed speech is rendered twice in quick succession, then again at :53 ("the whole structure she'd come in behind simply wasn't there anymore") and again at :105 ("no clean machine"). Four is a motif; two four lines apart is a stutter. *Recommendation: let :23 end on the physical wrongness alone and keep the speech's collapse for :27, where the dialogue enacts it.*

5. **:27 — "they were the polished thing, the right thing to wear to apologize"** (word precision). Two abstract "thing" placeholders in one clause, in a paragraph otherwise built of hard nouns (buckles, heels, inch). *Recommendation: collapse to one and let a concrete noun carry it.*

6. **:31 — "pushed the tail of it half off her shoulder" … "two fingers under the tail of it"** (repetition). "The tail of it" twice in three sentences inside the chapter's best paragraph. *Recommendation: vary the first mention; the second is the one that matters, since it's what his fingers find.*

7. **:9 / :31 / :45 / :121 — "the way a uniform helps" / "the way you take a glass from someone about to drop it" / "the way you feel walking out of an exam" / "the way you hold a thing you mean to keep"** (repetition — chapter-wide construction). Four generalizing second-person similes; the last is the strongest and the one the ending rests on. *Recommendation: keep :31 and :121, recast :45 (and :9 per finding 1) so the closing simile arrives unpreceded.*

8. **:73 — "and she understood that he had set it there for her to find, and the small deliberate kindness of it, a man moving quietly that morning laying out a clean folded shirt so she'd have something when she woke, did something to her chest."** (overwork). The sentence re-narrates the folded clean shirt the paragraph has already described in detail ("clean, washed soft… squared off in a neat fold"), then names the feeling the image had already produced. *Recommendation: cut the restaging appositive and let the fold itself do the work.*

9. **:75 — "dressed, in jeans and a dark shirt with the sleeves shoved up his forearms, washing the dishes… up before her, sleeves up, doing the dishes"** (overwork/repetition). The same inventory appears twice in one paragraph, forty words apart. *Recommendation: cut the second listing and keep only "the unbearable ordinary fact of him."*

10. **:75 / :97 / :103 — "bright with early-afternoon light" / "the bright afternoon light" / "in the flat bright light of the afternoon"** (repetition). The same light rendered three times, the last two six lines apart, where its job at :103 is to be pitilessly exposing and it can't be if it's already been said twice. *Recommendation: keep :75 to establish and :103 to expose; cut the middle one.*

11. **:81 / :85 / :97 / :103 — "She felt the small economical movements…" / "She felt the laugh go through his back" / "She felt it come around and settle" / "She felt her own face change. She felt her eyes go somewhere far off"** (overwork — filter verbs). The kitchen section's whole point is body outrunning mind, and five filtered perceptions in twenty lines put a pane of glass in front of it; :85's "before she heard it" earns its filter, the others don't, and the three consecutive "She felt" at :103 is the densest patch in the chapter. *Recommendation: keep :85, render the rest direct.*

12. **:91 — "The suddenness of it, fingers dug in exactly where she couldn't bear it, a hand that knew what it was doing to a ticklish sleep-warm body"** (overwork). Three appositives explaining "she came apart" after it has already landed, plus the doubled modifier "ticklish sleep-warm." *Recommendation: keep one appositive — the knowing hand — and cut the explanation of the suddenness.*

13. **:95–:97 — "He reached over without hurry and turned off the tap." / "The kitchen went quiet. The water stopped and the room was just the two of them breathing"** (rhythm/overwork). The single-line paragraph at :95 is a perfect beat; :97 then re-reports the tap ("The water stopped") and re-reports the quiet in the same breath, blunting the silence it's describing. *Recommendation: cut "The water stopped and" so :97 opens straight into the two of them breathing.*

14. **:97 / :101 / :103 — "the thin shirt" / "the thin cotton of her underwear" / "the thin worn cotton"** (word precision). Three "thin" in seven lines, all naming the same insufficiency of fabric. *Recommendation: vary two of the three; the underwear at :101 has the strongest claim on the word.*

15. **:83 — "That is the entire point of soaking, you let them sit and the water does the work, and here you are undermining the soak."** (dialogue shape). Vee's mock-legal bit is one of the chapter's pleasures, but "That is" runs grammatical where a girl draped on a man's back mid-lobby would contract; the surrounding speech already compresses ("They're supposed to soak"). *Recommendation: contract it and leave the rest of the aria alone.*
