## Findings

1. **Repetition — line 5, opening sentence.** "The Mercedes was **at the curb** when Vee came down… and it sat **at the curb** the way expensive things sit." The second "at the curb" blunts an otherwise clean expensive-things cadence in the chapter's very first breath. Recommend cutting the second "at the curb" (the sentence already knows where the car is).

2. **Repetition / PRE-RULED (echo ledger FIX-AT #2, "a beat past").** Line 7: "it stayed **a beat past** when it might have stopped"; line 17: "held the look **a beat past** nothing." Book-wide FIX-AT tic, and doubled within this chapter ten lines apart. Recommend keeping the line-7 use (the smile, the chapter's opening tell) and varying line 17 per the ledger.

3. **Overwork — line 13, the wind paragraph.** Two full personifications back to back: Vee's hair "having a torrid affair with the wind, the whole boisterous length of it carrying on without her," then Randi's hair "flirting with the wind and had decided how far it would go." The contrast is the point, but two courtship metaphors in one paragraph split the joke; the second (Randi's controlled flirtation) is the one doing character work. Recommend keeping one personification and letting the other hair be rendered plainly.

4. **Repetition — "boisterous" twice, lines 13 and 15.** "the whole **boisterous** length of it" / "kept her own **boisterous** hair pinned down." A felt word spent twice in adjacent paragraphs. Recommend cutting the second occurrence ("her own hair" carries it).

5. **Precision — line 15.** "studied Randi the way you **study** what you'd like to **learn**" — study/learn is a near-tautology; the chapter's other definitional moves ("the way expensive things sit," "the way money is lit") each add something the first term didn't have. Recommend sharpening the second term so it earns the construction.

6. **Repetition / precision — "glamorous," lines 15 and 164.** "made herself look at the road instead of **the glamorous girl**" / "a girl in a beautiful car in the sun with the most **glamorous** woman she knew." The line-15 use is also a label where the paragraph just spent four clauses showing the glamour. Recommend cutting or replacing the line-15 "glamorous girl" tag and letting line 164's use stand alone.

7. **Rhythm / overwork — line 21, the Sinatra sentence.** The gloss "— the one where he gives in to a woman he knows he shouldn't, calls it witchcraft, that ancient pull he wouldn't switch off even if he could —" explains the song's thematic relevance after the allusion has already landed, and stretches an already long sentence past its shape. Recommend trimming the gloss to the identifying stroke and cutting the explanation of what the song means.

8. **Repetition — the self-referential doubling construction, five times.** Line 11 "she set the thought where she set those thoughts"; line 31 "did the thing she did"; line 59 "So she did what she did"; line 71 "her voice do the thing it did"; line 73 "listened the way she listened." Each is good; five in one chapter makes the move audible as a move. Recommend keeping the two strongest (line 31, where the habit is then unpacked; line 73, Randi's listening) and varying the others.

9. **Repetition — the lamp/beam attention imagery, four instances.** Line 45 "turned the full warm beam of her attention on… like a room light coming on"; line 67 "the full beam came back on, aimed and warm"; line 76 "Randi's whole face was turned to her like a lamp"; line 82 "the beam full up again." A recurring beam that switches on and off may be design (Randi's attention as appliance), but lines 67 and 82 render it in near-identical wording. Recommend keeping the establishing image (45) and one return, and varying the wording of whichever returns remain.

10. **Overwork — line 73, paragraph past its last sentence.** After "Randi listened the way she listened, which was completely" and the concrete tracking of her eyes, the bridge "It was, Vee thought, the nicest part of Randi, this. The way she made you feel like the sort of girl who got asked things and had answers worth hearing" restates what the tracking just showed; "Nobody else listened like that." is the true close. Recommend cutting or compressing the two explanatory sentences so the paragraph steps straight to its last line.

11. **Repetition / overwork — line 158, the wine arithmetic re-run.** "The glass of wine alone had cost more than everything Vee had eaten" repeats the computation the chapter already performed devastatingly at line 37 (the Rombauer against the soup and the coffee); "She did not do the arithmetic out loud" is stronger if the narration hasn't just done the arithmetic either. Recommend cutting or drastically trimming the restating sentence.

12. **Repetition (soft — verify design) — "folded," lines 82, 88, 138.** Randi's tightness "folded away somewhere"; Vee "had meant to keep this part folded up"; the secret "she had folded up the smallest and pushed the deepest." If the fold is a deliberate shared verb (both women folding things out of sight), protect it; if not, three uses in fifty lines is one too many and the line-82 use is the most replaceable.
