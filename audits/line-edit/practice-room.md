# Line edit — practice-room (2026-08-07)

**Verdict: 20 findings** — 6 overwork, 6 within-chapter repetition, 3 rhythm, 2 word precision, 1 dialogue shape, 2 pre-ruled (echo ledger).

The chapter's own standard is set by four passages, and the rest should be measured against them. **Line 67** — "A moment late, she realized what she'd just agreed to. Randi's smile brightened. It went down through her like a swallow of something warm." — three short sentences, no explanation, the consent and its cost delivered in the gap between them. **Line 75**, the single long unbroken sentence returning her to the kitchen, is the chapter's best rhythm: the syntax refuses to stop because the memory does. **Line 85** ("she wasn't only telling it. She was doing it again. Here. Now. In a piano closet with Randi's eyes on her.") earns its short stabs. And **line 121** — "made by nothing but a woman talking in a chair" — is the chapter's cleanest single stroke: concrete, unhelped, and it does not tell us what it means. Where the chapter falls short, it is almost always the same failure: a strong beat followed by a sentence that translates it. The professor's lecture, the shoes, and the goodbye are all well-shaped and mostly left alone below.

## Findings

3. **Lines 39, 113, 127 — "the whole bright weight of her attention coming on like a lamp" / "the whole worldly weight of it in the gesture" / "she looked up with the whole warm face turning on at once."**
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Three uses of `the whole ___ [weight/face]` in one chapter, two of them also "turning on like a light." Line 127's "whole warm face turning on at once" is the lamp figure a second time, un-signalled, in a chapter where line 39 already spent it at the hinge — and 127 is the weaker instance, since the point there is Randi's *easy* dismissal, not her beam.
   **Recommendation:** keep 39's lamp with the frame varied (finding 1), and recast 113 and 127 off both the `whole ___` frame and the switching-on figure.

4. **Line 53 — "something in her had gone very still and very interested, the way a cat goes still."**
   *Class: overwork.* Three problems stacked: doubled "very," the simile restating the verb it follows ("gone very still … the way a cat goes still"), and the `Something [verb]ed in [X]` frame that took its first FIX-AT at `gone:45` (ruling #32). The chapter can do this in five words — line 83 ("The room held still") proves it.
   **Recommendation:** cut the simile or the "very still," not both halves of the same observation, and prefer a visible act to the "something in her" frame.

5. **Lines 93–95 — "She couldn't find the end of it. … Randi let her hang there a moment, kindly, the way you let someone find their feet. Then she gave her the end of the sentence by answering the question Vee couldn't ask."**
   *Class: overwork.* The unfinished question is stated three times in three sentences: *couldn't find the end* → *gave her the end of the sentence* → *the question Vee couldn't ask*. The second sentence is also pure stage direction for a move Randi's next line ("Oh, baby") performs unaided.
   **Recommendation:** cut "Then she gave her the end of the sentence by answering the question Vee couldn't ask." and let the dialogue do it.

6. **Line 87 — "'I bet he did,' Randi said softly. That was all. She didn't say what she herself could see."**
   *Class: overwork.* "That was all" already renders the withholding; the following clause names what was withheld, which is precisely the information the beat's power depends on not having. It also adds a third "see" inside three lines (81, 87, 89).
   **Recommendation:** cut "She didn't say what she herself could see."

7. **Line 113 — "One shoulder lifted and fell, the whole worldly weight of it in the gesture."**
   *Class: overwork.* The shrug is the worldliness; the appositive tells the reader how to read a gesture that has just been shown cleanly. "One shoulder lifted and fell" is the chapter's register; the tail is not.
   **Recommendation:** cut "the whole worldly weight of it in the gesture."

8. **Line 115 — "and the thing her body had been building toward since the morning, the actual physical fact of it, warm and undeniable between her legs, and she stood up too fast."**
   *Class: rhythm / word precision.* The middle clause has no verb — it reads as a dropped predicate rather than a designed fragment, because it sits inside a chain of finite "and" clauses on either side. "The actual physical fact of it" is also an abstraction inserted between two concrete phrases that already have the fact.
   **Recommendation:** give the middle clause a verb or break the sentence before it, and cut "the actual physical fact of it."

9. **Line 123 — "Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it."**
   *Class: overwork.* The caught hand pressed flat to her own knee has already refused; "Not here. Not over a story." is the refusal in her voice. The final sentence is the moral gloss on both, and its two halves say the same thing twice.
   **Recommendation:** cut "To do that would be to say yes to it, and" and let the refusal stand as the second half alone.

10. **Lines 125 and 133 — "She never did settle whether Randi had seen. There was no way to go back and look." / "And Vee couldn't tell whether she had."**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition (same beat rendered twice).* The unresolvable question of whether Randi saw is closed out twice, eight lines apart, and 133 is the stronger position — it lands on Randi's performed innocence and ends a paragraph. Line 125's second sentence additionally explains its own first.
    **Recommendation:** cut both sentences at 125 and let 133 carry the beat alone.

11. **"warm" — lines 5, 19, 67, 75, 77, 115, 127, 133.**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Eight uses in 140 lines, and the crucial ones are close together: "something warm" (67), "the warm wet weight of his hand" (75), "It was warm" (77), "warm and undeniable" (115). The word is the chapter's temperature word and cannot be spent this freely; 77's flat "It was warm" is the one that must survive, since it is Vee's own report.
    **Recommendation:** thin the decorative uses (67's "something warm," 127's "whole warm face," 133's "warm as anything") so 75/77 keep their charge.

12. **Line 75 — "the warm wet weight of his hand landing where the shirt had ridden up."**
    *Class: echo candidate (ruling #5 family).* Ruling #5 protects the construction and varies the exact wording, with `the-bench` as the Vol-1 keeper. "Warm wet weight of" is a variant rather than the literal string, so it is not automatically in scope — but it is the same three-beat rendering, and it sits in the chapter's single best sentence, which argues for either claiming it or varying it deliberately rather than by default.
    **Recommendation:** raise with the author as a #5 sibling; if it stands, record it, and note it also collides locally with 115's "soft wet heaviness."

13. **Lines 3 and 29 — "not for any reason she could have said out loud in a room with the lights on" / "felt the color come up her neck for no reason she'd have admitted to."**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Same construction, same function (unsayable motive), twenty-six lines apart. Line 3's version is far the better — the lights-on clause does real work — and 29's is a throwaway on a blush the sentence has already shown.
    **Recommendation:** cut "for no reason she'd have admitted to" at 29.

14. **Lines 5 and 21 — "the way it always did, a half-second ahead of the rest" / "The hour came apart the way it always did."**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The identical four-word habitual tag twice in sixteen lines. (Line 135's "the way she always did" is PROTECTED under ruling #10 and is not at issue — which is exactly why the two unprotected ones should not crowd it.)
    **Recommendation:** vary or cut at 21, the weaker of the two.

15. **Lines 95 and 141 — "the way you let someone find their feet" / "She noticed it the way you notice a held note after the song has already moved on."**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The second-person explanatory simile twice; 141's is the chapter's closing image and must not have a rehearsal. Cutting 95 (finding 5) resolves this.
    **Recommendation:** protect 141 by cutting 95.

16. **Line 27 — "There was no time to go anywhere. It was Randi who thought of the practice rooms in the music building…"**
    *Class: rhythm.* The paragraph is moving well in reported speech, and "There was no time to go anywhere" is a flat logistics sentence inserted to justify what the next sentence already implies. "It was Randi who thought of" is also a cleft that buries the agency it means to mark.
    **Recommendation:** cut the logistics sentence and let Randi's idea arrive on its own.

17. **Line 25 — "It was a small thing and she didn't notice she'd done it."**
    *Class: overwork.* Line 23's "To Randi. Only to Randi." has just made the point with maximum compression; the paragraph then opens by telling us it was small and unnoticed before going on to demonstrate the reversal concretely (Randi's hand at her elbow, then Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist). The demonstration is excellent and does not need the thesis sentence in front of it.
    **Recommendation:** cut the opening sentence and begin the paragraph at "For weeks it had gone the other way."

18. **Line 107 — "You know what that does to a person, counting your own. It was humiliating."**
    *Class: dialogue shape / word precision.* Randi has just given three concrete images (over his knee, skirt up, counting out loud) and then supplies the abstract label. She is a woman who lets the listener do that work — the whole scene turns on her *not* naming things (line 87). The label also pre-empts line 111's reversal, which is stronger if humiliation is inferred rather than announced.
    **Recommendation:** cut "It was humiliating."

19. **Lines 121 and 135 — "Under the thin fluorescent light" / "the bad fluorescent hum."**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* A distinctive set-noun twice in fourteen lines, both times as generic institutional atmosphere. 135's is the better use (sound, returning her to the world after the soundproofed room).
    **Recommendation:** vary or cut at 121. ("Thin" also appears at 89 and 121.)

20. **Lines 115 and 131 — "she stood up too fast" / "It came out a beat too fast."**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition (minor).* "Too fast" twice as the tell of the same suppressed state, sixteen lines apart. Both are good; the second is better placed, and "it came out" is PROTECTED (#15).
    **Recommendation:** low priority — vary 115 if it can be done without touching the sentence's rhythm.
