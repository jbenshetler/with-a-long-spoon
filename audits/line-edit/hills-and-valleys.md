## Findings

1. **`:1` — "when the weather let them" / "they talked where it let them"** (opening paragraph, two clauses apart).
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The same permission-granting verb twice in the chapter's first sentence-and-a-half; the second use is the meaningful one (the road governs the talk) and the first blunts it before it arrives.
   **Recommendation:** vary or cut the first "let them" so the second lands as the chapter's governing idea.

2. **`:3` "and never once let it go unmentioned" / `:13` "and it had never once cost him anything"**
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* "Never once" is a distinctive intensifier and both uses close a paragraph on the same cadence, ten lines apart, without design linking them.
   **Recommendation:** vary one of the two.

3. **`:13` — "She remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather, and she'd been doing it across this handlebar for two years, and it had never once cost him anything."**
   *Class: overwork.* Three ideas chained where the simile has already done the work; the two-year clause and the cost clause say the same thing (this is old, this is safe) in sequence, and the paragraph's true last sentence may be "Sheri was the kind."
   **Recommendation:** cut one of the two trailing clauses.

4. **`:19` — "And then he was past the name before he'd decided to be"**
   *Class: pre-ruled echo (FIX-AT, `echo-rulings.md` #8 / #23).* This is one of the two remaining Volume One occurrences of the "before he'd decided" construction; three of six slots are spent and this one **varies by default** — it may be kept only on the author's explicit direction at this review.
   **Recommendation:** vary the construction here unless the author explicitly keeps it.

5. **`:21`+`:23`+`:25` — "the look she got when she'd caught him out." / "Listen to you," she said. / He didn't know what there was to listen to.**
   *Class: overwork.* The narration diagnoses the catch before Sheri delivers it; the dialogue and his blankness afterward are the whole beat, and the pre-labelling steals the surprise from both.
   **Recommendation:** cut the "caught him out" gloss and let "Listen to you" be the first news of it.

6. **`:27` — "Her bike was a small frame, not much bigger than a child's, and there was never as much of her as the work she got out of it"** (against `:3` "She was small on the bike and fast on it, all of her folded down into the work").
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The same observation — small body, disproportionate output, "the work" — rendered twice in the same terms; "small" appears three times across the two paragraphs. The `:27` version is the better one (it has the ponytail and the standing legs), so `:3` is the redundant statement.
   **Recommendation:** thin the small-and-fast claim at `:3` to the fact he needs there (she outclimbs him) and let `:27` show it.

7. **`:27` — "He marvelled at it, as ever, and saved his breath for the hill"**
   *Class: overwork.* "Marvelled" names the response the preceding image just produced; the rest of the sentence (saving breath, arriving a length behind) already carries the admiration in action.
   **Recommendation:** cut the naming of the emotion and keep the physical clauses.

8. **`:31` — "She'd do it, too. She could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years; he'd seen her do it, and seen what it was worth on the other side, which was everything."**
   *Class: overwork / repetition.* "She'd do it" and "he'd seen her do it" repeat the verb inside four clauses, and "which was everything" explains the loyalty the image ("keep it burning for years") has already delivered; the paragraph's charge is in the middle clause and drains after it.
   **Recommendation:** cut either the opening "She'd do it, too" or the closing gloss.

9. **`:45` — "the cold going out of the morning, and that was the whole of it."**
   *Class: rhythm (ends one beat late).* The cold leaving the morning closes the frame opened at `:5` ("The cold sat in his fingers") and is the chapter's true last image; the summarizing tag after it tells the reader the scene is over instead of letting the temperature do it.
   **Recommendation:** end the chapter on the cold.
