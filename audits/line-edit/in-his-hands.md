# Line edit — in-his-hands (2026-08-07)

**Verdict: 19 findings** — 6 within-chapter repetition, 6 overwork, 3 word precision, 3 rhythm, 1 dialogue shape (plus 9 echo candidates listed at the end; no FIX-AT entry in `echo-rulings.md` names this chapter).

The chapter is at its best where it stays outside her head. The opening paragraph (`:7`) is the strongest thing here — the long unbroken run of the orgasm, the red curtain, and the landing on "with no grace left to spend" is a single breath that earns its length. `:53`, Pace posing her limb by limb with a kiss at each placement, is the chapter's architectural centre and needs almost nothing. `:55`'s photo sequence — the shutter, the tilt of the shade, "Hm… Yeah. That's the one," and the look over the top of the phone — is exact, unhurried, and does its characterization entirely in behavior. `:75`–`:77`'s cooking, post-compression, moves well. `:93`'s "each word set down clean and apart from the last" and `:99`'s wine moved an inch are the chapter's two best small gestures. What follows are places where the chapter falls short of that standard, not of any external one.

## Findings

1. **`:9`–`:11` — the unstoppable smile rendered twice.**
   "he was already looking at her, smiling, and she smiled back and could not have stopped it if she'd tried" … then "the smile still on him, and she looked back down at him and the smile would not leave her face."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The same gesture — his smile, her helpless answering smile — is staged twice in consecutive paragraphs with the same shape (his smile noted, hers noted as involuntary). The second is the better placed of the two (it sets up `:13`); the first spends the beat early.
   **Recommendation:** cut the involuntary clause from `:9`, letting the paragraph end on "she smiled back."

2. **`:17` — the theory spoiled three times.**
   "Which rather spoiled her theory, and pleased her to no end. *Not just about sex for him, then.*"
   *Class: overwork.* The action (his lips closing over her risen nipple) spoils the theory; the narration says the theory is spoiled; the italic then restates the corrected theory. Three passes at one turn. The italic is the one worth keeping — it is her voice and it rhymes with `:15`.
   **Recommendation:** cut the narrated verdict "Which rather spoiled her theory," and let the italic do the work.

3. **`:17`, `:19`, `:27` — "pleased" three times in eleven lines.**
   "pleased her to no end" / "heard how pleased she was" / "half-lit, pleased."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Not a striking word, but three uses inside one continuous exchange makes it read as the narrator's only available register for her state.
   **Recommendation:** vary or cut the middle one (`:19`), the least load-bearing of the three.

4. **`:19` — the thesis stated flat, and a doubled verb.**
   "No man had ever wanted her body like this one did, and with Pace she was a girl who had a body, not a body that happened to have a girl attached to it." … "and heard how pleased she was to hear herself say it."
   *Class: overwork / word precision.* The first sentence is the chapter's argument stated as argument, arriving immediately after the scene has just dramatized it; the chiasmus is the good half, the "No man had ever" opener the redundant half. Separately, "heard how pleased she was to hear herself" doubles the verb inside nine words.
   **Recommendation:** cut the "No man had ever wanted her body like this one did" clause, and remove one of the two "hear"s.

5. **`:19` / `:47` — "a girl who had ___" twice.**
   "a girl who had a body" … "a girl who had decided to be looked at."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The self-definition frame used twice, twenty-eight lines apart, both times as a paragraph's clinching phrase. `:47`'s is the stronger and the one the scene turns on.
   **Recommendation:** recast `:19`'s to break the frame, keeping the body/girl inversion.

6. **`:27` — "Something turned over low in her, half-lit, pleased."**
   *Class: within-chapter repetition (cross-chapter frame).* This is the `Something [verb]ed in [X]` frame that took its first FIX-AT at `gone:45` (ruling #32) and is logged for the end-of-pass harvest; here it also carries two trailing adjectives where one would land harder.
   **Recommendation:** recast to a visible or bodily act and drop one modifier.

7. **`:35` — "thinking" after the gesture that shows it.**
   "she brought a nail up to her front tooth and tapped it three times, thinking."
   *Class: overwork.* The nail-tap *is* the thinking; naming it turns a fine piece of business into a stage direction with a caption. "She lay with that a moment. Then…" also pre-announces deliberation the gesture would deliver alone.
   **Recommendation:** cut the trailing "thinking".

8. **`:47` — "wreck" and "wrecked" three lines apart.**
   "she brushed the wreck of her hair back into something" … "she was not the wrecked, sated woman who'd dropped onto the sheet."
   *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Two different senses, close enough to collide; the first is the fresher of the two.
   **Recommendation:** vary the second, which has "sated" already doing the work.

9. **`:47` — the paragraph continues past its last sentence.**
   "…until she'd found the version of herself she wanted him to keep. When she came back she was not the wrecked, sated woman… She was composed, arranged, a girl who had decided to be looked at."
   *Class: rhythm / overwork.* "the version of herself she wanted him to keep" already lands the transformation; the two sentences after it re-describe it twice more (negatively, then positively). The final clause is the best of the three.
   **Recommendation:** cut the middle sentence ("When she came back she was not the wrecked, sated woman…") and let the final clause follow directly.

10. **`:47` / `:49` / `:53` — "arrange" three times in six lines.**
    "composed, arranged" / "started to arrange herself" / the posing paragraph.
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* `:49`'s "started to arrange herself" is the necessary one, because "No, not like that" answers it.
    **Recommendation:** vary `:47`'s "arranged".

11. **`:53` — the general statement and the itemized list both.**
    "and at each place his hands set her he put his mouth there after: a kiss to the point of her shoulder, a kiss to the rise of her hip, a kiss to her knee."
    *Class: overwork.* The colon-list is vivid and exact; the clause before it summarizes the pattern the list is about to demonstrate, so the reader gets the rule and then the instances.
    **Recommendation:** cut the summarizing clause and let the three kisses arrive unannounced.

12. **`:53` — "where he wanted her" / "where he wanted them" inside four clauses.**
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The identical formula twice in one sentence flattens a sentence otherwise built on varied verbs (pressed, set, drew, laid, moved).
    **Recommendation:** cut or vary the second.

13. **`:53` / `:59` — the calendar image spent twice.**
    "laid out like a woman in an old calendar" … "She had wanted pinup, glamour, a girl on a calendar."
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The first is narration's image and is precise; the second hands it to her as a want she is checking off, six lines later, and reads as the chapter reminding the reader of its own simile.
    **Recommendation:** cut "a girl on a calendar" from `:59`, where "pinup, glamour" already covers it.

14. **`:59` — three sentences appraising one photograph.**
    "The girl on the screen was composed and certain and lovely, lovelier than anything she'd have caught of herself alone, and it took her a second to credit that it was her. She looked, she thought, beautiful."
    *Class: overwork.* "composed and certain and lovely" / "lovelier than" / "beautiful" is one judgment made three ways before the paragraph reaches its actual point — that the beauty is *his* seeing. Note also that "composed" repeats `:47`.
    **Recommendation:** cut "She looked, she thought, beautiful." and go straight to the his-eyes turn.

15. **`:67` / `:69` — "cold" three times, "the fridge" twice, in three lines.**
    "the cold filter pitcher in the fridge" / "cold and clean" / "a cold bottle from the fridge."
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* The middle one, on her tongue, is the one worth keeping; the two appliance references are logistics.
    **Recommendation:** drop the redundant "from the fridge" at `:69` and one of the two "cold"s.

16. **`:67` / `:85` — "coming up for air" twice.**
    "without coming up for air" (drinking) … "she came up for air and reached for the wine" (eating).
    *Class: within-chapter repetition.* Same idiom, same physical register, once per section — and the second is doing more work.
    **Recommendation:** vary `:67`.

17. **`:75`–`:77` — the pan-as-speaker figure four times.**
    "the pan said so, loud at first, then settling to a steady talk" / "the pan's talk changed" / "when they'd said what he wanted to hear" / "it hissed at him… and hissed at him too."
    *Class: rhythm / repetition.* The figure is good and is doing the timekeeping the triage pass assigned it, but four instances across two paragraphs turns a conceit into a system. The "turned the breasts without looking at them" beat is the payoff and should be the figure's last appearance.
    **Recommendation:** thin to two — keep "the pan said so… steady talk" and "the pan's talk changed", and drop one of the two "hissed at him"s.

18. **`:77` — the wine aside buried mid-procedure.**
    "and she told him so, and he told her the pan needed it worse than she did, which she doubted out loud."
    *Class: dialogue shape / rhythm.* The chapter's only exchange of banter in the cooking stretch is reported in indirect speech inside a 120-word procedural sentence, so the one moment of two-way voice gets the same rhythm as the stock and the lemon.
    **Recommendation:** break the aside out of the sentence so the exchange has its own beat.

19. **`:79`, `:97`, `:101`, `:107` — four small overworks in the closing movement.**
    "She had used to just eat." (*word precision* — the construction stumbles where the sentence wants to be flat and quick); `:97` "she took her hand off it. **She didn't ask.** She looked at her plate…" (*overwork* — the withdrawal stated three times, and "She didn't ask" is the redundant middle); `:101` "The fondness in it was plain and unguarded, a man talking about a friend." (*overwork* — the pie line already performed the fondness, and the appositive then glosses the gloss); `:107` "He said it the way a person says it about someone they're glad to be able to feed… **Of course. The two of them at one table, neither with anywhere else to be.** She found she was glad, plainly and without complication…" (*rhythm* — the section reaches its true end at "neither with anywhere else to be," and the final sentence explains the relief the flicker's going-out already showed; "plainly and without complication" also chimes against `:101`'s "plain and unguarded" six lines up).
    **Recommendation:** cut "She didn't ask." and the "a man talking about a friend" appositive; trim the closing sentence to its last clause or end the chapter one sentence earlier.
