# Line audit — a-round (2026-08-01)

**Verdict:** 6 issues found — 1 fix, 2 clarification, 3 nitpick. No timeline, dialogue-logic, or canon contradictions.

## Findings

1. **The riser is never established during the measuring section.** The header and the chronology entry both place the measuring "on the riser," and the prose later assumes it — "Maybe she could get *back up on the box*" (l. 116, while she's on the couch), "'Back up,' he said… stepped back onto the low platform" (l. 124), "the apologetic stoop she'd been holding *since she stepped up*" (l. 188) — but nowhere in the measuring section does she step onto anything; she undresses and "stood there," with no platform mentioned. Worse, the first on-page appearance of the riser (l. 124) carries a first-discovery aside — "of course there was a clean-edged little riser in the corner of a woodworker's house, of course he'd built it" — *after* l. 116 has already casually called it "the box," so the marveling lands on her second mounting instead of her first. **Severity: fix.** Recommended: establish the step up onto the riser at the start of the measuring section (or wherever it actually happens) and move or cut the of-course discovery beat so it coincides with her first encounter with it.

2. **Bare breast vs. the pinned bodice, and the kneeling geometry.** "her bare breast came soft against the side of his face as he pinned, warm skin to his jaw" (l. 202). By this point the dress has been built "from the top down" — "a panel pinned at her shoulder, smoothed down her front" (l. 126), "the heel of his palm held the bodice up" (l. 134) — so a reader reasonably pictures her bust covered in silk; it's unclear how the breast is bare. Compounding it: l. 160 establishes the riser puts her *hips* at his kneeling eye level, and l. 192 has him back at the hem, so breast-to-jaw contact requires a substantial bend the text doesn't show (the "leaning into his work" gesture is doing a lot of unstated stooping). **Severity: clarification.** Recommended: make explicit either that the bodice at this stage leaves her breasts uncovered (pinned front panel only, sides open) or that he has risen to high-kneel/stand for these pins, so the contact is physically legible.

3. **"the whole time … he hadn't looked down once" (l. 182) can misread against earlier looks.** Locally this is scoped to the interval her eyes were shut during the hem circuit, but "the whole time she'd been dying" invites a wider reading that collides with l. 44 ("His eyes dropped — just for a moment"), l. 54 ("Twice his gaze slowed — at her breasts…"), and l. 66 ("his eyes went exactly where she'd thought they would"). A literal reader may snag on the apparent contradiction. **Severity: clarification.** Recommended: pin the timeframe explicitly to the eyes-shut interval (e.g., anchor "the whole time" to the moment she shut her eyes).

4. **"went to her head like the wine the color of the cloth" (l. 202) — no wine exists in the afternoon.** Only tea has been served (this scene) and none in {{Two Towels}}; the silk is merely "wine-like" in color. If read as a comparison to wine she's drunk, there is no wine to point to; as a generic simile it works, but the definite article ("the wine") nudges the wrong reading. **Severity: nitpick.** Recommended: either soften the article to a generic comparison or accept as-is if the figurative reading is intended.

5. **Seam-measurement endpoints are inconsistent (l. 86 vs. l. 88).** The outside seam runs "hip to floor," but the inside seam stops "along the inside of her leg to her knee" — two different endpoints for the same skirt length (and dresses have no inside seam at all, which a tailoring-minded reader may notice; arguably that's the point — the measurement is method/pretext — but it isn't signaled). **Severity: nitpick.** Recommended: either match the endpoints or let Vee register (even glancingly) that this measurement isn't strictly necessary, which would convert the inconsistency into charge.

6. **Free-floating "Wryly." (l. 42).** "*Maybe he'll think it's the cold air.* Except he'd thoughtfully turned up the heat. Wryly." The lone adverb has no clear anchor — it can attach to her self-correction, to "thoughtfully turned up the heat," or read as a stage direction with no verb. A reader stumbles on what, exactly, is wry. **Severity: nitpick (clarification-adjacent).** Recommended: anchor the adverb to its intended action or thought.
