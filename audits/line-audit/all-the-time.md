1. **Line 37** — "She dropped her voice, leaned in over the soup that hadn't come."
   Problem: Vee explicitly did *not* order the soup — line 23 has her pass over "the safe cheap thing" and order the eggs Benedict, and line 55 confirms the Benedict arrives. If this is a deliberate callback to line 27's "not a story you could tell over soup," it still reads literally as a prop error — a first-pass reader is likely to stumble on a soup that was never ordered failing to come.
   Severity: clarification.
   Recommended fix: either anchor the phrase to the food actually ordered/absent (the plates that hadn't come) or make the joke unmistakably a non-order ("the soup she hadn't ordered"), author's choice.

2. **Line 43** — "Randi listened the way she listened, which was total, her wine forgotten…"
   Problem: the only drinks established on the table are the two mimosas (line 17); "her wine" primes a wine glass that was never served. A mimosa technically contains wine (line 21, "a thread of the wine under it"), so this is defensible, but the noun mismatch is a small stumble — lines 63 and 93 correctly use the neutral "her glass."
   Severity: nitpick.
   Recommended fix: use "her glass" or "her mimosa" for consistency with the rest of the scene.

3. **Lines 95–113** — phone custody: line 95 Vee "turned the screen across the table"; line 97 Randi is "thumbing back through" (she has it); line 103 Vee "turned the phone back across" with no handback shown; line 111 Randi "looked back at the screen"; line 113 Vee "pulled up the real one… turned the phone around and put it in Randi's hand" — again with no return to Vee shown.
   Problem: the phone changes hands twice (Randi→Vee) off the page. Readers will mostly infer it, but the 111→113 jump — Randi looking at the screen, then Vee pulling up a photo on the same phone in her own hands one sentence later — is the spot where a careful reader stops to re-stage the table.
   Severity: clarification.
   Recommended fix: add one small return beat (Randi sliding/handing the phone back) somewhere in 105–111 so the 113 handoff has the phone in Vee's possession.

4. **Line 79 vs. line 135** — "told close, in a dark booth on a slow afternoon" vs. "Out in the bright noon."
   Problem: if they exit into "bright noon" after the checks, the mid-meal moment at line 79 is late morning, not afternoon. Line 79 is half a generalization (Vee theorizing about stories like hers), which softens it, but as written it also describes the present booth and contradicts the exit time.
   Severity: nitpick.
   Recommended fix: adjust one end — a "slow morning" at line 79 or a slightly later exit register at line 135 — or lean line 79 fully into the generic register so it no longer describes this sitting.
