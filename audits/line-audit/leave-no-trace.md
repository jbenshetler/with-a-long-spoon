## Findings

1. **fix** — "…and stopped under the overhang where the rain wasn't, and put it in park" (drop-off, ~line 251) vs. "She pushed the door open and the rain came in and she went, the three steps to the dry under the overhang" (~line 261), plus "He'd parked illegal so she wouldn't have to cross the rain" (~line 253). If the truck is already stopped under the overhang where the rain wasn't, rain cannot come in her door and there are no three wet steps to cross to reach "the dry under the overhang." Recommended: decide whether the truck is fully under the overhang (then cut the rain-comes-in / three-steps beat) or only near it (then adjust "under the overhang where the rain wasn't" to something short of full shelter).

2. **clarification** — "Lettuce against the bread. Keeps the bread from going through." (summit lunch, ~line 89). The logic is inverted or elliptical: the lettuce keeps the wet (mustard/meat) from going through the bread — as written, the *bread* is what "goes through," and a reader stumbles on what the bread would go through. Recommended: reword so the moisture, not the bread, is the thing being kept from going through (the brief's intent is "keeps the bread dry").

3. **clarification** — "He pulled the truck up onto the curve at the entrance — the fire lane, the red-painted curb" (~line 251). "Up onto the curve" sits directly beside "curb" and reads as a typo for it (pulling *onto the curb*); "the curve" as the curved entrance drive only becomes clear at "the two sets of taillights went down the curve" twelve lines later. Recommended: disambiguate the first "curve" (or accept the near-homophone deliberately, but confirm it's not a typo).

4. **nitpick** — "He got to her door a step ahead of her and opened it and she climbed in and hauled it shut. The rain on the cab roof was louder inside than out… He was already in, turning the key" (~line 165). He opens her door from outside, yet is "already in" the instant she's shut hers — he'd have to round the truck in the time she takes one step up. Readable as compression, but the "already" slightly overclaims. Recommended: soften "already" or give him the half-beat of crossing.

5. **nitpick** — "A rock at the trail-edge, knee-high, dry… She stepped over and turned and sat up on it. Her feet didn't reach the ground." (~lines 47–49). Seated on a knee-high rock, an adult's feet reach the ground by definition; the dangling feet need a taller rock (the elevation geometry the brief wants would survive "thigh-high"). Recommended: raise the rock or drop the feet-not-reaching detail.

6. **nitpick** — "The console was folded up between the seats — old truck, the bench unbroken across" (~line 195). "Between the seats" and "the bench unbroken across" pull against each other (a bench has no "seats" plural with a between); the parenthetical is doing repair work on its own noun phrase. A fold-up bench armrest is real hardware, but the phrasing makes a reader re-parse. Recommended: name it as the bench's fold-up armrest (or similar) rather than a console "between the seats."
