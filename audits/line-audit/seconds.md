1. **Line 29/31 vs line 5 — hand-movement continuity.** Line 5: "His hand rested on her shin under the quilt, warm, not moving, the steady weight of a man who was not in a hurry." Line 29: "His hand on her shin had stopped," and line 31: "his hand on her shin began to move *again*." A hand established as not moving (and re-anchored motionless at line 17) cannot "stop," and "again" implies prior motion the scene never gave it. Severity: **fix**. Recommend either seeding light motion earlier (e.g. at line 17) or dropping "had stopped"/"again" so the stillness reads as deepened stillness.

Line 43 repeats the phrase ("her cheek against where his mouth had been"), and the coda at line 65 remembers the posture as "her cheek against his shoulder," which is the only physically workable landing spot. Severity: **clarification** — the intended posture (cheek to his shoulder/chest) is recoverable but a literal reader stumbles. Recommend re-anchoring the phrase to a spot on him.

3. **Line 65 — coda's memory of the saying moment.** "She had said it once, in the pre-dawn chill of his porch … with her cheek against his shoulder and the sun coming up." At the moment she said the sentence (lines 19–27) she was legs-across-his-lap looking at the moon in the dark; cheek contact and the paling sky came only after his reply. As memory-compression it is defensible, but "the sun coming up" while she said it contradicts the carefully staged pre-dawn dark. Severity: **nitpick**. Recommend loosening the clause so it frames the whole morning rather than the instant of saying, or dropping "the sun coming up."

Severity: **nitpick**. Recommend one word anchoring it as the dorm's.
