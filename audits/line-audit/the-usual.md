## Findings

1. **"He'd been to California in November for a conference"** (line 17) — **fix**. Canon establishes exactly two fall conferences: the **Berkeley (California) trip is Wed Oct 28** (three days, the Induction week — `meta-plan-chronology.md` guard 20, `the-induction.md`), and the **early-November** trip is **Chicago** (`above-him.md`, chronology guard 20: "Don't collapse them or reuse the city"). "California in November" conflates the two — either the month should match the Berkeley trip (October/late fall) or the reference risks implying a third, uncanonical trip. Recommend aligning the month (or vaguing it to "this fall") to the Berkeley/Oct trip, since the Berkeley color ("a made-up place") is the payload.

2. **"I got maybe four seconds of her at the bar before she started throwing things"** (line 49) — **clarification**. In the drafted blowup (`lesson.md`) Vee throws nothing physical — the eruption is verbal, and she leaves Pace holding both drinks; in `hills-and-valleys.md` (lines 7–11) Sheri reports watching Vee the rest of that night sitting quietly, glaring, nursing one drink. Read literally, "throwing things" contradicts both; it works only as Sheri's hyperbole for the blowup. Recommend the author confirm hyperbole is the intended read (and that "four seconds" squares with Sheri then watching her all evening) or soften the literal-object implication.

3. **"You met her?"** (line 89) — **clarification**. Per Sheri's answer (line 91), Pace was physically present at the meeting — "Y'all came in dancing, I hugged you hello." A man who walked his date into his own regular hall and hugged Sheri hello would know Sheri met her, so his surprised question reads odd unless it means "you count that as meeting her?" A reader who tracks the logic may stumble. Recommend confirming the surprise is meant as not-having-clocked-the-encounter, or adjusting the question's angle.

4. **"he would not bring her down today"** (line 3) — **clarification**. The "her" has no antecedent at the scene's first sentence; Sheri does not appear until line 5, and a first-time reader — primed by the book to expect Vee — may attach the pronoun to the wrong woman for two paragraphs. Recommend deciding whether the ambiguity is intentional; if not, an early anchor for the referent would clear it.

5. **"She dragged the shake back over"** (line 49) — **nitpick**. Sheri already claimed the shake at line 25 ("drew the shake to her by its base") and nothing in between shows it leaving her side of the table, so "back over" implies an unshown displacement. Recommend either showing it pushed aside during the meal or dropping "back."

6. **"His tea had left its rings down the inside of the glass where he'd drunk it down"** (line 37) — **nitpick**. The tea is never delivered on the page — the waitress takes the order (line 15) and the kid brings the two plates and the shake (line 25) — so Pace's drink first appears already mostly consumed. Covered in spirit by "the usual," but a delivery-of-drinks beat or earlier glance at the glass would close the gap.
