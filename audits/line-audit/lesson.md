## Findings

1. **Bottle count.** Line 181: "She set the bottle on the ledge and walked off" vs. line 185: "He stood where she'd left him with both bottles. … He set the beers down on the bar, untouched." She left her bottle on a ledge, and "stood where she'd left him" says he hasn't moved — so he can't be holding both. Severity: **fix**. Recommended: either have her push the bottle back at him / leave it beside him, or have line 185 give him one bottle (or show him collecting hers).

2. **Transport and the boom box.** Lines 35/41: he meets her in the dorm common area carrying the boom box and they *walk* across campus to the pad; line 123: "The Rusty Nail was loud and warm when they walked in"; lines 227–231: she takes a cab home with a fare "more than she'd guessed," implying the bar is well off campus. How they got from the pad to the bar — and where the boom box went — is never accounted for. Pace's truck is canon (`scenes/under-the-rug.md` line 61) but never appears here, and line 9's "He's picking me up" primes a vehicle that the scene then contradicts with the walk. Severity: **clarification**. Recommended: one clause placing the truck (boom box into the cab, the drive out) between the pad and the bar would close the gap.

3. **"He moved the pickup to seven."** Line 9. The original pickup time is never established, so the reader must infer both the direction of the move and that the move is what creates lesson time ("So there's time. To teach me") — several readers will stumble on whether seven is earlier or later, and on how a 7 p.m. pickup leaves time for a lesson before a dance. Severity: **clarification**. Recommended: a word anchoring the direction (earlier) or the dance's start time.

4. **Music restarts.** Line 41: "He set the boom box down and clicked it on. Country music, turned low" — and they dance through lines 51–93 with no note of the music stopping. Line 97 then opens "He pressed play on the ancient boombox, the tape warbling up to speed," which reads as a first start. A tape ending during the frame work is plausible but unmarked. Severity: **nitpick**. Recommended: mark the tape change/flip, or make line 97 a resume rather than a cold start.

5. **Right-arm / space physics.** Lines 77–83: "Your right arm … You're holding yourself out" → "the space between them, the few inches." Her right arm is the one extended straight out to the side in his left hand (line 57); a stiff lateral arm doesn't obviously produce frontal inches between the bodies — the left arm on his shoulder is what governs that distance. A dance-literate reader may hitch. Severity: **nitpick**. Recommended: attribute the held-out space to the frame/left arm, or leave as-is if the imprecision is Vee's.

6. **Underage drinking, unmarked.** Vee is a junior (`meta/meta-arch-vivienne.md` line 59), so ~20 in October; Pace hands her a longneck (line 167) and she "nursed one drink" at the bar (line 199). Not necessarily an error for a country bar, but the door beat (line 125's hand stamps) is where a 21+/under distinction would live and none is made. Severity: **nitpick**. Recommended: decide whether the bar cards and, if it matters, let the stamp beat carry it; otherwise accept as venue realism.

Cross-scene note (belongs to the *other* scene's ledger, recorded here for completeness): `scenes/broken-in.md` line 7 — "what was left of him in the cloth," the very next morning — sits oddly against this scene's washed shirt ("fabric softener and no one," line 237). Readable as habit/figurative, but worth a look when Broken In is audited.
