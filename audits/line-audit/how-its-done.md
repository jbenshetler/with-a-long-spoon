# Line audit — how-its-done (2026-08-01)

**Verdict:** 7 issues found — 1 fix, 2 clarifications, 4 nitpicks. No plot-logic or cross-scene canon errors; the load-bearing beats (bra reveal, "cold in the house" self-contradiction, seventeen days, smoothie-place/Brooke callback, warm-house detail) all check out against `a-round.md`, `two-towels.md`, `dear.md`, and the chronology (Sun Oct 4; Vee met Pace Thu Sep 17 → the fitting Oct 3 is day 17 inclusive; "a few weeks" with Randi since ~Sep 8–9 is right).

## Findings

1. **Elapsed-time contradiction: an 11 a.m. departure and a noon lunch return in evening light.** — Outbound: "they went down the long drive past the dorms with the top down at eleven in the morning"; and "If I have wine at noon…" fixes lunch at ~noon. Return: "They came back across town with the late sun coming sideways," then "the sound of it thinned into the ordinary evening," and the courtyard "gone gold in the low sun." A soup-and-salmon lunch that ends with Randi looking for the check immediately after the intimacy peak cannot plausibly run to golden-hour/evening (~5:30–6:30 p.m. in early-October Blacksburg) — some four-plus hours are unaccounted for. **Severity: fix.** Recommendation: either move the departure/lunch later, tone the return light down to mid-afternoon, or insert an acknowledgment that the lunch ran long.

2. **Garment continuity: the in-car "collar of the silk" vs. the booth's low-cut camisole.** — In the car: "the wind finding the collar of the silk and laying it open another half-inch against her collarbone." At the booth: Randi removes the cream cashmere and "underneath it the champagne silk caught what light there was… where the camisole cut low." A camisole has no collar to be laid open, and if the cashmere was worn in the car it would cover the silk. **Severity: clarification.** Recommendation: reconcile what silk garment is exposed in the car (open cashmere over camisole? a collared silk shirt?) so the two descriptions name the same thing.

3. **Prop continuity: the ordered wine never appears on the page.** — Randi orders "a glass of the Rombauer," but her only on-page drink is water ("her thumb had found the base of her water glass"; "lifted the water at last and drank"), and the wine resurfaces only in the check paragraph ("The glass of wine alone had cost more than everything Vee had eaten"). A reader tracking the table may wonder whether the wine ever arrived or was touched. **Severity: clarification.** Recommendation: give the wine one small on-page beat (arrival or a sip) or let the check line carry a hint that it sat mostly untouched.

4. **The waiter materializes without arriving.** — "The waiter wrote it down without comment" is his first mention; Randi begins ordering ("The salmon here is stupid… I'll have that") with no waiter established at the table — only the host has appeared. Vee's "I'll do the tomato soup" is likewise addressed to someone never shown arriving. **Severity: nitpick.** Recommendation: a half-clause of the waiter's arrival before the first order would close the gap.

5. **"Best thing that's happened to me all month" on October 4.** — Randi's Saturday story ("Best thing that's happened to me all month") lands on the 4th of the month, making "all month" a four-day span. Reads as colloquial hyperbole and most readers won't blink, but it's literally near-empty. **Severity: nitpick.** Recommendation: leave, or swap "all month" for a span that has content (e.g. "in weeks") if literalism bothers the author.

6. **"You said he's a looker, this man" — no on-page antecedent.** — Randi cites a statement Vee never makes in this scene (and Vee's account here mentions the dress, the warm house, the measuring — not his looks). It's presumably from an off-page brunch, and Randi's off-page knowledge of Pace is itself a plant, but a reader auditing the conversation can't locate the referent. **Severity: nitpick.** Recommendation: verify the author intends this as an off-page callback (or a deliberate Randi tell) rather than an attribution slip; no change needed if intended.

7. **Geography: "off Route 9."** — Virginia's Route 9 is in Loudoun County, ~200 miles from Blacksburg; the plausible local corridors are US 460, VA 8, or US 11. If the setting keeps real Blacksburg geography (as "Hahn," "Vawter Hall," and campus references suggest), the route number is off. **Severity: nitpick.** Recommendation: confirm whether the road is meant to be fictional-generic; if real geography is the standard, swap to a plausible local route.
