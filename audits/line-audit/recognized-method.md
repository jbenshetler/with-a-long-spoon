2. **Line 11 — "the label she had made the day before … It had seemed, at three in the morning the night before, the first night she'd been able to do anything but lie there stunned, like exactly the right amount of funny."** — *clarification*. The dance is Thursday night; the label is printed Friday daytime ("the day before"); but "three in the morning the night before" is the small hours of Saturday — after the printing — and "the first night she'd been able to do anything but lie there stunned" also points at Friday night. As written, the joke seems to be judged funny (and by implication conceived) hours after the label was already made. Recommended fix: pin the 3 a.m. moment to the conception (Thursday night can't be it, per "lie there stunned") or make explicit that the 3 a.m. beat is a re-approval of a label already printed.

A reader tracking the apology may stumble on whose "fine" this is. Recommended fix: an early attribution or gesture tying the first sentence of the paragraph to Pace.

The washing implement changes from cloth to scrub brush with no beat in between (line 109 then confirms "the scrub brush" world by implication of the soapy sink). 5. **Line 91 — "dry fingers against the bare warm skin of her side"** — *nitpick*. Two lines earlier she grips "his wet forearm" (line 89), and at line 101 his left hand is "warm and wet from the dishwater"; the right hand that was just holding the scrub brush is the least likely to be dry. The wet/dry contrast is clearly wanted (it pays off at line 101), but the dry hand is assigned to the scrubbing hand. Recommended fix: swap which hand is dry, or account for the dry hand (e.g., it was the towel/holding hand).

6. **Line 13 — "she had the heels and the pearls and the dress"** — *nitpick*.
