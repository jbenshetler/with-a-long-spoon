### 1. Randi's seat — across the table vs beside Vee

**WHERE (chapter):**
> "She picked up her coffee and held it between both hands and looked across the table at Randi, who had her laptop open in front of her"

**AGAINST (same chapter, twice):**
> "Beside her, Randi's hand moved to her coffee cup. Then Vee looked at the man who was looking at her."
> "He turned back to Vee. Beside her, Randi's hand went to her coffee cup, held it with both hands a second longer than the sip required"

and
> "He pulled out the empty chair at their table without dragging it and sat." / "He sat across from her and listened."

**WHY:** If Randi is across the table from Vee and Pace takes the empty chair "across from her," Randi cannot also be "beside" Vee for both of the hand-on-cup beats; the layout is rendered two ways.
**CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM — a square four-top lets "across the table" mean diagonally, but the text never establishes that, and the two "beside her" lines carry the chapter's key Randi-tell.

### 3. "The day they'd met, in the lecture hall" — the adjective bit was delivered the day *after* Vee met Randi

**WHERE (chapter):**
> "She had watched Randi do it the day they'd met, in the lecture hall, the practiced little swerve, and it had looked so easy and so good that some part of her had tucked it away to try on later."

**AGAINST (Ch4, `scenes/see-you-later.md`):**
> line 3: "The morning after the campus collision."
> line 13: "She had met Randi yesterday. She'd been coming out of the dining hall with Meg from her floor…"
> line 39 (in the lecture hall, to Cassie): "Hi — I'm Randi. Yes, like the adjective. No, it's not a coincidence. Yes, my parents had a sense of humor."

**WHY:** Vee met Randi on the lawn (collision day); the "adjective" swerve Vee watched was performed in the lecture hall the next morning, so "the day they'd met, in the lecture hall" places two events on one day that Ch4 puts on two.
**CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM — a reading survives in which "they" = Randi and Cassie (the line *was* Randi's introduction to Cassie, on the day those two met). But the sentence's subject is Vee's own memory, and "they" most naturally reads as Vee-and-Randi. Ledger note: this is *not* a date/weekday question — it's which event happened in which place.
