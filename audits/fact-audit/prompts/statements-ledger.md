Below is the full drafted text of one volume of a novel, in reading order.

Build a STATEMENTS LEDGER: for each principal character, what they have SAID
ALOUD about themselves and about others, to whom, in which chapter — and,
beside it, what the narration SHOWS them actually feeling or doing on the same
subject. This ledger will be used to find places where a character's account
shifts, so record what the text says, not what you infer.

Record only these kinds of statement:

- **Self-disclosure** — desires, feelings, preferences, history, what they
  liked or didn't ("I liked it, honestly").
- **Denials** — anything a character says is not so about themselves.
- **Claims about other characters** — what one character tells another about a
  third.
- **Promises and plans** — what they say they will do.

Skip small talk, logistics, and banter that commits to nothing.

Format, one section per character (use the name the text uses most):

## <Name>

- **SAID** (ch N, to <audience>): "<verbatim quote>" — subject: <a few words>
- **SHOWN** (ch N, narration): "<verbatim quote>" — subject: <a few words>

Group entries on the same subject together so a shift is easy to see. Record
the CHAPTER, never a date. Where the text is ambiguous, say so rather than
resolving it.
