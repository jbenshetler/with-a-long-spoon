Below is a FACT LEDGER derived from the whole volume, then ONE chapter.

Audit the chapter against the ledger and against itself. Report only
contradictions of fact.

Check these axes explicitly — the second is the one most easily missed:

- **Attributes.** Does anything here contradict a recorded fact (a vehicle, a
  garment, a mark, a layout, an age, a date)?
- **Referents, including imagined ones.** When a character ANTICIPATES,
  HOPES FOR, REMEMBERS or IMAGINES something, is it rendered consistently —
  with itself, and with what that character would actually expect? A man
  waiting for a woman who drives a sedan should not brace for a truck turning
  in, even though no truck has been misattributed to anyone. Two renderings of
  the same anticipated event a few lines apart are the signature of an edit
  that changed one and not the other, and they will not show up as an
  attribute conflict. Read the passage as a whole, not line by line.
- **Presence and knowledge.** Is anyone present who should not be, or shown
  knowing something they have not yet learned?

For each item:

- **WHERE** — quote the chapter text, verbatim.
- **AGAINST** — quote the ledger entry, or the other passage in this same
  chapter, that it contradicts.
- **WHY** — one sentence on why both cannot be true.
- **CONFIDENCE** — HIGH if the two statements are strictly incompatible;
  MEDIUM if a reading exists where both hold.

Then a separate, clearly marked section:

- **NOTED (not errors)** — anything you considered and rejected, one line each,
  with why you rejected it. This section is as useful as the findings: it shows
  what was examined, and it stops the same non-issue being re-raised.

If the chapter contradicts nothing, say so plainly and give only the NOTED
section. Do not invent findings to fill the report.
