Build a FACT LEDGER: the falsifiable, concrete attributes the prose
establishes. This ledger will be used to audit individual chapters for
contradictions, so it must record what the text actually says, not what you
infer or expect.

Cover at least:

- **People** — name, any aliases, age if stated, physical marks, distinguishing
  features, what they own and drive, where they live, family facts.
- **Vehicles** — who drives what, make/model/colour, and any detail attached.
- **Places** — layout and fixtures where the text commits to them, especially
  any location used repeatedly.
- **Objects** — named or distinctive items, who owns them, where they live.
- **Knowledge state** — who knows what, and the chapter where they learn it.
  Record the CHAPTER, never a date.
  This is the most error-prone axis in a book built on concealment, so be
  precise about the chapter number.

For each entry cite the chapter number(s) where it is established. Where the
text is genuinely ambiguous, say so rather than resolving it — an ambiguity
recorded as a fact will generate false contradictions later.

Where two chapters already disagree, record BOTH readings and mark the entry
CONFLICT. That is a finding in itself.
