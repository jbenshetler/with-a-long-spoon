## FINDING 1 — Randi showers between the bed and the door, in a scene that does not let her

**WHERE (bedroom, end of the first movement):**
> "He kissed her forehead and got up and started pulling on his clothes, and she watched him from the bed, taking inventory as she always did, and after a moment she reached for hers too.
>
> "Let's go for a walk on campus," he said.
>
> "Yeah?" Already up, already reaching for the bright easy version of herself, the one for daylight. […]
>
> "Yeah," he said, and held the door for her."

**AGAINST (same chapter, on the quad, ~two pages later):**
> "She had showered and put her face back on and dressed and walked out the door game for whatever the day was."

**WHY** — The bedroom scene runs unbroken from bed to door: she reaches for her clothes *from the bed*, is "already up" during the next line of dialogue, and he holds the door immediately after; the later summary inserts a shower and a full redo of her face *before* she dressed, in a gap the scene has closed.

**CONFIDENCE** — MEDIUM. A reading exists where "and held the door for her" is a compression that silently elides an intervening shower, but the prose has her reaching for her clothes first, which puts the shower on the wrong side of "dressed."

## FINDING 2 — "the closed camel-coat project" names a woman the chapter never showed, and the only candidate it could name is rendered laughing in public

**WHERE (Vee coming out of the dining hall):**
> "laughing at something with her whole body the way the polished girls and the matched pairs and the closed camel-coat project would never laugh at anything in public, would never let go of the wheel that completely"

**AGAINST (same chapter — the full roster of women Pace pointed at):**
1. > "Leggings and an oversized quarter-zip in the Greek pastel, a canvas tote with a water bottle clipped to it, box-fresh white trainers"
2. > "both in the matched athleisure that read like a uniform — high-waisted, cropped, one in lavender and one in a sage"
3. > "round wire glasses she kept pushing up with one knuckle, a green knit beanie though it wasn't cold"
4. > "a striking girl mid-laugh in a knot of friends, a blazer and a white-blond blowout in loose curls" … "a smile she was already wearing before anyone had said anything to earn it"

**WHY** — The summing-up sentence is built as a one-to-one recap of the candidates ("polished girls" = 1, "matched pairs" = 2), but no one on the walk wears a camel coat; the nearest fit, the blazer girl, is the one woman explicitly introduced *mid-laugh in public*, which is exactly what the sentence says that figure would never do.

**CONFIDENCE** — MEDIUM. A reading exists where "the camel-coat project" is a generic social type rather than a callback — but then it is the only item in a list of three whose other two members are specific women from this same afternoon.
