## FINDINGS

**1. The common-room tally contradicts the stated Saturday baseline**

- **WHERE** — "Saturday morning, before ten. There was usually one girl on the couch with a coffee and a laptop. There were three girls today. Sloane on the couch with the coffee and the laptop. Kinsey cross-legged on the rug with a bowl of cereal. And Brooke at the long table by the window with the chapter laptop and a coffee and the spreadsheet open — the morning admin she did at this hour every Saturday in this seat."
- **AGAINST** — the same passage's own clause: "the morning admin she did at this hour every Saturday in this seat"; and the ledger: "Brooke — Does chapter admin on the house laptop at the long table by the window on Saturday mornings (Ch 2)."
- **WHY** — If Brooke is invariably at that table at that hour on a Saturday, the usual Saturday-morning count cannot be "one girl"; Brooke is counted here as part of the day's surplus while simultaneously being established as a fixture of the baseline.
- **CONFIDENCE** — MEDIUM. A reading exists where "usually one girl on the couch" is a statement about *the couch* only and the "three" is plain enumeration rather than a contrast — but the sentence pair is built as expectation-then-anomaly, and Brooke is the third item in the anomaly.

- **"closed it, this time without the unhurried click"** (end) vs. the front door "closed… quietly" (open) — "this time" reads most naturally against her *habit* (established two paragraphs in), not against an earlier door in the chapter. No contradiction.
