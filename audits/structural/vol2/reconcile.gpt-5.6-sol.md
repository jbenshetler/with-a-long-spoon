### Cross-cutting

- C1, C2, and C6 are one information-architecture problem at three scales: Volume Two needs one more experience of concealment under pressure, while the moral reckoning and evidentiary payoffs must remain delayed. The governing distinction is that Pace’s architecture guards against force but not deception (`meta/meta-orientation.md:43-50`).

- C3 and C5 share Pace’s declaration vow. A Daphne reminder may better prepare the withheld word, but explaining Daphne’s causal function would blunt the deliberately incomplete ending and collapse the later disclosure staircase (`meta/meta-arch-pace.md:193-205`).

- C4 and C7 are two readings of the same enclosure: the house’s repeated erotic-domestic rhythm crowds out the social world. Compressing *The New Ordinary* and giving Cassie one independent-life beat in *Bare* could answer both without adding a chapter, but both moves reopen existing design choices (`meta/meta-triage-new-ordinary.md:73-80`; `meta/meta-plan-chronology.md:628`).

- C6 should not be solved as a single “pay off everything” problem. The drawer and Brooke are delayed mechanisms; MIRA is a completed wound with an existing Ch. 43 reminder (`meta/meta-plan-chronology.md:231`, `meta/meta-plan-chronology.md:375-377`; `scenes/old-acquaintances.md:21`).

- The strongest new evidence is not that the architecture lacks answers, but that several intended effects now require readers to carry information across a volume boundary or a long middle interval. The decisions are therefore whether to add reminders and pressure beats—not whether to replace the planned reveal, ending, or final Cassie thesis scene (`meta/meta-plan-chronology.md:524-538`).
