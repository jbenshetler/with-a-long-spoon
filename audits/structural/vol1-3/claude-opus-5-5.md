LETTER

Dear Helen,

Volume Three has the best four-chapter run in the series so far: the mixer, the confession in Pace's bed, Cassie's question, and Randi's Standards hearing (61, 62, 64, 65). Those chapters show what the book can do when events force people to choose. Most of my notes are about the rest of the volume: too often it postpones those choices by retelling things the reader has already seen.

**1. The retelling problem.** Many chapters in this volume replay scenes we have already watched, now as a story Vee tells someone. The coat on the porch (Ch 46) is retold to Kayla and Meg (Ch 50), then to Randi in full (Ch 51), with the drawer, the shoes and the police lights reprised almost line for line. The breakfast and the "pleasure princess" idea (Ch 47) come back in Ch 51. The truck after the roadhouse (Ch 52) comes back in Ch 53. The painting (Ch 60) comes back in Ch 66.

I understand why. Watching Randi hear these stories is the dramatic irony the series runs on. But once the reader knows the event, a retelling earns its place only when the telling itself changes something. Ch 53 ("I do"), Ch 58 ("Not my thing") and Ch 66 ("If it doesn't mean no anymore") each turn on a single beat that does that. The pages leading up to those beats mostly re-serve the meal. I'd cut each retelling down to the moment where Randi does something with it.

**2. The restroom pattern.** Vee feels aroused at a table with Randi, goes to a restroom, and either stops or finishes. That has now happened four times across the series: the practice room (27), brunch (31), the loft (51) and the brunch after the painting (66). Your own text names the pattern: "The third restroom she'd touched herself in" (Ch 51). Ch 66 changes the shape, because Randi's mouth finally appears in the fantasy, and that should feel like the last one of its kind. Right now it reads as the next one.

**3. The central question doesn't move until Chapter 65.** Since Gstaad (Ch 42), both women have held the same position: I want her, and telling her would cost me the friendship. Vee restates it in 51, 58, 63 and 66. Randi restates it in 65. Inside that, the physical escalation is well staged: neck kiss, second neck kiss, longer goodbye kiss, the mixer, Randi's kiss on Vee's neck. But the stakes stay internal until Brooke acts. Even the hearing ends cheaply for Randi: "She only lost Thursdays." Ask what the back half of this volume makes someone lose that can't be got back.

**4. Pace has gone quiet at the moment he matters most.** Nineteen of the twenty-one chapters are in Vee's point of view. Pace gets one (55), and Randi gets half of one (65). Volume Two spent Pace chapters building his rule: "She consents — every time — or I'm done." In Ch 62 he trades Vee's pleasure for her confession while hiding that Randi is his lover. Then he asks "Anything else you want to tell me?" after Randi has already told him about the neck kisses (Ch 55). In Ch 67 he gets Vee to promise she'll tell him whenever she touches herself thinking of him, which hands Randi a new source of information. A reader can't tell whether the book knows how far Pace has drifted from his own rule. One short Pace chapter after 62 would settle that, and it would keep the irony the jacket sells from depending entirely on the reader's memory of Volume One.

**5. Time slips at the climax.** Ch 60 ends with "Tomorrow was Randi's — the skirt, the mixer". Ch 61 then opens with Randi inviting Vee to the mixer and lending her the skirt. That is a flashback with no signal, sitting right before the volume's big set piece. The cabin weekend in Ch 67 is also undated, and Ch 56 committed Pace to Sheri's Valentine's dance. If the cabin trip falls on Valentine's weekend, the reader will notice the clash.

**6. Some scene shapes repeat in miniature.** Pace cataloguing Vee's body while she stands still and passive has appeared three times: the fitting (14), the painting with her eyes closed (60), and the blindfold in the cabin (67). Ch 67's twist, where she has to touch herself while he watches, earns it, but it arrives seven chapters after the painting with the same rules. Randi's hidden-feeling tells have also been used too often: the glass rim pressed under her thumbnail (V1 Chs 1, 15, 18; V3 Chs 51, 52), the bitten knuckle, "went still." I flag this only because the book now leans on these gestures to signal Randi's feeling, and the reader has learned to read them as a code.

**7. Threads are piling up.** Several threads remain open across three volumes:
- the scar Vee kisses past in Ch 60
- Pace's father
- Daphne
- the PPP/Miranda discovery (Ch 36), absent from this volume
- Vee's credit-card debt from the shoes
- the "Love" bracelet and who locked it on Randi

You don't have to pay all of these off now, but the reader should feel at least one of them tighten here.

**8. The opening doesn't match the jacket.** The jacket leads with the foyer kiss. Ch 47 opens on Pace's bed and never mentions it, and Vee doesn't face it directly until Ch 63.

What to protect: the hearing chapter, Sheri, the "boyfriend" payoff, the slow build of the tasting question from "gross" to "he's enthusiastic," Marsh's lectures as a quiet chorus, and the title paying off in the key.

Warmly,
Your editor
