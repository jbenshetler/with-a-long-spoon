### C1. The volume's central build, the dress and the induction, peaks without being paid off, and the last chapter is resolved contentment with no forward hook.
*2 editors (A, B) · severity major/moderate · chapters 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 19, 20, 21, 22*

- **A1** (claude-opus-5-5, opening-ending): The volume's central build, the dress and the induction, peaks without being paid off, and the last chapter is resolved contentment with no forward hook.  
  Evidence: The induction is set up in Ch 10 ("Still weeks off"), the dress is built in Ch 13-14, finished in Ch 19, and the shoes bought in Ch 20, but no induction scene ever comes. Ch 22 closes on "She had never been so fortunate, or so happy." The bench ("waiting for the next time," Ch 1; "Do you think she'll like the bench?", Ch 3) and the threesome never return.  
  Question: Is the induction meant to open Book Two? If so, what in Chapters 21-22 tells the reader that something is about to break?
- **B1** (gpt-5.6-sol, opening-ending): The volume ends after an extended run of fulfillment rather than on the promised public payoff or a destabilizing turn.  
  Evidence: Chapter 19 completes the dress and gives Vee another sexual culmination; Chapter 20 supplies the shoes; Chapter 21 brings Randi and Pace into domestic intimacy; Chapter 22 supplies vulnerable backstory and closes, “She had never been so fortunate, or so happy.” The induction and any collision with the secret remain ahead.  
  Question: What irreversible event makes this volume complete while also forcing the reader into Volume Two?
- **B6** (gpt-5.6-sol, pacing): The dress arc occupies enough of the volume to promise the induction itself as a climax, but the arc stops at preparation.  
  Evidence: Chapter 12 anticipates the house invitation; Chapter 13 introduces the silk; Chapter 14 devotes a full chapter to fitting; Chapter 15 retells it; Chapter 19 reveals the finished dress; Chapter 20 displays it and shops for shoes. The ceremony repeatedly named as the dress’s purpose does not occur.  
  Question: Is the true payoff the private transformation, the public entrance, or both—and has the volume ended at the intended one?

### C2. Vee's experiences are routinely retold to friends, and the retellings rarely add or change anything.

  Evidence: The truck scene is narrated in Ch 10, retold to Cassie at the end of Ch 10, and retold again in Ch 12 ("Okay, but the shirt. You have to tell the shirt."). The first half of Ch 15 repeats Ch 14 beat by beat (the bra, "the house was warm", the knees). Ch 17's Cassie scene is the exception: Vee withholds for the first time.  
  Question: Which retellings change the relationship between the people in the room, and could the rest be summarized in a sentence?
- **A7** (claude-opus-5-5, repetition): Randi's lunches repeat one template, and the repeated opening anecdote stops earning its place.  
  Evidence: Ch 9: "My love life is a disaster." Ch 15: "So I let this beautiful idiot take me home." Ch 17: "I ran into an old boyfriend on campus." Each anecdote is followed by drawing Vee out and an escalating goodbye. The goodbyes escalate; the anecdotes don't, and we never learn whether they are invented or disguised stories about Pace.  
  Question: Are these anecdotes cover stories, and could one of them visibly crack?
- **B4** (gpt-5.6-sol, repetition): The repeated partial-confession-to-Cassie/full-confession-to-Randi structure begins to feel procedural.  
  Evidence: Chapter 9 gives Cassie the date report and Randi the romantic report; Chapter 15 gives Cassie the fitting and Randi its erotic excavation; Chapter 17 gives Cassie an edited first-night account and Randi the intimate version. Each run is dominated by Vee recounting a prior scene rather than confronting a new event.  
  Question: Which retelling changes a relationship enough to require a full scene, and which can be shortened or replaced by aftermath?
