LETTER

Dear Helen,

A Polite Invitation does something most first volumes in this genre don't try: it builds a love triangle out of dramatic irony and runs it almost entirely through food, clothes and consent. Chapter 1 is a bold opening. The bench scene works as a seduction and as a portrait of a wound, and the turn on "There you are, Randi" changes everything the jacket has led us to expect. Randi's decision in the mirror is the strongest structural beam in the book: "He could have the face. The face was very good. The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." Protect it. My notes are about what the volume does with that beam after it's in place, in order of importance.

1. The volume ends before its own climax and gives the reader no push forward. From Chapter 10 on, the book builds toward one set piece: the russet dress becomes the burgundy silk, then the fitting, the finished gown, the shoes and the credit card. The induction never arrives. Neither does the threesome the game was started for, or the bench Randi asked about ("Do you think she'll like the bench?", Ch 3), or any threat that Vee will find out. Chapter 22 is a lovely, quiet backstory chapter, and it ends on "She had never been so fortunate, or so happy." Contentment can work as dramatic irony, but here nothing in the last two chapters points at a coming break. Either move the induction into this volume as its climax, or end on a beat that cocks the gun: Randi's jealousy, Brooke, Cassie, the debt, the scar.

2. Every event gets told two or three times. Vee has an experience, then tells Cassie, then often tells Randi, and then Randi reports to Pace. The Randi retellings earn their place because Randi is extracting information and the reader knows why. The others mostly don't. Chapter 12 retells the truck scene a third time ("Okay, but the shirt. You have to tell the shirt."). The first half of Chapter 15 retells Chapter 14 almost beat for beat. Chapter 17's Cassie scene does work, because Vee withholds from her for the first time. Cut or compress the retellings that only repeat, and keep the ones where something changes in the telling.

3. Randi goes dark as a viewpoint. She is the jacket's secret engine ("the first woman Randi has ever let herself want"), and she owns the most powerful interior moment in the book. Yet after the first half of Chapter 6 we never enter her head again. From then on we read her through Pace, who misreads her by design, and through Vee, who doesn't know what she's looking at. Her tells are beautifully planted: the thumbnail pressed under a cap or a glass rim (Ch 1, 15, 18), the white-gold band, the bitten knuckle. But the change the jacket promises happens almost entirely off the page. One Randi chapter late in the volume, Chapter 18 or Chapter 21 from her side, would make that promise real.

4. The risk of discovery is set up and then dropped. Chapter 2 plants a bomb: Brooke files Pace's name, Sloane knows the white pickup behind Hahn, and Randi sits on the sorority's Standards board. In Chapter 9 Brooke meets Vee and gets "Vee's my dear friend". After that the sorority disappears, even though Vee talks about "Pace" to dorm friends who share a campus with Randi's sisters. Readers can see this danger, and they need to feel it getting closer.

5. Pace's ethics never meet the deception underneath them. His whole creed is choice: "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything." (Ch 18). Yet in four chapters from his point of view he never once considers that Vee chose without the key fact, which is that their meeting in Chapter 5 was staged. If that blindness is the point, give him one moment where he nearly sees it. Otherwise readers will take it for the author's blind spot rather than his. The jacket's best line ("Every yes was freely given. That was the problem.") depends on this.

6. A few beats recur so often they've become mechanical. Randi's lunches follow one shape: she tells a comic story about a date gone wrong, draws Vee out, then says a warm goodbye. That happens in Chapter 9, 15 and 17. The escalation in the goodbyes (hug, then kiss, then a longer kiss) earns the repetition. The repeated opening anecdote does not. The mother's "floozie" voice turning into heat appears in Chapter 4, 10, 14, 16 and 19, and Chapter 16 alone uses it several times. The arc is real, but the device needs to change each time or appear less often.

7. Smaller items. The bench disappears even when Vee is in that bedroom. Chapter 18's fight about the timetable is never resolved on the page. Kayla and Theo are introduced and dropped. Cassie's ominous "Goodbye, Vee" is never paid off. Pace's two accounts of Daphne contradict each other. And there are several timeline slips, listed below.

What to protect: the food arc (Randi's portioning in Chapter 18 against her clean plate in Chapter 21), the planted clues Vee walks past (the two towels, the "foreign" accent that is really Kentucky), the math images that double as themes, and the humor inside the sex scenes. Readers will come back for those.

With admiration, and impatience for the induction.

FINDINGS
FINDING 1
CATEGORY: opening-ending
CHAPTERS: 10, 13-14, 19-22
SEVERITY: major
CLAIM: The volume's central build, the dress and the induction, peaks without being paid off, and the last chapter is resolved contentment with no forward hook.
EVIDENCE: The induction is set up in Ch 10 ("Still weeks off"), the dress is built in Ch 13-14, finished in Ch 19, and the shoes bought in Ch 20, but no induction scene ever comes. Ch 22 closes on "She had never been so fortunate, or so happy." The bench ("waiting for the next time," Ch 1; "Do you think she'll like the bench?", Ch 3) and the threesome never return.
QUESTION: Is the induction meant to open Book Two? If so, what in Chapters 21-22 tells the reader that something is about to break?

FINDING 2
CATEGORY: repetition
CHAPTERS: 9, 10, 12, 15, 17
SEVERITY: major
CLAIM: Vee's experiences are routinely retold to friends, and the retellings rarely add or change anything.
EVIDENCE: The truck scene is narrated in Ch 10, retold to Cassie at the end of Ch 10, and retold again in Ch 12 ("Okay, but the shirt. You have to tell the shirt."). The first half of Ch 15 repeats Ch 14 beat by beat (the bra, "the house was warm", the knees). Ch 17's Cassie scene is the exception: Vee withholds for the first time.
QUESTION: Which retellings change the relationship between the people in the room, and could the rest be summarized in a sentence?

FINDING 3
CATEGORY: pov
CHAPTERS: 1-2, 6, 7-22
SEVERITY: major
CLAIM: Randi's interior viewpoint, the volume's most powerful, disappears after the first half of Ch 6, so the change the jacket promises in her happens off the page.
EVIDENCE: Randi's viewpoint covers Ch 1 (the bathroom), Ch 2 and the first half of Ch 6. Ch 18 and 21 are from Pace, who misreads her ("He did not know what he had done," Ch 18). Everything else is from Vee. Her inner state is carried only by tells: "set the sharp inner edge of the cap under her thumbnail" (Ch 1), "set the edge of it in under the nail, bearing down" (Ch 15).
QUESTION: Where does Randi revisit her Chapter 1 vow from the inside, and why not let us see it?
