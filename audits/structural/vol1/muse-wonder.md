LETTER
Dear Helen,

The core engine of A Polite Invitation is strong, and it is also the volume's central structural danger. You have built a slow, deliberate erotic seduction in which Pace's patience, attentiveness and control of food, clothing, time and looking gradually allow Vee to inhabit a body she has been taught to apologize for. That build pays off: the fitting in Chapter 14, the first night in Chapter 16, the finished dress in Chapter 19 and the induction walk in Chapter 23 form a genuine arc from concealment to chosen display. When Vee thinks, "She took a breath, and made the second step long," the volume has its earned climax. Protect that progression.

The problem is that the book jacketed and structured as Vee's awakening begins as someone else's story, and for three chapters the reader is asked to invest in the wrong contract. Chapter 1 is an intense, accomplished Pace/Randi scene whose woman is not named as Randi until "There you are, Randi." near its end. Chapter 2 follows Randi back to the sorority; Chapter 3 only reaches Vee through the pointing game. Because the copy promises that Randi and Pace have “planned the best year of Vivienne Thorne's life,” a reader can eventually reconstruct the design, but inside the volume the opening asks us to bond with Randi as heroine, then replaces her with Vee in Chapter 4. That displacement is thematically suggestive — Vee is being chosen without knowing it — but it delays the protagonist whose transformation must carry a separately sold Book One.

The middle then repeatedly stops forward motion to recap motion already made. Chapter 9 retells Chapter 8 to Cassie and largely retells it again to Randi. Chapter 15 retells Chapter 14 first to Cassie, then in a more dangerous form to Randi. Chapter 17 again gives Cassie a bowdlerized account and Randi a fuller one. Individual scenes are good — especially Randi stopping Vee with "You don't have to tell me." — but the pattern becomes: event, Cassie hears the safe version, Randi extracts the erotic version. By the third iteration the reader knows more than Vee and is waiting for consequence rather than learning anything new about her desire. These chapters also thin Cassie into a device. Her early warnings are exact: "Goodbye, Vee." lands because Cassie sees replacement happening. Yet once she has registered the threat, the book increasingly leaves her in dorm-room reaction shots while Randi takes both the friendship plot and the erotic plot.

A related sag comes from the repetition of the courtship method. Pace chooses food, withholds haste, asks permission, listens, feeds, waits. The first applications are revelatory — “May I choose?” in Chapter 8 is a whole characterization in three words. But meals, walks, coffees, lunches, smoothies and brunches recur with the same emotional beat: Vee expects judgment, receives attention, loosens. Randi's lunches similarly recur as extraction scenes. The method is the meaning, up to a point; after that point it needs variation in cost. For much of Chapters 4-17 nobody can oppose the design except Vee's internalized mother-voice. Brooke threatens exposure in Chapters 2, 9 and 20 but never acts. Cassie notices but does not intervene. Pace's supervisor, Berkeley, Daphne and the scar arrive late and mostly as deferred biography. As a result the escalation is almost entirely sexual and psychological, while the external world remains oddly frictionless.

Randi's late escalation is therefore essential, and it works. The uneaten stew in Chapter 18, her insistence “How long are you going to take?”, the pillow-talk appropriation of Vee in Chapter 21, and the phone theft in Chapter 24 finally convert private jealousy into action. Chapter 24 is an excellent volume ending: it pays off Randi's watching, copying, shoe-buying and mouth-kissing in a violation that is intimate rather than merely cliffhanging. But it also exposes an imbalance. Randi ends the book as its most active agent; Vee ends it unaware that the decisive event has occurred. That is a fine hook into Book Two, less a complete Book One reckoning. The volume does satisfy its Vee/Pace build, but the advertised triangle — Randi wanting Vee, Vee never noticing “how much she wants Randi too” — remains mostly subtext, report and one-sided fantasy. If each volume must stand alone, the triangle needs a clearer in-volume payoff or confrontation before Randi crosses the phone line.

Finally, watch chronology and information load in the last quarter. Chapter 22 stops the Berkeley/induction climax to deliver Peter's name, age, childhood, Daphne, powerlifting and the scar in one porch conversation. Much is moving, especially “Less than staying,” but the density makes the chapter feel like a series bible being discharged before the finale. Pace is 22, a finishing PhD, a patentee, furniture-maker, tailor, powerlifter, cook and childhood prodigy. Each gift serves the erotic thesis — competence as attention — but together they strain the social world around a small graduate-student house, and the volume never lets another character seriously test the legend.

This is publishable material with a distinctive ethic of looking and consent. Its revision need is not more heat; it is compression of recaps, earlier consequence for Cassie and Brooke, and a decisive Vee/Randi beat that belongs to this volume rather than only to its hook.
