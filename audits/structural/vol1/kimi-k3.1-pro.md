LETTER

This volume has a real emotional climax and a strong final hook. Vee’s movement from concealment to deliberate visibility is carefully prepared, and her long second step at the induction pays off an arc built through the pool, the truck, the fitting, and her sexual encounters with Pace. Randi’s final intrusion into his phone is also an effective escalation: she stops merely harvesting Vee’s confidences and begins manufacturing intimacy between them. The problem is that these two climaxes do not quite resolve the dramatic project the opening establishes. The first three chapters make “for us” the central objective, but by the end Vee has neither discovered the game nor made a choice involving both Randi and Pace. The induction completes Vee/Pace’s courtship arc; the phone scene advances Randi’s obsession without Vee’s knowledge. As a separately sold volume, that leaves the originating triangle promising a further book rather than delivering a decisive turn of its own. I would not automatically answer that with an earlier threesome. I would ask what irreversible change in the three-person relationship this volume intends to complete.

The larger pacing issue is the recursive middle. Chapters 13–21 repeatedly move from an intense Vee/Pace encounter to a Cassie debrief, then a Randi debrief, then a Randi/Pace encounter in which the same information becomes erotic material. That design produces excellent dramatic irony, but not every retelling adds enough new pressure. Chapters 15 and 17 are especially similar in shape: Vee edits the experience for Cassie, supplies Randi with a more intimate account, misreads Randi’s physical tension, and receives affection that escalates toward a kiss. Around them, the fitting, first sex, and finished-dress sex are all substantial scenes. The result is not a lack of incident but a sense that each incident is being emotionally processed two or three times before the plot moves again. Preserve the confession structure, which is central to Randi’s power, but require each recurrence to alter what Vee conceals, what Randi risks, or what Pace misunderstands.

Pace needs more dramatic pressure as a character. His insistence that “She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it” sits directly against his covert selection of Vee and his disclosures of her private bodily details to Randi. That contradiction is potentially the book’s richest flaw, but he never approaches recognizing it, while his competence continues to accumulate: mathematician, patent holder, powerlifter, woodworker, cook, dressmaker, and exceptionally perceptive lover. Chapter 22 supplies wounds and history, but not a present-tense choice that costs him anything. His blindness to Randi is most convincing when rooted in his refusal to interpret closed doors; it becomes less convincing when the same man who reads minute changes in breathing repeatedly reduces her anger, hunger, and withdrawal to desire.

Cassie is the other major opportunity. Her early perception is sharp enough to promise an independent dramatic function: “Goodbye, Vee,” the question about alcohol, and the state-trooper jokes all establish protective skepticism. She subsequently becomes almost entirely a receptive audience. Her loyalty and the pool chapter are worth protecting, but the woman who notices what Vee edits should remain capable of noticing what Vee is losing—time, money, academic focus, and perspective.

Finally, chapter 22 arrives heavily laden with Daphne, Pace’s family, his age, his childhood exile, and the unexplained scar. It is a good intimate conversation, but it opens several doors immediately before the induction rather than deepening the one about to matter. Vee’s research ambitions and her shoe debt similarly recede after being given emotional weight. The ending itself should survive revision nearly intact, apart from its duplicated sexual blocking and the unestablished convenience of Randi knowing both the relevant birthday and Vee’s phone-code significance. The final image of her bag “over what it was keeping” is exactly the kind of hook this volume needs: quiet, actionable, and dangerous.

FINDINGS

FINDING 1
CATEGORY: opening-ending
CHAPTERS: 1-3, 20-24
SEVERITY: major
CLAIM: The volume completes Vee’s individual emergence but not a decisive turn in the triangular relationship established as its central plot.
EVIDENCE: Chapter 3 makes the objective explicit: “Go get her.” “For us?” “For us.” Chapters 20–24 then cover shoe shopping, Randi and Pace’s domestic intimacy, a long fireside conversation, Vee’s induction without either lover, and Randi’s secret phone intrusion; no scene lets Vee discover the selection or make a choice involving both of them. Chapter 18 is still asking, “How do I get her from where she is to us, on your schedule?”
QUESTION: What irreversible development in the three-person relationship do you want a reader to feel Book One itself has completed?

FINDING 2
CATEGORY: pacing
CHAPTERS: 13-21
SEVERITY: major
CLAIM: The middle loses momentum by repeatedly presenting an encounter and then processing it through two or three structurally similar retellings.
EVIDENCE: Chapters 13–14 give the house tour and fitting; chapter 15 retells it to Cassie and then Randi. Chapter 16 gives first sex; chapter 17 again gives separate Cassie and Randi debriefs. Chapter 18 converts the fallout into Randi/Pace sex, chapter 19 supplies another extended dress-and-sex encounter, chapter 20 opens with both friends hearing about it, and chapter 21 has Randi recount the shoe trip to Pace. Each scene has local value, but the sequence repeatedly restores anticipation rather than escalating it.
QUESTION: Which retellings genuinely change the balance among the three principals, and which merely confirm information the reader already possesses?

FINDING 3
CATEGORY: characterization
CHAPTERS: 3, 5-6, 11, 18, 21-22
SEVERITY: major
CLAIM: Pace’s consent ethic and his covert management of Vee are a fruitful contradiction, but he remains too static and too insulated from its consequences.
EVIDENCE: In chapter 18 he insists, “She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything.” Yet chapter 3 shows him directing the selection game, chapter 5 places him in the engineered café meeting, and chapter 11 has him offer Randi intimate sensory knowledge: “She wears gardenia,” followed by details of Vee’s hair and body. Chapter 22 explains his history but does not make him reconsider any present action.
QUESTION: Where in this volume can Pace’s self-conception become dramatically costly, rather than remaining a blindness only Randi and the reader can see?

STRENGTHS

STRENGTH 1
CHAPTERS: 4-23
CLAIM: Vee’s movement from managed concealment to chosen visibility is an earned, volume-length arc with a vivid physical climax.
EVIDENCE: In chapter 4, “She had worn the cardigan over the better shirt on purpose this morning.” The truck, fitting, first sex, and dress progressively enlarge her choices until chapter 23: “She took a breath, and made the second step long.” That action pays off character rather than merely costume.

STRENGTH 2
CHAPTERS: 1, 5, 8, 10, 16, 18, 21
CLAIM: Food and appetite provide a differentiated structural language for care, shame, power, and intimacy across both courtships.
EVIDENCE: Chapter 1 establishes Pace’s precision: “He had sliced it thin because she would eat thin slices and would not eat thick ones.” Chapter 8 turns “May I choose?” into trust, while chapter 18 leaves Randi’s small bowl untouched except for one line through the sauce. Chapter 21 reverses that image when she finishes everything and says, “That was lovely.”

STRENGTH 3
CHAPTERS: 7, 9-10, 15, 17
CLAIM: Cassie and Vee’s friendship supplies noncompetitive intimacy and gives Vee a believable refuge outside the seduction plot.
EVIDENCE: Chapter 7’s underwater withdrawal and subsequent water fight show care without interrogation: “You can't grieve and fight your best friend for a mouthful of pool at the same time; the splashing won.” Their later debriefs also reveal Vee’s self-editing through what she chooses not to say.

STRENGTH 4
CHAPTERS: 10, 13-15, 19-20, 23
CLAIM: The flannel and dress are effective serial objects, carrying memory, labor, class difference, erotic risk, and Vee’s changing self-image.
EVIDENCE: Chapter 13 makes the silk personal rather than merely expensive: “He'd matched *her*.” Chapter 20 gives Randi the apt formulation, “He didn't make this *for* you,” she said. “He made it *to* you.” The completed dress then organizes the shoe expedition, Pace’s absence, the induction, and Randi’s final theft of the photograph.

STRENGTH 5
CHAPTERS: 1-3, 6, 11, 15, 18, 21, 24
CLAIM: Randi’s transformation is shown through controlled changes in appetite, sexual behavior, speech, and thresholds rather than explained all at once.
EVIDENCE: Chapter 2 says her not-crying “felt like there being no door.” Chapter 11 has her leave Pace for the first time instead of staying overnight. Chapter 18 removes her customary “Tell me,” while chapter 21 permits the admission, “She got past me before I could help it.” Chapter 24 turns that accumulated hunger into action.

STRENGTH 6
CHAPTERS: 4-6, 9, 11, 15, 17-18
CLAIM: The confession-and-retelling structure creates potent dramatic irony because Vee experiences generosity while the reader watches information become leverage.
EVIDENCE: Vee’s accounts return in Randi and Pace’s bedroom through the repeated “Tell me” of chapters 6 and 11. In chapter 15 Randi’s gentle “You don't have to tell me” closes the very opening Vee was about to use, demonstrating her control without requiring an explicit explanation.

STRENGTH 7
CHAPTERS: 23-24
CLAIM: The final phone sequence is an excellent next-volume hook because Randi’s deception leaves evidence outside her control.
EVIDENCE: Vee answers the fabricated message with “I'm keeping this forever,” and Randi deletes the exchange only from Pace’s phone; Vee’s own thread remains. Randi also retains the induction photograph after sending it to herself. The closing image—“in it her phone had gone dark again over what it was keeping”—gives her a concrete object, a new intention, and a danger the reader can anticipate.
