LETTER

Dear Helen,

There is remarkable psychological and sensual intelligence on the page in A Polite Invitation. At the scene level, you write desire as an extension of character: Vee's movement from inherited bodily shame to chosen exhibition, Randi's split between polished control and terrifying aliveness, and the class anxieties quietly governing Vee's choices all feel lived-in and exact. When the explicit scenes arrive, they earn their length because every physical beat shifts power or self-knowledge. My structural notes below are aimed at making sure Volume 1 delivers a complete, cohesive dramatic experience on its own terms while setting up Book Two cleanly.

The largest issue facing the manuscript is that Volume 1 defers its central premise past the last page. Both the jacket copy and Chapters 1 through 3 promise a three-person erotic and psychological entanglement set in motion around Pace's spanking bench and Randi's newly exposed hunger for another woman. Yet after Pace stages his five-minute coffee-shop introduction in Chapter 5, the three principals never share a room again for the remaining twenty chapters of the book. Chapters 4 through 24 function almost entirely as a sweet, slow-burn dyadic courtship between Pace and Vee, interrupted periodically by Randi's separate lunches with Vee and nighttime visits to Pace. While Vee's solo arc—the making and wearing of the burgundy gown at her induction in Chapter 24—pays off handsomely, and Chapter 25 delivers a sharp cliffhanger into Book Two, the triad itself barely budges. The bench that anchors Chapter 1 and closes Chapter 3 never re-opens; Randi's seduction of Vee goes no further than two brief goodbye kisses and a thumb across a toe; and the jacket's thematic promise that every yes was freely given and that this was the problem never becomes a problem for Vee in this volume, since she ends Chapter 24 ecstatic and wholly ignorant of the scheme. Readers buying Book One separately need a first-stage triad payoff—at minimum, all three characters in the same room under real erotic and dramatic pressure—before the curtain falls.

Closely tied to that deferral is a severe point-of-view imbalance. Vee carries sixteen of the twenty-five chapters, Pace carries roughly seven, and Randi receives only one full POV chapter (Chapter 2) plus three brief split-chapter sections, disappearing completely from interiority across nineteen consecutive chapters (Chapters 7 through 24). Shutting the reader out of Randi's head for four-fifths of the novel forces her crucial transformation—from playing a defensive game to genuinely falling for Vee—offstage, reduced to dialogue reports in Chapter 22. Meanwhile, Pace has POV chapters only with Randi and never with Vee. That partition insulates Pace from any internal reckoning with the lie he is running on a woman he claims must choose every step freely, while creating a sharp characterization inconsistency: the preternaturally perceptive dominant of Chapters 1 and 3 becomes conveniently obtuse in Chapters 11, 16, 19, and 22 whenever Randi is visibly hurting in front of him. At the same time, stacking Pace's resume—twenty-two-year-old finishing math PhD prodigy, licensed medical-imaging patent holder, competitive powerlifter, fine cabinetmaker, gourmet cook, BDSM furniture engineer, and overnight couture silk dressmaker—pushes him toward idealized wish-fulfillment rather than human proportion.

Pacing across the first two-thirds also suffers from tonal whiplash and systemic scene repetition. Opening at maximum kink intensity on the bench in Chapter 1 and dropping into thirteen chapters of chaste college courtship (Chapters 4 through 16), with the kink register vanishing for the rest of the volume, strands the erotic promise of the opening. That middle stretch is further slowed by a recurring recap loop: Vee experiences a milestone with Pace, immediately recounts it to Cassie in the dorm (Chapters 9, 10, 15, and 18) or to dorm friends Meg and Kayla (Chapter 12), and then recounts the same event again to Randi over smoothies or lunch (Chapters 9, 15, and 18). Randi's lunches do vital dramatic work through interrogation and subtext, whereas the dorm and dining-hall recaps mostly replay scenes the reader has just watched. On the other side of the story, the six nighttime Pace-and-Randi chapters (Chapters 6, 11, 16, 19, 22, and 25) lean heavily on the same house-and-bed ritual, and the open rift that finally erupts between them in Chapter 19 evaporates off-page before Chapter 22.

Several late-volume plot mechanics and timeline details also need tightening. Chapter 25's emotional gut-punch relies on Pace having changed his phone passcode from Randi's birthday to Vee's, yet Vee never tells Pace her birthday on the page, Pace's own POV never registers replacing Randi, and Randi recalls the exact digits from a fleeting glance at Vee's driver's license ten days earlier in Chapter 18. Minutes later, Randi deletes her forged text and Vee's thrilled replies from Pace's phone believing she has restored everything to how she found it, overlooking the obvious fact that Vee still holds the messages on her own phone and will inevitably mention them to Pace. Earlier, in Chapter 19, Randi accuses Pace of keeping Vee from Friday to Sunday every weekend, when Vee has spent only one Friday night with him in the entire book. And in Chapters 18 and 23, twin last-minute scheduling emergencies—Brooke's officer meeting and a supervisor's premature baby—conveniently pull both Randi and Pace out of Vee's induction, sparing the plot the very collision it has been building toward.

Finally, a handful of secondary threads and world details—Brooke's sorority oversight vanishing after Chapter 9, Kayla's promised Saturday hair session disappearing between Chapters 12 and 13, Randi's unexplained bolt from brunch in Chapter 18, three separate deferred Pace backstory mysteries in Chapter 23, sorority chapter night shifting from Friday to Thursday, and curbside vest-wearing valet service at Roanoke's small regional airport—need either follow-through or pruning.

As you revise, protect Vee's physical arc around the dress, the class friction in her friendship with Randi, the dual-layered tension of their lunches, and the devastating opening bench-and-mirror sequence. Those elements are already doing extraordinary work; bringing the triad itself to a genuine Volume 1 reckoning will let the whole book match them.

Warmly,
Your Editor
